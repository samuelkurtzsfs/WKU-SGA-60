12 September 2013

Senate passed international student seat amendment

President Keyana Boka and executive vice president Mark Reeves authored a constitutional amendment adding a senate seat for Navitas or English as a Second Language international students. The Judicial Council had ruled an earlier version unconstitutional, but after a revision in an emergency meeting the Senate passed it with only two votes against. Boka said it would make WKU's SGA the first student government in the state with an international senate seat.

In the recordHerald, 12 Sep 2013

4 April 2013

Boka, Reeves and Seay won the spring executive elections

Executive vice president Keyana Boka was elected 2013-14 SGA president with 626 votes, 47 percent, over administrative vice president Cain Alvey with 419 votes and Austin Wingate with 300. Mark Reeves won executive vice president with 632 votes, 57 percent, over Brad Cockrel, and Nicki Seay ran unopposed for administrative vice president. Twenty-six student senators were also elected.

In the recordHerald, 4 Apr 2013

11 October 2011Tradition

Campus Clean-up ran during Make A Difference Week

Campus improvements chair Keyana Boka organised the Campus Clean-up for 11 October 2011, with service credit offered and gloves donated by the biology department. Seventeen senators volunteered alongside student athletes and a fraternity pledge class, and the group worked from Centennial Mall up the Hill to Cherry Hall and back to DUC. Boka said the clean-up ran twice a year, in Make A Difference Week each autumn and during Greek Week each spring.

6 February 2014

SGA plans a Frankfort rally over WKU's $1.8 million budget cut

At the first spring meeting President Keyana Boka announced SGA would organize a trip to Frankfort called "Rally for Retention" in response to WKU's $1.8 million budget cut, with speeches and one-on-one meetings with legislators. Executive Vice President Mark Reeves outlined the two-year state budget then being voted on and said students needed to be involved. Boka also reported talks with dining services about 24-hour dining, with Einstein's Bagels under consideration for its location in a 24-hour study area.

In the recordHerald, 6 Feb 2014

3 December 2013

Talisman brings its funding shortfall to SGA

Talisman representatives met President Keyana Boka and Brad Cockrel on Nov. 29 to discuss long-term options for paying for the yearbook. The previous May the Talisman had faced a proposed $93,000 cut, 47 percent of its $198,000 budget, before an alumni-backed "Save the Talisman" campaign reduced the cut. Its budget stood at $127,000, including $45,000 in one-time university funds.

In the recordHerald, 3 Dec 2013

20 November 2013

South Campus forum turns on the closing of the campus store

SGA held its weekly meeting at South Campus as an open forum and President Keyana Boka asked those present to discuss the recent closing of the South Campus WKU store. Senators debated the lack of communication between the officials who closed the store and South Campus students. Executive Vice President Mark Reeves said the same thing had happened the previous year when the food court closed, calling it part of a broader failure to consult South Campus students.

In the recordHerald, 20 Nov 2013

9 April 2013

Judicial Council disqualified president-elect Boka, 3-2

In an emergency meeting, SGA's Judicial Council voted 3-2 to disqualify president-elect Keyana Boka after runner-up and outgoing administrative vice president Cain Alvey complained that a self-promotional email had gone to Honors College students at Boka's request, sent by Honors College graduate assistant Destiny Savage, violating the election code. The disqualification would have made Alvey, who had placed second in the April 4 vote, the incoming president; Boka said she would appeal.

23 September 2010

Twelve senators elected in fall election with 847 voters

Twelve students won seats in the fall senate election, in which 847 total voters cast ballots on TopNet. Keyana Boka, Natalie Broderick and Eppiphanie Benton were elected freshman senators, running unopposed, while 25 students competed for the at-large seats. Winners included Diego Leal Ambriz, who would run for president that spring.

In the recordHerald, 23 Sep 2010

9 November 2011

Senate defeats DUC name change resolution 19-8

A resolution to rename Downing University Center as Downing Student Union, written by campus improvements chair Keyana Boka, was voted down 19-8 with one abstention after a lengthy open debate. Executive Vice President Kendrick Bryan suggested putting the question on the spring ballot, but senators noted architects working on the DUC renovation would need an answer sooner.

In the recordHerald, 9 Nov 2011

3 September 2013

Judicial Council strikes down the international senate seat amendment

SGA announced that the Judicial Council had ruled unconstitutional a constitutional amendment proposed by President Keyana Boka and Executive Vice President Mark Reeves. The amendment would have changed the senate's composition by adding one seat for an international student. The ruling was reported to the senate at its second meeting of the semester.

24 October 2013

SGA spends $1,598 on five charging stations with the libraries

SGA and WKU Libraries agreed to buy five KwikBoost phone and laptop charging stations for $1,598, each with eight charging attachments. President Keyana Boka said Dean of University Libraries Connie Foster brought her the idea. Three stations were planned for the Educational Resources Center, Helm 100 and Helm 2, with the other two going to the Glasgow and Owensboro campuses.

28 March 2013

Presidential debate drew full field of candidates

Ahead of the spring 2013 election, outgoing president Cory Dodds opened a debate in Ransdell Hall Auditorium among presidential candidates Cain Alvey, Keyana Boka and Austin Wingate, plus executive vice presidential candidates Brad Cockrel and Mark Reeves and administrative vice presidential candidate Nicki Seay. Boka said SGA members needed better training in their roles, Alvey said the presidency's responsibilities went beyond the constitution, and Wingate, a former Speaker of the Senate, called for better representation across student groups.

May 2014

SGA suspends its bylaws to clear bills at the last meeting

At the final meeting of the semester SGA suspended its bylaws to pass multiple bills on first read, beginning with Bill 17-14-S allocating $1,817.10. President Keyana Boka reported a 4.8 percent tuition increase that would cost most students about $209 more per semester, along with a $4.2 million increase for student financial assistance and a one percent faculty salary adjustment. Speaker Paige Settles said one remaining scholarship bill would be handled at a short meeting during the end-of-year banquet.

13 February 2014

Executive Council voided the amendment stripping the president's judicial appointments

An amendment removing the SGA president's sole power to appoint judicial council justices and raising the council to seven justices passed at the Tuesday meeting, over President Keyana Boka's objection that it bypassed both presidential appointment and the senate. Boka said the president is elected by the student body to make appointments in all three branches and that she was wary of the direction, while Chief Justice Seth Church argued that vesting appointments in the executive branch as a whole created a fairer system. After the meeting the Executive Council reviewed the constitution, found too few senators had attended for the vote to count and declared the amendment unconstitutional, sending it back to first read. The SGA office had temporarily moved to Garrett after flooding in Cravens.

Campus Cleanup and Safety Walk on the same afternoon

SGA ran its spring Campus Cleanup from 4 to 5 p.m. on 17 April 2012 and held the Campus Safety Walk immediately after the senate meeting the same evening. Campus improvements chair Keyana Boka said the two were paired because neither took long. On the walk members noted dim areas and blue emergency lights that were not working.

30 March 2012

Dodds elected SGA president for 2012-13; Boka and Alvey win uncontested VP races

Cory Dodds was elected SGA president for 2012-13 with 44 percent of the vote, over senator Corey Johnson with 35 percent and former senator Austin Wingate with 21 percent. Sophomore Keyana Boka was elected executive vice president and Cain Alvey administrative vice president; both vice-presidential races were uncontested.

21 April 2014Festival

SGA opened Earth Week by planting the community garden

SGA and the Office of Sustainability kicked off Earth Week on 21 April 2014 with a planting party in the community garden behind the sustainability house at 503 Regents Avenue. President Keyana Boka said she was glad SGA could support the garden, which SGA's campus improvement committee then took on maintaining, growing tomatoes, squash, sunflowers and peppers. 19 March 2013

SGA passed a resolution opposing the $150 study abroad application fee

The senate passed a resolution opposing a new $150 application fee for Study Abroad and Global Learning, due to begin with summer 2013 programs, arguing it could put program costs out of reach. Executive Vice President Keyana Boka said SGA took a formal stance because the fee had come up so suddenly, and the University Senate passed a similar resolution seeking to delay the fee until fall 2013. A separate amendment proposed by Dodds, to make SGA members ineligible for SGA-awarded study abroad scholarships, failed. Dodds said 11 of the 125 such scholarships and grants awarded that year had gone to SGA members, which he called a conflict of interest.

5 December 2012Tradition

Dine with Decision Makers moved to the Kentucky Museum

SGA held Dine with Decision Makers at the Kentucky Museum on 5 December 2012, seating officials from across Kentucky and WKU administrators at tables matched to students by interest or field. President Cory Dodds opened the evening and executive vice president Keyana Boka said the prayer. Wadsworth, Ohio senior Sarah Brazier gave the keynote, arguing that higher education built a productive economy and an informed citizenry rather than only advancing individuals.

iPad lending programme opened with the Educational Resources Center

SGA and the Educational Resources Center launched an iPad-lending programme giving students, staff and faculty access to tablets loaded with test preparation applications such as MCAT materials. 2 April 2015

Campaign spending caps range from $350 for president to $50 for senators

Under the spring 2014 election packet, SGA presidential candidates could not spend more than $350, executive vice president candidates $300, administrative vice president candidates $250 and senatorial candidates $50. President Nicki Taylor said she ran unopposed and did not spend a cent, but pointed to the contested 2013 race between Cain Alvey and Keyana Boka. Taylor said Alvey spent every dollar of his $350 limit.

Senate bought T-shirts for the autumn Campus Clean-up

The senate unanimously approved funding on 9 October 2012 for promotional T-shirts for volunteers at the Campus Clean-up on 23 October, timed with Make a Difference Day. 10 May 2013

Budget cuts strip SGA's automatic inflation increase

University budget cuts announced that week removed the automatic increases that SGA, the Campus Activities Board and leadership programs normally received under the Higher Education Price Index, saving the university $8,000. Charley Pride, director of Student Activities and SGA's adviser, said no money was taken away, which was better than losing money. Incoming President Keyana Boka said the loss could affect new programs SGA had wanted to start.

What this is

A year-by-year record of student government at Western Kentucky University, from the ratification of the Associated Students constitution in April 1966 to the present. Every entry names the source it came from.

Corrections

The names here start with the plaque in the SGA Chambers, and the plaque is known to be wrong in places. Where the archive disagrees with it, this site follows the archive and records the change on the corrections page. If you served, or you know that a year here is wrong, the project wants to hear from you through the Student Government Association office.

What it rests on

Everything here comes from what is publicly available online: the digitised Herald and SGA's own papers on TopSCHOLAR, the Talisman yearbooks, wkuherald.com, the university's news pages and the Wayback Machine. That is a fraction of what happened. Student government has met most weeks for sixty years, and the greater part of it was never written down, or was written down on paper that has never been scanned. A thin year here is a thin record, not a quiet one.
