21 September 2022

Fall election turnout doubled and vice president Garrison Reed joined the state postsecondary council

Fall 2022 election results, announced at midnight, seated Rush Robinson, Callison Padgett and Rhett Helland as freshman senators, Neel Patel for the Gatton Academy, Shy'Ante Williams as graduate senator and Salvador Leon Golib as international student senator. President Cole Bornefeld said the vote total rose from 398 the previous fall to 805. SGA also announced that student body vice president Garrison Reed had been appointed student representative on Kentucky's Council on Postsecondary Education, with a term running through 30 June 2023.

30 August 2023

Kurtz seated a five-member Judicial Council and seven committee heads

At SGA's first meeting of the fall semester, the senate approved President Sam Kurtz's nominations of Ashley Ranger, Anna Kate Hardin, Ellen Henderson, Morgan Gammons and Garrison Reed to the 2023-24 Judicial Council. Kurtz said a record number of students had applied and were interviewed over the summer before the field was narrowed to five. He also announced seven committee heads, among them Rush Robinson for Campus Improvements and Sustainability and Karley Solorzano for Diversity, Equity and Inclusion. The new appointees took the oath of office at the meeting.

In the recordHerald, 30 Aug 2023

20 September 2023

First bill of the 23rd Senate funded the Pink Walk

At its fifth meeting the 23rd Senate passed its first piece of legislation, Bill 1-23-F, allocating $500 from SGA's scholarship budget to the SGA Pink Walk, an October breast cancer awareness event for students and the Bowling Green community. The money covered bracelets, ribbons, water and raffle prizes. The bill was written by Donte Reed, Rush Robinson and Anne-Marie Wright and passed unanimously, 28-0.

In the recordHerald, 20 Sep 2023

27 September 2023

Fall election winners sworn in

SGA announced its fall election results on Sept. 20 over an Instagram livestream, electing Savanna Kurtz, Malick Ibrahim and Chloe Ralston as freshman senators, ShyAntye'e Williams as graduate senator, Sim Guan Zhou as international senator and Livi Ray as Gatton Academy senator. Five of the six were sworn in at the following Tuesday meeting. Sophomore senator Rush Robinson received the year's first Senator of the Month award.

22 February 2024

Senate backs a $5 per semester sustainability fee

Resolution 26-23-S, written by Sophomore Senator Rush Robinson, put SGA behind charging students a $5 sustainability fee each semester with the proceeds going to the Office of Sustainability. Robinson also authored Bill 25-23-S, giving that office $500 from the legislative discretionary budget for the food pantry, scholarships and an Earth Day event. Both passed unanimously.

5 March 2024

Rally trip to Frankfort, new voucher and cleanup plans

Kurtz previewed a senate trip to Frankfort for the Rally for Higher Education, where SGA planned to meet state Rep. Kevin Jackson and other Kentucky campus governments, and urged senators to seek re-election to preserve institutional knowledge. Chief of Staff Donté Reed introduced a need-based scholarship awarding four Uber vouchers and the Community Builder Excellence Scholarship for incoming Bowling Green-area freshmen. Campus Improvements and Sustainability chair Rush Robinson moved Earth Day from April 22 to April 19 and set a tentative Bowling Green cleanup for March 28.

3 September 2024

Senate passes bill declaring SGA nonpartisan

Bill 50-23-S reaffirmed that SGA represents the student body rather than acting as a political group and stated its duty to put forward only nonpartisan legislation. It passed unanimously. The same meeting named Rush Robinson chair of the Campus Improvements and Sustainability Committee, swore in Hadley Whipple as Secretary of the Senate and added Hailey Stapleton to the Judicial Council.

29 October 2024Tradition

Hill-O-Ween drew just over a hundred students

SGA held Hill-O-Ween at Centennial Mall on 29 October 2024, handing out sweets and running activities with the Hilltopper Organization of Latin American Students and the Accessibility Ambassadors. Students painted succulent pots and made candy apples. Junior senator Rush Robinson, who organised it, counted just over 100 people. At the meeting that evening the senate allocated $325 for a Pack the Pantry food drive run with WKU ROTC through November.

4 April 2025

Senate renamed the DEI Committee the Action and Opportunity Committee

Senators unanimously passed Bill 21-25-S, a constitutional amendment restructuring the Diversity, Equity and Inclusion Committee as the Action and Opportunity Committee and retitling Chief Diversity Officers as Chief Engagement and Opportunity Officers, following President Trump's executive order dismantling DEI mandates. Rush Robinson moved to table the bill to the fall, but senators chose to vote; with fewer than the 21 members needed for a two-thirds vote present, absent members were called onto Zoom to reach quorum. Co-author Gammons called the change mostly a formality and said a loss of federal funding had been alluded to as a possible issue. The amendment then went to a student referendum during the 13-15 April election.

16 April 2025

Student body ratifies DEI committee amendment with 88% support

Bill 21-25-S, renaming the DEI Committee the Action and Opportunity Committee, passed the campus-wide constitutional vote with 88% in favor. Rush Robinson was elected student body president unopposed on a ticket with Savanna Kurtz and Gabriel Jerdon, and 31 senators were named. DEI Committee Chair Megan Farmer said the committee's core work was not changing and that the new name better communicated its actions.

Hadley Whipple won the Speaker of the Senate vote over Caden Lucas and Sophia Bryant at the first meeting of the 25th Senate. 19 August 2025

Robinson opens the year with Pearce Ford Tower air conditioning

At the first meeting of the fall, President Rush Robinson said Executive Director for Housing and Residence Life Catherine LaRoche had assured him the Pearce Ford Tower HVAC system was being repaired and that residents could move temporarily to Hugh Poland Hall. Robinson said 29 of the more than 600 PFT residents had chosen to relocate, and that a chiller unit was also being arranged. Karlee Powell was elected Secretary of the Senate.

October 2025Tradition

Big Red Rise gave seniors a sunrise breakfast

SGA held the first Big Red Rise on the morning of 1 October 2025, gathering students on a dark South Lawn to watch the sunrise over doughnuts and coffee before fall break. President Rush Robinson said freshmen were already celebrated with Topper Walk and M.A.S.T.E.R. Plan, and that Big Red Rise was made as a send-off for graduating seniors each semester. He called it a trial run and said SGA hoped to hold another sunrise or sunset event in the spring.

28 April 2026

26th Senate seated; tie vote for speaker resolved in Butler's favor

At the first meeting of the 26th Senate, outgoing president Rush Robinson passed the traditional red suit jacket to Caden Lucas, and the body swore in Gabi Pace as chief of staff, Cayden Bailey as chief communications officer, Maggie Phelps to the judicial council and defeated presidential candidate Jaden Marshall as an at-large senator. The race for speaker of the senate ended in a 9-9 tie between Amelia Tucker and Veronica Butler, and Chief Justice Sophie Sterling confirmed that neither the constitution nor the bylaws addressed a tie. Outgoing speaker Hadley Whipple ordered a re-vote, which elected Butler as speaker.

In the recordHerald, 28 Apr 2026

5 June 2026

Regents raise tuition over the student regent's lone no vote

The Board of Regents approved a $204 tuition increase, of 1.69 per cent, taking tuition to $6,240 a semester, the sixth consecutive yearly rise, alongside a 5 per cent housing increase and a 2 per cent meal plan increase within a $405 million operating budget. Every regent voted for it except outgoing SGA president Rush Robinson, who said the board had to do everything it could to preserve affordability. It was his last meeting as student regent before Caden Lucas took the seat. The board also suspended several majors, kinesiology and geological sciences among them, for reworking and mergers.

What this is

A year-by-year record of student government at Western Kentucky University, from the ratification of the Associated Students constitution in April 1966 to the present. Every entry names the source it came from.

Corrections

The names here start with the plaque in the SGA Chambers, and the plaque is known to be wrong in places. Where the archive disagrees with it, this site follows the archive and records the change on the corrections page. If you served, or you know that a year here is wrong, the project wants to hear from you through the Student Government Association office.

What it rests on

Everything here comes from what is publicly available online: the digitised Herald and SGA's own papers on TopSCHOLAR, the Talisman yearbooks, wkuherald.com, the university's news pages and the Wayback Machine. That is a fraction of what happened. Student government has met most weeks for sixty years, and the greater part of it was never written down, or was written down on paper that has never been scanned. A thin year here is a thin record, not a quiet one.
