12 September 2013

Senate passed international student seat amendment

President Keyana Boka and executive vice president Mark Reeves authored a constitutional amendment adding a senate seat for Navitas or English as a Second Language international students. The Judicial Council had ruled an earlier version unconstitutional, but after a revision in an emergency meeting the Senate passed it with only two votes against. Boka said it would make WKU's SGA the first student government in the state with an international senate seat.

10 September 2013

Executive Council floated plus/minus grading, set fall calendar

At the Executive Council's weekly meeting, Executive Vice President Mark Reeves reported that the University Senate Council had discussed reviving a plus/minus grading system and that Provost Gordon Emslie was interested in SGA's support. The council set a Nov. 9 Picnic with the President for the presidents' council and deans, and swore in Samantha Johnson to an executive cabinet seat vacated by the resignation of Keith Watson.

3 September 2013

Judicial Council strikes down the international senate seat amendment

SGA announced that the Judicial Council had ruled unconstitutional a constitutional amendment proposed by President Keyana Boka and Executive Vice President Mark Reeves. The amendment would have changed the senate's composition by adding one seat for an international student. The ruling was reported to the senate at its second meeting of the semester.

In the recordHerald, 3 Sep 2013

4 April 2013

Boka, Reeves and Seay won the spring executive elections

Executive vice president Keyana Boka was elected 2013-14 SGA president with 626 votes, 47 percent, over administrative vice president Cain Alvey with 419 votes and Austin Wingate with 300. Mark Reeves won executive vice president with 632 votes, 57 percent, over Brad Cockrel, and Nicki Seay ran unopposed for administrative vice president. Twenty-six student senators were also elected.

In the recordHerald, 4 Apr 2013

26 April 2013

SGA votes to end the automatic annual athletic fee increase

The senate passed a resolution supporting an end to the automatic annual increase in the student athletic fee once WKU joined Conference USA on July 1, 2014. Senator and executive vice president-elect Mark Reeves, who initiated it, argued that increased revenues made the automatic increase unnecessary. The resolution stated that WKU athletics paid $80,000 in the Sun Belt Conference but received only $40,000 back in revenue sharing.

In the recordHerald, 26 Apr 2013

28 March 2013

Presidential debate drew full field of candidates

Ahead of the spring 2013 election, outgoing president Cory Dodds opened a debate in Ransdell Hall Auditorium among presidential candidates Cain Alvey, Keyana Boka and Austin Wingate, plus executive vice presidential candidates Brad Cockrel and Mark Reeves and administrative vice presidential candidate Nicki Seay. Boka said SGA members needed better training in their roles, Alvey said the presidency's responsibilities went beyond the constitution, and Wingate, a former Speaker of the Senate, called for better representation across student groups.

In the recordHerald, 28 Mar 2013

6 February 2014

SGA plans a Frankfort rally over WKU's $1.8 million budget cut

At the first spring meeting President Keyana Boka announced SGA would organize a trip to Frankfort called "Rally for Retention" in response to WKU's $1.8 million budget cut, with speeches and one-on-one meetings with legislators. Executive Vice President Mark Reeves outlined the two-year state budget then being voted on and said students needed to be involved. Boka also reported talks with dining services about 24-hour dining, with Einstein's Bagels under consideration for its location in a 24-hour study area.

25 September 2012

Fall senate seats filled after two resignations

Two senators resigned over time commitments and student Shea Wyatt was named to fill in. New freshman senators Barrett Greenwell, Taylor Ruby, Cole McDowell and Paige Settles were sworn in along with Gatton Academy senator Linda Cruz and graduate senators Mark Reeves and Katie Martin.

In the recordHerald, 27 Sep 2012

5 November 2013

Herald editorial faulted SGA over the priority registration policy

A Herald editorial accused SGA of failing to represent students on the new priority registration policy, which capped students with priority registration at 16 hours on their priority day and exempted seniors and graduate students. The editorial said executive vice president Mark Reeves voted for the policy at the University Senate on 10 September without SGA questioning or debating it first. It also faulted SGA for scheduling an open forum for 12 November, after the policy was already set to govern fall registration. Representing students was SGA's one job, the editorial argued, and it had not been done on this issue.

In the recordHerald, 5 Nov 2013

25 April 2013

Senate opposed automatic athletic-fee increases ahead of C-USA move

The senate passed a resolution against the annual automatic increase in the student athletic fee, arguing the revenue-sharing WKU would gain on joining Conference USA that July would outweigh the roughly $40,000 the university lost annually in the Sun Belt. Executive vice president-elect Mark Reeves, who introduced it, said the resolution set a precedent against automatic fee increases generally.

In the recordHerald, 26 Apr 2013

20 November 2013

South Campus forum turns on the closing of the campus store

SGA held its weekly meeting at South Campus as an open forum and President Keyana Boka asked those present to discuss the recent closing of the South Campus WKU store. Senators debated the lack of communication between the officials who closed the store and South Campus students. Executive Vice President Mark Reeves said the same thing had happened the previous year when the food court closed, calling it part of a broader failure to consult South Campus students.

In the recordHerald, 20 Nov 2013

What this is

A year-by-year record of student government at Western Kentucky University, from the ratification of the Associated Students constitution in April 1966 to the present. Every entry names the source it came from.

Corrections

The names here start with the plaque in the SGA Chambers, and the plaque is known to be wrong in places. Where the archive disagrees with it, this site follows the archive and records the change on the corrections page. If you served, or you know that a year here is wrong, the project wants to hear from you through the Student Government Association office.

What it rests on

Everything here comes from what is publicly available online: the digitised Herald and SGA's own papers on TopSCHOLAR, the Talisman yearbooks, wkuherald.com, the university's news pages and the Wayback Machine. That is a fraction of what happened. Student government has met most weeks for sixty years, and the greater part of it was never written down, or was written down on paper that has never been scanned. A thin year here is a thin record, not a quiet one.
