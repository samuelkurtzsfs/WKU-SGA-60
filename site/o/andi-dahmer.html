10 April 2017

Judicial Council exemptions turn the presidential race into a three-way contest

The Judicial Council granted constitutional exemptions under Article 2.2.5 to Lily Nellans and Kenan Mujkanovic, both of whom had served less than one semester, allowing them to run against Andi Dahmer for president. A Herald editorial argued the rulings set a precedent letting candidates with no SGA experience seek the presidency and the accompanying seat on the Board of Regents. Only Dahmer had been eligible under the constitution as written.

In the recordHerald, 10 Apr 2017

19 April 2017

Andi Dahmer elected to succeed Richey

Louisville sophomore Andi Dahmer, a senator and chair of the MyCampusToo committee, was elected the next student body president, winning 930 of 1,579 votes cast against Kenan Mujkanovic's 305 and Lily Nellans's 212. Her running mates Savannah Molyneaux and Kara Lowry won executive vice president and administrative vice president. Dahmer said she would start with legislation on study abroad affordability and sensitivity training to improve retention among marginalized groups.

In the recordHerald, 19 Apr 2017

19 April 2017

Dahmer elected; all six referendums pass

Andi Dahmer won the presidency with 930 of 1,579 votes cast, ahead of Kenan Mujkanovic at 305 and Lily Nellans at 212, with Savannah Molyneaux and Kara Lowry elected to the vice presidencies. Students ratified all six referendums, making the SAVES, MyCampusToo and Sustainability committees permanent, lowering the GPA requirement to 2.0, creating regional campus ambassadors and requiring more accessible executive discretionary spending reports.

In the recordHerald, 19 Apr 2017

17 August 2017

Dahmer joined national solidarity letter after Charlottesville

President Andi Dahmer was one of more than 120 college student body presidents nationwide who signed an open letter backing the University of Virginia after the violent Charlottesville protests, and said developing campus-wide "cultural competency training" was a goal for the semester.

In the recordHerald, 17 Aug 2017

24 October 2017

Dahmer condemned a blackface Halloween incident at senate meeting

President Andi Dahmer opened the eighth meeting of the Senate by telling members that a student had worn blackface to a Halloween party the previous weekend and that it was not acceptable. She also hosted student body presidents from other Kentucky universities on campus that weekend and reminded senators that Herald reporters were allowed into all university committee meetings.

31 October 2017Programme

DACA Day of Action funded at $100

The senate unanimously approved Bill 21-17-F on 31 October 2017, giving $100 for a DACA Day of Action hosted by the Hilltopper Organization of Latin American Students with the Center for Citizenship and Social Justice. The all-day event had students write to legislators urging passage of a clean DREAM Act, and the money bought paper, pens, pencils, food, drinks and posters. Bowling Green junior Francisco Serrano co-authored it with President Andi Dahmer.

9 November 2017

Narrower DACA solidarity resolution passed 32-1

At its 7 November meeting the senate passed, by 32 votes to 1, a resolution co-authored by President Andi Dahmer and former senator Francisco Serrano standing in solidarity with WKU students who were DACA recipients. It was a revision of the clean DREAM Act resolution rejected the previous week, reframed to address only the WKU student community rather than call on legislators, which Dahmer said kept it within "a jurisdiction that we do have." Dahmer noted WKU was one of the last schools in Kentucky to pass a measure of this kind. The same meeting unanimously funded the Go With the Flow program, allocating over $580 for free menstrual products in five campus buildings.

In the recordHerald, 9 Nov 2017

28 February 2018Lecture

First Campus Safety Panel replaced the safety walk

SGA held its first Campus Safety Panel on the second floor of Downing Student Union on 28 February 2018, with campus officials taking questions from students. Campus improvements chair Garrett Edmonds said he had proposed a panel because it had been too cold to hold a safety walk since he took office. Parking director Jennifer Tougas was among the panelists; President Andi Dahmer asked what could be done about hate crimes on campus, and Chief Diversity Officer Lynne Holland answered that students had to hold each other accountable.

In the recordHerald, 1 Mar 2018 - "SGA hosts panel to discuss campus safety"

19 March 2018Lecture

First women in leadership forum drew about twenty people

SGA held its first forum on women in leadership on the evening of 19 March 2018. The panel was Ogden College dean Cheryl Stevens, history professor Patti Minter, faculty regent and former provost Barbara Burch, and Educational Talent Search director Martha Sales, and about 20 students and Bowling Green residents attended. Minter described the psychic energy it took to deal with assumptions about female professors day in and day out. The idea came from the women on SGA's executive board, including Andi Dahmer, Savannah Molyneaux, Kara Lowry and Amy Wyer.

In the recordHerald, 20 Mar 2018 - "SGA hosts first women in leadership forum"

3 April 2018

Dahmer declines to seek a second term

Andi Dahmer announced on Facebook that she would not run for re-election as student body president and student regent, and endorsed Conner Hounshell, Savannah Molyneaux and Kara Lowry for the executive board. The announcement followed Herald reporting on months of internal conflict in the senate. Election applications closed the same day, with voting set for April 16 and 17.

In the recordHerald, 3 Apr 2018

4 September 2018

Former SGA president sues WKU, Caboni and two administrators

Andi Dahmer filed suit against WKU, President Timothy Caboni, Assistant General Counsel Andrea Anderson and Director of Student Activities Charley Pride, citing verbal, mental and emotional abuse and alleging sex discrimination and failure to follow the university's Discrimination and Harassment Policy. The university called the allegations "maliciously untrue" and said it would vigorously defend the claims. WKU said its Title IX office found no Title IX violation but did find Student Code of Conduct violations, for which students were sanctioned.

What this is

A year-by-year record of student government at Western Kentucky University, from the ratification of the Associated Students constitution in April 1966 to the present. Every entry names the source it came from.

Corrections

The names here start with the plaque in the SGA Chambers, and the plaque is known to be wrong in places. Where the archive disagrees with it, this site follows the archive and records the change on the corrections page. If you served, or you know that a year here is wrong, the project wants to hear from you through the Student Government Association office.

What it rests on

Everything here comes from what is publicly available online: the digitised Herald and SGA's own papers on TopSCHOLAR, the Talisman yearbooks, wkuherald.com, the university's news pages and the Wayback Machine. That is a fraction of what happened. Student government has met most weeks for sixty years, and the greater part of it was never written down, or was written down on paper that has never been scanned. A thin year here is a thin record, not a quiet one.
