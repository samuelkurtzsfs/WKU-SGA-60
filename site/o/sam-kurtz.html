15 September 2021

New senators seated and freshman election results announced

The senate unanimously voted in four new senate members, two judicial council members and Trib Singh as director of academic and student affairs. Speaker of the Senate Tess Welch introduced committees on legislative operations, student experience, mental health, sustainability, campus improvement, diversity and student outreach. That night the fall election results named Addison Falkner, Sam Kurtz and Jenna Wells freshman senators and Arivumani Srivastava the Gatton Academy senator.

September 2022

First meeting filled senate and cabinet vacancies

At its first meeting of the year on Aug. 30, the senate approved all of President Bornefeld's nominees, including Juan Thomas, Will Hemenover and Sydney Denney as senators-at-large, Aniya Johnson as director of information technology, Donte' Reed as director of enrollment and student experience, and Isaac Kind and Garrett Baum to the judicial council. 5 October 2022

Executives report on a campus listening tour as enrollment chief briefs senate

Bornefeld said he, Garrison Reed and Sam Kurtz had been holding a listening tour to increase SGA's accessibility to students, citing feedback from the Panhellenic Council. Ethan Logan, vice president of enrollment, told senators WKU's retention rate stood at 76.66% and discussed reaching students who do not speak English as a first language. Senator Salvador León asked whether students could serve as bilingual counselors.

25 January 2023

Senate approved five nominations and reviewed a shrinking budget

The senate unanimously confirmed five nominations, including Adan Canizalez as chair of student enrollment and experience and Miles Huff, a track athlete, to the judicial council. Administrative Vice President Sam Kurtz urged senators to sponsor more resolutions, which use university funds, because only about $39,460.76 remained of SGA's $100,000 annual budget for bills. Speaker Julie Mishchuk floated a constitutional amendment requiring every senator to sponsor at least one bill or resolution.

25 January 2023

Vice president urges resolutions over spending bills as budget dwindles

Administrative Vice President Sam Kurtz pushed senators to sponsor more resolutions because of the shrinking SGA budget. Five nominations were confirmed unanimously, including Adan Canizalez as chair of student enrollment and experience and Miles Huff to the Judicial Council, filling the vacancy left by Holden Schroeder. Justin Goins was serving as chief justice and nominated Isaac King as associate chief justice.

19 April 2023

Kurtz ticket elected unopposed

Sam Kurtz, the outgoing administrative vice president, was elected student body president on a ticket that ran unopposed, with Salvador León as administrative vice president and Annalise Finch as executive vice president. Voting opened on TopNet on 17 April. Fourteen at-large senators were elected, along with the college, class and first-generation seats. Results were read at about 12:30 a.m. in the Downing Student Union senate chambers by Judicial Council Chief Justice Justin Goins.

30 August 2023

Kurtz seated a five-member Judicial Council and seven committee heads

At SGA's first meeting of the fall semester, the senate approved President Sam Kurtz's nominations of Ashley Ranger, Anna Kate Hardin, Ellen Henderson, Morgan Gammons and Garrison Reed to the 2023-24 Judicial Council. Kurtz said a record number of students had applied and were interviewed over the summer before the field was narrowed to five. He also announced seven committee heads, among them Rush Robinson for Campus Improvements and Sustainability and Karley Solorzano for Diversity, Equity and Inclusion. The new appointees took the oath of office at the meeting.

24 January 2024

Midyear resignations reshuffle the executive cabinet

President Sam Kurtz announced the resignations of Chief of Staff Lindsey Kelly and of Donte Reed as director of enrollment and student experience. The senate approved Kurtz's nominations of Reed as the new chief of staff and Sarah Vincent as director of enrollment and student activities. 9 February 2024

Judicial council censures Salvador León

The SGA judicial council voted 6-0 to censure Administrative Vice President Salvador León after a Feb. 7 hearing on allegations that he spent funds, including a $425.25 Preston Center deposit for Neurodiversity Acceptance Week, before the senate passed the funding bill. León argued text messages showed President Sam Kurtz had accepted the spending, while Kurtz said he asked León multiple times not to spend before the bill passed. The council recommended no further disciplinary action beyond the censure.

In the recordHerald, 9 Feb 2024

15 February 2024

SGA launched an Uber voucher program and moved against parking fees

President Sam Kurtz and Chief of Staff Donte Reed announced 450 Uber vouchers worth $10 each, given out through weekly social media giveaways, senate meeting attendance, SGA events and need-based scholarships. Resolution 18-23-S, written by Speaker of the Senate Sydney Denney and Reed, put SGA on record supporting a $50 cap on student parking fees, noting citations ranged from $10 to $600. Bill 19-23-S set aside $700 from the legislative discretionary budget to pay citations for financially vulnerable students, excluding tickets for disability, safety zone or fire lane violations. Bill 23-23-S allocated a further $1,045 for Dental Hygiene Center and ID Center vouchers, and all the legislation passed unanimously.

In the recordHerald, 15 Feb 2024

8 August 2024

Student regent presses administrators over student worker funding

At the Board of Regents retreat, Student Regent Sam Kurtz said a friend working in the biotechnology center had been told some 2023 funding for student jobs would not return in 2024. Kurtz raised the concern during a budget model presentation as units across WKU were being ordered to cut 7.5% of their approved spending and absorb a 10% overhead charge on self-generated revenue. He asked the room, "Isn't that the point of why we're all here, to look out for the students?" and afterwards told the Herald the answers he got were cookie-cutter.

In the recordHerald, 8 Aug 2024

28 August 2024

Senate passed $100,000 budget at first meeting

At its first meeting of the year on Aug. 27, the SGA Senate passed a $100,000 budget for 2024-25, including $20,000 for organizational aid, $23,500 for scholarships and $10,705 in legislative discretionary funding. Executive leaders told the Herald editorial board they intended to spend the full allocation, and President Sam Kurtz credited WKU President Caboni with keeping the budget level for several years.

A mental health survey, and a warning about the election

The Senate passed Bill 4-25-S funding a DEI Committee tabling event to gather anonymous mental health survey responses. Dean of Students Martha Sales told the Senate 'you cannot isolate and educate at the same time'. 24th Senate held final meeting

The 24th Senate met for the last time on April 15 with no legislation on the agenda, spending the meeting on farewells. President Sam Kurtz, Vice President Donte Reed and Chief Financial Officer Ethan Taylor each received a standing ovation, and the Senate recorded 295 spring scholarship applications, far above the roughly 40-applicant average of past years.

What this is

A year-by-year record of student government at Western Kentucky University, from the ratification of the Associated Students constitution in April 1966 to the present. Every entry names the source it came from.

Corrections

The names here start with the plaque in the SGA Chambers, and the plaque is known to be wrong in places. Where the archive disagrees with it, this site follows the archive and records the change on the corrections page. If you served, or you know that a year here is wrong, the project wants to hear from you through the Student Government Association office.

What it rests on

Everything here comes from what is publicly available online: the digitised Herald and SGA's own papers on TopSCHOLAR, the Talisman yearbooks, wkuherald.com, the university's news pages and the Wayback Machine. That is a fraction of what happened. Student government has met most weeks for sixty years, and the greater part of it was never written down, or was written down on paper that has never been scanned. A thin year here is a thin record, not a quiet one.

Text on this site is the work of the project. Photographs, documents and legislation are reproduced from Western Kentucky University's own open archives and from the College Heights Herald and the Talisman; each item names the collection, issue and page it came from and links to the original.
