16 April 2013

Judicial Council declined to contest Bailey's reinstatement of Boka

In an emergency meeting, the Judicial Council unanimously decided not to challenge Bailey's memorandum reinstating Boka, saying it did not believe it had the power to do so, while expressing official disapproval. Chief Justice Seth Church called the intervention "a serious infringement on our autonomy" and said it set a bad precedent. Boka said the council had conflicts of interest and needed to be held accountable by a neutral party in the administration.

In the recordHerald, 16 Apr 2013

13 February 2014

Executive Council voided the amendment stripping the president's judicial appointments

An amendment removing the SGA president's sole power to appoint judicial council justices and raising the council to seven justices passed at the Tuesday meeting, over President Keyana Boka's objection that it bypassed both presidential appointment and the senate. Boka said the president is elected by the student body to make appointments in all three branches and that she was wary of the direction, while Chief Justice Seth Church argued that vesting appointments in the executive branch as a whole created a fairer system. After the meeting the Executive Council reviewed the constitution, found too few senators had attended for the vote to count and declared the amendment unconstitutional, sending it back to first read. The SGA office had temporarily moved to Garrett after flooding in Cravens.

In the recordHerald, 13 Feb 2014

15 October 2014

Senate defeated the nonsectarian prayer policy resolution

After more than 20 minutes of debate the senate defeated Resolution 4-14-F, which would have supported adopting the University Senate's nonsectarian public prayer policy for university-sponsored events. Chief of Staff Seth Church explained that a nonsectarian prayer differs from a sectarian one referring to a specific deity. President Nicki Taylor said the senate was split three ways, among nonsectarian prayer, sectarian prayer and no prayer at all, and that the division sank the resolution. The same night senators passed Bill 9-14-F allocating $250 in organizational aid to the Phi Gamma Delta fraternity.

In the recordHerald, 15 Oct 2014

29 October 2014

Glasgow rewrites its constitution after struggling to keep members

Amendments to Glasgow's SGA constitution received their first read on the main campus. Chief of Staff Seth Church said Glasgow's constitution had been modeled on the main campus document and that the regional campus had struggled with student involvement and retaining SGA members, so it was decided to change the constitution drastically. Glasgow's SGA had already voted to approve the amendments, leaving the main campus senate to ratify them.

In the recordHerald, 29 Oct 2014

30 October 2014

President establishes a Cultural and Diversity Affairs Committee

SGA created a Cultural and Diversity Affairs Committee chaired by Seth Church, who developed the idea for a leadership practicum focused on diversity within SGA and on campus. Church said he collaborated with President Nicki Taylor to create it. Taylor said making SGA more diverse had been an ongoing conversation since her first year and that establishing ad hoc committees was a power she held as president.

In the recordHerald, 30 Oct 2014

20 November 2014

Executive Cabinet vetoed the smoke-free campus resolution hours after it passed

The senate passed Resolution 11-14-F, supporting a smoke-free campus, on a 14-10 vote, and the SGA Executive Cabinet vetoed it after the meeting. Chief of Staff Seth Church argued a ban was unfair to students required to live on campus for two years, while author William Berry said he left the location of any smoking areas to the administration and that the ban infringed no student's rights. Senator Haley Jones countered that smoking is legal and that students over 18 can buy tobacco. The policy would not have covered other tobacco products or electronic cigarettes, and senators kept the power to override the veto with a two-thirds vote.

In the recordHerald, 20 Nov 2014

3 February 2015

Limited smoking campus resolution passed 13-11

SGA passed Resolution 1-15-S, supporting a limited smoking campus, by a 13-11 vote. The resolution would restrict tobacco use to 39 designated areas that the Administrative Council would gradually phase out over three years, while leaving electronic cigarettes and smokeless tobacco unrestricted. Co-author Seth Church said it was roughly the fifth version of the resolution since the smoking debate began the previous semester.

In the recordHerald, 3 Feb 2015

3 March 2015

SGA backed a $10 million parking garage funded by a $30 per semester tuition rise

SGA passed a resolution supporting construction of a $10 million parking structure on part of Creason Lot, funded by a $30-per-semester tuition increase. Authors Zach Jones, Brian Chism and Nathan Cherry estimated the roughly 500-car garage would take about 18 months to build, and the resolution suggested a temporary gravel lot on the site of Tate Page Hall after its anticipated demolition. President Nicki Taylor said the structure had been before the parking and transportation committee for the whole academic year. Chief of Staff Seth Church objected that the plan was rushed and left out the regional campuses.

In the recordHerald, 3 Mar 2015

18 March 2015

Breast milk expression resolution passes unanimously without debate

Resolution 4-15-S, supporting creation and maintenance of private areas on campus where students, faculty and staff could express breast milk, passed unanimously and without debate. Chief of Staff Seth Church, who wrote it, said a non-traditional student who had just had her third child brought the problem to him and found the existing unlocked room in a department hall uncomfortable. Church said he wanted no fewer than three permanent spaces.

In the recordHerald, 18 Mar 2015

What this is

A year-by-year record of student government at Western Kentucky University, from the ratification of the Associated Students constitution in April 1966 to the present. Every entry names the source it came from.

Corrections

The names here start with the plaque in the SGA Chambers, and the plaque is known to be wrong in places. Where the archive disagrees with it, this site follows the archive and records the change on the corrections page. If you served, or you know that a year here is wrong, the project wants to hear from you through the Student Government Association office.

What it rests on

Everything here comes from what is publicly available online: the digitised Herald and SGA's own papers on TopSCHOLAR, the Talisman yearbooks, wkuherald.com, the university's news pages and the Wayback Machine. That is a fraction of what happened. Student government has met most weeks for sixty years, and the greater part of it was never written down, or was written down on paper that has never been scanned. A thin year here is a thin record, not a quiet one.

Reuse

Text on this site is the work of the project. Photographs, documents and legislation are reproduced from Western Kentucky University's own open archives and from the College Heights Herald and the Talisman; each item names the collection, issue and page it came from and links to the original.
