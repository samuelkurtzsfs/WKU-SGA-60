27 January 2010

Two more senators resign; Frankfort rally buses announced

At the Jan. 27 meeting, Aaron Pawley and Eileen Forsythe resigned as senators, Pawley because of a difficult course load and Forsythe because she was studying abroad in England. President Kevin Smiley announced free buses to carry 75 students to a Feb. 9 rally in Frankfort where advocates from Kentucky's public universities would lobby legislators on higher education funding. Western student Colton Jessie was slated to speak at the rally.

In the recordHerald, 27 Jan 2010

April 2010

Colton Jessie elected SGA president for 2010-11

Bowling Green junior Colton Jessie won the SGA presidency with 63 percent of the vote, taking 726 votes to 424 for Administrative Vice President Justin Thurman, on a platform of sustainability, handicap accessibility and changing the tailgating policy. Kendrick Bryan was re-elected executive vice president with 731 votes to Currie Martin's 415, and Wade Pierce ran unopposed for administrative vice president with 1,016 votes. Results were announced at midnight Wednesday.

In the recordHerald, 1 Apr 2010

14 September 2010Other

SGA paid marketing students $885 for koozies and bottle openers

WKU's American Marketing Association partnered with SGA to market the organisation after AMA president Currie Martin, a former senator, argued that exposure was SGA's weakness. The senate passed a bill giving AMA $885 to buy 500 koozies and 500 keychain bottle openers carrying the SGA logo and the Provide-A-Ride phone number. President Colton Jessie called it a win for both sides, giving marketing students a real client.

19 October 2010Tradition

Campus Clean-up drew about 35 volunteers

Campus improvements committee head Kaylee Egerer told the senate on 19 October 2010 that around 35 people had turned out to help at that day's Campus Clean-up. President Colton Jessie had announced the week before that the clean-up would carry recycling bags as well as trash bags for the first time.

In the recordHerald, 19 Oct 2010 - "SGA: Safety Walk to take place next week"

7 December 2010

SGA funds extended library hours for finals week

The senate approved $600 to keep the library open until 2 a.m. during finals week, covering extra staff time and lighting. President Colton Jessie said continuing the service was never in question, calling finals "an important time for students."

In the recordHerald, 7 Dec 2010

4 February 2011Service

Provide-A-Ride outgrew its taxi contractor

By February 2011 President Colton Jessie said Provide-A-Ride had become too big for Franklin Taxi to run and students were waiting hours for a car. Between 31 August and 13 November 2010 the service carried 6,111 passengers on 1,189 calls, more than two-thirds of the whole previous year's total in three and a half months. Provide-A-Ride had carried 9,200 passengers on 1,772 calls across 2009-10.

In the recordHerald, 4 Feb 2011 - "WKU's Provide-A-Ride growing too large for current provider"

18 November 2011

SGA paid a surprise $15,000 Provide-A-Ride bill from aid and scholarship funds

SGA paid Parking Services a $15,000 retroactive charge for the previous year's Provide-A-Ride service, taking $7,500 each from the organizational aid and student scholarship funds. President Billy Stephens said he was "shocked" to be notified of the payment the week before, and that Parking Services had no record of receiving the money from the previous administration. Because the SGA budget does not roll over between years, no prior-year money was available to cover it. 2010-11 president Colton Jessie said his administration had budgeted the payment.

What this is

A year-by-year record of student government at Western Kentucky University, from the ratification of the Associated Students constitution in April 1966 to the present. Every entry names the source it came from.

Corrections

The names here start with the plaque in the SGA Chambers, and the plaque is known to be wrong in places. Where the archive disagrees with it, this site follows the archive and records the change on the corrections page. If you served, or you know that a year here is wrong, the project wants to hear from you through the Student Government Association office.

What it rests on

Everything here comes from what is publicly available online: the digitised Herald and SGA's own papers on TopSCHOLAR, the Talisman yearbooks, wkuherald.com, the university's news pages and the Wayback Machine. That is a fraction of what happened. Student government has met most weeks for sixty years, and the greater part of it was never written down, or was written down on paper that has never been scanned. A thin year here is a thin record, not a quiet one.

Reuse

Text on this site is the work of the project. Photographs, documents and legislation are reproduced from Western Kentucky University's own open archives and from the College Heights Herald and the Talisman; each item names the collection, issue and page it came from and links to the original.
