1971Programme

Lyne's account: entertainment becomes a cultural programme

In a signed statement in the 1971 Talisman, president John Lyne wrote that the entertainment side of Associated Students had been expanded into a cultural programmes concept, that big-name concerts continued but names outside the top forty such as Ravi Shankar had been brought in, and that theatrical events had been sponsored, including the co-sponsorship of "1776". He recorded a student-run lecture series as that year's significant addition, said the student discount programme with local merchants had been maintained, and said the organisation was working towards a legal service for students that would include a legal defence fund and a student advocates' corps.

1976Other

ASG sets up a Student Activities Committee to choose the acts

After a semester of argument over who should decide what came to campus, ASG adopted a Student Activities Committee to bring more student opinion into the booking and publicising of concerts and lecturers. Seventeen members were chosen from 63 applicants and divided into three sub-committees: seven for concerts with three alternates, and five each for lectures and publicity with two alternates apiece. Activities vice-president Rick Kelley said the committee was formed to widen student say over booking and publicity. The dispute behind it lingered from the ASG meeting of 2 December 1975 and sharpened when assistant dean of student affairs Ron Beck argued that any programme the university presented ought to have educational value; students wrote to the Herald against him. The yearbook gives no date for the committee's adoption, only that it was in place in time to help bring the free Spinners and Wet Willie concert of 30 January 1976.

1980

ASG surveyed 28 universities on open house hours

ASG surveyed 28 institutions comparable to Western and found that Western's open house policy gave the fewest visitation hours per semester of any of them. The Herald reported the finding on 27 September 1979 under the line that Western came last in a 28-college housing poll. ASG also published the results of the previous spring's open house referendum, which showed a majority of students surveyed wanted weekly open houses. The policy finally adopted came from the student affairs office rather than from ASG.

What this is

A year-by-year record of student government at Western Kentucky University, from the ratification of the Associated Students constitution in April 1966 to the present. Every entry names the source it came from.

Corrections

The names here start with the plaque in the SGA Chambers, and the plaque is known to be wrong in places. Where the archive disagrees with it, this site follows the archive and records the change on the corrections page. If you served, or you know that a year here is wrong, the project wants to hear from you: people who held an office can be given the keys to their own year on the contribute page.

What it rests on

Everything here comes from what is publicly available online: the digitised Herald and SGA's own papers on TopSCHOLAR, the Talisman yearbooks, wkuherald.com, the university's news pages and the Wayback Machine. That is a fraction of what happened. Student government has met most weeks for sixty years, and the greater part of it was never written down, or was written down on paper that has never been scanned. A thin year here is a thin record, not a quiet one.

Text on this site is the work of the project. Photographs, documents and legislation are reproduced from Western Kentucky University's own open archives and from the College Heights Herald and the Talisman; each item names the collection, issue and page it came from and links to the original.
