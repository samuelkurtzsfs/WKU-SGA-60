1966-67Jim Haynes (president)

April 1966President Kelly Thompson approves the constitution. The proposed constitution of the Associated Students of Western Kentucky University was completed in late March and approved on 1 April. The copy held by WKU Archives is dated 7 April 1966. In the recordTopSCHOLAR - SGA Constitution
April 1966Thompson's approval letter is addressed to committee chair Reed Morgan. The archived copy of Thompson's approval letter names the organizing committee that drafted the constitution: Reed Morgan as chair, John Lovett as vice chair. Morgan's committee did the drafting; Jim Haynes was elected to lead the government it created six weeks later. In the recordUA3/3/1, Letter to Organizational Committee for a Western Student Organization, 1 Apr 1966
26 April 1966Students ratify the constitution, 1,812 to 726. A four-day referendum. 2,538 students voted - a larger raw turnout than most SGA elections have managed since. In the recordWKU SGA history
18 May 1966Jim Haynes elected first president. Seven weeks from presidential approval to a seated student president. Western Kentucky State College became Western Kentucky University on 16 June, so the university and its student government are almost exactly the same age. In the recordWKU SGA history
29 September 1966The new government opens its first full year. The Herald's first issue of the fall reported the student senate ratifying a government appointment as the Associated Students began their first full academic year. Students had approved the constitution and elected officers the previous spring. In the recordHerald 46:1, 29 Sep 1966
27 October 1966Doing well, but lacking communication. Midway through the fall semester the Herald judged that Associated Students leaders were performing well but were failing to communicate with the students they represented. It was the first public report card on the year-old government. In the recordHerald 46:5, 27 Oct 1966
10 November 1966Government and administration meet on campus problems. The Herald reported Student Government Association leaders meeting with President Kelly Thompson to discuss campus problems, an early instance of the year-old government dealing directly with the administration. In the recordHerald 46:7, 10 Nov 1966
9 March 1967Herald warns of a 'popularity bandwagon' in the spring election. A week before the presidential campaign opened, the Herald ran a piece by Fran Salyers arguing that popularity, not merit, was set to decide the Associated Students election. In the recordHerald 46:16, 9 Mar 1967
16 March 1967Two vie for the presidency. The Herald reported two candidates seeking the Associated Students presidency, with campaigns for the organization's second presidential election set to open the following Monday. In the recordHerald 46:17, 16 Mar 1967
4 May 1967Swearing-in set for May 10. The Herald announced that newly elected Associated Student officers would be sworn in on May 10, 1967, completing the organization's first full transfer of leadership. In the recordHerald 46:22, 4 May 1967
12 May 1967First year judged: organization, progress, apathy. Reviewing the government's first year, the Herald found organization and progress alongside persistent student apathy. The same issue carried president-elect William Menser's plans for 1967-68 and a letter arguing that the Associated Students really worked. In the recordHerald 46:23, 12 May 1967
18 May 1967Herald presses students to vote as the year closes. Days after the outgoing administration's year-end review, the Herald ran a piece headlined 'Goof Government Needs Student Vote,' pressing students toward greater turnout as Menser's incoming administration took over. In the recordHerald 46:24, 18 May 1967
30 May 1967Dean Keown proposes a budget for the new government. Dean of Students Charles Keown sent President Kelly Thompson a memo proposing a student fee structure to create a budget for the Associated Student Government. He attached fee models gathered from universities across the South and Midwest. In the recordUA3/3/1 Keown memo, 30 May 1967

1968-69Reed Morgan (role unresolved) · Bill Straeffer (president) · Paul Gerard, III (student regent)

29 April 1968Straeffer and Whitley vie for the presidency. The Herald's pre-election coverage set out the field: Bill Straeffer against Charles Whitley for president, Terry Gilpin against Bob Van Corbach for vice president, and contested races down to secretary, treasurer, and congressman at large. In the recordHerald 47:28, 29 Apr 1968
2 May 1968Straeffer elected president, 1,732 to 1,098; turnout 34 percent. The Associated Students' internal election-results memo lists the full slate: Bill Straeffer over Charles Whitley for president, Terry Gilpin over Bob Van Corbach for vice president, Becky Cooper over Mary Miller for secretary, Tonii Rizzo over John Combs for treasurer, and John Cobelli and Janie Heathcoat elected congressmen at large over Billy Goodman and Scott Phillips. Total turnout was 2,894 votes, about 34 percent of enrollment. In the recordUA3/3/1 Memo Re: Results of Election, Ron Beck, 3 May 1968Read it here

What this is

A year-by-year record of student government at Western Kentucky University, from the ratification of the Associated Students constitution in April 1966 to the present. Every entry names the source it came from.

Corrections

The names here start with the plaque in the SGA Chambers, and the plaque is known to be wrong in places. Where the archive disagrees with it, this site follows the archive and records the change on the corrections page. If you served, or you know that a year here is wrong, the project wants to hear from you through the Student Government Association office.

Reuse

Text on this site is the work of the project. Photographs, documents and legislation are reproduced from Western Kentucky University's own open archives and from the College Heights Herald and the Talisman; each item names the collection, issue and page it came from and links to the original.

Built 4 August 2026. The whole record is readable as data: years.json. Method and scope are set out on the about page.
