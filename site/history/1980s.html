1980

The ASG president also held the student regent seat

The 1980 Talisman's account of the Board of Regents records that ASG president Steve Fuller was also sworn in as student regent, in the same passage reporting the board's budget, two regent appointments and $66,000 of university money for renovating the president's home. The two offices were separated during the next session: after Marcel Bush resigned in January 1982 the seat went to a campus-wide election, and Sandra Norfleet won the runoff in February 1982.

18 September 1980

A survey found students uninformed about ASG

Diane Comer reported the student opinion poll committee's first survey under the line that students were uninformed about the Associated Student Government. The Talisman gives the numbers: 29 per cent said they would recognise ASG president Steve Fuller, 31 per cent said they would recognise university centre director Ron Beck, and 28 per cent thought Beck was the ASG president. Seventy-four per cent said they would recognise university president Donald Zacharias. Committee chairman Kevin Kinne said he was disappointed so few could tell Fuller and Beck apart and more surprised that a quarter did not know Zacharias.

10 November 1980Festival

ASG's International Week

Associated Student Government sponsored International Week, which ran nightly from Monday 10 November 1980 and closed with a presentation on Brazil and Colombia on the Thursday evening. Two or three countries were featured each night through films, lectures, folk dances and slides given by students and faculty from those countries, and programme chairman Osama Sharif opened the week with a talk on Palestine and a Red Cross film on Palestinian children. The 1981 Talisman, which spells him Sharif and calls him international students committee chairman, credits the week to student government and the International Students Committee, places the nightly programmes in the university center, and lists nine countries: Switzerland, Palestine, Iceland, Malaysia, Colombia, Brazil, India, Pakistan and France.

1981

ASG petitioned the governor over cuts to higher education

In the spring ASG circulated a petition asking state government to weigh the importance of higher education in Kentucky's future before making further cuts, and sent it to Governor John Y. Brown, finance secretary George Atkins and the Council on Higher Education. The petition argued that a quality education required financial support for faculty and facilities. President Steve Fuller said many students did not realise how serious the cuts were, and that they meant dismissing faculty. The Talisman dates the petition only to the spring.

The discount cards that never existed

ASG announced it would file complaints with the Better Business Bureaus of Georgia and Texas against University Press Inc. of Lewisville, Texas, and Atlanta, which had taken $275 from each of at least four Bowling Green merchants for ASG-endorsed student discount cards that were never produced. 29 September 1981Service

14,000 cards, a dead phone line and a returned letter

President Marcel Bush had spent three weeks trying to reach the company printing 14,000 ASG discount cards: the Texas number was disconnected, an answering service pushed her to an Atlanta number nobody answered, and a letter to the Texas address came back from the post office. On the day complaints were being filed, company representative Phil Bewley finally telephoned to say the cards had shipped from New York and that printing problems had caused the delay. The Herald noted that until two years earlier ASG had printed discount cards at its own expense as a free student service, charging merchants nothing, and had dropped it when costs became prohibitive.

What this is

A year-by-year record of student government at Western Kentucky University, from the ratification of the Associated Students constitution in April 1966 to the present. Every entry names the source it came from.

Corrections

The names here start with the plaque in the SGA Chambers, and the plaque is known to be wrong in places. Where the archive disagrees with it, this site follows the archive and records the change on the corrections page. If you served, or you know that a year here is wrong, the project wants to hear from you: people who held an office can be given the keys to their own year on the contribute page.

What it rests on

Everything here comes from what is publicly available online: the digitised Herald and SGA's own papers on TopSCHOLAR, the Talisman yearbooks, wkuherald.com, the university's news pages and the Wayback Machine. That is a fraction of what happened. Student government has met most weeks for sixty years, and the greater part of it was never written down, or was written down on paper that has never been scanned. A thin year here is a thin record, not a quiet one.

Reuse

Text on this site is the work of the project. Photographs, documents and legislation are reproduced from Western Kentucky University's own open archives and from the College Heights Herald and the Talisman; each item names the collection, issue and page it came from and links to the original.
