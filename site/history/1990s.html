In the recordSGA Legislation, Resolution 90-10-F, "Left Handed Desks"
29 November 1990ASG kills resolution on earthquake-prediction absences. The Associated Student Government killed Resolution 90-12-F, which had asked that student absences tied to Iben Browning's widely publicized forecast of a major New Madrid Fault earthquake be excused. Browning had predicted the quake for on or near 3 December 1990; it prompted regional anxiety and some school closures, but no earthquake occurred. The Herald reported the resolution's defeat in its 29 November 1990 issue. In the recordSGA Legislation, Resolution 90-12-F, "Excused Absences for Earthquake Prediction"

1991ASG overhauls its by-laws, including veto-override procedure. In the spring term the Associated Student Government passed a cluster of by-law bills: Bill #91-4-S suspended the by-laws to allow a vote on a pending matter, Bill #91-5-S amended the by-laws on how resolutions could be submitted, and Bill #91-6-S changed the by-laws on overriding a presidential veto. Falmlen won the Associated Student Government presidential election in results the Herald reported under the headline "Heather Falmlen Wins Amid Associated Student Government Election Protests." 29 October 1991ASG asks that Iracane not be re-elected regents chair. The Associated Student Government passed Resolution 91-6-F, authored by Mark Miller and Eric McWilliams and sponsored by Mistianna Holcomb, asking that Joe Iracane not be re-elected chair of the Board of Regents. The resolution's text stated that Iracane, chairman for eight consecutive years, was under investigation by the FBI, the IRS and U.S. attorneys in Indiana and Kentucky over an alleged $6 million payment to Pyramid Mining Co. in return for contracts and a separate alleged $14,500 payment to the company for a consulting contract; it also accused him of overstepping into day-to-day university operations reserved for the president under the Board's bylaws. The resolution asked student regent Heather Falmlen to vote against him. No source found reports the investigation's outcome. The same day's Herald covered the vote under "Associated Student Government Opposes Joseph Iracane." In the recordSGA Resolution 91-6-F, "Board of Regents Chair Term Limit"
31 October 1991ASG asks the regents to elect a new leader. ASG urged the Board of Regents to elect a new leader in place of chairman Joseph Iracane, two days after the Herald reported the organization's opposition to him. It was a rare direct challenge to the university's governing board. In the recordHerald 67:19, 31 Oct 1991
6 November 1991Regents re-elect Iracane despite ASG's resolution. 2 April 1992ASG resolves to support football despite budget cuts. The Associated Student Government passed Resolution 92-7-S, declaring its support for the WKU football program in spite of budget cuts. The resolution's own text argued the university had already awarded 67 football scholarships it would be contractually bound to honor even if the program were suspended, calling a suspension that still paid non-playing scholarship athletes an unnecessary expense. This entry corrects the resolution's date: an earlier record in this archive gave 4 February 1992, but the resolution itself is dated introduced 2 April 1992, shortly before the Board of Regents' 1 May vote to keep the program. In the recordSGA Legislation, Resolution 92-7-S, "Support of Football Program"

What this is

A year-by-year record of student government at Western Kentucky University, from the ratification of the Associated Students constitution in April 1966 to the present. Every entry names the source it came from.

Corrections

The names here start with the plaque in the SGA Chambers, and the plaque is known to be wrong in places. Where the archive disagrees with it, this site follows the archive and records the change on the corrections page. If you served, or you know that a year here is wrong, the project wants to hear from you through the Student Government Association office.

Reuse

Text on this site is the work of the project. Photographs, documents and legislation are reproduced from Western Kentucky University's own open archives and from the College Heights Herald and the Talisman; each item names the collection, issue and page it came from and links to the original.
