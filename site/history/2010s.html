2010-11

Colton Jessie (president)

11 entries · 26 index lines

14 September 2010

SGA raised scholarship budget to $20,000

After running out of scholarship money the previous year, SGA increased its scholarship budget from $12,000 to $20,000 and lowered general senate funding from $20,000 to $12,000 to cover the change. Study abroad awards were capped at $500 for a semester trip and $250 for a winter or summer term so more students could receive money. SGA also finalized a new study away scholarship for travel inside the country and set aside $400 for it in the winter term.

In the recordHerald, 14 Sep 2010

23 September 2010

Twelve senators elected in fall election with 847 voters

Twelve students won seats in the fall senate election, in which 847 total voters cast ballots on TopNet. Keyana Boka, Natalie Broderick and Eppiphanie Benton were elected freshman senators, running unopposed, while 25 students competed for the at-large seats. Winners included Diego Leal Ambriz, who would run for president that spring.

In the recordHerald, 23 Sep 2010

19 November 2010

Ransdell brought $49 million DUC renovation plan to SGA

President Gary Ransdell and head architect Jeff Stivers told SGA at its Nov. 16 meeting that officials were moving ahead on a plan to completely renovate Downing University Center at a cost of up to $49 million. Students would pay about $140 extra per year in fees for up to 20 years, and Ransdell promised students would be involved in every decision. SGA had to pass resolutions on the design and fees before the Board of Regents could approve the project.

In the recordHerald, 19 Nov 2010

7 December 2010

SGA funds extended library hours for finals week

The senate approved $600 to keep the library open until 2 a.m. during finals week, covering extra staff time and lighting. President Colton Jessie said continuing the service was never in question, calling finals "an important time for students."

In the recordHerald, 7 Dec 2010

10 December 2010

Senate tabled DUC resolution over lack of student input

Senators tabled the bill supporting the DUC renovation at the Dec. 7 meeting, saying they had not received enough student input just three weeks after learning of the project. Jessie backed the delay, saying he wanted to hear students' voices before moving forward, even though it pushed SGA's presentation to the Board of Regents from January 21 to April 22. Campus Improvements Chair Kaylee Egerer said many students did not yet know they would pay $140 a year for the project.

In the recordHerald, 10 Dec 2010

11 February 2011

Herald reported Greeks held 40 percent of SGA seats

The Herald reported that 21 of SGA's 50 members belonged to Greek organizations, more than 40 percent, while less than 8 percent of the student body was in a fraternity or sorority. Sixteen of the 21 were fraternity men, and two fraternities, Sigma Chi and Lambda Chi Alpha, accounted for nine of them. Officers defended the imbalance, and the story noted SGA's operating budget for 2010-11 was $121,335, drawn from tuition.

In the recordHerald, 11 Feb 2011

22 February 2011

SGA unanimously passed DUC renovation resolution

SGA unanimously passed the resolution supporting DUC renovations at its Feb. 22 meeting, the bill's second reading. Project costs were capped at $49,128,545, with a student fee of up to $70 per semester for 20 years and $1.5 million per year from WKU. The resolution, authored by Kaylee Egerer, asked for assurance of a 24-hour study space and called for construction to begin as soon as possible while interest rates were low.

In the recordHerald, 22 Feb 2011

March 2011

SGA members joined Rally for Higher Education in Frankfort

SGA members traveled to Frankfort on March 1 for the annual Rally for Higher Education, where SGA secretary Katie Stillwell was the keynote speaker representing students statewide. Her speech urged legislators to protect money already set aside for higher education. Jessie, the previous year's keynote speaker, joined chief of staff Charlie Harris and other state SGA officers for a lunch with Gov. Steve Beshear.

In the recordHerald, 1 Mar 2011

April 2011

Administrative Vice President Wade Pierce to hand post to fraternity brother

Pierce, who chaired the organizational aid committee, said he would be succeeded as administrative vice president by Devon Hilderbrandt, his committee co-chair and his "little brother" in Lambda Chi Alpha. Hilderbrandt ran unopposed in that spring's election.

In the recordHerald, 1 Apr 2011

7 April 2011

Stephens won presidency with 56 percent of 1,066 votes

Billy Stephens, a junior from Hawesville, defeated senior Diego Leal Ambriz for SGA president, taking 56 percent of the 1,066 total votes in results announced just after midnight on April 7. Kendrick Bryan was re-elected executive vice president and Devon Hilderbrandt won administrative vice president, both unopposed. All 35 senate candidates were elected to the 36 open seats, each needing just one vote to win.

In the recordHerald, 7 Apr 2011

6 May 2011

Senate tabled tobacco-free campus resolution

After nearly 45 minutes of debate, the senate tabled a resolution supporting a tobacco-free campus until the next semester. The resolution rested on a survey of more than 400 students in which 47.82 percent supported a ban, but senators argued the survey was biased because tombstones displaying the dangers of smoking had been laid outside DUC the day it was given. The campus improvements committee then said it would not support a ban until the two-year on-campus living requirement was removed.

2011-12

Billy Stephens (president)

11 entries · 50 index lines

6 September 2011

Senate reconvenes, appointments confirmed, tobacco vote postponed

At the semester's first senate meeting, held after the previous week's session was canceled, senators postponed a vote on the tobacco-free campus resolution so new members could review it first. President Billy Stephens made all of his executive-branch, judicial-committee and committee-chair appointments in a single blanket vote that passed unanimously, and Speaker of the Senate Kaylee Egerer told senators to keep generating new ideas for their committees.

In the recordHerald, 7 Sep 2011

16 September 2011

SGA passes tobacco-free campus resolution with housing condition

The SGA senate voted unanimously for a resolution supporting a campus-wide tobacco ban, but only if WKU dropped its requirement that students live on campus for two years. President Gary Ransdell had said the Board of Regents would not consider a ban until SGA, the University Senate and Staff Council all passed resolutions. SGA was the only body to attach the housing stipulation.

In the recordHerald, 16 Sep 2011

23 September 2011

Fall senate election fills 11 seats with 977 votes cast

SGA announced its fall senator election results just after midnight on Thursday, with 11 new senators elected including Christopher Costa, Corey Johnson and Mallory Treece. Twenty students ran for the seats and 977 students voted. President Billy Stephens said he was happy with the turnout for a fall election and that he got the contested race he wanted.

In the recordHerald, 23 Sep 2011

30 September 2011

SGA to lend iPads through WKU's Educational Resource Center

SGA partnered with the Educational Resource Center in Gary Ransdell Hall to provide two first-generation iPads for the ERC to load textbooks onto and lend out to students at no cost, part of President Billy Stephens' push to make the organization more visibly useful to students.

In the recordHerald, 30 Sep 2011

9 November 2011

Senate defeats DUC name change resolution 19-8

A resolution to rename Downing University Center as Downing Student Union, written by campus improvements chair Keyana Boka, was voted down 19-8 with one abstention after a lengthy open debate. Executive Vice President Kendrick Bryan suggested putting the question on the spring ballot, but senators noted architects working on the DUC renovation would need an answer sooner.

In the recordHerald, 9 Nov 2011

15 November 2011

Herald editorial tells SGA to drop renaming and keep its promises

The Herald editorial board wrote that SGA should focus on student issues rather than renaming a building honoring former president Dero Downing, who had recently died. It said Stephens had barely addressed his platform promises, including a dead day before finals and an improved Provide-a-Ride service, which had seen no visible progress since SGA cut ties with Franklin Taxi.

In the recordHerald, 15 Nov 2011

15 November 2011

Two senators removed for absences; ten faced judicial review

Ten of SGA's 36 senators were called before the judicial board during the fall semester for excessive absences from senate and committee meetings. Two of them, Kevin Adams and Kevin Preston, were dismissed from the senate after failing to attend their judicial hearings, which the council interpreted as intent to resign. The rest received warnings, and Stephens said he would appoint replacements to the two empty seats.

In the recordHerald, 15 Nov 2011

18 November 2011

SGA pays surprise $15,000 bill for previous year's Provide-A-Ride

SGA paid Parking Services a $15,000 retroactive payment for the prior year's Provide-A-Ride service after Parking Services found no record of the previous administration's payment. The money was split evenly between the organizational aid and student scholarship funds, $7,500 from each. Stephens said he was "shocked" by the bill, while 2010-11 president Colton Jessie insisted his administration had budgeted the money.

In the recordHerald, 18 Nov 2011

24 February 2012

SGA approves $50,026 spring budget after $54,000 fall spending

The senate approved a spring 2012 budget of $50,026, divided among operations, public relations, executive programming and senate programming, with $10,500 set aside for organizational aid and $12,000 for scholarships. Chief of Staff Katie Stillwell told senators SGA had spent about $54,000 in the fall, $15,000 of it the retroactive Provide-A-Ride payment.

In the recordHerald, 24 Feb 2012

28 March 2012

Chief of Staff Eric Smiley approved after Stillwell's resignation

The senate approved President Billy Stephens' choice of Eric Smiley as chief of staff after Katie Stillwell resigned the post; Smiley had served as chief of staff two years earlier under his brother Kevin Smiley's administration. The same meeting passed five bills, including two Organizational Aid grants of $500 each to six campus groups (the Chinese Students and Scholars Association, Geology Club, Chinese Music Club, Relay for Life, International Club and Baptist Campus Ministry), $500 toward the April 19 International Night, $398 reimbursing two Kindle Fires raffled at the WKU Student Research Conference, and $500 toward Big Red's trip to the 2012 Mascot Nationals. Senators also passed a resolution for a second campus cleanup on April 17 and moved $3,000 from general senate funds to Organizational Aid so SGA could spend down its budget before the semester's end; announcements noted that spring executive elections would open at 12:01 a.m. the next day.

In the recordHerald, 28 Mar 2012

30 March 2012

Dodds elected SGA president for 2012-13; Boka and Alvey win uncontested VP races

Cory Dodds was elected SGA president for 2012-13 with 44 percent of the vote, over senator Corey Johnson with 35 percent and former senator Austin Wingate with 21 percent. Sophomore Keyana Boka was elected executive vice president and Cain Alvey administrative vice president; both vice-presidential races were uncontested.

2012-13

Cory Dodds (president)

15 entries · 31 index lines

6 September 2012

Senate unanimously opposed the bi-term and per-credit-hour tuition proposal

SGA unanimously passed a resolution opposing an administration proposal to emphasize bi-term courses and shift tuition to a per-credit-hour model. Dodds argued the change would charge students more, since full-time students then paid for 12 hours but could enroll in up to 18. He called it a financial burden students should not have to bear.

In the recordHerald, 6 Sep 2012

25 September 2012

Fall senate seats filled after two resignations

Two senators resigned over time commitments and student Shea Wyatt was named to fill in. New freshman senators Barrett Greenwell, Taylor Ruby, Cole McDowell and Paige Settles were sworn in along with Gatton Academy senator Linda Cruz and graduate senators Mark Reeves and Katie Martin.

In the recordHerald, 27 Sep 2012

13 November 2012

SGA worked to restart a student government for the Glasgow campus

SGA officials traveled to WKU's Glasgow campus to help restart its dormant student representative body and connect it to main campus. Dodds said Glasgow students had no mechanism for their voices to be heard except contacting main-campus SGA. Roy Ratliff, nominated interim president of the Glasgow student body, said its top priority was establishing a writing center, for which the group had set aside $2,700.

In the recordHerald, 13 Nov 2012

14 November 2012

Senate reviewed its budget and heard a retention pitch

Speaker of the Senate Christopher Costa opened the floor to Joelle Carter, assistant vice president for Retention and Student Services, who asked senators for ideas on keeping students enrolled to graduation, and SGA's current budget balance was distributed to members.

In the recordHerald, 14 Nov 2012

15 February 2013

SGA passed its teaching assistant evaluation resolution

On Feb. 5 the senate passed a resolution creating teaching assistant evaluations, a program Dodds had campaigned on and drafted with his executive officers to pitch to Provost Gordon Emslie. Nicki Seay, chair of the Legislative Research Committee, wrote the bill, which aimed to make TA evaluation uniform across departments. The resolution next needed review by the Academic Quality Committee and approval by the University Senate.

In the recordHerald, 15 Feb 2013

20 February 2013

Senate tabled a plus-minus grading resolution

Senator Hannah Garland's resolution to adopt plus and minus grades for high and low letter grades was tabled after public relations chair Mac Mullins argued for a more formal proposal and more student input first; a similar plan had drawn SGA resistance when the provost proposed it in 2007.

In the recordHerald, 21 Feb 2013

26 February 2013

Senate amended the constitution, adding regional-campus seats

After two to three years of executive discussion, the senate passed most of a package of constitutional amendments, replacing class-year senate seats with college-based representation and guaranteeing seats for regional campuses such as Glasgow and Owensboro. A bylaw that would have made SGA members permanently ineligible for SGA-sponsored scholarships was stripped out and did not pass.

In the recordHerald, 1 Mar 2013

March 2013campus

Provost Emslie cancelled the bi-term proposal SGA had opposed

Provost Gordon Emslie announced at the Feb. 21 University Senate meeting that he was dropping his bi-term emphasis proposal and had no plans to revive it, citing input from students and faculty. Both SGA and the University Senate had passed resolutions against it. Dodds said the revocation was in the student body's best interest, saying "I strongly opposed it."

In the recordHerald, 1 Mar 2013

19 March 2013

SGA passed a resolution opposing the $150 Study Abroad application fee

The senate passed a resolution opposing a new $150 application fee for Study Abroad and Global Learning, set to begin with summer 2013 programs, arguing it could put program costs out of reach; the University Senate passed a similar resolution. A separate amendment proposed by Dodds to make SGA members ineligible for SGA-awarded Study Abroad scholarships failed. Dodds said 11 of the 125 such scholarships and grants that year had gone to SGA members, which he called a conflict of interest.

Outgoing President Cory Dodds opened a candidates' debate in Ransdell Hall Auditorium by describing to the audience how much influence the students elected to SGA's executive positions would actually have on WKU's campus, ahead of the election that would choose his successor.

In the recordHerald, 29 Mar 2013

4 April 2013

Boka, Reeves and Seay won the spring executive elections

Executive vice president Keyana Boka was elected 2013-14 SGA president with 626 votes, 47 percent, over administrative vice president Cain Alvey with 419 votes and Austin Wingate with 300. Mark Reeves won executive vice president with 632 votes, 57 percent, over Brad Cockrel, and Nicki Seay ran unopposed for administrative vice president. Twenty-six student senators were also elected.

In the recordHerald, 4 Apr 2013

9 April 2013

Judicial Council disqualified president-elect Boka, 3-2

In an emergency meeting, SGA's Judicial Council voted 3-2 to disqualify president-elect Keyana Boka after runner-up and outgoing administrative vice president Cain Alvey complained that a self-promotional email had gone to Honors College students at Boka's request, sent by Honors College graduate assistant Destiny Savage, violating the election code. The disqualification would have made Alvey, who had placed second in the April 4 vote, the incoming president; Boka said she would appeal.

In the recordHerald, 9 Apr 2013

16 April 2013

Judicial Council declined to contest Bailey's reinstatement of Boka

In an emergency meeting, the Judicial Council unanimously decided not to challenge Bailey's memorandum reinstating Boka, saying it did not believe it had the power to do so, while expressing official disapproval. Chief Justice Seth Church called the intervention "a serious infringement on our autonomy" and said it set a bad precedent. Boka said the council had conflicts of interest and needed to be held accountable by a neutral party in the administration.

In the recordHerald, 16 Apr 2013

16 April 2013

Costa stepped down as speaker; Settles elected to succeed him

Christopher Costa did not seek another term as speaker of the senate, and senators elected Paige Settles to replace him. The same meeting confirmed presidential appointments Roy Ratliff as senator-at-large and Jackie Stewart as associate justice, with Chief of Staff Travis Taylor and public relations chair Mac Mullins giving reports.

In the recordHerald, 17 Apr 2013

25 April 2013campus

Senate opposed automatic athletic-fee increases ahead of C-USA move

The senate passed a resolution against the annual automatic increase in the student athletic fee, arguing the revenue-sharing WKU would gain on joining Conference USA that July would outweigh the roughly $40,000 the university lost annually in the Sun Belt. Executive vice president-elect Mark Reeves, who introduced it, said the resolution set a precedent against automatic fee increases generally.

What this is

A year-by-year record of student government at Western Kentucky University, from the ratification of the Associated Students constitution in April 1966 to the present. Every entry names the source it came from.

Corrections

The names here start with the plaque in the SGA Chambers, and the plaque is known to be wrong in places. Where the archive disagrees with it, this site follows the archive and records the change on the corrections page. If you served, or you know that a year here is wrong, the project wants to hear from you through the Student Government Association office.
