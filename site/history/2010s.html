2010-11

Colton Jessie (president)

11 entries · 26 index lines

14 September 2010

SGA raised scholarship budget to $20,000

After running out of scholarship money the previous year, SGA increased its scholarship budget from $12,000 to $20,000 and lowered general senate funding from $20,000 to $12,000 to cover the change. Study abroad awards were capped at $500 for a semester trip and $250 for a winter or summer term so more students could receive money. SGA also finalized a new study away scholarship for travel inside the country and set aside $400 for it in the winter term.

In the recordHerald, 14 Sep 2010

23 September 2010

Twelve senators elected in fall election with 847 voters

Twelve students won seats in the fall senate election, in which 847 total voters cast ballots on TopNet. Keyana Boka, Natalie Broderick and Eppiphanie Benton were elected freshman senators, running unopposed, while 25 students competed for the at-large seats. Winners included Diego Leal Ambriz, who would run for president that spring.

In the recordHerald, 23 Sep 2010

19 November 2010

Ransdell brought $49 million DUC renovation plan to SGA

President Gary Ransdell and head architect Jeff Stivers told SGA at its Nov. 16 meeting that officials were moving ahead on a plan to completely renovate Downing University Center at a cost of up to $49 million. Students would pay about $140 extra per year in fees for up to 20 years, and Ransdell promised students would be involved in every decision. SGA had to pass resolutions on the design and fees before the Board of Regents could approve the project.

In the recordHerald, 19 Nov 2010

7 December 2010

SGA funds extended library hours for finals week

The senate approved $600 to keep the library open until 2 a.m. during finals week, covering extra staff time and lighting. President Colton Jessie said continuing the service was never in question, calling finals "an important time for students."

In the recordHerald, 7 Dec 2010

10 December 2010

Senate tabled DUC resolution over lack of student input

Senators tabled the bill supporting the DUC renovation at the Dec. 7 meeting, saying they had not received enough student input just three weeks after learning of the project. Jessie backed the delay, saying he wanted to hear students' voices before moving forward, even though it pushed SGA's presentation to the Board of Regents from January 21 to April 22. Campus Improvements Chair Kaylee Egerer said many students did not yet know they would pay $140 a year for the project.

In the recordHerald, 10 Dec 2010

11 February 2011

Herald reported Greeks held 40 percent of SGA seats

The Herald reported that 21 of SGA's 50 members belonged to Greek organizations, more than 40 percent, while less than 8 percent of the student body was in a fraternity or sorority. Sixteen of the 21 were fraternity men, and two fraternities, Sigma Chi and Lambda Chi Alpha, accounted for nine of them. Officers defended the imbalance, and the story noted SGA's operating budget for 2010-11 was $121,335, drawn from tuition.

In the recordHerald, 11 Feb 2011

22 February 2011

SGA unanimously passed DUC renovation resolution

SGA unanimously passed the resolution supporting DUC renovations at its Feb. 22 meeting, the bill's second reading. Project costs were capped at $49,128,545, with a student fee of up to $70 per semester for 20 years and $1.5 million per year from WKU. The resolution, authored by Kaylee Egerer, asked for assurance of a 24-hour study space and called for construction to begin as soon as possible while interest rates were low.

In the recordHerald, 22 Feb 2011

March 2011

SGA members joined Rally for Higher Education in Frankfort

SGA members traveled to Frankfort on March 1 for the annual Rally for Higher Education, where SGA secretary Katie Stillwell was the keynote speaker representing students statewide. Her speech urged legislators to protect money already set aside for higher education. Jessie, the previous year's keynote speaker, joined chief of staff Charlie Harris and other state SGA officers for a lunch with Gov. Steve Beshear.

April 2011

Administrative Vice President Wade Pierce to hand post to fraternity brother

Pierce, who chaired the organizational aid committee, said he would be succeeded as administrative vice president by Devon Hilderbrandt, his committee co-chair and his "little brother" in Lambda Chi Alpha. Hilderbrandt ran unopposed in that spring's election.

7 April 2011

Stephens won presidency with 56 percent of 1,066 votes

Billy Stephens, a junior from Hawesville, defeated senior Diego Leal Ambriz for SGA president, taking 56 percent of the 1,066 total votes in results announced just after midnight on April 7. Kendrick Bryan was re-elected executive vice president and Devon Hilderbrandt won administrative vice president, both unopposed. All 35 senate candidates were elected to the 36 open seats, each needing just one vote to win.

In the recordHerald, 7 Apr 2011

6 May 2011

Senate tabled tobacco-free campus resolution

After nearly 45 minutes of debate, the senate tabled a resolution supporting a tobacco-free campus until the next semester. The resolution rested on a survey of more than 400 students in which 47.82 percent supported a ban, but senators argued the survey was biased because tombstones displaying the dangers of smoking had been laid outside DUC the day it was given. The campus improvements committee then said it would not support a ban until the two-year on-campus living requirement was removed.

6 September 2011

Senate reconvenes, appointments confirmed, tobacco vote postponed

At the semester's first senate meeting, held after the previous week's session was canceled, senators postponed a vote on the tobacco-free campus resolution so new members could review it first. SGA ties tobacco ban support to ending two-year housing rule

The SGA senate passed a tobacco-free campus resolution that supported a ban only if the requirement for students to live on campus for two years was eliminated. Speaker of the Senate Kaylee Egerer said SGA backed the ban "if and only if" the housing requirement went. President Gary Ransdell had said the Board of Regents would not consider a tobacco-free campus until the University Senate, Staff Council and SGA each passed their own resolutions.

President Gary Ransdell had said the Board of Regents would not consider a ban until SGA, the University Senate and Staff Council all passed resolutions. SGA was the only body to attach the housing stipulation.

Ransdell tells SGA the two-year housing requirement cannot be dropped

What this is

A year-by-year record of student government at Western Kentucky University, from the ratification of the Associated Students constitution in April 1966 to the present. Every entry names the source it came from.

Corrections

The names here start with the plaque in the SGA Chambers, and the plaque is known to be wrong in places. Where the archive disagrees with it, this site follows the archive and records the change on the corrections page. If you served, or you know that a year here is wrong, the project wants to hear from you through the Student Government Association office.
