18 August 2001Bedo questions timing of 9 percent tuition increase, then votes for it. WKU's Board of Regents voted on Friday, August 17 to raise tuition 9 percent starting in fall 2002, the first tuition hike in university history devoted entirely to faculty and staff pay. Student Regent Leslie Bedo said she was troubled that the vote came before students officially began fall classes, telling the board it sent a message that the university would slide the increase through before students could respond. Despite her concerns, Bedo joined the unanimous vote approving the increase, which raised in-state undergraduate tuition $138 a semester, from $1,422 to $1,560. In the recordDaily News, 18 Aug 2001
7 September 2001Regents approve $32.5 million Diddle Arena bond deal funded by student athletics fee. The Board of Regents approved an agreement with the City of Bowling Green to sell up to $32.5 million in bonds to renovate E.A. Diddle Arena, with the university repaying the debt service through its student athletics fee. Planned improvements included air conditioning, new seating, luxury boxes, expanded parking and a pedway from new parking areas to the arena. The renovation was scheduled to run from April to September 2002. In the recordWKU News, 7 Sep 2001
13 September 2001Students organize candlelight vigil after September 11 attacks. 2002-03Jamie Sears (president)

22 August 2002Record enrollment of 17,500 strains campus. Western opened the fall with enrollment ballooning to 17,500, nearly 1,000 more students than the year before and a 20 percent increase since 1997. President Gary Ransdell warned that growth would be "painful" amid decreased state funding, and the Board of Regents approved a 10.4 percent tuition increase for 2003-04. In the recordHerald, 22 Aug 2002
19 September 2002Members demand answers on missing gazebo money. SGA members Brooke Smith, Dana Lockhart and Sam Stinson petitioned congress over $28,000 they said was unaccounted for, including about $5,000 that a April 2001 bill had set aside to build an on-campus gazebo that never appeared. President Jamie Sears said of the gazebo fund, "I have absolutely no idea where that money went." The petition was withdrawn after the executive council formed an ad hoc financial review committee. In the recordHerald, 19 Sep 2002
24 September 2002Herald editorial blasts SGA over 'Gazebo-Gate'. The Herald's 11-member editorial board attacked SGA leaders over what it called "Gazebo-Gate," saying the money had gone to computers for the SGA office instead of the promised gazebo. It called leaders' ignorance about the missing money "shameful" and challenged Sears by name to "do her job" and provide real answers, writing that "I don't know" did not cut it. In the recordHerald, 24 Sep 2002
October 2002Gazebo funds traced to SGA general fund. Vice president of finance Ross Pruitt reported that the $13,000 SGA had put toward the gazebo mistakenly went back into the organization's general fund and was spent on other things, including computers for SGA. Sears said she and former student activities director Scott Taylor decided over the summer where the leftover budget money would go, with other executive council members consulted by email. Pruitt said leaders from the past two administrations shared the blame, saying "I didn't do my homework." In the recordHerald, 1 Oct 2002
24 October 2002Congress votes down enrollment cap resolution. SGA congress rejected by a large margin a resolution asking Western to implement an enrollment cap, with Sears among those calling the measure too drastic. The failed proposal argued the university and state budgets had not kept pace with enrollment growth from 14,543 students in 1997 to about 17,770. Provost Barbara Burch and President Ransdell both said a cap was not the best answer to the growth problem. In the recordHerald, 24 Oct 2002
5 December 2002Three resolutions pass on faculty pay, enrollment, parking. SGA passed two resolutions responding to a shortage of 75 faculty members and the lowest-paid faculty in the state, asking that unbudgeted tuition surplus money, about $800,000 that year, be used to hire faculty, and encouraging President Ransdell to create a task force on enrollment. A third resolution requested a change to the policy that gave Hilltopper Athletic Foundation donors student parking spaces, including the Grise Hall lot, at basketball games. In the recordHerald, 5 Dec 2002
6 February 2003Freshman committee appointments tabled. SGA tabled Sears's proposal to appoint three freshmen, Tim Howard, Katie Dawson and Robert Watkins, as the student members of a joint University Senate and SGA enrollment growth committee. Sears said she picked freshmen so they could serve the committee's expected two to three year life, but critics including associate justice Dana Lockhart said the appointments would make it hard for SGA to be taken seriously. In the recordHerald, 6 Feb 2003
4 March 2003SGA joins Frankfort rally against budget cuts. About 200 students from six public universities rallied at the state Capitol against cuts to higher education, after the Kentucky House passed a budget cutting Western's funding by more than $1 million. Western sent a delegation of roughly 20 people including SGA members, organized through the board of Kentucky's student body presidents. Sears said 17 people "took the initiative to get on the bus." In the recordHerald, 4 Mar 2003
4 March 2003Congress puts $3 radio fee referendum on ballot. SGA unanimously approved a bill placing a referendum on the April 2-3 general election ballot letting students vote on a $3 student fee for campus radio station Revolution 91.7-WWHR, which was operating on a $7,000 budget unchanged since 1998. Executive vice president John Bradley, who championed the measure, said it would empower students and boost voter turnout. A yes vote would send the fee recommendation to the Board of Regents for a final decision. In the recordHerald, 4 Mar 2003
8 April 2003Bradley elected president; 2,014 vote. A total of 2,014 students voted in the spring SGA election, with results announced shortly after 1 a.m. Friday by chief justice Holly Skidmore. Paducah junior John Bradley won the presidency unopposed with 1,662 votes, Patti Johnson beat Dana Lockhart 1,185 to 685 for executive vice president, and Jessica Martin beat Shawn Peavie 1,056 to 686 for vice president of administration. The $3 WWHR radio fee referendum passed 1,066 to 765 and moved to the Board of Regents. In the recordHerald, 8 Apr 2003
29 April 2003SGA closes year after 55 pieces of legislation. SGA held its last meeting of the year on April 22, having heard and debated more than 55 pieces of legislation, including a resolution that earmarked funds for new benches across campus. Sears counted the Frankfort rally among the year's greatest accomplishments, and the Revolution 91.7 fee measure stood as the first student referendum in SGA history, awaiting a final Board of Regents vote on May 9. President-elect Bradley said the high election turnout was the semester's best moment and pledged to fight student apathy the next year. In the recordHerald, 29 Apr 2003

What this is

A year-by-year record of student government at Western Kentucky University, from the ratification of the Associated Students constitution in April 1966 to the present. Every entry names the source it came from.

Corrections

The names here start with the plaque in the SGA Chambers, and the plaque is known to be wrong in places. Where the archive disagrees with it, this site follows the archive and records the change on the corrections page. If you served, or you know that a year here is wrong, the project wants to hear from you through the Student Government Association office.

Text on this site is the work of the project. Photographs, documents and legislation are reproduced from Western Kentucky University's own open archives and from the College Heights Herald and the Talisman; each item names the collection, issue and page it came from and links to the original.
