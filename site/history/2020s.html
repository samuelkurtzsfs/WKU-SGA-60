2020-21J. Garrett Edmonds (president)

24 September 2020Town hall debate pitted the sitting EVP against a senator. SGA held a town hall debate in the DSU auditorium between presidential candidates Garrett Edmonds, the sitting executive vice president, and senator MJ Mayo. Edmonds's original running mate for administrative vice president, Nathan Terrell, had dropped off the Edmonds-Norvell ticket and was replaced by Abbey Norvell; the debate covered WKU's COVID-19 response and the summer's Black Lives Matter protests on campus. In the recordWKU Talisman, Voter's Guide, 28 Sep 2020
29 September 2020Edmonds elected president over Mayo. Garrett Edmonds, the sitting executive vice president, was elected student body president over senator MJ Mayo in balloting held on TopNet Sept. 28-29, succeeding Will Harris. Edmonds had campaigned on creating a Director of Outreach position to link campus organizations more directly with SGA. In the recordHerald, 6 Oct 2020
30 September 2020Norvell elected administrative vice president; EVP still open. SGA held a virtual election party over Zoom to announce results. The Edmonds-Norvell ticket defeated MJ Mayo for the presidency, and Abbey Norvell was elected administrative vice president alongside Edmonds. The Herald reported no official decision had yet been made on a separate executive vice president post, with an announcement expected at the next SGA meeting. In the recordHerald, 30 Sep 2020
7 October 2020Senate passed the annual budget 28-5 and elected Shelby Robertson speaker. At the first official senate meeting under the new leadership, Shelby Robertson defeated Brenna Matthews to become speaker of the senate and the budget passed 28-5. Edmonds emphasized a $6,000 Projects Fund, including $1,000 for Housing and Residence Life PPE and an expansion of the Go with the Flow menstrual products program into Pearce Ford Tower. Senator MJ Mayo challenged the balance of stipends; Edmonds said all senate stipends except the top three offices had been cut to feed the Projects Fund. In the recordHerald, 7 Oct 2020
13 October 2020Cabinet set office hours, an outreach committee, and pushed inclusive language. At the executive cabinet's first recorded meeting, Edmonds set up officer office hours and an ad hoc outreach committee for RSO leadership. Speaker Shelby Robertson pushed to connect senators with similar legislative interests, adopt inclusive pronoun language in the chamber, and find a parliamentarian. In the recordSGA Executive Cabinet minutes, 13 Oct 2020Read it here
21 October 2020Keller confirmed AVP 29-1; two new committee chairs elected. The senate confirmed Isaac Keller as administrative vice president 29-1 after Edmonds said he wanted a deputy willing to challenge him when they disagreed. Roderick Maul and Tess Welch were elected committee chairs, and senators voted to form a new Ad Hoc committee chaired by Me'Lon Craighead. In the recordHerald, 21 Oct 2020
11 November 2020Judicial nominations confirmed; senate revives a Pass/D/Fail request. The senate unanimously confirmed Edmonds's nominations of Derek Collins and Lexi Mayne as associate justices and Addison McCoun as senator at large. Senators passed a Tess Welch/Eric Barry bill to increase campus accessibility and split funding for SGA-branded masks and hand sanitizer between the senate and SGA's PR team. A closing bill by Tate McClard and Jacob Congleton, with every senator listed as a co-author, asked WKU to restore the Pass/D/Fail grading option it had offered in spring 2020 but declined to bring back that fall. In the recordHerald, 11 Nov 2020 (corrected)
16 November 2020Edmonds launched a P/D/F petition. President Garrett Edmonds released a student petition asking WKU to offer a Pass/D/Fail grading option for the fall semester, citing inconsistent course modalities and the strain of quarantine on students without an online-learning fallback. The petition reached half its 1,000-signature goal within an hour and the target was raised to 5,000. In the recordHerald, 18 Nov 2020
18 November 2020Associate provost rebuffed SGA's P/D/F request; computers bill passes 28-3. Edmonds told senators that Associate Provost Rob Hale had defended the university's decision to extend the drop deadline rather than restore Pass/D/Fail grading, and said he was "deeply disturbed" by the response; senator Brenna Mathews argued the policy left students choosing between attending in person or paying for an extra semester. Edmonds urged senators to keep circulating his petition, which had passed 3,000 signatures, and the senate voted 28-3 to fund recycled computers for the SGA office, a number scaled back to meet CDC guidelines. In the recordHerald, 18 Nov 2020
December 2020WKU relented and allowed pass grades after SGA's P/D/F campaign. Provost Cheryl Stevens told faculty that students who faced COVID-related problems could opt for a pass grade instead of a B or C, reversing the university's earlier refusal. The Faculty Senate had voted 40-9 in favor and SGA voted unanimously for the option, presenting nearly 20 pages of student testimony; SGA's petition had drawn over 3,500 signatures in three days. Students had to email their professor by Jan. 13, with the final decision left to the faculty member. In the recordHerald, 1 Dec 2020
2 December 2020Fall semester closed with a safe-driving pledge and organization funding. At the last meeting of the fall semester, Edmonds thanked senators for the work that led to the new P/D/F option. Senators unanimously passed a Matthew Thomas bill committing SGA's social media to a "Drive Safe Hilltoppers" safety campaign, and unanimously funded a bill supporting HonorsToppers, College Republicans, Zeta Phi Beta and the Sociology and Criminology Club. In the recordHerald, 2 Dec 2020
20 January 2021Spring semester opened over Zoom with a late-fee pledge. At its first meeting of the spring semester, held on Zoom, SGA heard Edmonds say the provost search was in its final stage with interviews starting within weeks, and that he planned to look at changing or removing the late fees charged when a student's financial aid arrives late. Senators unanimously nominated Jamison Moorehead as director of IT and funded Dance Big Red and a Delta Sigma Pi virtual leadership conference. In the recordHerald, 20 Jan 2021
26 January 2021Committee chairs set spring priorities. Legislative Research Chair Dawson McCoun said the shift to remote meetings after March 2020 had made it harder to connect with students and pass legislation. Diversity and Inclusion Chair Fatin Yaro announced a Feb. 24 diversity event and legislation to set consequences for discrimination within student organizations, and Chief of Staff Matt Barr said he would continue the "Go with the Flow" program installing free menstrual-product dispensers on campus. SGA planned a return to masked, socially distanced in-person meetings that semester. In the recordHerald, 26 Jan 2021
2 February 2021Presidential task force sought input on slaveholding college namesakes. WKU's Presidential Task Force on Naming and Symbols, formed by President Timothy Caboni in fall 2020, collected input from students and the community on the namesakes of Potter College and Ogden College, both built on wealth from slave labor. Two Potter descendants said it was time to find a new name. The task force planned to audit names and send recommendations to the administration. In the recordHerald, 2 Feb 2021
3 February 2021New senator seated; rally with Gov. Beshear announced. SGA nominated pre-law sophomore Will Dykeman as a new senator and Edmonds announced a virtual Student Body President rally for March 26 featuring Kentucky Gov. Andy Beshear, with Edmonds working to add state Rep. Charles Booker. Senators unanimously funded a bill by AVP Isaac Keller supporting Amazing Tones of Joy, Black Women of Western, Queer Student Union and the Business Association for Development, and voted $2,500 for ISEC's Black Magic Masquerade Ball on a bill by Me'Lon Craighead. In the recordHerald, 3 Feb 2021
11 February 2021Outreach signage and safe-driving bills pass. Guest speaker Ethan Logan, vice president of enrollment and student experience, briefed senators on how his office could help SGA. Edmonds pointed senators toward an upcoming provost-search update and urged them to consider running in the April officer elections. Senators unanimously passed a Me'Lon Craighead bill funding 50 laminated, QR-coded outreach signs for campus and a Matthew Thomas bill adding 12 safe-driving signs to campus parking lots; a bill on WKU's Naming and Symbols Task Force was held for the following week. In the recordHerald, 11 Feb 2021
3 March 2021SGA backed renaming buildings tied to racism and funded a community garden. The senate passed legislation by Brenna Matthews and Tess Welch offering SGA's support to the Naming and Symbols Task Force in changing the names of WKU buildings with ties to racism or injustice. Matthews said ties to slave owning and the Confederate army did not belong on a campus committed to diversity. Senators also unanimously funded a community garden outside the Office of Sustainability and allocated $3,500 to four student organizations. In the recordHerald, 3 Mar 2021
18 March 2021Constitution amended; Caboni's contract extended. Edmonds told senators the WKU Board of Regents had extended President Timothy Caboni's contract four years. The senate elected Addison McCoun to head the Legislative Research Committee after Dawson McCoun's resignation, passed a Tess Welch/Erika Puhakka bill amending constitutional wording 19-4, and funded a Sustainability Committee spring photo contest on a bill by Matthew Waninger and Elizabeth DeLozier. In the recordHerald, 18 Mar 2021
31 March 2021Largest organizational aid bill passed; chief justice resigned before elections. Edmonds announced that Chief Justice Erika Puhakka had stepped down and declined to nominate a replacement so close to elections, and warned that only 13 people were running for senate. The senate passed its largest organizational aid bill ever, funding five student groups, and passed 16-0 with two abstentions a bill by Kyle Phillips asking WKU to support a codified vetting process for campus dining vendors after a controversial social media post by the owner of Lost River Pizza. In the recordHerald, 31 Mar 2021
May 2021Edmonds addressed the Class of 2021 at the delayed commencement. As SGA president, Edmonds spoke at WKU's Fall 2020/Spring 2021 commencement ceremony, telling graduates that the class's perseverance through the pandemic year had brought them to their day of celebration. 2021-22Matthew Wininger (president)

6 August 2021Northeast Hall renamed for Margaret Munday. Student Regent Matthew Wininger was sworn in at the Board of Regents meeting that renamed Northeast Hall for WKU's first African American student to enrol - the first building on campus named after an African American. In the recordWKU News
September 2021SGA opened the fall term with confirmations and new election codes. SGA held its first meeting of the fall semester, the second meeting of the 21st Senate, with President Matthew Wininger welcoming new and returning senators. All senators present were confirmed by unanimous consent and sworn in by Chief Justice Holden Schroeder, along with executive appointments Jamison Moorehead as IT director, Alexis Courtenay as public relations director and Parker Raybourne as chief of staff. New election codes for the next semester also passed with unanimous consent. In the recordHerald, 1 Sep 2021
10 September 2021Senate confirmed the year's budget. At the third meeting of the 21st Senate on Sept. 7, SGA passed Bill 1.21f, confirming its budget for the year. Dean of Students Martha Sales spoke on leadership, and Wininger announced a planned donor drive in partnership with at least four other student organizations. Chief of Staff Parker Raybourne announced a Hilltopper Hangout event for Sept. 24. In the recordHerald, 10 Sep 2021
15 September 2021New senators seated and freshman election results announced. The senate unanimously voted in four new senate members, two judicial council members and Trib Singh as director of academic and student affairs. Speaker of the Senate Tess Welch introduced committees on legislative operations, student experience, mental health, sustainability, campus improvement, diversity and student outreach. That night the fall election results named Addison Falkner, Sam Kurtz and Jenna Wells freshman senators and Arivumani Srivastava the Gatton Academy senator. In the recordHerald, 15 Sep 2021
6 October 202121st Senate passed its first resolution, on advisor evaluations. At its Oct. 5 meeting SGA passed Resolution 1.21f, addressing the lack of an advisor evaluation system, by a 21-2 vote, the first resolution of the 21st Senate. Bill 2.21f, barring the speaker of the senate from voting on legislation, passed 21-2, and two bills scrubbing outdated 2007-era bylaws passed by unanimous consent. Parking director Jennifer Tougas discussed a possible monthly parking tag subscription and campus bookstore operations. In the recordHerald, 6 Oct 2021
December 2021Resolution condemning critical race theory bans passed 16-15. After weeks of debate, the senate passed Resolution 7.21f, which condemned the banning of critical race theory teachings in postsecondary education ahead of an anticipated bill in the Kentucky legislature. The vote was 16 in favor, 15 against, with two abstentions. Sponsor Olivia Blackmon said the measure was meant to ensure intellectual freedom in the curriculum. In the recordHerald, 1 Dec 2021
12 December 2021EF-3 tornado struck Bowling Green, killing 11 in Warren County. An EF-3 tornado with 150 mph winds slammed into Bowling Green early on Dec. 11, killing 11 people according to the coroner and destroying homes and businesses across Warren County. Gov. Andy Beshear signed a state of emergency and President Biden signed an emergency declaration. The disaster shaped SGA relief efforts the following semester. In the recordHerald, 12 Dec 2021
18 January 2022First 2022 meeting funded tornado-drained food pantry. At its first meeting of 2022, SGA passed Bill 1.22s by unanimous consent, providing $1,000 to restock the WKU Office of Sustainability food pantry, whose food and toiletries had been drained by the tornado recovery effort. Wininger announced an initiative to create a student resource handbook for underclassmen. Speaker Tess Welch announced spring election dates of April 18-19. In the recordHerald, 18 Jan 2022
16 February 2022Syllabi clause, crosswalk resolutions and Title IX meetings passed. At its Feb. 15 meeting SGA unanimously passed Resolution 6-22-S to add an SGA-related clause to course syllabi, which the Herald reported would be the first of its kind in SGA history. Three resolutions supported new crosswalks below the Van Meter green space and on Chestnut and Normal streets, and Bill 6-22-S required a committee chair or delegate to meet with the Counseling Center director and Title IX coordinator each semester. The senate also approved a task force for the iWKU app and funding for a financial success workshop. In the recordHerald, 16 Feb 2022
14 April 2022Judiciary Council cleared Bornefeld's campaign over student-all email. After several SGA members complained that presidential candidate Cole Bornefeld's student-wide email about the Swipe Out Hunger initiative violated the election code's ban on self-promotion, the Judiciary Council held a roughly hour-long Zoom hearing on April 13 and voted in favor of his campaign. Speaker Tess Welch argued the signed email constituted self-promotion, while a statement from President Wininger, read by Chief Justice Holden Schroeder, said nothing in the code required executive approval for a student-all email. Senators said the initiative saw a record number of donations after the email went out. In the recordHerald, 14 Apr 2022
20 April 2022Cole Bornefeld elected with 49% of 1,448 votes. Alexis Courtenay took 40% and Olivia Feck 11%. Sam Kurtz and Garrison Reed were elected to the two vice presidencies. In the recordWKU News
20 April 2022End-of-year allocations funded scholarships, organizational aid and planetarium donation. Hours before the 2022 election results were announced, the senate passed a slate of funding bills: $8,000 in organizational aid at $500 per applicant, and $15,500 across five scholarship programs including summer class, study abroad, ISEC book and Scholar Level scholarships, plus $500 to Black Student Alliance scholarships for the first time. The senate also approved $325 for the Hardin Planetarium, $500 for a student worker appreciation event, $300 for the CEC Run/Walk for Autism and $570.02 for an e-waste recycling bin on main campus. In the recordHerald, 20 Apr 2022

President Cole Bornefeld said the group was building a one-stop resource guide for students and trying to promote SGA at WKU's regional campuses in Elizabethtown, Fort Knox, Glasgow, Owensboro and Somerset. Speaker Julie Mishchuk said the underlying goal of the year was financial assistance for students. In the recordHerald, 30 Aug 2022

WKU formally dedicated the $35 million Commons at Helm Library, a renovation funded through a partnership with Aramark, and President Timothy Caboni announced early plans for a similar makeover of Cherry Hall. SGA President Cole Bornefeld spoke at the ceremony, saying the building had been full of excitement and community since it opened. In the recordHerald, 31 Aug 2022
September 2022First meeting filled senate and cabinet vacancies. At its first meeting of the year on Aug. 30, the senate approved all of President Bornefeld's nominees, including Juan Thomas, Will Hemenover and Sydney Denney as senators-at-large, Aniya Johnson as director of information technology, Donte' Reed as director of enrollment and student experience, and Isaac Kind and Garrett Baum to the judicial council. Administrative Vice President Sam Kurtz spoke on the budget and Chief Justice Holden Schroeder swore in the new members. In the recordHerald, 1 Sep 2022
21 September 2022Fall election turnout doubled; Reed appointed to state council. Fall 2022 election results announced at midnight seated Rush Robinson, Callison Padgett and Rhett Helland as freshman senators, plus Neel Patel for Gatton Academy, Shy'Ante Williams as graduate senator and Salvador Leon Golib as international student senator. President Bornefeld said the vote total rose from 398 the previous fall to 805. SGA also announced that Vice President Garrison Reed had been appointed student representative on Kentucky's Council on Postsecondary Education, with a term running through June 30, 2023. In the recordHerald, 21 Sep 2022
25 January 2023Senate approved five nominations and reviewed a shrinking budget. The senate unanimously confirmed five nominations, including Adan Canizalez as chair of student enrollment and experience and Miles Huff, a track athlete, to the judicial council. Administrative Vice President Sam Kurtz urged senators to sponsor more resolutions, which use university funds, because only about $39,460.76 remained of SGA's $100,000 annual budget for bills. Speaker Julie Mishchuk floated a constitutional amendment requiring every senator to sponsor at least one bill or resolution. In the recordHerald, 25 Jan 2023
February 2023Voucher bills funded dental clinic, ID center and Preston Center. The senate unanimously passed Bill 28-23-S, putting $500 toward free teeth cleanings at the WKU Dental Health Clinic after voucher use rose 660 percent in the fall, and Bill 27-23-S, allocating $2,000 in discount vouchers split 60/30/10 among the Preston Center, the ID center and the dental hygiene clinic. A $260 bill funding a poetry mic night with the Talisman also passed with one nay vote. Former SGA President Matthew Wininger opened the meeting as guest speaker. In the recordHerald, 1 Feb 2023
15 February 2023Narcan resolution aimed to put overdose spray in residence halls. SGA unanimously passed Resolution 2-23-S on Feb. 7 supporting placement of Narcan, an opioid overdose reversal spray, in WKU residence halls. SGA had planned to spend about $1,000 of its own budget, but a University of Kentucky harm reduction specialist helped secure the product through a government grant instead. Housing and Residence Life planned to install the spray in AED-style boxes across its 15 dorms by Aug. 1 to allow time for staff training. In the recordHerald, 15 Feb 2023
17 February 2023The Speaker takes the President to the Judicial Council. Speaker of the Senate Yulia 'Julie' Mishchuk requested a censure hearing against President Cole Bornefeld over anti-transgender posts he had liked on Instagram. Members of the Queer Student Union and both vice presidents attended. The Judicial Council voted unanimously against censure. In the recordHerald, Feb 2023
19 February 2023A Title IX report seeks the President's removal. Filed with Student Conduct. Under the constitution, removing an executive who will not resign requires written endorsement from at least 20% of the Senate, a wait of at least fourteen days and a two-thirds vote of the full Senate sitting as a court. In the recordHerald, March 2023
22 February 2023Menstrual product legislation passed as QSU protested. At its Feb. 21 meeting SGA passed a resolution supporting menstrual product dispensers on the first floor of every academic building, a $200 bill to distribute 35 menstrual cups at tabling events, and $100 for a community relations survey. Queer Student Union president Alistar Flowers announced QSU had removed SGA meetings from its list of campus safe spaces after the judicial council declined to censure President Bornefeld, and said QSU would not associate with SGA until a public apology was released. In the recordHerald, 22 Feb 2023
22 March 2023Election reform bill failed 19-15. Bill 39-23-S, which would have moved the transfer, non-traditional and first-generation senate seats to the fall election and required a two-thirds senate vote to convert vacant seats to at-large seats, failed 19-15, short of the two-thirds needed. Sponsor Neel Patel called the growing reliance on presidential appointments a dangerous practice for a government organization. The same meeting unanimously funded the April 3 Afro-Latinidad cultural event and rejected funding for Dance Big Red. In the recordHerald, 22 Mar 2023
13 April 2023Senate merged committees and funded Mental Health Awareness Week. At the April 11 meeting the senate passed Bill 44-23-S, a constitutional amendment merging the Community Relations and Outreach committees starting in 2023-24, which Speaker Mishchuk said would create a stronger committee amid low membership. Senators also unanimously funded a slate of Mental Health Awareness Week events for April 23 through 28, including an Out of the Darkness suicide prevention walk, and passed a $62 terrarium tabling bill. President Bornefeld reported 44 applicants for the upcoming SGA elections. In the recordHerald, 13 Apr 2023
19 April 2023Sam Kurtz elected on an unopposed ticket. With Salvador Leon as administrative vice president and Annalise Finch as executive vice president. In the recordHerald, April 2023

It passed unanimously with 28 votes. In the recordHerald, 20 Sep 2023

SGA announced its fall election results on Sept. 20 over an Instagram livestream, electing Savanna Kurtz, Malick Ibrahim and Chloe Ralston as freshman senators, ShyAntye'e Williams as graduate senator, Sim Guan Zhou as international senator and Livi Ray as Gatton Academy senator. They also unanimously passed Bill 12-23-F, allocating $200 from the legislative discretionary fund for a Boys and Girls Club of Bowling Green toy drive, and swore in newly elected ISEC senator Ashlee Potter. Three bills from the Legislative Operations Committee: fix references to a 'University Senate' that had since split into separate Faculty and Staff senates, reconcile bylaws language about expelling senators with a constitution that only provides for impeachment, and update committee names that had drifted. In the recordHerald, Nov 2023

6 February 2024Cabinet requests censure hearing against León. Kurtz told the senate the Executive Cabinet had filed a formal report seeking a Judicial Council censure hearing against Administrative Vice President Salvador León. The Executive Cabinet, excluding León, asked for a censure; the hearing was set for Feb. 7. In the recordHerald, 6 Feb 2024
9 February 2024Judicial council censures Salvador León. The SGA judicial council voted 6-0 to censure Administrative Vice President Salvador León after a Feb. 7 hearing on allegations that he spent funds, including a $425.25 Preston Center deposit for Neurodiversity Acceptance Week, before the senate passed the funding bill. León argued text messages showed President Sam Kurtz had accepted the spending, while Kurtz said he asked León multiple times not to spend before the bill passed. The council recommended no further disciplinary action beyond the censure. In the recordHerald, 9 Feb 2024
15 February 2024Uber voucher program and parking fee measures. The senate also passed Resolution 18-23-S supporting a cap on student parking fees at $50 and Bill 19-23-S allocating $700 to help financially vulnerable students pay parking fines, along with $1,045 in Dental Hygiene Center and ID Center vouchers. In the recordHerald, 15 Feb 2024

Kevin Jackson and other Kentucky campus governments, and urged senators to seek re-election to preserve institutional knowledge. Chief of Staff Donté Reed introduced a need-based scholarship awarding four Uber vouchers and the Community Builder Excellence Scholarship for incoming Bowling Green-area freshmen. A $280 bill funded a Hope Harbor Denim Day event for Sexual Assault Prevention Month. In the recordHerald, 13 Mar 2024

Executive leaders told the Herald editorial board they intended to spend the full allocation, and President Sam Kurtz credited WKU President Caboni with keeping the budget level for several years. What this is

A year-by-year record of student government at Western Kentucky University, from the ratification of the Associated Students constitution in April 1966 to the present. Every entry names the source it came from.

Corrections

The names here start with the plaque in the SGA Chambers, and the plaque is known to be wrong in places. Where the archive disagrees with it, this site follows the archive and records the change on the corrections page. If you served, or you know that a year here is wrong, the project wants to hear from you through the Student Government Association office.

Reuse

Text on this site is the work of the project. Photographs, documents and legislation are reproduced from Western Kentucky University's own open archives and from the College Heights Herald and the Talisman; each item names the collection, issue and page it came from and links to the original.
