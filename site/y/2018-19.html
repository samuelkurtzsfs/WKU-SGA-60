Stephen Mayer president and student regent, 2018-19

Office
President and student regent
Term recorded here
2018-19
Place in the line
the 53rd president and the 50th student regent
Standing of the record
As recorded on the plaque. Not yet corroborated in the archive.

Confirmed by an independent WKU record outside the plaque: the University Senate's Nov. 15, 2018 meeting agenda lists him under reports as "SGA President: Stephen Mayer."

Mayer was elected SGA president and student regent in April 2018 on the MEA ticket with Garrett Edmonds and Harper Anderson, taking 35 percent of 2,378 presidential votes against Conner Hounshell's 33 percent and Kenan Mujkanovic's 32 percent; 2,447 students voted overall. His ticket won only after the Judicial Council reversed a disqualification it had imposed days before the election over the campaign's use of a Pepe the Frog meme chalked on campus sidewalks, downgrading the penalty to a campaign suspension.

A biology and chemistry double major who had served three semesters as a senator and studied at Harlaxton College, Mayer opened his term in September 2018 promising greater transparency from both the Board of Regents and SGA, and required senators to log a weekly office hour. As student regent he was an outspoken critic of WKU's fee structure, especially the $450 online academic fee, and opposed that year's 4 percent tuition increase, saying he did not see the need to put the financial burden "on the students' backs."

The year, in order39

4 September 2018
#

Former SGA president sues WKU, Caboni and two administrators

Andi Dahmer filed suit against WKU, President Timothy Caboni, Assistant General Counsel Andrea Anderson and Director of Student Activities Charley Pride, citing verbal, mental and emotional abuse and alleging sex discrimination and failure to follow the university's Discrimination and Harassment Policy. The university called the allegations "maliciously untrue" and said it would vigorously defend the claims. WKU said its Title IX office found no Title IX violation but did find Student Code of Conduct violations, for which students were sanctioned.

Herald, 4 Sep 2018

11 September 2018
#

Senate rejected first Judicial Council nominee of the term

The senate voted 8-7-1 against confirming Wood Brown, a Pike fraternity member and law clerk, as associate justice of the Judicial Council, after Mayer said his constitutional duty required him to appoint whom he judged best despite recusing himself from the interview process; senators argued the Judicial Council's own top-ranked applicant, not Brown, should have been chosen. Mayer's next nominee, Alex Rivera, was confirmed the following week.

SGA Senate meeting minutes, 11 Sep 2018

14 September 2018
#

Senate votes down both of the new president's executive nominees

Stephen Mayer's nominees were rejected in his first confirmation votes: Wood Brown for associate justice failed 8-7 with one abstention and one exemption, and Anna McAvoy for director of academic and student affairs failed 6-9, both short of the required two-thirds. Senators questioned Mayer's ability to judge candidates when he had been sequestered from the Judicial Council's interviews. The 2018-19 budget of $100,000, a $15,000 reduction from the prior year, passed 13-4.

Herald, 14 Sep 2018

19 September 2018
#

Almost 900 students voted in a fall election with nearly 70 candidates

Voting for the SGA senate ran on TopNet on 17 and 18 September, with almost 900 students casting ballots among nearly 70 candidates. SGA held a results party in the Senate Chambers, and the counts came in about fifteen minutes past midnight on 19 September. Student Body President Stephen Mayer called the turnout amazing. Seven senators at large, three freshman senators and an Intercultural Student Engagement Center senator were seated.

almost 900 votes cast for nearly 70 candidates

Herald, 19 Sep 2018

20 September 2018
#

Senate asked WKU to cancel classes on Election Day and to loosen the conduct code

SGA passed Resolution 5-18-F 15-0, asking the university to cancel classes on 6 November 2018 so students could get to the polls, and Bill 7-18-F, giving $300 to the get-out-the-vote campaign Reg 2 Vote. Francis Wilson of Reg 2 Vote told senators that of 290,000 Kentucky students registered in 2016, only about 13 percent voted. Resolutions 1-18-F and 2-18-F, which asked WKU to strike Student Code of Conduct language barring students from refusing a direction from a university official and from audio recording student-faculty meetings, both passed 14-0. Mayer's nominee Alex Rivera was confirmed as associate justice of the Judicial Council 12-3 after an earlier nomination had failed.

Herald, 20 Sep 2018

23 September 2018
#

WKU denies Dahmer's claims in court filing

WKU filed its response on Sept. 19 denying that Dahmer suffered a hostile educational environment or sexual harassment during her presidency, and stating she never reported to the Office of Student Conduct or the Title IX coordinator but instead told her residence hall director, who triggered the investigation. The university said the investigation found Student Code of Conduct violations by certain SGA members, who were sanctioned. It argued the GroupMe messages were never intended for Dahmer and so caused her no harm. Dahmer is seeking $75,000 in damages.

Herald, 23 Sep 2018

25 September 2018
#

Academic and Student Affairs directorship filled after two failed tries

The senate confirmed Aubrey Kelly as Director of Academic and Student Affairs 24-3-4, three weeks into the term. Initial nominee Ashley Cox was not confirmed at the first meeting after senators questioned her committee attendance, then withdrew when a family member was hospitalized; her replacement, Anna McAvoy, failed confirmation 6-9-1 on Sept. 11 after a senator raised concerns about her scholarship-grading experience.

SGA Senate meeting minutes, 25 Sep 2018

26 September 2018
#

First Year Village housing plan presented to SGA

Executive Vice President Garrett Edmonds presented plans for a freshman First Year Village of two new pod-style residence halls at the bottom of the Hill, replacing Bemis Lawrence and Barnes-Campbell halls on the site of the Pearce-Ford Tower parking lot. The senate also approved Bill 6-18-F, funding a sustainability film festival, by a 21-4 vote with one abstention.

Herald, 26 Sep 2018

3 October 2018
#

Bill to cut off Greek organization funding postponed 17-15

Bill 10-18-F, written by Senator Amy Wyer and Diversity and Inclusion Chair Mark Clark to bar SGA funding to Interfraternity Council and Panhellenic Council chapters, was pulled from the agenda and postponed to Oct. 16 on a 17-15 vote because of its content. Two funding bills moved forward the same night, giving $150 to the student ID voucher program and $1,500 to the Office of Study Away.

Herald, 3 Oct 2018

17 October 2018
#

Greek defunding bill fails by one vote after long debate

Bill 10-18-F was defeated 16-17. Author Amy Wyer, herself a member of Alpha Gamma Delta, cited the University of Alabama's Machine coalition and said only one non-Greek member had been elected to that student government since 1914, warning "I fear we have the potential to move in this direction." Former senator Alex Sargent argued against telling students who pay the same tuition that their social needs matter less. A motion to table failed 21-14, short of two-thirds.

SGA created a first-generation student scholarship

The senate passed a bill by Campus Improvements Chair Matt Barr creating a recurring $2,000 scholarship for first-generation college students, approved 31-1. It also passed organizational aid Bill 11-18-F, sending $1,400 to Delta Sigma Theta, Phi Beta Sigma and the jump rope club, and allocated $100 to advertise the Campus Safety Walk.

Senate creates a recurring $2,000 first-generation scholarship

A bill by Campus Improvements Chair Matt Barr establishing a $2,000 scholarship awarded once per academic year to first-generation students passed 31-1. The Herald cited a 2017 WKU Fact Book study finding an estimated 34.8 percent of first-year, first-time students in fall 2016 were first-generation. Two organizational aid bills also passed, including $1,400 split among Delta Sigma Theta, Phi Beta Sigma and the jump rope club.

13 November 2018
#

Colonnade textbook library tabled amid questions about the bookstore

Bill 18-18-F, allocating $1,000 to stock a used-textbook library for Colonnade courses in the SGA offices, was tabled 24-3 with one abstention after debate over funding and over whether the collection would stay current as Colonnade courses change. Senator Amy Wyer asked why the WKU Bookstore was "making us buy books they can't resell." Two bylaw amendments passed the same night, one adding flexibility in appointing regional ambassadors and one adding protection against discrimination on the basis of gender identity.

28 November 2018
#

Senate votes 30-0 to defend transgender student protections

Resolution 7-18-F, written by Diversity and Inclusion Chair Mark Clark in response to reports that the Trump administration was considering narrowly redefining sex, pledged to preserve and improve protections for transgender and nonconforming students regardless of federal action. It passed 30-0. Resolution 8-18-F requiring all new campus buildings to meet LEED standards passed 29-0, and Bill 23-18-F tightening executive board appointments to require a two-thirds senate vote passed 28-1.

#

Contested appointment and Identity Evropa denouncement ended the fall term

President Stephen Mayer's nomination of graduate student Emily Anne Pride, daughter of a former SGA adviser, to a vacated senate seat sparked about 90 minutes of debate before she was confirmed 17-9 with two abstentions. The senate then passed Resolution 10-18-F, supporting denouncement of the white nationalist group Identity Evropa and its attempts to recruit on campus, 28-0, and allocated $430 to the Black Leadership Academic Conference through Bill 24-18-F.

Twenty-four SGA members went to the Rally for Higher Education

SGA sent 24 members, the executive board and selected senators, to the annual Rally for Higher Education in Frankfort, joining delegations from Murray State and other universities across the Commonwealth. The WKU delegation was led by Executive Vice President Garrett Edmonds, who said higher education was not just about students but about culture and communities. 13 February 2019
#

Study abroad scholarship bill pulled after five competing motions

Bill 2-19-S, which would have barred students who had already received an SGA study abroad scholarship from reapplying, was pulled from the agenda after debate produced five motions ranging from amendments to a 30-second cap on further speeches. Author Jayden Thomas said "I don't think it's fair to award scholarships to the same people who get them over and over again." Speaker Asha McWilliams said the debate showed the senate "doing what it is supposed to do."

20 February 2019
#

Rewritten study abroad bill passes with a first-timer bonus

Bill 7-19-S replaced the outright ban on repeat applicants with a 15 percent scoring bonus for students who had not previously studied abroad. Senator Josh Zaczek argued during debate that the change was "detracting from a merit-based scholarship" and that applicants' claims could not be verified. Authors Jayden Thomas and Amanda Harder had rewritten the bill after concerns about its exclusionary effect.

Herald, 20 Feb 2019

27 February 2019
#

Resolution passed to relocate Confederate marker

After lengthy debate, the senate passed Resolution 1-19-S by a 29-1 vote with one abstention, calling for the marker near the Kentucky Museum identifying Bowling Green as the Confederate capital of Kentucky to be relocated to a display inside the museum. Authors Kara Lowry, Conner Hounshell and Symone Whalin argued the marker was a reminder of oppression against people of color, and Mayer said the resolution would go to the WKU Administrative Council. The senate also allocated $1,000 to send the Mock Trial team to a national tournament.

Herald, 27 Feb 2019

27 February 2019
#

Senate votes 29-1 to move the Confederate capital marker

Resolution 1-19-S, written by senators Kara Lowry, Conner Hounshell and Symone Whalin, called the marker near the Kentucky Museum identifying Bowling Green as the Confederate capital of Kentucky a reminder of oppression, and passed 29-1 with one abstention. An amendment during the meeting changed the ask from removal to relocation into a museum display. Lowry said that was "a good compromise for people who believe that [the marker] is preserving history." Mayer said the resolution would go to the Administrative Council rather than the University Senate.

Dahmer adds a retaliation claim, alleging Caboni pulled a Rhodes recommendation

Dahmer moved to amend her complaint to add retaliation, claiming Caboni withdrew his endorsement of her Rhodes Scholarship application "without any reason or explanation" after she sued. Text messages from Office of Scholar Development Director Audra Jennings indicating Caboni would endorse her were submitted as evidence, along with a later email in which Jennings said "I cannot discuss the matter with you." 27 March 2019
#

Mayer declined to seek re-election as election codes were set

Mayer announced he would not seek re-election because he was applying to medical schools, and the senate approved the spring 2019 election codes 31-1, with campaigning to begin April 1 and voting on TopNet April 15-16. Chief Justice Jacob McAndrews announced he would resign from the Judicial Council to run for elected office. The senate also passed four spending bills, rejected a change to senator office-hour rules and tabled a bill creating a first-generation senate seat.

27 March 2019
#

Mayer will not seek re-election; chief justice resigns to run

Stephen Mayer told the senate he would not seek re-election because he was applying to medical school. Spring election codes passed 31-1 after every proposed amendment was struck down, with one friendly amendment from Senator John May barring sitting Judicial Council members from running; Chief Justice Jacob McAndrews then announced he would resign to campaign. 3 April 2019
#

Senate passed a transparency resolution 30-2 after the dean's sudden resignation

A week after Potter College Dean Larry Snyder resigned abruptly, the senate passed Resolution 4-19-S by 30 votes to 2, saying top university officials must explain major decisions and carry them out in a manner that protects the dignity of those involved. Mayer's renomination of Wood Brown to the Judicial Council was struck down 17-11, short of two-thirds, after Associate Justice Annalicia Carlson said the council preferred Erika Puhakka. A bill creating a senate seat for first-generation college students passed 33-0. A resolution brought by the Transgender Non-Binary student organization, asking WKU to let students' preferred names appear on class rosters, IDs and email addresses, also passed 33-0.

Herald, 3 Apr 2019

8 April 2019Programme
#

Second Unite for Fairness night filled DSU with handwritten letters

SGA and the Queer Student Union held the second Unite for Fairness event in Downing Student Union, where students wrote letters to Bowling Green city commissioners in support of a fairness ordinance. State Representative and WKU history professor Patti Minter, a member of the Bowling Green fairness movement since 1999, gave a presentation on its history before students began writing. SGA Director of Public Relations Noah Moore, who had helped organise the first event the previous semester, said a handwritten letter would reach a commissioner more than an email or a phone call.

9 April 2019
#

No-confidence vote and provost's resignation shook the university

After Snyder was asked to resign as Potter College dean on March 26, protests from faculty, staff and students led the University Senate to an unprecedented vote of no confidence in Provost Terry Ballman on April 4, and she resigned the next day. Ogden dean Cheryl Stevens was named acting provost, and days later the Comprehensive Academic Program Evaluation committee recommended suspending 101 of WKU's 380 academic programs.

Senate votes 23-1 for a $5 student fee to fund the Herald

Judge orders WKU to release records to Dahmer

A judge ruled in favor of releasing records Dahmer sought in discovery, including complaints relating to Charley Pride, Title IX investigation statements, and records of other SGA members' discrimination or harassment complaints since August 2017. #

Impeachment hearing for a Judicial Council member called off

SGA scheduled and then canceled an impeachment hearing for Associate Justice Annalicia Carlson after determining too few senators would attend. What this is

A year-by-year record of student government at Western Kentucky University, from the ratification of the Associated Students constitution in April 1966 to the present. Every entry names the source it came from.

Corrections

The names here start with the plaque in the SGA Chambers, and the plaque is known to be wrong in places. Where the archive disagrees with it, this site follows the archive and records the change on the corrections page. If you served, or you know that a year here is wrong, the project wants to hear from you through the Student Government Association office.

What it rests on

Everything here comes from what is publicly available online: the digitised Herald and SGA's own papers on TopSCHOLAR, the Talisman yearbooks, wkuherald.com, the university's news pages and the Wayback Machine. That is a fraction of what happened. Student government has met most weeks for sixty years, and the greater part of it was never written down, or was written down on paper that has never been scanned. A thin year here is a thin record, not a quiet one.

Reuse

Text on this site is the work of the project. Photographs, documents and legislation are reproduced from Western Kentucky University's own open archives and from the College Heights Herald and the Talisman; each item names the collection, issue and page it came from and links to the original.
