Andi Dahmer — the term

Andi Dahmer, a Louisville sophomore who had served as a senator and chair of the MyCampusToo committee, was announced as the next student body president shortly after midnight on April 19, 2017, winning 930 of the 1,579 votes cast against Kenan Mujkanovic's 305 and Lily Nellans' 212. Her running mates Savannah Molyneaux and Kara Lowry were elected executive vice president and administrative vice president, and Dahmer entered office already drafting legislation aimed at improving retention, making study abroad more affordable and supporting sensitivity training.

Her year in office was marked by internal division. The senate saw longer debates, more than 15 proposed constitutional amendments compared with about 10 the year before, and an emergency Judicial Council meeting on Jan. 29, with contention peaking around the DACA debates of October and November 2017, when the senate failed a clean DREAM Act resolution before passing a resolution supporting DACA students. Dahmer said the year had become "almost toxic in nature" and blamed partisan ideologies brought into what was supposed to be a nonpartisan organization.

In April 2018 Dahmer told the Herald she had endured months of harassment as president and student regent, including senators cursing at her in the SGA office, a group message thread of eight people called the "Justice Friends" that wished her physical harm, and a profane note left on her car on Feb. 9 that prompted a police report. Of the note she said, "That's when I really started fearing for my life on this campus." Her residence hall director reported her case to the Title IX office, which found it did not meet the legal standard for harassment; it was processed instead through the Office of Student Conduct, which issued no-contact orders against two senators, and she did not seek reelection.

In 2018 Dahmer, whose full name is Loandria "Andi" Dahmer, sued WKU, university president Timothy Caboni, director of student activities Charley Pride and assistant general counsel and Title IX coordinator Andrea Anderson, alleging sex-based discrimination and harassment by fellow SGA members, negligence by the university in responding to it, and that Caboni retaliated against her by declining to write a letter of recommendation for a Rhodes Scholarship application. U.S. District Judge David Hale granted the defendants summary judgment in March 2021, finding WKU had not been deliberately indifferent and that the incidents were not severe enough to constitute actionable harassment; the Sixth Circuit Court of Appeals partially revived the case in 2022, directing the district court to consider whether student-on-student harassment had occurred before February 2018. The parties settled in January 2024, with WKU's insurance carrier paying Dahmer $10,000 and the university's attorney describing the payment as nominal and made without any admission of wrongdoing.

36 senators this year.

This was the 16th Senate. Under the constitution as amended spring 2017, the Senate was composed of 36 seats: 3 each for senior, junior, sophomore and freshman classes, 2 graduate, 1 ESL international student, 1 Gatton Academy, 6 academic-college, and 14 at-large.

What happened, in order

2017-08-31

First senate meeting passes constitutional amendments

SGA held its first senate meeting of the year and approved three of five proposed constitutional amendment bills, which then went to the student body for a vote in the fall elections. The passed bills added constitutional review duties to the Judicial Council, made the chief justice backup speaker of the senate, and required senators to post one office hour per week. Senators also debated Bill 5-17-F, which proposed limiting the legislative powers of the executive branch.

Herald, 31 Aug 2017 ↗
2017-11-09

SGA passes resolution supporting DACA students

At its Nov. 7 meeting the senate passed a resolution, co-authored by President Andi Dahmer and Francisco Serrano, standing in solidarity with WKU students who were DACA recipients. The measure passed 32-1 and was a revision of the clean DREAM Act resolution rejected the previous week, reframed to address only the WKU student community. Dahmer noted WKU was one of the last Kentucky schools to pass something of this nature.

Herald, 9 Nov 2017 ↗
2018-01-29

Judicial Council censured PR committee chair over election-discrepancy complaint

At an emergency meeting, the Judicial Council took up a complaint public relations committee chair Will Hurst had raised at the Jan. 23 senate meeting, alleging the executive board failed its constitutional duty to advertise the fall election and citing a discrepancy in vote totals between constitutional amendments that passed and failed. The council found the executive board had complied with the constitution and issued a censure against Hurst; Dahmer said she was pleased the council had upheld the board's conduct. The censure was confirmed at the Feb. 6 senate meeting.

Herald, 29 Jan 2018 ↗
2018-02-07

SGA members rally in Frankfort for higher education

About 20 WKU SGA members joined roughly 100 students from Kentucky's public universities at the Kentucky Board of Student Body Presidents' annual Rally for Higher Education on the Capitol steps in Frankfort on Feb. 6. Dahmer said the board of student body presidents had been working consistently since August to plan the rally.

Herald, 7 Feb 2018 ↗
2018-04-04

LGBTQ activists address senate after senator's remarks

Several LGBTQ student activists spoke to the senate on April 3 after Senator Dallas McKinney had cited the Bible on March 27, reading verses saying the "wicked" deserved death, as his reason for voting against Bill 13-18-S, which funded $250 for stoles for the Lavender Recognition Ceremony honoring LGBTQ graduates. The bill passed in the senate, and speakers including SGA member Brigid Stakelum and Diversity and Inclusion Committee Chair Mark Clark called the remarks dehumanizing and discriminatory.

Herald, 4 Apr 2018 ↗
2018-04-18

Mayer wins presidency after disqualification reversed

Stephen Mayer was elected SGA president and student regent, with Garrett Edmonds as executive vice president and Harper Anderson as administrative vice president. Their MEA ticket had been disqualified on April 16 after a complaint over a Pepe the Frog meme in a campaign chalk drawing, but the Judicial Council reversed the disqualification to a suspension before results were announced. Mayer won 35 percent of the 2,378 votes cast in the presidential race, ahead of Conner Hounshell with 33 percent and Kenan Mujkanovic with 32 percent.

Herald, 18 Apr 2018 ↗
2018-04-24

Dahmer details months of harassment

In a Herald investigation, Dahmer said members of SGA had cursed at her, called her derogatory names and exchanged group messages in a GroupMe called the "Justice Friends" wishing her harm over the fall and spring semesters, and that a profane note left on her car on Feb. 9 led her to file a WKU police report and avoid her residence hall for two weeks. Her case was reported to WKU's Title IX office, which told her the conduct did not meet the legal standard for harassment, and it was processed instead by the Office of Student Conduct, which issued no-contact orders against two senators.

Herald, 24 Apr 2018 ↗
