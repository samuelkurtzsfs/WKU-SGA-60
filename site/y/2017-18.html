Andi Dahmer president and student regent, 2017-18

Office
President and student regent
Term recorded here
2017-18
Place in the line
the 52nd president and the 49th student regent
Standing of the record
As recorded on the plaque. Not yet corroborated in the archive.

Took office alongside incoming university president Timothy Caboni, who began 1 July 2017.

Andi Dahmer, a Louisville sophomore who had served as a senator and chair of the MyCampusToo committee, was announced as the next student body president shortly after midnight on April 19, 2017, winning 930 of the 1,579 votes cast against Kenan Mujkanovic's 305 and Lily Nellans' 212. Her running mates Savannah Molyneaux and Kara Lowry were elected executive vice president and administrative vice president, and Dahmer entered office already drafting legislation aimed at improving retention, making study abroad more affordable and supporting sensitivity training.

Her year in office was marked by internal division. The senate saw longer debates, more than 15 proposed constitutional amendments compared with about 10 the year before, and an emergency Judicial Council meeting on Jan. 29, with contention peaking around the DACA debates of October and November 2017, when the senate failed a clean DREAM Act resolution before passing a resolution supporting DACA students. Dahmer said the year had become "almost toxic in nature" and blamed partisan ideologies brought into what was supposed to be a nonpartisan organization.

In April 2018 Dahmer told the Herald she had endured months of harassment as president and student regent, including senators cursing at her in the SGA office, a group message thread of eight people called the "Justice Friends" that wished her physical harm, and a profane note left on her car on Feb. 9 that prompted a police report. Of the note she said, "That's when I really started fearing for my life on this campus." Her residence hall director reported her case to the Title IX office, which found it did not meet the legal standard for harassment; it was processed instead through the Office of Student Conduct, which issued no-contact orders against two senators, and she did not seek reelection.

In 2018 Dahmer, whose full name is Loandria "Andi" Dahmer, sued WKU, university president Timothy Caboni, director of student activities Charley Pride and assistant general counsel and Title IX coordinator Andrea Anderson, alleging sex-based discrimination and harassment by fellow SGA members, negligence by the university in responding to it, and that Caboni retaliated against her by declining to write a letter of recommendation for a Rhodes Scholarship application. U.S. District Judge David Hale granted the defendants summary judgment in March 2021, finding WKU had not been deliberately indifferent and that the incidents were not severe enough to constitute actionable harassment; the Sixth Circuit Court of Appeals partially revived the case in 2022, directing the district court to consider whether student-on-student harassment had occurred before February 2018. The parties settled in January 2024, with WKU's insurance carrier paying Dahmer $10,000 and the university's attorney describing the payment as nominal and made without any admission of wrongdoing.

17 August 2017
#

Dahmer joined national solidarity letter after Charlottesville

President Andi Dahmer was one of more than 120 college student body presidents nationwide who signed an open letter backing the University of Virginia after the violent Charlottesville protests, and said developing campus-wide "cultural competency training" was a goal for the semester.

SGA paid for legal observer training with the National Lawyers Guild

Bill 3-17-F allocated $200 from senate discretionary funding for a Legal Observer Training event on 19 October 2017 in DSU 3024, run in partnership with the Nashville chapter of the National Lawyers Guild. The training was free to students and qualified them to serve as legal observers in Kentucky and across the United States. The bill passed on first reading on 28 August 2017.

31 August 2017
#

First senate meeting passes constitutional amendments

SGA held its first senate meeting of the year and approved three of five proposed constitutional amendment bills, which then went to the student body for a vote in the fall elections. The passed bills added constitutional review duties to the Judicial Council, made the chief justice backup speaker of the senate, and required senators to post one office hour per week. Senators also debated Bill 5-17-F, which proposed limiting the legislative powers of the executive branch.

Herald, 31 Aug 2017

31 August 2017
#

Senate rejects bid to strip the executive branch of legislative power

At the first meeting of the year the senate considered five proposed constitutional amendments. Bill 5-17-F, written by Public Relations Chair William Hurst to bar the executive branch from writing legislation, failed 4-8 after Hurst argued it "limited the chances" for senators to gain drafting experience. Bills adding constitutional review duties to the Judicial Council, making the chief justice acting speaker, and requiring one office hour per week from senators all passed.

Herald, 31 Aug 2017

6 September 2017
#

SGA funds legal observer training for student demonstrators

Bill 3-17-F allocated $200 from senate discretionary funds to facilitate legal observer training run in partnership with the Nashville chapter of the National Lawyers Guild, covering flyers, food and a gift for the trainers. Senator Dylan McCormick told the senate that "in this political climate" it helped to have people with those skills on hand. The bill was approved unanimously.

Herald, 6 Sep 2017

13 September 2017
#

Senate votes 12-2 to sponsor Bowling Green's first Pride festival

Bill 9-17-F committed $250 from senate discretionary funds, matched by $250 from the executive budget, to make SGA a green sponsor of the inaugural Bowling Green Pride Festival hosted by BG Fairness. Senator Jordan Tackett questioned whether SGA was ready to face backlash, while Chief of Staff Conner Hounshell called the event "a celebration of identity." Senator Lucas Knight tied the bill to the city commission's failure to pass a Fairness Ordinance.

Herald, 13 Sep 2017

26 September 2017
#

Senate voted 29-1 for a required sexual assault course in the Colonnade

Resolution 1-17-F, supporting the addition of a one-credit-hour sexual assault education course to Colonnade requirements for first-year students, passed 29-1 with two abstentions at the Tuesday night meeting. It was co-authored by public relations chair William Hurst, S.A.V.E.S. chair Ashlyn Jones and three other members, and argued that existing options such as HAVEN and Green Dot carried no penalty for skipping them. Hurst said a student could pass four years at WKU without any sexual assault training, calling that "a disgrace, to say the least." Senator Alex Sergent described the resolution as closing holes in an existing net rather than adding a graduation hurdle, and the same meeting funded a No-Shave November beard contest and fixed the timing of constitutional-amendment elections in the bylaws.

Herald, 27 Sep 2017

25 October 2017
#

Resolution backing a clean DREAM Act fails 12-17

Resolution 2-17-F, urging Congress to pass a clean DREAM Act after DACA was rescinded, failed with 12 votes in favor and 17 opposed. Senator Alex Sergent said it was not the university's place to "put our political hat in a ring," and William Hurst said "we serve the majority." Legislative Research Chair Lily Nellans countered that senators are meant to vote for their constituents' interests, "which oftentimes means protecting marginalized minority communities."

DACA Day of Action funded at $100

The senate unanimously approved Bill 21-17-F on 31 October 2017, giving $100 for a DACA Day of Action hosted by the Hilltopper Organization of Latin American Students with the Center for Citizenship and Social Justice. The all-day event had students write to legislators urging passage of a clean DREAM Act, and the money bought paper, pens, pencils, food, drinks and posters. Bowling Green junior Francisco Serrano co-authored it with President Andi Dahmer.

The No Shave November beard contest flopped

SGA launched its first No Shave November beard-growing contest in 2017 to raise money for cancer prevention, funded by a sustainability committee bill passed on 26 September. Sustainability chair Ian Hamilton, who had taken part in the observance himself, said participants would donate what they saved by not buying shaving supplies. Entries were low, and Hamilton said SGA needed more discussion about what the month was for and how the effort could actually help people with cancer.

Herald, 9 Nov 2017 - "SGA hosts beard contest, raises money for cancer"

9 November 2017
#

Narrower DACA solidarity resolution passed 32-1

At its 7 November meeting the senate passed, by 32 votes to 1, a resolution co-authored by President Andi Dahmer and former senator Francisco Serrano standing in solidarity with WKU students who were DACA recipients. It was a revision of the clean DREAM Act resolution rejected the previous week, reframed to address only the WKU student community rather than call on legislators, which Dahmer said kept it within "a jurisdiction that we do have." Dahmer noted WKU was one of the last schools in Kentucky to pass a measure of this kind. The same meeting unanimously funded the Go With the Flow program, allocating over $580 for free menstrual products in five campus buildings.

Herald, 9 Nov 2017

What this is

A year-by-year record of student government at Western Kentucky University, from the ratification of the Associated Students constitution in April 1966 to the present. Every entry names the source it came from.

Corrections

The names here start with the plaque in the SGA Chambers, and the plaque is known to be wrong in places. Where the archive disagrees with it, this site follows the archive and records the change on the corrections page. If you served, or you know that a year here is wrong, the project wants to hear from you through the Student Government Association office.

What it rests on

Everything here comes from what is publicly available online: the digitised Herald and SGA's own papers on TopSCHOLAR, the Talisman yearbooks, wkuherald.com, the university's news pages and the Wayback Machine. That is a fraction of what happened. Student government has met most weeks for sixty years, and the greater part of it was never written down, or was written down on paper that has never been scanned. A thin year here is a thin record, not a quiet one.
