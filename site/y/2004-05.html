Nick Todd president, 2004-05

Office
President
Term recorded here
2004-05
Place in the line
the 38th president
Standing of the record
Confirmed in the sources below.
Not yet on the plaque
The Chambers plaque does not carry this name.

Elected SGA president in March 2004 with 772 of 1,232 votes and sworn in on 27 April, then resigned in July, citing personal conflicts within the association, after an investigation into money missing from an SGA Dining Dollars account. Executive vice president Katie Dawson acted as president until a special election in September, which Patti Johnson won unopposed. No plate on the Chambers wall carries his name; the 2004-05 plate reads Patti Johnson. Whether Todd ever took the student regent seat is not settled in the sources found so far.

Nick Todd, a Henderson senior and SGA's outgoing vice president of finance, won the presidency in March 2004 with 772 of the 1,232 votes cast. Days before the new officers were sworn in on 27 April, the judicial council found $611 missing from the SGA Aramark Dining Dollars account, which had opened the year with $5,000. A senator drafted articles of impeachment against the finance post Todd was about to leave, and the council ruled a two-week process could not finish before he left it anyway. Only Todd, outgoing president John Bradley and the adviser held cards to the account.

He submitted his resignation in July, citing personal conflicts within the association. In May the university's internal auditor had reported more than $800 of questionable purchases from the account between August 2003 and May 2004, mostly small buys at campus restaurants with no receipts, and recommended he reimburse the money along with $71 charged on the SGA purchasing card after a spring break car rental. Campus police determined no police action was necessary. In September the dean of Student Life said Todd had been sanctioned by judicial affairs in July, disputing Todd's public account that losing the card was the only action taken against him.

Executive vice president Katie Dawson acted as president until the special election of 14 and 15 September. Patti Johnson, the previous year's executive vice president, was the only student to come forward for the office, saying she ran because of the turmoil within the organization, and was sworn in on 23 September. The Herald noted at the time that a re-election for SGA president is rare.

Also on the wall
2005-06

Filled the presidency through the summer of 2004. Dawson had run as Nick Todd's running mate in the March 2004 election and was executive vice president when he resigned in July; she acted as president until the special election of 14 and 15 September, which Patti Johnson won unopposed. No source found says she was sworn in. The Herald reported both of that year's swearings-in, Todd's at the April banquet and Johnson's at the September reception, and described Dawson only as acting president, which is how this site records her. She is counted among the presidents all the same, because she held the office: the rule here is that holding it counts, however briefly and by whatever route. She was elected president in her own right the following spring and is on the Chambers wall for 2005-06 only, so the plaque does not record this stretch. The student regent seat did not pass to her.

Katie Dawson held the SGA presidency for about two months in the summer of 2004 without having been elected to it. She had come up through the cabinet as Nick Todd's running mate in the March election and was his executive vice president when he resigned in July amid the Dining Dollars investigation. She acted in the office until students voted in a special election that September.

Hers is the only one of the archive's four mid-year vacancies filled this way. In 2004 the presidency was treated as vacant and put back to the students, with the executive vice president acting until they had voted. When Rob Watkins resigned in November 2006 and when Johnathon Boles resigned in January 2009, the executive vice president succeeded to the office instead, Jeanne Johnson being sworn in as president at that year's Winter Banquet. The same organisation met the same kind of vacancy in two different ways within five years.

She was elected president in her own right for 2005-06, and the Chambers wall carries her for that year alone.

Patti Johnson president and student regent, 2004-05

Office
President and student regent
Term recorded here
2004-05
Place in the line
the 40th president and the 36th student regent
Standing of the record
As recorded on the plaque. Not yet corroborated in the archive.

Confirmed: president-elect Nick Todd resigned in July 2004 amid a dining-funds investigation; Johnson, who had been executive vice president the year before, ran unopposed in the September 2004 special election and was sworn in Sept. 23, matching the plaque year. A Board of Regents resolution read into the 22 Jul 2005 minutes confirms she served as the board's student member from August 2004 to July 2005, when Katie Dawson was sworn in to replace her.

Patti Johnson, a senior from Marion, had been active in SGA for over three years and served on committees including public relations and athletics before becoming executive vice president in 2003-04. Nick Todd, the vice president of finance, was elected president for 2004-05 in March 2004, but before the new officers were sworn in on April 27, SGA's judicial council fielded an allegation that $611 was missing from the SGA Dining Dollars account, an account that had started the year with $5,000. Senator Josh Collins drafted articles of impeachment against Todd's vice presidency, but the judicial council ruled a two-week impeachment process could not finish before he left that office anyway. Johnson had not planned to run for president herself, but after Todd's resignation that July she entered the September 2004 special election as the only candidate to show interest, saying she stayed on because of "the turmoil within the organization" and that she knew "what it takes to get the job done."

Johnson ran unopposed for president and student regent in the Sept. 14-15 online election, the group having been without an elected president since July. She said she was disappointed that students would not "really get to make a decision," but still planned light campaigning, and she wanted to continue reviewing the plus/minus grading proposal and to make faculty evaluations available to students.

Johnson received 1,291 votes in the two-day uncontested election and was sworn in as president at SGA's fall reception on Sept. 23, 2004, one week after her election. She said committees had been formed and members had begun proposing legislation, and she looked forward to being sworn in as student regent at the Oct. 29 Board of Regents meeting, declaring it was "back to business." She served through the year until Katie Dawson was elected to succeed her in the March 2005 spring election.

Johnson served as the Board of Regents' student member from August 2004 to July 2005, a term confirmed by the resolution of appreciation the board read into its July 22, 2005 minutes when Katie Dawson, the executive vice president who had served alongside her all year, was sworn in to replace her. Dawson had come up through the cabinet as Todd's running mate in the original March 2004 election and had acted as interim president during the summer vacancy before Johnson's win.

The year, in order23

20 April 2004
#

SGA judicial council fielded impeachment bid against president-elect Todd

Days before new officers were due to be sworn in on April 27, SGA's judicial council found that $611 was missing from the SGA Aramark Dining Dollars account, which had started the academic year with $5,000 and had about $1,200 left. Senator Josh Collins drafted articles of impeachment against Todd's outgoing vice presidency of finance, but the council ruled a two-week impeachment process could not be completed before he left that office regardless. Only Todd, outgoing president John Bradley and adviser Charley Pride held cards to the account.

Herald, 20 Apr 2004 - "SGA President-Elect Under Investigation" (Wayback)

July 2004
#

Nick Todd resigns the presidency

Nick Todd, a Henderson senior elected in March with 772 of the 1,232 votes cast, submitted his resignation as SGA president in July 2004, citing personal conflicts within the association. The former vice president of finance had come under investigation in April over the misuse of more than $600 from an SGA Dining Dollars account. Executive vice president Katie Dawson acted as president until a new election, set for 14 and 15 September. The Herald reported the resignation when students returned, on 24 August.

Herald, 24 Aug 2004 - Todd cites personal conflicts in resignation

24 August 2004
#

SGA sets online special election for September

SGA scheduled an online presidential election for Sept. 14-15 to replace the candidate elected president in March. Students who had cast ballots for president the previous spring were asked to vote again.

Herald, 24 Aug 2004

26 August 2004
#

Internal audit recommended Todd repay SGA money

WKU's Office of the Internal Auditor reported that $872 in questionable purchases were made from the SGA Dining Dollars account between August 2003 and May 2004, mostly small buys at campus restaurants with no receipts. The report recommended that Todd reimburse the money, plus $71 for a charge made with the SGA purchasing card after a spring break car rental. Campus police determined no police action was necessary.

Herald, 26 Aug 2004

26 August 2004
#

Internal auditor finds more than $800 in questionable purchases

The Office of the Internal Auditor reported in May that more than $800 meant for SGA activities was spent on questionable purchases from an SGA Dining Dollars account. The report recommended that Nick Todd reimburse the money to SGA.

Herald, 26 Aug 2004

26 August 2004
#

Campus reaction to re-election divided

The Herald reported that reactions on campus to the Dining Dollars investigation and Todd's resignation varied, noting that a re-election for SGA president is rare. Bowling Green sophomore Alex Hall said he had voted for Todd in the March election.

Herald, 26 Aug 2004

26 August 2004
#

Former SGA officers write to rebut Todd

Western alumni who had held SGA office wrote to the Herald objecting to Nick Todd's remarks in the Aug. 24 issue. The writers, who had served with Todd in SGA, said they were dismayed by his lack of integrity in the matter.

Herald, 26 Aug 2004

31 August 2004
#

Big Red Card debit proposal put to SGA

A proposal to create new student identification cards that would also serve as debit cards was presented to SGA for approval earlier in the month. Auxiliary Services Director Rob Chrisler described the proposal to the Herald.

Herald, 31 Aug 2004

2 September 2004
#

Patti Johnson emerged as the only candidate in the special election

At a meeting for students interested in running in SGA's September special elections, Marion senior Patti Johnson, the previous year's executive vice president, was the only student to show interest in the presidency. She said she decided to run because of "the turmoil within the organization." Applications were also taken for the 23 open senate seats ahead of online voting on Sept. 14-15.

Herald, 2 Sep 2004

2 September 2004
#

About 13 students attend SGA candidate interest meeting

Roughly 13 students, ranging from freshmen to fifth-year seniors, met Tuesday to discuss the organization and the September elections. Applications were being accepted for president and for senate seats.

Herald, 2 Sep 2004

21 September 2004
#

Dean of Student Life contradicted Todd on sanctions

Howard Bailey, dean of Student Life, said Todd had been sanctioned by judicial affairs in July over the alleged misuse of university funds, disputing Todd's public claim that losing access to the Dining Dollars card was the only action taken against him. Bailey said federal privacy law barred him from revealing the sanctions. Todd acknowledged he was also no longer allowed to handle any SGA funds.

Herald via Wayback, 21 Sep 2004

23 September 2004
#

SGA approved a $110,000 budget

SGA approved a $110,000 budget for the 2004-05 academic year, up from $105,000 the year before, despite earlier expectations of a $15,000 to $17,000 cut under the university's budget reduction plan. Most of the money went toward operating fees, campus improvements and organizational aid. Adviser Charley Pride credited steady enrollment growth for the increase.

Herald via Wayback, 23 Sep 2004

28 September 2004
#

Johnson sworn in as president after winning 1,291 votes

Patti Johnson was sworn in as SGA president at the organization's fall reception on Sept. 23, after receiving 1,291 votes in the uncontested online election held Sept. 14 and 15. The 23 new senators were seated in the first fall senate election held under SGA's new constitution. Johnson was due to be sworn in as student regent at the Oct. 29 Board of Regents meeting.

Herald via Wayback, 28 Sep 2004

28 October 2004Tradition
#

Dialogue Day announced as a decade-old idea revived

The Herald reported on 28 October 2004 that SGA would hold Dialogue Day from 11 a.m. to 2 p.m. on 4 November at Java City. Senator Jessica Martin said students could go through an entire college career without meeting their dean. Dawson said the programme had been an SGA idea a decade earlier but had only run regularly for the previous three years.

Herald, 28 Oct 2004 - "SGA to hold forum" (Wayback)

4 November 2004Tradition
#

Dialogue Day put deans in the coffee shop

SGA held Dialogue Day at Java City in Helm Library on 4 November 2004, an open forum where students could raise any topic with university administrators. More than 20 students and seven administrators took part, among them Gene Tice, vice president for student affairs and campus services, and Bob Reber, associate dean of the Gordon Ford College of Business. SGA paid for refreshments; executive vice president Katie Dawson said the point was to get names attached to faces so students would feel less intimidated about making appointments.

SGA funded refreshments through legislation co-written by senator Tim Howard

College Heights Herald 80:19, 9 Nov 2004 - "Dialogue Day Offers Open Forum" (TopSCHOLAR)

16 November 2004
#

SGA proposed a faculty evaluation pilot to the University Senate

SGA prepared to propose a two-year pilot program to the University Senate that would add five questions on matters such as office hours, punctuality and textbook use to faculty evaluations and post the answers on TopNet for students. Senator Josh Collins wrote the proposal with Troy Ransdell and Morgan Phelps after SGA scaled back an earlier push to release entire evaluations. The questions could appear on evaluations at the end of spring 2005 if the study was approved.

Herald via Wayback, 16 Nov 2004

8 February 2005Service
#

SGA funded a recycling centre in Downing University Center

On 8 February 2005 the senate passed legislation paying for a recycling centre in DUC and eight new benches near the dorms. Campus services manager Greg Fear said the centre would be three heavy-duty bins taking plastic, aluminium and paper, replacing cheaper dorm bins that students had destroyed six years earlier. The bins were to cost about $2,000 from the campus improvements committee budget.

about $2,000 from the campus improvements committee budget

College Heights Herald 80:34, 10 Feb 2005 - "SGA votes to add recycling center in DUC" (TopSCHOLAR)

17 March 2005
#

Katie Dawson elected SGA president for 2005-06

Executive Vice President Katie Dawson defeated Senate Speaker Josh Collins 1,268 to 472 in the spring election's only contested race to become the next SGA president. Students also passed a package of 40 constitutional amendments 1,193 to 262 and voted 1,019 to 669 to ask the Board of Regents to institute a $9 per semester Talisman yearbook fee. Mason Stevenson and Melissa Paris won the two vice presidencies unopposed, and all 25 senate candidates on the ballot were elected.

Herald via Wayback, 17 Mar 2005

31 March 2005campus
#

Goodrum found not guilty in Katie Autry murder trial

A jury in Owensboro found Lucas Goodrum, 23, of Scottsville not guilty of the May 2003 murder of Pellville freshman Melissa "Katie" Autry, who was set on fire in her Poland Hall dorm room and died three days later. The verdict came March 21 after three hours of deliberation, ending a three-week trial that had been moved from Warren County. Stephen Soules had earlier pleaded guilty to murder, rape, sodomy, arson and robbery and expected life in prison.

Herald via Wayback, 31 Mar 2005

7 April 2005Service
#

Provide-A-Ride extended to Thursday nights

SGA, the university Parking and Transportation Committee and Yellow Cab agreed to extend the free late-night Provide-A-Ride service to Thursdays from the first week of classes in autumn 2005. A single 15-passenger van ran 10 p.m. to 2 a.m. Thursday through Saturday, free with a WKU ID and $2 without, at a cost to the university of about $150 a night. The new one-year contract also covered rides 30 minutes before and 90 minutes into home football games. President Patti Johnson said Thursday was as busy as Friday and Saturday.

about $150 per night per van

Herald, 7 Apr 2005 - "Provide-a-ride extends service" (Wayback)

14 April 2005
#

SGA funded the Buy-A-Book program

SGA passed legislation 22-2 on April 5 allocating $2,000 to the Buy-A-Book program, and the University Bookstore pledged to match the amount. The bill by senator Katharine Pettit was amended unanimously to help 20 students who needed financial assistance buying textbooks instead of 10. Recipients were to be chosen by the academic affairs committee based on a required essay and need.

Herald via Wayback, 14 Apr 2005

18 April 2005Tradition
#

Campus Cleanup Day handed out green T-shirts and free food

SGA ran its Campus Cleanup Day on 18 April 2005, with volunteers meeting in front of Cherry Hall and splitting into teams assigned to sections of campus. Senators had unanimously voted $1,000 from the campus improvements budget on 1 March for green "Cleaning up the Hill" T-shirts, and about $500 from the Aramark budget for food at the Campus Engagement Day reception that followed on South Lawn. Facilities management donated 300 pairs of gloves and 200 trash bags.

$1,000 for T-shirts plus about $500 for the reception

Herald, 19 Apr 2005 - "Volunteers clean up campus, get t-shirts, food" (Wayback)

19 April 2005Other
#

Senate spent $12,000 in under twenty minutes

On 19 April 2005 SGA senators applauded after spending more than $12,000 in fewer than twenty minutes at the end of the budget year. The purchases included bicycle racks, an emergency phone for the community college, about 1,000 red towels for promotional events at $2,000 from the public relations budget, and up to $115 for 2,500 bookmarks advertising tutorial and educational assistance programmes. About $35,000 was still unspent, and president-elect Katie Dawson was to distribute it over the summer before the university reclaimed it.

more than $12,000 spent; about $35,000 still unspent

Herald, 21 Apr 2005 - "SGA spends money left in budget" (Wayback)

What this is

A year-by-year record of student government at Western Kentucky University, from the ratification of the Associated Students constitution in April 1966 to the present. Every entry names the source it came from.

Corrections

The names here start with the plaque in the SGA Chambers, and the plaque is known to be wrong in places. Where the archive disagrees with it, this site follows the archive and records the change on the corrections page. If you served, or you know that a year here is wrong, the project wants to hear from you: people who held an office can be given the keys to their own year on the contribute page.

What it rests on

Everything here comes from what is publicly available online: the digitised Herald and SGA's own papers on TopSCHOLAR, the Talisman yearbooks, wkuherald.com, the university's news pages and the Wayback Machine. That is a fraction of what happened. Student government has met most weeks for sixty years, and the greater part of it was never written down, or was written down on paper that has never been scanned. A thin year here is a thin record, not a quiet one.

Reuse

Text on this site is the work of the project. Photographs, documents and legislation are reproduced from Western Kentucky University's own open archives and from the College Heights Herald and the Talisman; each item names the collection, issue and page it came from and links to the original.
