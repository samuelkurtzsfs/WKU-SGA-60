Keyanna Boka president and student regent, 2013-14

President and student regent

Standing of the record
As recorded on the plaque. Not yet corroborated in the archive.

Appears in the WKU Archives regents index as student regent, 2013. Plaque spells the first name KEYANNA; the archives index reads Boka, Keyana - confirm which is correct.

Boka, the outgoing executive vice president, was elected 2013-14 SGA president on 4 April 2013 with 626 votes (47 percent), beating administrative vice president Cain Alvey (419 votes) and Austin Wingate (300). Five days later the Judicial Council voted 3-2 to disqualify her after Alvey, the runner-up who stood to become president if she were removed, complained that a self-promotional email had gone to Honors College students at her request. Boka appealed to Howard Bailey, WKU's vice president for Student Affairs, who reinstated her; the Judicial Council declined to contest his ruling in an emergency meeting on 16 April, with Chief Justice Seth Church calling the intervention "a serious infringement on our autonomy" and Boka countering that the council itself had conflicts of interest.

In office, Boka's fall 2013 senate created a new international student senate seat for Navitas/ESL students and finalized a $125,000 budget, part of which sent her and executive vice president Mark Reeves on a Washington, D.C. higher-education lobbying trip. She fielded student anger over new two-tier priority registration rules at a November open forum with Provost Gordon Emslie, and announced the permanent closure of Thompson Complex North Wing, long slated for demolition. A senate attempt to reverse SGA's support for the Talisman's student fee was struck down by the Judicial Council as unconstitutional, as was the underlying 2005 policy.

In spring 2014 Boka organized a Frankfort rally against a $1.8 million state budget cut to WKU and opposed a senate amendment stripping the president's sole power to appoint Judicial Council justices, which was sent back to committee after the council found too few senators had been present to pass it. At the year's final meeting she announced a 4.8 percent tuition increase for the coming year, and the senate passed a resolution backing a Good Samaritan policy to protect students who called 911 during drug or alcohol overdoses.

The year, in order39

28 March 2013
#

Presidential debate drew full field of candidates

Ahead of the spring 2013 election, outgoing president Cory Dodds opened a debate in Ransdell Hall Auditorium among presidential candidates Cain Alvey, Keyana Boka and Austin Wingate, plus executive vice presidential candidates Brad Cockrel and Mark Reeves and administrative vice presidential candidate Nicki Seay. Boka said SGA members needed better training in their roles, Alvey said the presidency's responsibilities went beyond the constitution, and Wingate, a former Speaker of the Senate, called for better representation across student groups.

Herald, 28 Mar 2013

27 August 2013Service
#

The Purple Line got a third bus after carrying close to 20,000 riders

The Safe Ride shuttle SGA had created with Parking and Transportation in 2012 to replace Provide-A-Ride, known on campus as the Purple Line, entered its second year with a third bus added. Transit manager Stephen Rowland said SGA wanted to keep the line's 15-minute run time and the extra bus was needed to hold the schedule across 16 stops and a 45-minute circuit, and that the line had carried close to 20,000 passengers in its first year. The line ran Thursday, Friday and Saturday from 10 p.m. to 2 a.m. Driver Quinten Haynie said Homecoming week and the opening weeks of class were the busiest.

3 September 2013
#

Judicial Council strikes down the international senate seat amendment

SGA announced that the Judicial Council had ruled unconstitutional a constitutional amendment proposed by President Keyana Boka and Executive Vice President Mark Reeves. The amendment would have changed the senate's composition by adding one seat for an international student. The ruling was reported to the senate at its second meeting of the semester.

Herald, 3 Sep 2013

6 September 2013
#

Emergency Judicial Council meeting clears reworded international seat

SGA called an emergency Judicial Council meeting to review revisions to the amendment the council had rejected on Aug. 29 for violating WKU's non-discrimination clause. Boka and Reeves changed the wording from "one international student senator" to a Navitas or English as a Second Language international student seat. Boka said the emergency session was routine protocol for any meeting outside the council's regular schedule.

Herald, 6 Sep 2013

10 September 2013
#

Executive Council floated plus/minus grading, set fall calendar

At the Executive Council's weekly meeting, Executive Vice President Mark Reeves reported that the University Senate Council had discussed reviving a plus/minus grading system and that Provost Gordon Emslie was interested in SGA's support. The council set a Nov. 9 Picnic with the President for the presidents' council and deans, and swore in Samantha Johnson to an executive cabinet seat vacated by the resignation of Keith Watson.

SGA Executive Council minutes, 10 Sep 2013

10 September 2013
#

Herald editorial questions SGA's handpicked approach to diversity

A Herald editorial examined the proposed permanent senate seat for an international student, ruled unconstitutional on Aug. 29 and then revised and approved in a six-minute emergency meeting. The editorial said it supported SGA's effort to expand representation but questioned whether a designated seat was the most effective means. Boka and Reeves had said their intention was to better represent WKU's international student population.

Herald, 10 Sep 2013

11 September 2013
#

SGA reviewed 24-hour dining, grading and registration proposals

SGA gave first read to a proposal for a 24-hour on-campus dining option, with the Organizational Aid Committee sponsoring the resolution and assistant vice president for auxiliary services Gary Meszaros set to attend the next meeting. Reeves told senators Provost Gordon Emslie was reviving a plus/minus grading proposal and had brought priority registration and major-declaration changes to University Senate.

Herald, 11 Sep 2013

12 September 2013
#

Senate passed international student seat amendment

President Keyana Boka and executive vice president Mark Reeves authored a constitutional amendment adding a senate seat for Navitas or English as a Second Language international students. The Judicial Council had ruled an earlier version unconstitutional, but after a revision in an emergency meeting the Senate passed it with only two votes against. Boka said it would make WKU's SGA the first student government in the state with an international senate seat.

Herald, 12 Sep 2013

12 September 2013
#

Student petition for 24-hour dining collects 700 signatures for SGA

Bowling Green sophomore Nick Conrad, a McCormack Hall resident assistant, gathered more than 700 signatures in about a week on a petition asking for a 24-hour dining option on campus, with a stated goal of 5,000. Conrad said he studied late and knew many students on similar schedules. He had met with SGA representatives who were interested in the petition.

Herald, 12 Sep 2013

19 September 2013
#

564 voted in the fall election; referendum created the Navitas/ESL seat

A total of 564 students voted in the fall SGA election. The referendum creating a Navitas and English as a Second Language international student senate seat drew 426 votes and passed with 310 yes votes, 73 percent. Six freshman senators were elected out of seven candidates, led by Jay Todd Richey with 27 percent, along with two graduate senators, a Gatton Academy senator and an Arts and Letters senator. Most college senate seats went to unopposed candidates.

Nicki Seay ran unopposed as SGA's homecoming queen candidate

At the first meeting after the fall election, the senate nominated and voted on its homecoming queen candidate. Administrative Vice President Nicki Seay was nominated, ran unopposed and took the nomination. The same meeting swore in fifteen new senators and heard that the Campus Improvements Committee was developing a community garden with GreenToppers.

26 September 2013
#

Senate funds transcript vouchers with $300

SGA passed a bill allotting $300 to the Transcript Voucher Program fund, letting students collect vouchers from the SGA office instead of paying the $7 transcript fee. Academic Affairs committee head Megan Skaggs, who wrote the bill, said students already paid a great deal in tuition and fees. Funding for the long-running program had to be renewed by senate vote each time more money was needed.

Herald, 26 Sep 2013

October 2013
#

Council created an Election Code Advisory Board

In Opinion 2013FA-005 the Judicial Council established an Election Code Advisory Board under section 4.5.8 of the constitution, to rewrite election codes it described as having proven inadequate. The board was chaired by the chief justice, open to any SGA member or student willing to serve, and required to report to the Senate monthly until dissolved by a majority vote of the council. Its recommendations were not binding on the council.

Judicial Council Opinion 2013FA-005, 1 Oct 2013

9 October 2013
#

Resolution targets the internet policy behind WKU's FIRE red light rating

A resolution by Director of Public Relations Laura Harper received its first read, supporting amendment of university internet usage policies that the Foundation for Individual Rights in Education had deemed a violation of free speech. FIRE had given WKU a red light rating because at least one handbook policy violated the First Amendment. Computing Policy 12-13, covering email content likely to be perceived as offensive or disparaging, was the specific cause.

Herald, 9 Oct 2013

10 October 2013
#

SGA finalized a $125,000 budget after a planned increase fell through

SGA finalized a 2013-14 budget of $125,000 drawn from student tuition, after a planned funding increase did not come through. Director of Public Relations Laura Harper said the figure equated to about $6.25 per student. The organizational aid committee was allotted $30,000, with campus organizations able to receive up to $500 each, and the executive discretionary fund held $5,900, part of which covered a Washington, D.C. higher education lobbying trip by Boka and Reeves. Administrative Vice President Nicki Seay, who built the budget, said senate discretionary spending required a senate majority and executive discretionary spending an executive committee majority.

Herald, 10 Oct 2013

Internet policy resolution and ID voucher bill pass at a short meeting

With attendance thinned by a home football game where SGA ran a Safe Ride promotional booth, the senate passed Resolution 3-13-F on internet usage policies and Bill 2-13-F funding identification vouchers. Two further bills went into first read, one on organizational aid and one funding six winter term scholarship sessions. Laura Harper introduced the internet policy resolution.

24 October 2013
#

SGA proposes $1,598 toward five KwikBoost charging stations with the libraries

SGA and WKU Libraries proposed installing five KwikBoost phone and laptop charging stations, each with eight charging attachments, at $799 apiece. SGA's bill, introduced for first reading that Tuesday, would pay $1,598 for two of the five: one at the Glasgow campus and one in the Helm 2 periodicals room. WKU Libraries would fund the other three, planned for the Educational Resources Center, Helm 100 and the Owensboro campus. President Keyana Boka said Dean of University Libraries Connie Foster brought her the idea.

29 October 2013
#

Senate passes charging-station funding, then the executive council vetoes it

The Senate amended and passed the $1,598 charging-station bill, moving one station from Helm 2 to the Commons at Cravens Library at Sen. Cain Alvey's request. After the meeting adjourned, the Executive Council vetoed the bill, saying SGA could not unilaterally change the stations' locations without consulting its partner, WKU Libraries. Under SGA bylaws the Executive Council had 14 days to act on passed legislation; a two-thirds Senate vote could still override the veto.

Herald, 31 Oct 2013

31 October 2013
#

Priority registration complaints spurred open forum

Student complaints over new two-tier priority registration rules, including a 16-credit-hour cap during the priority period, pushed SGA to schedule a Nov. 12 open forum with Provost Gordon Emslie. Honors College seniors told senator Tyler Scaff some were considering dropping the honors major over the change. Boka said she had no official stance yet, though SGA had earlier approved the change through Reeves's vote at University Senate.

Herald, 31 Oct 2013

5 November 2013
#

Herald editorial faulted SGA over the priority registration policy

A Herald editorial accused SGA of failing to represent students on the new priority registration policy, which capped students with priority registration at 16 hours on their priority day and exempted seniors and graduate students. The editorial said executive vice president Mark Reeves voted for the policy at the University Senate on 10 September without SGA questioning or debating it first. It also faulted SGA for scheduling an open forum for 12 November, after the policy was already set to govern fall registration. Representing students was SGA's one job, the editorial argued, and it had not been done on this issue.

Herald, 5 Nov 2013

7 November 2013
#

Thompson Complex North Wing set to close permanently

Boka announced at the Tuesday SGA meeting that Thompson Complex North Wing, home to the biology and chemistry departments, would close permanently the next semester because the building could not be maintained over the winter. The building had been labeled for demolition since 2005, and its demolition along with renovation of the Center Wing was the university's top capital priority before the Kentucky General Assembly.

Herald, 7 Nov 2013

12 November 2013
#

An outdated charging-station bill is tabled again

The charging-station bill returned to the Senate floor, altered from its vetoed version, but senators tabled it after finding an outdated draft had been presented rather than the corrected one. A senator noted voting on the wrong version would violate SGA's constitution.

Herald, 13 Nov 2013

13 November 2013
#

Provost and registrar face students at SGA's priority registration forum

SGA hosted an open forum on priority registration during its weekly meeting, with Provost Gordon Emslie and University Registrar Freida Eggleton answering questions. The policy, already in effect for spring registration, split priority registration into a need tier and a benefit tier and capped qualifying students at 16 credit hours until open registration. Seniors and graduate students still registered first and were not subject to the cap.

Herald, 13 Nov 2013

20 November 2013
#

South Campus forum turns on the closing of the campus store

SGA held its weekly meeting at South Campus as an open forum and President Keyana Boka asked those present to discuss the recent closing of the South Campus WKU store. Senators debated the lack of communication between the officials who closed the store and South Campus students. Executive Vice President Mark Reeves said the same thing had happened the previous year when the food court closed, calling it part of a broader failure to consult South Campus students.

Herald, 20 Nov 2013

3 December 2013
#

Judicial Council voided Talisman fee stance reversal

After the yearbook's budget was cut to $127,000, senator Chris Costa moved at the Nov. 19 meeting to revoke SGA's stance supporting a $9 student fee for the Talisman that students had approved on a 2005 ballot, and two-thirds of SGA voted to reverse the stance. The Judicial Council ruled Costa's motion unconstitutional on Nov. 21, and also ruled the original 2005 policy unconstitutional because it was never passed according to SGA policy. Talisman editors then met with Boka and SGA members to discuss long-term funding options.

#

Talisman brings its funding shortfall to SGA

Talisman representatives met President Keyana Boka and Brad Cockrel on Nov. 29 to discuss long-term options for paying for the yearbook. The previous May the Talisman had faced a proposed $93,000 cut, 47 percent of its $198,000 budget, before an alumni-backed "Save the Talisman" campaign reduced the cut. Its budget stood at $127,000, including $45,000 in one-time university funds.

#

Student on crutches brings a bus stop bench proposal to the senate

Covington senior Logan Eckler went before SGA on Nov. 19 to propose benches at campus bus stops after several days on crutches following a judo injury. He said the bus system was invaluable for students who had difficulty walking but that few stops had seats. 6 February 2014
#

SGA reconvened and planned a Frankfort rally against budget cuts

At the first spring meeting President Keyana Boka announced SGA would organize a trip to Frankfort called Rally for Retention in response to WKU's $1.8 million state budget cut, with speeches and one-on-one meetings with legislators. Executive Vice President Mark Reeves outlined the two-year state budget then being voted on, warned that tough cuts were coming and said students needed to be involved. The body reviewed a proposal to revise the change-of-major advising process, and Boka reported talks with dining services about 24-hour dining with Einstein's Bagels under consideration for a 24-hour study area; five members had resigned the previous semester, leaving the legislative research and public relations chairs among the vacant seats.

Herald, 6 Feb 2014

13 February 2014
#

Executive Council voided the amendment stripping the president's judicial appointments

An amendment removing the SGA president's sole power to appoint judicial council justices and raising the council to seven justices passed at the Tuesday meeting, over President Keyana Boka's objection that it bypassed both presidential appointment and the senate. Boka said the president is elected by the student body to make appointments in all three branches and that she was wary of the direction, while Chief Justice Seth Church argued that vesting appointments in the executive branch as a whole created a fairer system. After the meeting the Executive Council reviewed the constitution, found too few senators had attended for the vote to count and declared the amendment unconstitutional, sending it back to first read. The SGA office had temporarily moved to Garrett after flooding in Cravens.

Herald, 13 Feb 2014

26 February 2014
#

Mid-year budget report showed a $46,883.62 surplus

SGA's mid-year report on its $125,000 budget showed a surplus of $46,883.62. Administrative Vice President Nicki Seay said the budget had changed little since December and that she had more funds to allocate back to the student body, and $54,000 had gone to the legislative branch for items such as organizational aid and grants. The senate approved Bill 3-14-S allocating organizational funds and suspended its bylaws to pass Resolution 3-14-S supporting WKU's participation in the Campus Pride Index. Hannah Neeper, Elizabeth Gribbins and AJ Stewart were approved as senators, leaving six senate seats open.

Herald, 26 Feb 2014

18 March 2014
#

SGA unanimously backs taking the Campus Pride Index

An SGA proposal that WKU take the Campus Pride Index, a survey measuring campus friendliness toward LGBT students, passed unanimously and was submitted to the Administrative Council. Student Identity Outreach president Andrew Salman first proposed it to the Office of Diversity, and senator Nicki Seay carried it into SGA. Salman said the index would diagnose areas where the university was not meeting the needs of its queer students, and Seay said WKU had never taken it before.

Herald, 18 Mar 2014

25 March 2014
#

Judicial Council structure amendment adopted, referred to the spring ballot

Resolution 1-14-S, first read on 4 February 2014, proposed to expand the Judicial Council from five justices to seven, have the Chief Justice chosen by majority vote of the council rather than appointed by the president, and create a new Associate Chief Justice office, with a related bylaw change moving minute-taking duty to that new office. The resolution's text calls for referring the amendments to the student body on the spring 2014 ballot, but the copy posted by SGA gives a second-reading date of 25 March 2014 with the Pass/Fail field left blank, so this document alone does not confirm the resolution actually passed that day. SGA's constitution as currently published does carry all of these exact provisions, confirming the changes were adopted at some point.

SGA Resolution 1-14-S

27 March 2014
#

Council overruled its own year-old joint campaigning rule

In Opinion 2014SP-001, In re joint campaigning (II), the Judicial Council overruled its 2013 opinion on candidates campaigning together and set a new formula: the lowest expenditure cap among joint campaigners, divided by their number, was the most each could put into shared material, and each then subtracted the whole joint total from their own allowance. The opinion worked the arithmetic for a senator pair and for a three-way presidential ticket, and said the council would take good-faith compliance leniently because the rule was new.

Judicial Council Opinion 2014SP-001, 27 Mar 2014

24 April 2014
#

Council ruled abstentions count toward quorum

In Opinion 2014SP-002 the Judicial Council was asked whether Resolution 5-14-S had passed properly. It held that abstaining members cast neither a yes nor a no vote but concur with the majority of those voting, citing a 1988 Kentucky Attorney General opinion, and that abstaining members do count toward quorum because quorum refers to the number present. Since no senator had noted a lack of quorum, quorum was presumed throughout and the resolution passed. Chief Justice Seth Church delivered the opinion, joined by Julia Payne and Jackie Stewart; Megan Lemmons took no part.

Judicial Council Opinion 2014SP-002, 24 Apr 2014

May 2014
#

Final meeting passed the Good Samaritan resolution and cleared funding bills

At the last meeting of the semester President Keyana Boka announced a 4.8 percent tuition increase, about $209 more per semester for most students, alongside a $4.2 million increase in student financial assistance and a one percent faculty salary adjustment. The senate suspended its bylaws to pass a slate of bills on first read, beginning with Bill 17-14-S allocating $1,817.10, funding Summer Term assistance, student organizations, student research and an Earth Week planting party. Resolution 6-14-S passed in support of adopting a Good Samaritan Policy protecting students who call 911 during drug or alcohol overdoses, and Speaker Paige Settles said one remaining scholarship bill would be handled at a short meeting during the end-of-year banquet.

Herald, 1 May 2014

What this is

A year-by-year record of student government at Western Kentucky University, from the ratification of the Associated Students constitution in April 1966 to the present. Every entry names the source it came from.

Corrections

The names here start with the plaque in the SGA Chambers, and the plaque is known to be wrong in places. Where the archive disagrees with it, this site follows the archive and records the change on the corrections page. If you served, or you know that a year here is wrong, the project wants to hear from you: people who held an office can be given the keys to their own year on the contribute page.

What it rests on

Everything here comes from what is publicly available online: the digitised Herald and SGA's own papers on TopSCHOLAR, the Talisman yearbooks, wkuherald.com, the university's news pages and the Wayback Machine. That is a fraction of what happened. Student government has met most weeks for sixty years, and the greater part of it was never written down, or was written down on paper that has never been scanned. A thin year here is a thin record, not a quiet one.

Text on this site is the work of the project. Photographs, documents and legislation are reproduced from Western Kentucky University's own open archives and from the College Heights Herald and the Talisman; each item names the collection, issue and page it came from and links to the original.
