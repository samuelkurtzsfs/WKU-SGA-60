Jay Todd Richey president and student regent, 2016-17

Office
President and student regent
Term recorded here
2016-17
Place in the line
the 51st president and the 48th student regent
Standing of the record
As recorded on the plaque. Not yet corroborated in the archive.
Also on the wall
2015-16

Re-elected. In office for Resolution 6-17-S, the reparations resolution, which passed 19-10-1 on 18 April 2017 and drew national coverage. Richey publicly framed it as a conversation starter; President Gary Ransdell stated within days that WKU would not adopt it.

His re-election made him WKU SGA's first two-term president since 1988 and one of only three since 1974. Over his two terms he led the creation of three new committees, Sustainability, MyCampusToo and SAVES (Standing Against Violence and Ending Self-harm), revamped SGA's website, authored nine bills and resolutions, and represented students on the Board of Regents, the University Senate and the presidential search committee that chose WKU's 10th president. A Herald profile in March 2017 described a president who arrived late to office hours from overrun meetings, fielded budget questions and calls from the chief of police, and admitted he still struggled with low self-esteem.

In a farewell letter published as his term ended, Richey wrote that fighting for college affordability had been a personal priority: SGA rallied at the Kentucky Capitol and lobbied the General Assembly against university budget cuts, and he chaired the Board of Student Body Presidents of Kentucky. He cited expanded services for military and veteran students, the Rave Guardian safety app, WKU's first eSports program, an SGA Voter Empowerment Initiative that registered hundreds of voters, new SGA scholarships including one for students with disabilities, and helping select president Tim Caboni. His successor Andi Dahmer took the oath of office on May 2, 2017.

The year, in order43

20 April 2016
#

Richey re-elected SGA president in high-turnout election

WKU students re-elected Jay Todd Richey as SGA president and elected Kate Hart as executive vice president in one of the highest-turnout elections in the association's history, with 2,442 voters. Richey defeated opponent Braxton Powell with 64 percent of the overall vote. Powell was elected a junior senator and his running mate Zach Jones a senior senator.

Herald, 20 Apr 2016

19 August 2016
#

Student regent publishes dissent from Medical Center partnership

Jay Todd Richey, serving as student regent, wrote an open letter to students explaining his vote against the Board of Regents' 6-4-1 approval of a partnership with the Medical Center to construct a building housing an athletics practice facility and the Department of Physical Therapy. He argued the deal would tie WKU to a single health care provider for up to 99 years and place the university inside a long-running competition among Bowling Green providers. He wrote that although bound to uphold the board's decision, he believed it was a bad deal for WKU.

Herald, 19 Aug 2016

31 August 2016
#

SGA raises its own student office workers to $10.10 an hour

Richey told the senate SGA office workers would be paid $10.10 an hour, saying the raise reflected the rising cost of tuition. "SGA is making a stand and saying that students deserve this wage," he said. Chief facilities officer Bryan Russell also briefed senators that Parking Structure 3 in the Creason lot would add more than 500 spaces, with construction running from September to August 2017.

Herald, 31 Aug 2016

7 September 2016
#

Senate funds first disability scholarships after student testimony

Bill 4-16-F, creating scholarships for students with disabilities, passed unanimously after sophomore Brendan Ward told senators his ADHD and dyslexia had cost him merit scholarships and that he was at WKU "only by loans and the government." The bill drew $500 each from the football tabling and food lines of the SGA budget. Two other students, Cheyenne Mitchell and Francisco Serrano, addressed the senate about racist vandalism and discrimination on campus.

Herald, 7 Sep 2016

12 September 2016campus
#

Ransdell condemns racist incidents after notes, vandalism

In the weeks before, racist notes had been left in a WKU assistant dean's office and a racial slur was scratched into a student's car following a parking dispute. President Gary Ransdell called the acts "cowardly" and "heinous," said they did not reflect the university's values, and called for campus dialogue on intolerance.

WKMS, 12 Sep 2016

13 September 2016
#

Senators raise campus racism at first fall meeting

At the third meeting of the 15th Senate, student speakers Jeb Veeck, Francisco Serrano and Spencer Wells addressed the recent racist incidents on campus. Veeck suggested a Q&A session on the issue and Serrano urged senators to help end racism on campus.

20 September 2016Programme
#

SGA co-sponsored the International Diplomats' Home Away From Home

Bill 7-16-F allocated $250 from senate discretionary funding to International Enrollment Management for the International Diplomats' charity event Home Away From Home, held on 29 September 2016 at the Honors College and International Center. The event was built so international students could meet American students and Bowling Green organisations, with all proceeds going to the International Center of Kentucky and a small prize for whoever donated most. The bill passed unanimously on 27 September.

$250

SGA Bill 7-16-F, Funding for the International Diplomats Event "Home Away From Home"

21 September 2016
#

Senate seat left vacant all summer by an uncorrected election error

Speaker Nathan Cherry told the senate that eleven at-large seats should have been filled the previous spring but only ten were, leaving SGA one senator short through the summer. "I failed to catch that," Cherry said. Lucas Knight was selected to fill the seat, and Richey said he was exploring a hybrid arrangement letting students question anonymous presidential candidates online during the closed search.

Herald, 21 Sep 2016

4 October 2016Programme
#

Earn-A-Computer gave away its first desktop

SGA presented the first computer under its Earn-A-Computer programme on 4 October 2016, an iMac awarded free to Nashville junior Bobby Scott after he completed 35 hours of community service. Scott worked with the Intercultural Engagement Center and with children at Warren Elementary School. A computer information technology major whose own machine had recently died, he told the senate he had badly needed it. SGA ran the programme with WKU Resource Conservation.

Herald, 10 Oct 2016 - "SGA awards first desktop in computer program"

12 October 2016
#

Two SGA members resign amid racism complaint; investigation nullified

After Chief Justice Cody Cox told the Oct. 11 senate meeting he had received a complaint that several SGA members made racist remarks, senator Braxton Powell and justice John McKinney resigned the next morning, citing scheduling conflicts and personal reasons respectively; neither confirmed, denied, nor acknowledged any link between the resignations and the complaint. The Judicial Council's planned investigation was dropped because the students in question had already left SGA, and the organization never released the identities of the accused or the nature of the alleged remarks. President Richey and MyCampusToo chair Helen Vickrey issued a statement the next day condemning the alleged acts.

Herald, 17 Oct 2016

12 October 2016
#

SGA creates SAVES committee on sexual assault and suicide prevention

The senate created the Standing Against Violence and Ending Self-harm special committee to work on sexual assault prevention, suicide prevention and expanded mental health resources with the Counseling and Testing Center. Senator Francisco Serrano was named chair and described his own recent experience of having water thrown at him and racial slurs shouted while walking outside Downing Student Union. Freshman Ryan Linton told the senate WKU's harassment policy did not explicitly protect gender identity.

Herald, 12 Oct 2016

26 October 2016
#

Senate rejected 2.0 GPA bill, backed housing diversity

SGA rejected a bill that would have let students vote to lower the organization's GPA requirement from 2.5 to 2.0, a change supporters said would make the body more representative; a Twitter poll found 61 percent of respondents opposed it. The senate also approved a resolution asking Housing and Residence Life to address the lack of diversity in on-campus housing under the university's 10-year housing plan.

Herald, 26 Oct 2016

26 October 2016
#

Senate rejects GPA change again, approves housing diversity resolution

A bill to let students vote on lowering SGA's GPA requirement from 2.5 to 2.0 was rejected, with Senator Kaycee Gibson saying representatives "are expected to be elitist" and Public Relations Chair Alex Sergent saying "we're the cream of the crop." An SGA Twitter poll had found 61 percent of respondents opposed the change. The senate did approve a resolution asking Housing and Residence Life to integrate honors and international students across Southwest and Northeast halls rather than segregating them.

Herald, 26 Oct 2016

31 October 2016
#

Kentucky student body presidents demand restoration of the 'Powerball Promise'

Richey, as chair of the Board of Student Body Presidents of Kentucky, co-signed a published letter with the student presidents of nine other Kentucky institutions calling on Gov. Matt Bevin and the legislature to stop diverting lottery money from need-based College Access Program and Kentucky Tuition Grant aid. The letter cited Kentucky Center for Economic Policy figures that underfunding had limited scholarships for 15,000 to 20,000 qualifying students in 2016. It named Bevin's veto of House Bill 10 as having blocked restoration of the funding.

Herald, 31 Oct 2016

8 November 2016Service
#

Ride To Vote drove students to the polls

SGA ran a Ride To Vote service on election day, 8 November 2016, driving any WKU student without transport to and from the polling stations. The programme was discussed at the senate meeting of 1 November alongside the Board of Student Body Presidents' letter to Governor Matt Bevin asking that lottery money be kept in need-based student financial aid.

Herald, 2 Nov 2016 - "SGA discusses Ride To Vote, financial aid"

15 November 2016Service
#

SGA paid to keep the library open later during finals

Bill 19-16-F allocated $1,200 from legislative discretionary funding to extend Helm-Cravens Library's hours during finals week in autumn 2016 and spring 2017, keeping it open until 2 a.m. Sunday through Thursday instead of midnight. The bill recorded that SGA had a standing tradition of paying for extended library hours each year, and that the library's signage would credit the sponsorship. It passed on 15 November 2016.

$1,200

SGA Bill 19-16-F, Funding for Extended Library Hours

16 November 2016
#

SGA launches SafeWalk and makes diversity and sustainability committees permanent

The senate approved bills making the Sustainability and MyCampusToo committees permanent standing committees under the constitution, and renamed MyCampusToo the Committee for Diversity and Inclusion after some found the old name "offensive and not inclusive." SGA also launched SafeWalk, a call line pairing students who felt unsafe with student escorts, and extended office hours so students had a place to go. Resolutions supporting an elevator in Gordon Wilson Hall and removal of styrofoam from WKU dining also passed.

Herald, 16 Nov 2016

30 November 2016
#

SGA created Jonesville scholarship fund

The senate created a scholarship memorializing Jonesville, the African American community founded by freed slaves in the 1870s that stood on land now occupied by Smith Stadium, Nick Denes Field and Downing Student Union until WKU bought and demolished it in the late 1960s. Senators debated whether the award should go only to African American students as repayment for Jonesville's condemnation or be opened to all minority students.

Herald, 30 Nov 2016

30 November 2016
#

Senate creates Jonesville Memorial Scholarship after debate over eligibility

SGA allocated $750, matched by $100 from the Center for Citizenship and Social Justice, for a scholarship memorializing Jonesville, the Black community WKU bought and displaced in the late 1960s for under $200,000. Senators debated opening it to all minority students, but the bill passed in its original form restricting it to first-year African American students from Warren County or Bowling Green schools. Senator Dillon McCormick called it "a kind of symbolic gesture to atone for the wrong that we did."

Herald, 30 Nov 2016

7 February 2017Programme
#

Love Has No Borders answered the travel ban

Bill 6-17-S allocated $100 for Love Has No Borders, held at the Faculty House on 10 February 2017 and co-sponsored with the International Diplomats, to celebrate WKU's international students after the executive order restricting entry from seven countries. The bill noted that 22 WKU students came from the affected countries and had asked for an event showing support from the student body. A further $100 came from executive discretionary funding and $138 from the public relations budget.

8 February 2017
#

Safe Ride bus service cut on Thursdays as ridership collapses

SGA ended Thursday operation of its Safe Ride Purple Line after ridership fell to about one rider per hour, against twenty per hour on Saturdays. Total ridership had dropped from roughly 5,000 in fall 2015 to nearly 1,500 in fall 2016, which Richey attributed to Uber's arrival in Bowling Green. He said the service would not be discontinued entirely without a replacement.

Herald, 8 Feb 2017

14 February 2017Lecture
#

Power UP political forum funded for Black History Month

Bill 5-17-S passed 30-0 on 14 February 2017, allocating $200 to the Intercultural Student Engagement Center for the Power UP Political Forum, a non-partisan student forum held on 20 February in DSU 2004 from 5:30 to 7 p.m. Planned topics included the origins of Black History Month, constitutional free speech guarantees and the demographic makeup of American legislatures. The money paid for refreshments and an honorarium for a guest speaker.

$200, covering refreshments and a speaker honorarium

SGA Bill 5-17-S, Funding for the Power UP Political Forum

15 February 2017
#

SGA creates regional ambassadors, requires senator office hours, raises campaign spending caps

The senate passed a bill creating non-voting regional ambassador positions for each regional campus and designating one senator as a South Campus liaison, and a second bill requiring every senator to keep one office hour per week. Speaker Nathan Cherry said the office-hour requirement would make senators feel more connected and more willing to write legislation. Senators also approved revised election codes raising campaign spending allowances, with presidential candidates permitted $300.

Herald, 15 Feb 2017

21 February 2017Programme
#

Jonesville and disability scholarships opened for applications

SGA said on 21 February 2017 that applications for the Jonesville Memorial Scholarship and the Students with Disabilities Scholarship would go on its website within days. The Jonesville award, approved the previous autumn, totalled $850 and was funded with the Center for Citizenship and Social Justice for first-year African American students from the Warren County or Bowling Green school systems. It memorialised Jonesville, the Black community WKU displaced when it bought the land to expand in the late 1960s.

$850 for the Jonesville Memorial Scholarship

Herald, 22 Feb 2017 - "SGA offers new scholarships for marginalized groups"

22 February 2017
#

Executive Council vetoes a unanimously passed resolution; author resigns

Senator Chase Coffey's resolution allowing former senators who resigned in good standing to attend SGA's end-of-year banquet passed the senate unanimously, then was vetoed by the Executive Council, which held it unconstitutional. Coffey resigned the same night over a conflict with a night class. The meeting also opened applications for the Jonesville Memorial Scholarship and SGA's first two $500 scholarships for students with disabilities.

Herald, 22 Feb 2017

23 February 2017
#

Housing office omits SGA's diversity resolution from its 10-year plan

Housing and Residence Life's 10-year plan made Southwest Hall honors housing in fall 2017 and Northeast Hall international housing in fall 2018, with no mention of the integration SGA had endorsed 27-1 the previous October. Senator Jody Dahmer said the resolution was deliberately written without detail so HRL could work out specifics. An SGA Twitter poll had found about 83 percent of respondents supported merging the two populations.

Herald, 23 Feb 2017

Big Red Bikes kept running on SGA money

The senate approved funding on 28 February 2017 for the Big Red Bikes free bicycle-lending programme and for the Community Farmers Market Double Dollars scheme. The bill gave Big Red Bikes just over $800 for brake pads, chains and locks after the Paula Nye Memorial Education Grant that had supported it ran out. Mechanic Colton Turley said the programme had 40 usable bicycles with 35 already rented out, 15 needing repair and 70 more donated but not yet ready.

Textbooks for Troops got $300 from the senate

Bill 15-17-S passed 29-0-1 on 21 March 2017, allocating $300 to WKU Military Student Services for its Textbooks for Troops programme, half from senate and half from executive discretionary funding. The programme, set up in 2011 for unemployed National Guard soldiers, lent textbooks for a full semester to military students, veterans and dependants not already receiving a book stipend. Military Student Services reported that more than 171 students had been helped in spring 2017 alone.

SGA votes 17-9 to require a nondiscrimination pledge before funding student groups

Bill 20-17-S, requiring student organizations to reaffirm a nondiscrimination statement including gender identity before receiving SGA organizational aid, passed 17-9-1. Richey said "SGA is not going to subsidize discrimination," while Senator Zach Jones argued the bill hampered diversity of opinion.

Senate refused to strip the executive of the power to propose legislation

#

Senate required biweekly reports of executive discretionary spending

Bill 24-17-S amended Constitution 2.5.3 to add biweekly reports of executive discretionary spending to the Administrative Vice President's duties, arguing that senate discretionary spending was set out in legislation while executive discretionary spending was not as easily accessible. It was first and second read on 4 April 2017 and passed 29-0-1. Students ratified it in the April referendum.

SGA Bill 24-17-S (data/legislation/2016-17/bill_24-17-s.pdf)

5 April 2017
#

GPA requirement lowered to 2.0 by exactly the required margin

After failing twice in the previous year, a bill lowering SGA's GPA requirement from 2.5 to 2.0 passed 24-7, receiving exactly the 24 votes needed from the 36-member senate, and went to the student body for ratification. Senator Brian Anderson called the opposing argument "paternalism that I don't think this organization should stand for." A separate bill to move committee chair appointments from the president to the speaker of the senate failed.

Herald, 5 Apr 2017

#

Judicial Council exemptions turn the presidential race into a three-way contest

The Judicial Council granted constitutional exemptions under Article 2.2.5 to Lily Nellans and Kenan Mujkanovic, both of whom had served less than one semester, allowing them to run against Andi Dahmer for president. A Herald editorial argued the rulings set a precedent letting candidates with no SGA experience seek the presidency and the accompanying seat on the Board of Regents. Only Dahmer had been eligible under the constitution as written.

Herald, 10 Apr 2017

SGA backed prioritizing faculty raises over construction

The senate passed a resolution supporting future faculty salary increases, written to mirror a concurrent resolution headed to the Faculty Senate and proposed by Administrative Vice President Hannah Neeper and Chief of Staff James Line. The resolution urged that salary increases be prioritized over future construction or renovation projects, citing WKU's second-to-last ranking in faculty pay among its 19 benchmark institutions in 2015 and a survey in which 57 percent of faculty rated their morale as poor or very poor.

Andi Dahmer elected to succeed Richey

Louisville sophomore Andi Dahmer, a senator and chair of the MyCampusToo committee, was elected the next student body president, winning 930 of 1,579 votes cast against Kenan Mujkanovic's 305 and Lily Nellans's 212. Her running mates Savannah Molyneaux and Kara Lowry won executive vice president and administrative vice president. #

Dahmer elected; all six referendums pass

Andi Dahmer won the presidency with 930 of 1,579 votes cast, ahead of Kenan Mujkanovic at 305 and Lily Nellans at 212, with Savannah Molyneaux and Kara Lowry elected to the vice presidencies. Students ratified all six referendums, making the SAVES, MyCampusToo and Sustainability committees permanent, lowering the GPA requirement to 2.0, creating regional campus ambassadors and requiring more accessible executive discretionary spending reports.

Academic Affairs and Student Affairs merged into one committee

Bill 28-17-S, first read 18 April 2017 and passed 28-0-0 on 25 April, combined the two committees into the Academic and Student Affairs Committee. What this is

A year-by-year record of student government at Western Kentucky University, from the ratification of the Associated Students constitution in April 1966 to the present. Every entry names the source it came from.

Corrections

The names here start with the plaque in the SGA Chambers, and the plaque is known to be wrong in places. Where the archive disagrees with it, this site follows the archive and records the change on the corrections page. If you served, or you know that a year here is wrong, the project wants to hear from you: people who held an office can be given the keys to their own year on the contribute page.

What it rests on

Everything here comes from what is publicly available online: the digitised Herald and SGA's own papers on TopSCHOLAR, the Talisman yearbooks, wkuherald.com, the university's news pages and the Wayback Machine. That is a fraction of what happened. Student government has met most weeks for sixty years, and the greater part of it was never written down, or was written down on paper that has never been scanned. A thin year here is a thin record, not a quiet one.

Text on this site is the work of the project. Photographs, documents and legislation are reproduced from Western Kentucky University's own open archives and from the College Heights Herald and the Talisman; each item names the collection, issue and page it came from and links to the original.
