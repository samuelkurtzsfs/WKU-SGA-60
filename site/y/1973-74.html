Steven Yater president and student regent, 1973-74

Office
President and student regent

Place in the line

Standing of the record
Confirmed in the sources below.

Confirmed by the Herald's own 16 April 1974 retrospective, 'Presidency Ends for Steve Yater; One Major Program Started,' which places his presidency closing out at the end of the 1973-74 year, matching the plaque term.

Steve Yater led the Associated Student Government through 1973-74. Early in his term he publicly commended the Board of Regents in the Herald, weeks after the board had increased ASG's budget allotment.

Yater's win was reported in the Herald's 13 April 1973 election returns; the same issue profiled him as making a good impression in his first contact with Congress and stressing a calm approach to the office. Carter Pence's companion article covered new officers being installed at a Congress party the same week, though the archive index does not yet name who besides Yater took office.

In April 1974, stung by election-season criticism, President Yater defended his administration in a letter to the Herald.

His final weeks in office stayed argumentative: the Herald of April 30 carried dueling letters under the heading 'Linda Jones vs. Steven Yater' as the student regent race closed out the year.

The Herald's own retrospective on his term, by Al Cross, ran 16 April 1974 under the headline 'Presidency Ends for Steve Yater; One Major Program Started' - the archive does not yet hold the article's text, only its headline crediting his administration with a single major accomplishment.

The organization

Everyone the record shows in office this year. Each name goes to their own page, with every office they held and everything the archive says they did.

The executive

Administrative Vice PresidentGlenn Jackson

Jackson was administrative vice president in 1973-74 and the yearbook's spokesman for the reorganization bill that abolished ASG's committee system and folded all programs into two areas, student affairs and academic affairs. He said the old system was too rigid and that ideas often fitted no committee, leaving some with nothing to do and others with too much. He also co-ordinated the free university, and cited lack of interest and difficulty finding teachers as its main problem. The Herald reported the same reorganization push in its own coverage that October and January, when Jackson introduced an interaction proposal and then a bill to reorganize ASG.

1974 Talisman, Associated Student Government, p. 56
TreasurerLee Goodpaster

Goodpaster was ASG treasurer in 1973-74 and appears in the same officer list and meeting caption. The yearbook mentions her separately in its elections and homecoming coverage. The same volume's Who's Who feature profiles her as an accounting major who was a member of Alpha Xi Delta sorority and an Alpha Tau Omega Little Sister; the 1975 Talisman further records that she received Alpha Xi Delta's Frances C. Bloker Pledge Trainer Award at the sorority's national convention in Chicago.

1974 Talisman, Associated Student Government, p. 56, and Who's Who, p. 95
SecretaryPam Stewart

Stewart was ASG secretary in 1973-74. She appears in the section's officer list and in a caption of officers conducting a meeting with Yater, LaCivita, Jackson and Goodpaster.

1974 Talisman, Associated Student Government, p. 56
Activities Vice PresidentThomas LaCivita

LaCivita was activities vice president in 1973-74 and again in 1974-75, making him one of the few officers to serve two consecutive years in this decade. The 1974 volume says he was elected in one of the most controversial elections in the school's history and carries a Herald reviewer's criticism of the concert programme; the 1975 volume has President Jeff Consolo crediting him with a more diversified entertainment series on a $42,000 budget. The 1974 Talisman traces the friction behind that review to a dispute between LaCivita and Ron Beck, assistant dean of student affairs: LaCivita said Beck put too little creativity into the entertainment program and did not keep up with the music scene, while Beck said cost, student appeal and any encouragement of drug use were what shaped which acts were booked, adding that he would not book a group he had not heard of; the two reconciled enough during the year to run a program that turned a profit after a slow start.

1974 Talisman, Associated Student Government, p. 56

The 1974 Talisman names Deboe, in its Alpha Phi Alpha page, as a member of the Judicial Council and as elected to Who's Who. A Robert Deboe had been approved to the Rules and Elections Committee at Congress's first meeting of 31 August 1972, reported in the Herald of 1 September 1972; the volumes do not say the two are the same man.

The year, in order35

13 April 1973
#

Yater wins the presidency; new officers installed

The Herald reported Steve Yater's win in the spring election under 'Associated Student Government Election Returns,' profiling him as making a good impression in his first Congress contact and stressing a calm approach. The same issue covered new officers being installed at a Congress party.

Herald 52:50, 13 Apr 1973 PDF

28 August 1973
#

Regents raise the ASG allotment

The Herald opened the year reporting a budget bulge: the Board of Regents voted to increase the Associated Student Government allotment. A companion piece introduced freshmen to ASG under the headline 'Every College Has One.'

Herald 53:1, 28 Aug 1973 PDF

28 August 1973Other
#

An $11,000 budget increase pays for the concert and lecture year

The Board of Regents approved an $11,000 increase in the student affairs budget allocated to ASG in place of a proposed head fee rise. President Steve Yater said the increase was better than a higher head fee because it took no additional money from students' pockets, and the Talisman credited it with paying for the Doobie Brothers, Seals and Crofts and the mini-concerts.

$11,000 increase in the ASG allotment

1974 Talisman, Associated Student Government

September 1973Concert
#

Street dance and Ginger open the year at the amphitheatre

ASG began the year with the traditional street dance during registration week, then followed a few days later with the rock band Ginger in a mini-concert at the outdoor amphitheatre. It was the first outdoor performance at the fine arts amphitheatre and the first of the year's mini-concert series.

1974 Talisman, Entertainment

September 1973Programme
#

Student-Faculty Interaction seminars

ASG set up a series of informative seminars under a Student-Faculty Interaction Program, run with the undergraduate advisement office. The programmes were informal meetings between students and department representatives to discuss possible majors, aimed at students who had not decided.

1974 Talisman, Associated Student Government

6 September 1973Festival
#

Stringbean headlines the second Bluegrass Festival

The second Bluegrass Festival was held on 6 September 1973 with the Hee Haw star Stringbean and other Nashville acts. The Herald reported five days later that a country-western 'git-together' had replaced the bluegrass festival.

1974 Talisman, Entertainment

18 September 1973
#

Free University continues, smaller

ASG's Free University of no-cost, no-credit evening courses continued for another year, though with fewer courses than before.

Herald 53:6, 18 Sep 1973 PDF

25 September 1973
#

Housing survey urges dorm improvements

An ASG housing survey urged improvement in dormitory conditions, part of a fall agenda that also included a committee on part-time student opportunities and a push for paper recycling.

Herald 53:8, 25 Sep 1973 PDF

October 1973Concert
#

Homecoming rock and roll revival: Jerry Lee Lewis cancels and Chuck Berry never shows

ASG's first attempt at a major concert, a 1950s Rock and Roll Revival for Homecoming, collapsed twice. Jerry Lee Lewis was first booked and cancelled over management problems, Chuck Berry was signed in his place and then took an unscheduled holiday in Las Vegas and ignored the date, leaving Gary U.S. Bonds to headline with the Crystals, Freddie Cannon and Lloyd Price. Ron Beck warned the crowd before the show and offered refunds to anyone who left within fifteen minutes, and he and ASG were still trying to recover the refund money from producer Richard Nader months later.

1974 Talisman, Entertainment

October 1973Tradition
#

Red Snapper plays the ASG Homecoming dance

The Homecoming dance sponsored by ASG ran from 8pm to midnight after the game against Morehead, with music by Red Snapper. Homecoming 1973 took its theme from the movies and drew a crowd of 19,500 to the game in the rain.

1974 Talisman, Homecoming

9 October 1973
#

Reginald Glass asks for minority representation

Jerry Elam reported in the Herald of 9 October 1973 that Reginald Glass, the ASG representative from the Graduate College, had asked for better representation for minority groups in student government and on the Academic Council. The 1974 Talisman recorded that Glass said the lack of Black and foreign students in these groups harmed both the minorities and the University, and that he resigned his Congress seat in protest.

Herald 53:12, 9 Oct 1973; 1974 Talisman, Associated Student Government, p. 61

13 October 1973Concert
#

Ethos plays the second mini-concert

The electronic-rock group Ethos from Fort Wayne, Indiana, played ASG's second mini-concert on 13 October 1973 in Van Meter Auditorium. Guitarist Bill Sharpe told the boogie-oriented audience that most of the boogies would have to be in their noses; the band was warmly received and returned in the spring as an off-campus support act.

1974 Talisman, Entertainment

29 October 1973Other
#

A student takes the concert complaints to Congress

Tony Agee, a junior from Albany, brought his and his friends' grievances before the ASG Congress at the meeting of 29 October 1973. He charged that ASG had misrepresented facts, used false advertising and put incompetent people in charge of booking; president Steve Yater blamed the producer of the Homecoming revival.

1974 Talisman, Entertainment

The soul-blues-jazz combo Amnesty played a mini-concert in Van Meter. The Talisman said much of the crowd enjoyed it but that it was the least successful show of the mini-concert series.

The Doobie Brothers play to 6,000

The Doobie Brothers played Diddle Arena on 4 December 1973 before a crowd of 6,000. The group had originally been booked for October and was postponed over a recording commitment; the Herald called the show average but the Talisman said it pleased most of the crowd.

11 December 1973Lecture
#

Edmund Muskie calls for Nixon's impeachment in an ASG lecture

ASG sponsored the lecture Senator Edmund Muskie of Maine gave at Western on 11 December 1973, on the restoration of confidence in government. Muskie called for the impeachment of President Nixon, saying he had decided on the night of the Saturday Massacre that impeachment was the only way the issue should be raised. He cited a Harris poll in which 19 per cent of Americans said they had confidence in the executive branch. The 1974 Talisman lists Muskie with Barry Goldwater and Elliot Richardson among the lectures ASG sponsored that year.

1974 Talisman, pp. 52-55 (Lectures); date from College Heights Herald 53:26, 4 Dec 1973

19 January 1974Concert
#

Seals and Crofts play the first free concert in more than a year

Seals and Crofts opened the spring semester on Saturday 19 January 1974 before what the 1974 Talisman called one of the largest crowds ever to see a show in Diddle Arena. The Talisman called it in some ways the best campus concert in more than a year and the first free concert since the autumn of 1972, scheduling and money having prevented one since.

College Heights Herald 53:32, 22 Jan 1974 PDF

22 January 1974Programme
#

The Free University shuts down after two semesters

The Herald reported that the Free U had shut down after two semesters. Coordinator Glenn Jackson blamed a lack of student interest and the difficulty of finding people to teach the classes, which had covered knitting, photography, yoga and other subjects.

College Heights Herald 53:32, 22 Jan 1974 PDF

25 January 1974
#

Glenn Jackson introduces the reorganization bill

Carl Clayworth reported in the Herald of 25 January 1974 that administrative vice president Glenn Jackson had introduced a bill to reorganize ASG. The 1974 Talisman recorded that the change folded ASG's programmes into student affairs and academic affairs, each under an area chairman, keeping the Activities, Rules and Elections, and Legislative Research Committees, after Jackson said the old system's many specific committees left ideas that fit no area and caused new committees to be constantly formed, with projects placed by the president subject to Congress approval.

Herald 53:33, 25 Jan 1974; 1974 Talisman, p. 60

February 1974Lecture
#

Raymond McNally lectures on Dracula

ASG brought Dr Raymond McNally of Boston College, co-author of 'In Search of Dracula', to lecture on vampirism before a large audience in the Garrett Conference Center Ballroom. He traced the legend to a fifteenth century Romanian prince and showed clips from vampire films.

1974 Talisman, Entertainment and Lectures

5 February 1974Service
#

ASG considers ending the legal loan fund

The Herald reported that ASG might end its legal loan fund. The Legal Rights Committee had been working towards a bail-bond fund for arrested students and had taken up drug expulsions, the refusal to show X-rated films in the student center and the 'Fly' censorship case.

3 March 1974Concert
#

Harry Chapin opens the major-mini concert series

ASG scheduled the folk singer and balladeer Harry Chapin for two shows in Van Meter on 3 March 1974. Activities vice president Tom LaCivita called it the first example of his new concept of major-mini concerts, in which medium-priced performers were brought in, sometimes free and sometimes as a pay event.

Jeff Consolo and Steve Henry won the spring ASG election, in a cycle whose primary the Herald said showed Greek influence. Henry resigned his post over the student discount program after the results.

Sam Ervin analyses Watergate

Senator Sam Ervin, chairman of the Senate Watergate committee, spoke at Western on 2 April 1974 under a contract ASG had signed in February. The Herald reported the lecture on 5 April, and in the same issue judged ASG's 1973-74 lecture series its best effort to date.

Herald calls the lecture series ASG's best effort to date

The Herald judged ASG's 1973-74 lecture series its best effort yet. The series had carried Senator Barry Goldwater, former attorney general Elliot Richardson and Senator Edmund Muskie, and ASG jointly sponsored the psychic Uri Geller with the University Lecture Series.

College Heights Herald 53:50, 5 Apr 1974 PDF

Marshall Tucker Band is upstaged by Freddie King

ASG brought the Marshall Tucker Band to Diddle Arena on Monday 8 April 1974, with Freddie King leading in. The Talisman said King, brought back for encores by a screaming crowd, was the biggest crowd pleaser, while Diddle's acoustics destroyed much of Marshall Tucker's set and the disappointed crowd began drifting towards the doors halfway through.

What this is

A year-by-year record of student government at Western Kentucky University, from the ratification of the Associated Students constitution in April 1966 to the present. Every entry names the source it came from.

Corrections

The names here start with the plaque in the SGA Chambers, and the plaque is known to be wrong in places. Where the archive disagrees with it, this site follows the archive and records the change on the corrections page. If you served, or you know that a year here is wrong, the project wants to hear from you: people who held an office can be given the keys to their own year on the contribute page.

What it rests on

Everything here comes from what is publicly available online: the digitised Herald and SGA's own papers on TopSCHOLAR, the Talisman yearbooks, wkuherald.com, the university's news pages and the Wayback Machine. That is a fraction of what happened. Student government has met most weeks for sixty years, and the greater part of it was never written down, or was written down on paper that has never been scanned. A thin year here is a thin record, not a quiet one.

Reuse

Text on this site is the work of the project. Photographs, documents and legislation are reproduced from Western Kentucky University's own open archives and from the College Heights Herald and the Talisman; each item names the collection, issue and page it came from and links to the original.
