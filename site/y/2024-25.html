Sam Kurtz president, 2024-25

Office
President
Term recorded here
2024-25
Standing of the record
As recorded on the plaque. Not yet corroborated in the archive.
Also on the wall
2023-24

Re-elected unopposed. Warned the Senate repeatedly that an unusually senior chamber meant the 2025 election mattered more than usual.

Kurtz opened the 2024-25 year presiding over the 24th Senate, which passed SGA's $100,000 budget at its first meeting on Aug. 27. Sitting down with the Herald editorial board alongside his executive cabinet, he described SGA as "a vessel for good" and a group of 45 to 50 people advocating for students, and credited President Caboni with keeping the budget untouched for several years.

Through the spring he presided over a run of student-welfare legislation, including the Gordon Wilson Hall elevator resolution and a resolution seeking 24/7 library hours during finals week. In February he announced a POLLapalooza push to raise election participation, telling the Senate, "My goal is that 3,000 students vote in our election."

Kurtz closed his term at the 24th Senate's final meeting on April 15, having served as student body president for two years and holding executive branch positions for most of his college career, and prepared to graduate at the end of the semester. He received a standing ovation after his last report, and his parting words to senators were "Please be for the students."

34 senators this year.

34 senators were elected in the April 2024 general election from a field of over 60 candidates; five more were seated after the fall 2024 special election, plus later appointments including Emaun Riley (ISEC) and Caden Lucas (Mahurin Honors College, the seat's first occupant). The 2024-25 Judicial Council, approved 27 August 2024, was Aziz Umarov, Madison Payne, Neill Kane and Sam Dorris.

The year, in order14

23 April 2024
#

24th Senate installed: Vincent elected speaker, Henderson named chief justice

At the 24th Senate's first meeting, newly elected senators were sworn in and Sarah Vincent, College of Health and Human Services senator, was elected Speaker of the Senate over Sophia Bryant. Senior senator Anne-Marie Wright was appointed Chief of Staff, and Associate Chief Justice Ellen Henderson was appointed Chief Justice. Outgoing Chief Justice Isaac King swore in the new officers, officially opening the 2024-25 term.

Herald, 24 Apr 2024

27 August 2024
#

Committee chairs and Judicial Council approved at fall opener

At the 24th Senate's first fall meeting, the Senate unanimously approved Kurtz's nominations of Aziz Umarov, Madison Payne, Neill Kane and Sam Dorris to the 2024-25 Judicial Council, and confirmed six committee chairs: Hannah Evans (Legislative Operations), Savanna Kurtz (Student Experience and Enrollment), Ryan Dilts (Outreach and Public Relations), Megan Farmer (DEI), Sophia Bryant (Student Mental Health and Wellbeing) and Annie Finch (Community Relations). President Timothy Caboni addressed the Senate on campus building renovations, the Mahurin Honors College restructuring and WKU's push for R2 research status.

Herald, 27 Aug 2024

28 August 2024
#

Senate passed $100,000 budget at first meeting

At its first meeting of the year on Aug. 27, the SGA Senate passed a $100,000 budget for 2024-25, including $20,000 for organizational aid, $23,500 for scholarships and $10,705 in legislative discretionary funding. Executive leaders told the Herald editorial board they intended to spend the full allocation, and President Sam Kurtz credited WKU President Caboni with keeping the budget level for several years.

Herald, 28 Aug 2024

2 October 2024
#

Fall election winners sworn in, school safety bill passed

Five senators elected in the fall election, three freshman senators plus an international and a Gatton Academy senator, were sworn in at the Oct. 1 meeting. The Senate passed three bills, including Bill 4-24-F to make school safety training mandatory for WKU professors and require posted emergency exits in classrooms, and two bills backing a Spooktober fundraiser for the Potter Children's Home Initiative.

Herald, 2 Oct 2024

22 October 2024
#

Senate backs Myanmar flag, funds organizational aid and calculators

The Senate unanimously passed Resolution 5-24-F backing a Myanmar flag on the empty pole outside the Mahurin Honors College, sought by senators after students said Burmese and Chin students had no flag representing them; Bill 9-24-F distributed $10,000 in fall Organizational Aid funding across 58 graded applications; and Bill 10-24-F added $540 to buy five more calculators for the Borrow-a-Calculator program, which had lent 28 calculators that semester. Kurtz appointed freshman Emaun Riley as ISEC senator and Mahurin Honors College senator Caden Lucas as the Senate's parliamentarian.

Herald, 23 Oct 2024

7 November 2024
#

Three senators removed for excessive absences

Senators Malick Ibrahim, Myricle Gholston and Karisha Petty were removed from their seats at a Nov. 6 censure meeting after each accumulated at least six unexcused absences from weekly senate meetings. The Judicial Council voted 7-0 to remove them under a constitutional provision requiring removal of any senator censured twice, calling the decision a matter of accountability rather than an attack on character.

Herald, 7 Nov 2024

5 February 2025
#

Trump DEI order prompted constitutional review

Chief Justice Morgan Gammons said the SGA Constitution might require amendment to its diversity, equity and inclusion provisions after President Trump's executive order directing federal agencies to end DEI mandates. Gammons said the Judicial Council would meet to decide whether an amendment was needed, noting that WKU receives federal funding as a public university. The Senate also filled several appointed positions at the same meeting.

Herald, 5 Feb 2025

11 February 2025
#

A mental health survey, and a warning about the election

The Senate passed Bill 4-25-S funding a DEI Committee tabling event to gather anonymous mental health survey responses. Dean of Students Martha Sales told the Senate 'you cannot isolate and educate at the same time'. Kurtz warned repeatedly that an unusually senior chamber made the spring election matter more than usual.

Herald, Feb 2025

26 February 2025
#

Resolution backed Gordon Wilson Hall elevator

The Senate passed Resolution 2-25-S supporting installation of elevators in Gordon Wilson Hall, prompted by wheelchair user Kiersten Washington, whose dance appreciation class was scheduled on the building's third floor. It also passed Resolution 1-25-S supporting 24/7 hours at The Commons at Helm Library during finals week, and Kurtz announced a POLLapalooza effort with a goal of 3,000 voters in the April election.

Herald, 26 Feb 2025

26 March 2025
#

Resolution supported mental health advisory board

The Senate passed Resolution 4-25-S supporting the formation of a Mental Health and Suicide Prevention Advisory Board to advise upper-level administration on student mental health policy, with Dean of Students Martha Sales planned to oversee its creation. Co-author Emma Harris, the Resident Staff Association president, said Counseling Center staffing shortages had pushed students to rely on resident advisors. The Senate passed eight bills at the same meeting.

Herald, 26 Mar 2025

4 April 2025
#

Senate voted to rename DEI Committee

Senators unanimously passed Bill 21-25-S, a constitutional amendment restructuring the Diversity, Equity and Inclusion Committee and renaming it the Action and Opportunity Committee, following President Trump's executive order dismantling DEI mandates. The meeting initially lacked the 21 members needed for a two-thirds vote, so absent members were called onto Zoom to reach quorum. The amendment then went to a student referendum during the April 13-15 election.

Herald, 4 Apr 2025

15 April 2025
#

Rush Robinson elected on a turnout of 966

Elected alongside class, college, transfer, first-generation, Intercultural Student Engagement Center and Honors College senators - constituencies the 1966 constitution had no concept of.

WKU News

16 April 2025
#

Students ratified DEI amendment with 88% of vote

In the spring election, the student body approved Bill 21-25-S, the constitutional amendment renaming the DEI Committee to the Action and Opportunity Committee, with 88% of votes in favor. Committee chair Megan Farmer said the committee at its core was not changing and the rename was meant to better communicate its actions. Results for 31 senate seats were announced the same night.

Herald, 16 Apr 2025

16 April 2025
#

24th Senate held final meeting

The 24th Senate met for the last time on April 15 with no legislation on the agenda, spending the meeting on farewells. President Sam Kurtz, Vice President Donte Reed and Chief Financial Officer Ethan Taylor each received a standing ovation, and the Senate recorded 295 spring scholarship applications, far above the roughly 40-applicant average of past years.

Herald, 16 Apr 2025

What this is

A year-by-year record of student government at Western Kentucky University, from the ratification of the Associated Students constitution in April 1966 to the present. Every entry names the source it came from.

Corrections

The names here start with the plaque in the SGA Chambers, and the plaque is known to be wrong in places. Where the archive disagrees with it, this site follows the archive and records the change on the corrections page. If you served, or you know that a year here is wrong, the project wants to hear from you through the Student Government Association office.

Text on this site is the work of the project. Photographs, documents and legislation are reproduced from Western Kentucky University's own open archives and from the College Heights Herald and the Talisman; each item names the collection, issue and page it came from and links to the original.
