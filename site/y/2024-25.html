2023-24

Re-elected unopposed. Warned the Senate repeatedly that an unusually senior chamber meant the 2025 election mattered more than usual.

Kurtz opened the 2024-25 year presiding over the 24th Senate, which passed SGA's $100,000 budget at its first meeting on Aug. 27. Sitting down with the Herald editorial board alongside his executive cabinet, he described SGA as "a vessel for good" and a group of 45 to 50 people advocating for students, and credited President Caboni with keeping the budget untouched for several years.

Through the spring he presided over a run of student-welfare legislation, including the Gordon Wilson Hall elevator resolution and a resolution seeking 24/7 library hours during finals week. In February he announced a POLLapalooza push to raise election participation, telling the Senate, "My goal is that 3,000 students vote in our election."

Kurtz closed his term at the 24th Senate's final meeting on April 15, having served as student body president for two years and holding executive branch positions for most of his college career, and prepared to graduate at the end of the semester. He received a standing ovation after his last report, and his parting words to senators were "Please be for the students."

23 April 2024
#

24th Senate installed: Vincent elected speaker, Henderson named chief justice

At the 24th Senate's first meeting, newly elected senators were sworn in and Sarah Vincent, College of Health and Human Services senator, was elected Speaker of the Senate over Sophia Bryant. Senior senator Anne-Marie Wright was appointed Chief of Staff, and Associate Chief Justice Ellen Henderson was appointed Chief Justice. Outgoing Chief Justice Isaac King swore in the new officers, officially opening the 2024-25 term.

Herald, 24 Apr 2024

8 August 2024
#

Student regent presses administrators over student worker funding

At the Board of Regents retreat, Student Regent Sam Kurtz said a friend working in the biotechnology center had been told some 2023 funding for student jobs would not return in 2024. Kurtz raised the concern during a budget model presentation as units across WKU were being ordered to cut 7.5% of their approved spending and absorb a 10% overhead charge on self-generated revenue. He asked the room, "Isn't that the point of why we're all here, to look out for the students?" and afterwards told the Herald the answers he got were cookie-cutter.

Herald, 8 Aug 2024

27 August 2024
#

Committee chairs and Judicial Council approved at fall opener

At the 24th Senate's first fall meeting, the Senate unanimously approved Kurtz's nominations of Aziz Umarov, Madison Payne, Neill Kane and Sam Dorris to the 2024-25 Judicial Council, and confirmed six committee chairs: Hannah Evans (Legislative Operations), Savanna Kurtz (Student Experience and Enrollment), Ryan Dilts (Outreach and Public Relations), Megan Farmer (DEI), Sophia Bryant (Student Mental Health and Wellbeing) and Annie Finch (Community Relations). President Timothy Caboni addressed the Senate on campus building renovations, the Mahurin Honors College restructuring and WKU's push for R2 research status.

Herald, 27 Aug 2024

27 August 2024
#

24th Senate approves $100,000 budget and seats first Honors senator

The Senate unanimously approved Chief Financial Officer Ethan Taylor's $100,000 budget, which raised legislative discretionary and office worker spending and cut public relations spending. Five senators were appointed, including Caden Lucas as the first Mahurin Honors College senator, along with a four-member Judicial Council. Caboni gave the university report and closed on meal plans, saying orange chicken had become a meal swipe.

Herald, 27 Aug 2024

27 August 2024
#

Executives break down the SGA budget for the Herald editorial board

Taylor told the Herald Editorial Board the 2024-25 budget included $20,000 for organizational aid, $23,500 for scholarships and $10,705 in legislative discretionary funding. Kurtz said the total had stayed at $100,000 for several years and credited Caboni with keeping it untouched. Chief Justice Ellen Henderson said the Senate would vote on election codes at its Sept. 3 meeting and stressed the Judicial Council's independence from the Senate and cabinet.

Herald, 27 Aug 2024

28 August 2024
#

Senate passed $100,000 budget at first meeting

At its first meeting of the year on Aug. 27, the SGA Senate passed a $100,000 budget for 2024-25, including $20,000 for organizational aid, $23,500 for scholarships and $10,705 in legislative discretionary funding. Executive leaders told the Herald editorial board they intended to spend the full allocation, and President Sam Kurtz credited WKU President Caboni with keeping the budget level for several years.

3 September 2024
#

Senate passes bill declaring SGA nonpartisan

Bill 50-23-S reaffirmed that SGA represents the student body rather than acting as a political group and stated its duty to put forward only nonpartisan legislation. It passed unanimously. The same meeting named Rush Robinson chair of the Campus Improvements and Sustainability Committee, swore in Hadley Whipple as Secretary of the Senate and added Hailey Stapleton to the Judicial Council.

Borrow-A-Calculator went live

Chief of Staff Anne-Marie Wright told the senate on 3 September 2024 that the Borrow-A-Calculator programme had started, run with the Center for Innovative Teaching and Learning, the Department of Mathematics and Ogden College. Students could borrow a new TI-84 graphing calculator free for one week and renew it, on presentation of a valid student ID.

10 September 2024
#

Vice president pushes for an on-campus polling place

Student Body Vice President Donte Reed told the Senate he was working with a student advocacy group to get a polling center on campus, saying he otherwise had to drive about three hours home to vote. Kurtz said SGA should find ways to calm tensions after the November election and remind students where to get help. Dean of Students Martha Sales spoke on stress and belonging and told senators to check on their strong friends.

17 September 2024
#

Fall ballot draws 20 freshman candidates and none for graduate seat

Chief Justice Ellen Henderson reported that 20 students were running for freshman senator, one for Gatton Academy senator, one for international senator and none for graduate senator, with voting set for Sept. 26 and 27 on TopNet. The Senate also passed Resolutions 1-24-F and 2-24-F backing a semester-long fundraising drive to raise $2,000 for Potter Children's Home, beginning with a Jump Air Zone event where 40% of proceeds would go to the fund. Both resolutions were written by sophomore senator Jade Ismail and passed unanimously, committing SGA to four fundraisers, one in September, two in October and one in December, each carrying a QR code pointing students at a GoFundMe. Resolution 2-24-F set out Soarin' in September at Jump Air Zone on Wednesday 25 September from 8 to 10 p.m., a $10 entry covering the two hours and socks, with every participant signing a liability waiver.

October 2024
#

Bill seeks mandatory school safety training for professors

Bill 4-24-F, authored by Senator Annalise Finch, called for school safety training to be made mandatory for all WKU professors and for emergency exits to be posted in every instructional classroom. Finch noted the training was not then mandatory and that K-12 rules do not transfer directly to college. Professors would be able to opt out for mental health reasons. The bill passed, and five senators elected in the fall race were sworn in.

Herald, 1 Oct 2024

The second Pink Walk drew survivors to South Lawn

SGA's second annual Pink Walk was held on the evening of 17 October 2024, created and hosted by Vice President Donte Reed with WKU Panhellenic, the Interfraternity Council and the National Pan-Hellenic Council, raising money for the Susan G. Komen Breast Cancer Foundation. Students gathered at the Big Red statue in Downing Student Union and walked along Centennial Mall to South Lawn, where SGA served burgers, snacks and drinks. Breast cancer survivors Michelle Denney and Robbin Taylor spoke, along with Tate Sine, whose mother Tonya had spoken at the previous year's walk after her diagnosis in September 2021.

First Topper Chef cooking class

SGA held the first Topper Chef cooking class on 4 November 2024 in Academic Complex room 202, where students cooked pork and vegetable tacos, beans, elotes and guacamole from 4 p.m. and ate together until 7:30. Senior Jenna Wells pitched it from her seat on the Student Mental Health and Well-being Committee, saying students needed to look after themselves outside the classroom and that basic cooking and cooking safety were useful skills. 6 November 2024
#

Three senators removed from the Senate after second censure

The Judicial Council voted 7-0 to remove Malick Ibrahim, Myricle Gholston and Karisha Petty for excessive unexcused absences, each having accumulated at least six. Section 2.2 of the bylaws allows three unexcused absences a semester, and Section 4.6.12 of the constitution requires removal after a second censure; all three had already been censured once and given extra office hours. The council's minutes said senators cannot represent students they are not present to serve. Petty later told the Herald her absences were due to medical issues.

Herald, 6 Nov 2024

Three senators removed for excessive absences

Senators Malick Ibrahim, Myricle Gholston and Karisha Petty were removed from their seats at a Nov. 6 censure meeting after each accumulated at least six unexcused absences from weekly senate meetings. The Judicial Council voted 7-0 to remove them under a constitutional provision requiring removal of any senator censured twice, calling the decision a matter of accountability rather than an attack on character.

Scholarship created in memory of Brent Chumbley

4 April 2025
#

Senate renamed the DEI Committee the Action and Opportunity Committee

Senators unanimously passed Bill 21-25-S, a constitutional amendment restructuring the Diversity, Equity and Inclusion Committee as the Action and Opportunity Committee and retitling Chief Diversity Officers as Chief Engagement and Opportunity Officers, following President Trump's executive order dismantling DEI mandates. Rush Robinson moved to table the bill to the fall, but senators chose to vote; with fewer than the 21 members needed for a two-thirds vote present, absent members were called onto Zoom to reach quorum. Co-author Gammons called the change mostly a formality and said a loss of federal funding had been alluded to as a possible issue. The amendment then went to a student referendum during the 13-15 April election.

SGA Pollooza put a student music festival on South Lawn

SGA staged Pollooza, an all-day music festival on South Lawn, on Sunday 13 April 2025, with food trucks, tailgating and a bill of performers drawn entirely from current WKU students. What this is

A year-by-year record of student government at Western Kentucky University, from the ratification of the Associated Students constitution in April 1966 to the present. Every entry names the source it came from.

Corrections

The names here start with the plaque in the SGA Chambers, and the plaque is known to be wrong in places. Where the archive disagrees with it, this site follows the archive and records the change on the corrections page. If you served, or you know that a year here is wrong, the project wants to hear from you: people who held an office can be given the keys to their own year on the contribute page.

What it rests on

Everything here comes from what is publicly available online: the digitised Herald and SGA's own papers on TopSCHOLAR, the Talisman yearbooks, wkuherald.com, the university's news pages and the Wayback Machine. That is a fraction of what happened. Student government has met most weeks for sixty years, and the greater part of it was never written down, or was written down on paper that has never been scanned. A thin year here is a thin record, not a quiet one.

Text on this site is the work of the project. Photographs, documents and legislation are reproduced from Western Kentucky University's own open archives and from the College Heights Herald and the Talisman; each item names the collection, issue and page it came from and links to the original.
