Steven Fuller president and student regent, 1980-81

Office
President and student regent
Term recorded here
1980-81
Place in the line
the 15th president and the 13th student regent
Standing of the record
As recorded on the plaque. Not yet corroborated in the archive.

Herald bylines consistently call him "Steve Fuller"; the plaque and this site list "Steven Fuller" - spelling unverified.

Steve Fuller entered the April 1980 Associated Student Government presidential race against David Rue and Tim Irons; the Herald covered ASG hopefuls debating the issues at an April 3 candidates' forum before reporting the three-way field on April 10.

Fuller won the presidency in the April 24 election, reported under the headline "Persistence Pays Off in Steve Fuller Campaign." Mark Wilson and Greg Zoeller were elected to other top ASG offices the same day; Tim Irons's loss was covered separately.

Fuller's fall term was consumed by a running fight over dormitory room inspections. A September 4 inspection measure was voted down amid criticism of how the vote was conducted; a September 11 bill calling for a ban on bugs in the dorms drew a mocking editorial cartoon and an editorial calling the university's protective inspection policy unnecessary; and by November 13 the Herald reported ASG had nearly slept through its own vote before re-endorsing inspections, followed a week later by a bill proposing a stricter room-entry rule.

In office, ASG under Fuller also proposed a change to the organization's own title in October 1980 and asked the state to evaluate higher education funding in February 1981, as budget worries dominated the legislative session. His successor, Marcel Bush, was elected that April.

Fuller entered the Associated Student Government presidential race in the spring of 1980 as one of three candidates. Michele Wood's Herald report was headlined "3 Running for Associated Student Government President," naming Steve Fuller, David Rue and Tim Irons.

During the 1980-81 year the Associated Student Government pressed the state on the condition of higher education, with Robert Pillow reporting in February 1981 that the body had asked the state to conduct an evaluation. The request came as the campus argued over budget cuts, including cuts to athletics.

The year, in order39

1980
#

The ASG president also held the student regent seat

The 1980 Talisman's account of the Board of Regents records that ASG president Steve Fuller was also sworn in as student regent, in the same passage reporting the board's budget, two regent appointments and $66,000 of university money for renovating the president's home. The two offices were separated during the next session: after Marcel Bush resigned in January 1982 the seat went to a campus-wide election, and Sandra Norfleet won the runoff in February 1982.

1980 Talisman, Board of Regents

3 April 1980
#

ASG hopefuls debate the issues; ASG weighs opposing a UCB change

Associated Student Government presidential hopefuls discussed the issues and courted votes at a candidates' forum ahead of the spring election. The same issue reported ASG considering opposition to a proposed change to the University Center Board's structure.

Herald 55:49, 3 Apr 1980 PDF

10 April 1980
#

Three seek the ASG presidency

Steve Fuller, David Rue and Tim Irons entered the race for Associated Student Government president.

Herald 55:51, 10 Apr 1980 PDF

24 April 1980
#

Fuller elected ASG president

Steve Fuller won the Associated Student Government presidency over David Rue and Tim Irons. Mark Wilson and Greg Zoeller were elected to other top ASG offices the same day.

Herald 55:55, 24 Apr 1980 PDF

26 August 1980
#

Fuller opened the year wanting a stronger voice in policy

Diane Comer reported in the Herald's back-to-school issue that the Associated Student Government wanted a stronger voice in policy making. It was the opening statement of Steve Fuller's term, and within a fortnight the congress was in the middle of the room inspection fight that ran to Christmas.

College Heights Herald 56:1, 26 August 1980 PDF

4 September 1980
#

ASG room inspection measure defeated amid criticism

A proposed ASG measure on dormitory room inspections was voted down, in a session a separate Herald article criticized for how the vote was conducted. The defeat opened a fall-long fight over dorm inspection policy.

Herald 56:3, 4 Sep 1980 PDF

11 September 1980
#

ASG bill targets pests in the dorms

ASG introduced a bill calling for a ban on bugs in residence halls; the Herald's editorial cartoon depicted a cockroach imperiled by the resolution. The paper's editorial page separately argued the university's protective inspection policy was unnecessary.

Herald 56:5, 11 Sep 1980 PDF

16 September 1980
#

ASG asked for longer check cashing hours

Resolution 80-6, dated 16 September 1980, requested extended hours for the university's check cashing service. It was filed the same night as Resolution 80-5 on open house visitor lists, at the start of a session whose legislation was dominated by residence hall questions.

SGA Resolution 80-6, Check Cashing

18 September 1980
#

A survey found students uninformed about ASG

Diane Comer reported the student opinion poll committee's first survey under the line that students were uninformed about the Associated Student Government. The Talisman gives the numbers: 29 per cent said they would recognise ASG president Steve Fuller, 31 per cent said they would recognise university centre director Ron Beck, and 28 per cent thought Beck was the ASG president. Seventy-four per cent said they would recognise university president Donald Zacharias. Committee chairman Kevin Kinne said he was disappointed so few could tell Fuller and Beck apart and more surprised that a quarter did not know Zacharias.

1981 Talisman, pp. 282-283, "A year of resolutions"

18 September 1980
#

ASG objected to how residence hall budget cuts were shared out

Resolution 80-7, dated 18 September 1980, concerned what ASG called the inequity of the cuts made to residence hall budgets. Money was tight across the university that autumn, and the congress returned to the theme in April 1981 with Resolution 81-1, recording its dissatisfaction with budget cuts generally.

SGA Resolution 80-7, Budget Cutback

22 September 1980
#

ASG made its own officers keep office hours

Resolution 80-9, dated 22 September 1980, required ASG officers to hold office hours and to report their availability. It is one of the few pieces of legislation from the session aimed at the organisation itself rather than at the university.

SGA Resolution 80-9, Officer Availability Reports

25 September 1980
#

Dean Keown criticised a resolution and members walked out before the vote

Diane Comer reported that Charles Keown, the dean of student affairs, criticised an ASG resolution and that members left the meeting before the vote was taken. The same issue reported that advance registration would be extended to all students. Keown stayed a critic of ASG's internal changes and publicly questioned the organisation's constitutional revisions again in March 1983.

College Heights Herald 56:9, 25 September 1980 PDF

2 October 1980
#

Low attendance at its own meetings disappointed the president

Diane Comer reported that the Associated Student Government's low attendance had disappointed president Steve Fuller. The same issue reported that students would grade faculty performance and carried a piece on how the room inspections left hall directors in the middle.

College Heights Herald 56:11, 2 October 1980 PDF

9 October 1980
#

ASG refused to destroy the open house visitor list

Diane Comer reported that ASG rejected a bill to destroy the open house list. The congress had filed Resolution 80-5 on open house visitor lists three weeks earlier, and returned to visitor lists again in Resolution 80-30 the following March. The privacy of those lists sat alongside the room inspection fight as the year's running argument about what the university could record about students.

College Heights Herald 56:13, 9 October 1980 PDF

14 October 1980
#

A joint resolution with Interhall Council on restroom stall doors

Resolution 80-10, dated 14 October 1980, was a joint resolution of the Interhall Council and ASG asking for stall doors to be installed in residence hall restrooms. Joint work with Interhall Council ran on through the decade, from this to the shared coed housing poll of September 1982.

SGA Resolution 80-10, IHC-ASG Resolution

23 October 1980
#

ASG endorsed a rape prevention measure

Diane Comer reported that the Associated Student Government had endorsed a rape prevention measure. Ten days earlier the congress had filed Resolution 80-11 under the title Coed Security. The same issue reported that the dormitory inspections would be argued in court and carried an editorial saying inspections should stop until a ruling came.

College Heights Herald 56:17, 23 October 1980 PDF

30 October 1980
#

ASG proposes a title change

The Associated Student Government proposed a title change for the organization. The proposal surfaced in the same week campus activities were limited for the national election day.

Herald 56:19, 30 Oct 1980 PDF

10 November 1980Festival
#

ASG's International Week

Associated Student Government sponsored International Week, which ran nightly from Monday 10 November 1980 and closed with a presentation on Brazil and Colombia on the Thursday evening. Two or three countries were featured each night through films, lectures, folk dances and slides given by students and faculty from those countries, and programme chairman Osama Sharif opened the week with a talk on Palestine and a Red Cross film on Palestinian children. The 1981 Talisman, which spells him Sharif and calls him international students committee chairman, credits the week to student government and the International Students Committee, places the nightly programmes in the university center, and lists nine countries: Switzerland, Palestine, Iceland, Malaysia, Colombia, Brazil, India, Pakistan and France.

College Heights Herald 56:22, 13 Nov 1980 PDF1981 Talisman, "A year of resolutions", pp. 282-83

13 November 1980
#

ASG re-endorses dorm inspections after nearly missing the vote

ASG voted to re-endorse dormitory room inspections, in a session the Herald reported the government had nearly slept through. The same issue covered ASG's sponsorship of an international film and dance series and an attorney's interest in a dorm inspection case.

Herald 56:22, 13 Nov 1980 PDF

18 November 1980
#

ASG asked for the Downing grill to open on Sundays

Resolution 80-19, dated 18 November 1980, requested that the Downing University Center grill open on Sundays and holidays. It was filed the same night as Resolution 80-18 on advanced registration.

SGA Resolution 80-19, Downing University Center Grill & Cafeteria

20 November 1980
#

ASG bill proposes a stricter room-entry rule

ASG introduced a bill proposing a stricter rule governing staff entry into student rooms, continuing the fall's running fight over dormitory inspections.

Herald 56:24, 20 Nov 1980 PDF

25 November 1980
#

The Herald said inactivity had improved ASG's image

The Herald's editorial page argued that the Associated Student Government's image was improved by inactivity, and ran a cartoon of an ASG clown in the same issue. A week later Marcel Bush, then a member and the following year's president, wrote in to defend the organisation.

College Heights Herald 56:25, 25 November 1980 PDF

4 December 1980
#

ASG approved the stricter room entry rules

Diane Comer reported that the Associated Student Government had approved stricter rules for entering student rooms, a fortnight after the bill was introduced. The bill set out more standardised procedures for inspections and maintenance than the Hilltopics handbook gave, and was ASG's answer to a semester in which it had twice refused to call for the inspections to be abolished. The same issue carried a letter from member Lonnie Sears saying ASG wanted feedback.

College Heights Herald 56:27, 4 December 1980 PDF

1981Festival
#

International Food Fest and fashion show

In the spring semester ASG followed International Week with an International Food Fest and fashion show. International students committee chairman Osama Sharif, a junior from Jerusalem, said attendance at the autumn programmes had been very good and that they were well put together. The Talisman called International Week the highlight of ASG's autumn semester.

Talisman 1981, "A year of resolutions"

1981Service
#

ASG's car pool match-up

ASG ran a car pool match-up scheme jointly with the Barren River Area Development District and hoped to continue it. Almost 450 students took part across the two semesters. It was one of the few concrete services the organisation carried through in a year the Talisman headlined as a year of resolutions.

almost 450 students enrolled

Talisman 1981, "A year of resolutions"

1981
#

ASG petitioned the governor over cuts to higher education

In the spring ASG circulated a petition asking state government to weigh the importance of higher education in Kentucky's future before making further cuts, and sent it to Governor John Y. Brown, finance secretary George Atkins and the Council on Higher Education. The petition argued that a quality education required financial support for faculty and facilities. President Steve Fuller said many students did not realise how serious the cuts were, and that they meant dismissing faculty. The Talisman dates the petition only to the spring.

1981 Talisman, pp. 282-283, "A year of resolutions"

22 January 1981
#

ASG passed a resolution backing President Zacharias

The Herald reported an Associated Student Government resolution backing the university president, in an issue reporting Donald Zacharias meeting a state panel over money and a student sharing his budget worries. The paper reported on 10 February that students would sponsor a rally to back Zack, and on 12 February that about 200 people had gathered for it. The Herald's index does not say ASG organised the rally, only that it had passed the resolution.

about 200 at the rally two weeks later

College Heights Herald 56:32, 22 January 1981 PDF

5 February 1981
#

ASG asked the state to evaluate higher education

The Associated Student Government called on the state to carry out an evaluation of higher education. Robert Pillow reported the request in the Herald of 5 February 1981, an issue dominated by budget pressure across Kentucky's universities and by controversy over cuts to the athletic budget.

Herald 56:36, 5 Feb 1981 PDF

12 February 1981
#

ASG put off action on the open house bill

Robert Pillow reported that the Associated Student Government had postponed action on the open house bill. A week later he reported the congress conducting a survey of its own on inspections, and by 26 February committee absentees were delaying ASG action altogether.

College Heights Herald 56:38, 12 February 1981 PDF

10 March 1981
#

ASG asked for video game machines in the residence halls

Resolution 80-28, dated 10 March 1981, requested that electronic video game machines be placed in residence halls. It belongs to a run of small quality-of-life measures that spring, alongside resolutions on ice machines, change machines and painting.

SGA Resolution 80-28, Electronic Games

24 March 1981
#

Resolution 80-32 asks for change machines in the dorms

ASG passed Resolution 80-32, requesting that change machines be installed in residence halls.

SGA Resolution 80-32, Change Machines

26 March 1981
#

Only one polling place was opened for the ASG election

Robert Pillow reported that one poll would be open for the Associated Student Government elections. Five days later he reported four candidates getting a surprise, and the Herald's editorial page ran a piece saying few candidates would have to sweat out the result. After the vote Pillow wrote a column headed Who Cares, arguing the election would win no award for organisation.

College Heights Herald 56:48, 26 March 1981 PDF

31 March 1981
#

Off-campus representatives given a role in landlord disputes

Resolution 80-34, dated 31 March 1981, set out the duties of ASG's off-campus housing representatives in landlord and tenant disputes. It followed the previous session's Resolution 79-16, which had put ASG behind the Uniform Landlord Tenant Reform Act.

SGA Resolution 80-34, Landlord & Tenant Disputes

2 April 1981
#

Sturgeon and Bush win the ASG primary

David Sturgeon and Marcel Bush won the Associated Student Government primary, in a campaign the Herald said saw candidates playing big time politics. The same issue carried a resolution asking for a new ASG logo.

Herald 56:50, 2 Apr 1981 PDF

9 April 1981
#

Bush defeats Sturgeon for the presidency

David Sturgeon was defeated in the Associated Student Government race, leaving Marcel Bush the winner after endorsements had split between the two. The congress also voted to keep the present ASG logo.

Herald 56:52, 9 Apr 1981 PDF

14 April 1981
#

Close races prompt ASG recounts

Close races prompted recounts in the Associated Student Government election. The recounts came in the same week the Council on Higher Education approved a tuition increase.

Herald 56:53, 14 Apr 1981 PDF

16 April 1981
#

The new administration was sworn in as the Herald counted the cost

The Herald reported the new Associated Student Government administration sworn in, alongside Robert Pillow's column headed Who Cares, which argued the election would not win an award for organisation, and a Bloss editorial cartoon on ASG. Marcel Bush had beaten David Sturgeon the week before, after recounts in the closest races.

College Heights Herald 56:54, 16 April 1981 PDF

21 April 1981
#

ASG recorded its dissatisfaction with budget cuts

Resolution 81-1, dated 21 April 1981, expressed ASG's dissatisfaction with budget cuts. It was filed with Resolution 81-2 on traffic tickets in the last days of the session, and it opens the numbering of a session that spent much of the following autumn on the state's cuts to higher education.

SGA Resolution 81-1, Budget Cuts

23 April 1981
#

ASG changes its mind on changing minds

The Herald reported that the Associated Student Government changed its mind on changing minds, capping a contentious election month. The same issue covered a withdrawn regent selection and the board's study of reorganizing the university.

Herald 56:56, 23 Apr 1981 PDF

What this is

A year-by-year record of student government at Western Kentucky University, from the ratification of the Associated Students constitution in April 1966 to the present. Every entry names the source it came from.

Corrections

The names here start with the plaque in the SGA Chambers, and the plaque is known to be wrong in places. Where the archive disagrees with it, this site follows the archive and records the change on the corrections page. If you served, or you know that a year here is wrong, the project wants to hear from you: people who held an office can be given the keys to their own year on the contribute page.

What it rests on

Everything here comes from what is publicly available online: the digitised Herald and SGA's own papers on TopSCHOLAR, the Talisman yearbooks, wkuherald.com, the university's news pages and the Wayback Machine. That is a fraction of what happened. Student government has met most weeks for sixty years, and the greater part of it was never written down, or was written down on paper that has never been scanned. A thin year here is a thin record, not a quiet one.

Reuse

Text on this site is the work of the project. Photographs, documents and legislation are reproduced from Western Kentucky University's own open archives and from the College Heights Herald and the Talisman; each item names the collection, issue and page it came from and links to the original.
