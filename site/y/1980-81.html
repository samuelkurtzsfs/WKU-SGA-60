Steven Fuller president and student regent, 1980-81

Office
President and student regent
Term recorded here
1980-81
Standing of the record
As recorded on the plaque. Not yet corroborated in the archive.

Herald bylines consistently call him "Steve Fuller"; the plaque and this site list "Steven Fuller" - spelling unverified.

Steve Fuller entered the April 1980 Associated Student Government presidential race against David Rue and Tim Irons; the Herald covered ASG hopefuls debating the issues at an April 3 candidates' forum before reporting the three-way field on April 10.

Fuller won the presidency in the April 24 election, reported under the headline "Persistence Pays Off in Steve Fuller Campaign." Mark Wilson and Greg Zoeller were elected to other top ASG offices the same day; Tim Irons's loss was covered separately.

Fuller's fall term was consumed by a running fight over dormitory room inspections. A September 4 inspection measure was voted down amid criticism of how the vote was conducted; a September 11 bill calling for a ban on bugs in the dorms drew a mocking editorial cartoon and an editorial calling the university's protective inspection policy unnecessary; and by November 13 the Herald reported ASG had nearly slept through its own vote before re-endorsing inspections, followed a week later by a bill proposing a stricter room-entry rule.

In office, ASG under Fuller also proposed a change to the organization's own title in October 1980 and asked the state to evaluate higher education funding in February 1981, as budget worries dominated the legislative session. His successor, Marcel Bush, was elected that April.

Fuller entered the Associated Student Government presidential race in the spring of 1980 as one of three candidates. Michele Wood's Herald report was headlined "3 Running for Associated Student Government President," naming Steve Fuller, David Rue and Tim Irons.

During the 1980-81 year the Associated Student Government pressed the state on the condition of higher education, with Robert Pillow reporting in February 1981 that the body had asked the state to conduct an evaluation. The request came as the campus argued over budget cuts, including cuts to athletics.

#

ASG room inspection measure defeated amid criticism

A proposed ASG measure on dormitory room inspections was voted down, in a session a separate Herald article criticized for how the vote was conducted. The defeat opened a fall-long fight over dorm inspection policy.

11 September 1980
#

ASG bill targets pests in the dorms

ASG introduced a bill calling for a ban on bugs in residence halls; the Herald's editorial cartoon depicted a cockroach imperiled by the resolution. The paper's editorial page separately argued the university's protective inspection policy was unnecessary.

The Associated Student Government proposed a title change for the organization. The proposal surfaced in the same week campus activities were limited for the national election day.

ASG voted to re-endorse dormitory room inspections, in a session the Herald reported the government had nearly slept through. The same issue covered ASG's sponsorship of an international film and dance series and an attorney's interest in a dorm inspection case.

David Sturgeon was defeated in the Associated Student Government race, leaving Marcel Bush the winner after endorsements had split between the two. The congress also voted to keep the present ASG logo.

What this is

A year-by-year record of student government at Western Kentucky University, from the ratification of the Associated Students constitution in April 1966 to the present. Every entry names the source it came from.

Corrections

The names here start with the plaque in the SGA Chambers, and the plaque is known to be wrong in places. Where the archive disagrees with it, this site follows the archive and records the change on the corrections page. If you served, or you know that a year here is wrong, the project wants to hear from you through the Student Government Association office.

Reuse

Text on this site is the work of the project. Photographs, documents and legislation are reproduced from Western Kentucky University's own open archives and from the College Heights Herald and the Talisman; each item names the collection, issue and page it came from and links to the original.
