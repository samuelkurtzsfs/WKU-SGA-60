Heather E. Falmlen president and student regent, 1991-92

Office
President and student regent
Term recorded here
1991-92
Place in the line
the 25th president and the 23rd student regent
Standing of the record
As recorded on the plaque. Not yet corroborated in the archive.

Elected Associated Student Government president in results the Herald reported 11 April 1991 amid election protests and a rival group's call to boycott the vote. WKU's own "On Campus" newsletter confirmed on 22 May 1991 that she held both the presidency and the student regent seat, consistent with other presidents of this period. Presided over the vote to rename the Associated Students as the Student Government Association: passed 7 April 1992, ratified by referendum 14 April, effective that autumn.

Falmlen won the Associated Student Government presidency in results the Herald reported on 11 April 1991 under the headline "Heather Falmlen Wins Amid Associated Student Government Election Protests." A Herald "Meet the Candidates" feature on 2 April had previewed four contenders for the race: Falmlen, Rob Evans, Van Hodge, and Dan Knowles. The same 11 April issue reported a rival group urging students to boycott the vote, an editorial calling the contest's "immature antics" a disappointment, and a published complaint that the Herald's own election coverage had been biased.

In office, her administration fought a running battle over the makeup of the Board of Regents. On 29 October 1991 the Associated Student Government passed Resolution 91-6-F asking that Joe Iracane not be re-elected chair of the board; the Herald covered the vote the same day under "Associated Student Government Opposes Joseph Iracane." The board went ahead and re-elected him a week later, the university's own "On Campus" newsletter reported on 6 November. On 30 January 1992 the Herald reported the Associated Student Government was separately planning a committee aimed at student access to the regent, in the same issue that carried an editorial cartoon, "Ventriloquist's Dummy," targeting Falmlen. On 4 February 1992 the body passed Resolution 92-7-S, declaring its support for the WKU football program in spite of budget cuts; the Board of Regents voted that May to keep the program.

That fall the body passed Resolution 91-2-F, asking for a change to Big Red Shuttle bus fares, and held its regular fall senate elections in October, including a separate freshman senator election on 15 October.

The signature action of her term was procedural: on 7 April 1992 the Associated Students voted to rename itself the Student Government Association, a change students ratified in a referendum a week later, effective that autumn. WKU's own "On Campus" newsletter had confirmed on 22 May 1991, at the start of her term, that Falmlen held both the presidency and the student regent seat - the same dual role held by the presidents before and after her in this period. The archive does not preserve further detail of her regency or of what she did after her term.

Other legislative business that fall included Resolution 91-7-F, backing the renewal of President Thomas Meredith's contract by the Board of Regents; a bill funding a "President for a Day" fundraiser (Bill #91-09-F); a bill funding renovation of the ASG chambers (Bill #91-03-F); and a bill proposing a new organizational slogan (Bill #91-11-F), introduced the same season the body renamed itself from the Associated Students to the Student Government Association.

The executive

TreasurerJoe Rains

Presented the 1991-92 budget for acceptance at the first meeting on 3 September 1991, reported a running budget summary of $794.98 a fortnight later, and ran organizational aid, setting a 14 February 1992 application deadline with interviews from 17 to 28 February. He was elected president for 1992-93.

ASG Minutes, 3 Sep 1991, 17 Sep 1991 and 21 Jan 1992 Herald, 16 Apr 1992, "Joe Rains Wins Big in Associated Student Government Election" Herald 67:51, 9 Apr 1992, on the Holcomb-Rains race and the name-change referendum

Set the Friday noon deadline for committee and organization reports, recruited volunteers for ASG's 25th anniversary plans, and was ASG's nominee to represent Western at the Mountain Laurel Festival in January 1992.

ASG Minutes, 17 Sep 1991 and 21 Jan 1992
Administrative Vice-PresidentJohn Seiber

Set the committee meeting times and the attendance policy for committee and Congress meetings at the start of the year, and pressed members to vote in the 5 November 1991 election. In January 1992 he reported that seven ASG members had been named to Who's Who among American college students.

ASG Minutes, 3 Sep 1991, 17 Sep 1991 and 21 Jan 1992
President / Student RegentHeather E. Falmlen

Held both offices at once, as was standard for this period.

ASG Minutes, 24 Sep 1991 and 21 Jan 1992; ASG Resolution 91-6-F

25 October 1991Festival
#

International Day, co-sponsored with the University Center Board

More than 800 high school students from around the state came to Western on Friday 25 October for the annual International Day, sponsored by the University Center Board, Associated Student Government, International Programs and Student Affairs. A bazaar on the third floor of Downing University Center sold handmade goods from other countries, including bracelets, ornaments, scarves and belts made by Indian tribes in Ecuador and African woodwork and silver and copper jewellery. Profits from the crafts went to Escuela Para Padres, a joint fundraising organisation working to prevent abuse of women and children; project coordinator Joan Martin said jewellery was the best seller.

Herald 67:18, 29 Oct 1991

29 October 1991
#

ASG asks that Iracane not be re-elected regents chair

The Associated Student Government passed Resolution 91-6-F, authored by Mark Miller and Eric McWilliams and sponsored by Mistianna Holcomb, asking that Joe Iracane not be re-elected chair of the Board of Regents. The resolution's text stated that Iracane, chairman for eight consecutive years, was under investigation by the FBI, the IRS and U.S. attorneys in Indiana and Kentucky over an alleged $6 million payment to Pyramid Mining Co. in return for contracts and a separate alleged $14,500 payment to the company for a consulting contract; it also accused him of overstepping into day-to-day university operations reserved for the president under the Board's bylaws. The resolution asked student regent Heather Falmlen to vote against him. No source found reports the investigation's outcome. The same day's Herald covered the vote under "Associated Student Government Opposes Joseph Iracane."

SGA Resolution 91-6-F, "Board of Regents Chair Term Limit"

31 October 1991
#

ASG asks the regents to elect a new leader

ASG urged the Board of Regents to elect a new leader in place of chairman Joseph Iracane, two days after the Herald reported the organization's opposition to him. It was a rare direct challenge to the university's governing board.

Herald 67:19, 31 Oct 1991

5 November 1991Programme
#

Voter registration drive signs up nearly 1,000 students

ASG ran a voter registration drive for the 5 November 1991 election, led by vice president John Seiber, who with other congress members registered nearly 1,000 Western students. A full-page ASG advertisement in the Herald the following February listed the drive alongside fall break, campus recycling, organizational aid, new picnic tables, a new copier for the science library and the radio show as the year's work, with forums on the election, student concerns, the class presidents and a question and answer session with the regents.

Herald 67:38, 13 Feb 1992 (ASG advertisement)

A bill to set up a Coach for a Game fundraiser

Bill 92-03-S, written by Mistianna Holcomb and sponsored by the public relations committee, proposed a Coach for a Game programme alongside the President for a Day event ASG already sponsored in the fall. It would have made it mandatory for every ASG member to sell ten raffle tickets at 50 cents each, with the proceeds going to a philanthropic cause. The document carries no introduction, reading or voting dates, so the file does not show when or whether it passed.

24 March 1992Service
#

A permanent voter registration booth at class registration

Bill 92-06-S, introduced on 24 March 1992 by Trent Lyda for the Legislative Research Committee, committed ASG to keeping a voter registration booth wherever class registration took place each fall and spring, to capitalise on the influx of unregistered eligible student voters. The committee was to coordinate the scheduling of students and organisations willing to staff it, and every ASG member was asked to work the booth for at least an hour during the registration period.

SGA Records, Bill 92-06-S, Establishment of Permanent Voter Registration Program

#

The renaming is introduced as a constitutional amendment

SGA Legislation, Bill #92-10-S, "Constitutional Amendment to Rename ASG to SGA"

2 April 1992
#

ASG resolves to support football despite budget cuts

The Associated Student Government passed Resolution 92-7-S, declaring its support for the WKU football program in spite of budget cuts. The resolution's own text argued the university had already awarded 67 football scholarships it would be contractually bound to honor even if the program were suspended, calling a suspension that still paid non-playing scholarship athletes an unnecessary expense. This entry corrects the resolution's date: an earlier record in this archive gave 4 February 1992, but the resolution itself is dated introduced 2 April 1992, shortly before the Board of Regents' 1 May vote to keep the program.

SGA Legislation, Resolution 92-7-S, "Support of Football Program"

7 April 1992

Associated Students votes to become the Student Government Association

The body voted to rename itself on 7 April and students ratified the change in a referendum on 14 April. It took effect that autumn.

What this is

A year-by-year record of student government at Western Kentucky University, from the ratification of the Associated Students constitution in April 1966 to the present. Every entry names the source it came from.

Corrections

The names here start with the plaque in the SGA Chambers, and the plaque is known to be wrong in places. Where the archive disagrees with it, this site follows the archive and records the change on the corrections page. If you served, or you know that a year here is wrong, the project wants to hear from you: people who held an office can be given the keys to their own year on the contribute page.

What it rests on

Everything here comes from what is publicly available online: the digitised Herald and SGA's own papers on TopSCHOLAR, the Talisman yearbooks, wkuherald.com, the university's news pages and the Wayback Machine. That is a fraction of what happened. Student government has met most weeks for sixty years, and the greater part of it was never written down, or was written down on paper that has never been scanned. A thin year here is a thin record, not a quiet one.

Reuse

Text on this site is the work of the project. Photographs, documents and legislation are reproduced from Western Kentucky University's own open archives and from the College Heights Herald and the Talisman; each item names the collection, issue and page it came from and links to the original.
