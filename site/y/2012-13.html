Cory Dodds president and student regent, 2012-13

Office
President and student regent
Term recorded here
2012-13
Place in the line
the 48th president and the 45th student regent
Standing of the record
As recorded on the plaque. Not yet corroborated in the archive.

Cory Dodds, a junior political science major from Smithland, Kentucky, was elected SGA president for 2012-13 in spring 2012 over Austin Wingate and Corey Johnson, after two years as SGA's director of information technology, during which he built a legislation and minutes archive for the SGA website that he said promoted transparency and accountability. Under the merged offices, Dodds also served as the student regent representing students on WKU's Board of Regents.

In office, Dodds led SGA in unanimously opposing an administration proposal to shift tuition to a per-credit-hour, bi-term model, which Provost Gordon Emslie dropped in February 2013 after student and faculty pushback. His administration also drafted a teaching assistant evaluation program the senate passed in February 2013, worked to restart a dormant student government at WKU's Glasgow campus, donated cameras to the Teaching and Learning Resource Center, and passed a resolution opposing a new $150 Study Abroad application fee; a Dodds amendment to bar SGA members from receiving SGA-awarded study-abroad scholarships, after 11 of 125 such awards had gone to SGA members that year, failed.

Dodds's term ended amid a contested succession: his successor, Keyana Boka, was elected in April 2013 but disqualified five days later by the Judicial Council 3-2 over a campaign email, then reinstated after appealing to Vice President for Student Affairs Howard Bailey. Later in 2012 Dodds was named a finalist for the Harry S. Truman Scholarship and, that November, WKU's first finalist for the George J. Mitchell Scholarship, the only Kentuckian among 20 national finalists. He went on to earn a law degree from Georgetown University, worked as directing attorney of the Children's Law Center and as a legal services staff attorney representing low-income clients in public benefits, housing and family law matters, and by the mid-2020s was a visiting professor at the University of Kentucky's J. David Rosenberg College of Law teaching Civil Procedure and Children and the Law, with research on rural "legal deserts" and the attorney shortage.

The year, in order36

28 August 2012
#

Judicial Council issued three written opinions, each carried 5-0

On 28 August 2012 Chief Justice Chris Jankowski published three opinions, each recording a unanimous 5-0 vote of the council. One set out the judicial review procedure, one interpreted the attendance policy and defined excused and unexcused absences, and one ruled on two surplus at-large senators, letting them serve out their terms while barring the president from refilling those two seats until the constitutional composition was met.

Judicial Council opinions, 28 Aug 2012 (wku.edu)Judicial Council opinion, judicial review, 28 Aug 2012

6 September 2012
#

Senate unanimously opposed the bi-term calendar and per-credit-hour tuition

SGA unanimously passed a resolution opposing the administration's proposal to emphasize bi-term courses and shift tuition to a per-credit-hour model. The bi-term plan would have put students in 80-minute classes on Mondays, Tuesdays, Thursdays and Fridays, and President Gary Ransdell had said it would let a student carry a full load plus a summer or winter course and graduate in three years. President Cory Dodds objected chiefly to the per-credit-hour shift, arguing it would charge students more because full-time students then paid for 12 hours but could enroll in up to 18. He called it a financial burden students should not have to bear.

Herald, 6 Sep 2012

12 September 2012
#

Senate creates a long-term goals committee by executive action

SGA voted unanimously on an executive action establishing a special long-term goals committee of at least eight members, including students from outside SGA appointed by President Cory Dodds. Dodds said the committee could draw up a set of goals to focus on for the next few years. A bill to distribute $800 in senate funding as eight $100 Study Away scholarships received its first read.

Herald, 12 Sep 2012

25 September 2012
#

Fall senate seats filled after two resignations

Two senators resigned over time commitments and student Shea Wyatt was named to fill in. New freshman senators Barrett Greenwell, Taylor Ruby, Cole McDowell and Paige Settles were sworn in along with Gatton Academy senator Linda Cruz and graduate senators Mark Reeves and Katie Martin.

Herald, 27 Sep 2012

26 September 2012
#

ID voucher pilot program introduced covering 80 student IDs

A bill in first read would have SGA pay $10 toward each regular-priced student identification card under an identification voucher pilot program, partially covering the cost of 80 cards. A second bill sought $225 for the Sigma Gamma Rho sorority's Hair Show Spectacular. A resolution setting the fall campus clean-up date was held over because it had not reached the Legislative Research Committee.

Herald, 26 Sep 2012

9 October 2012Tradition
#

Senate bought T-shirts for the autumn Campus Clean-up

The senate unanimously approved funding on 9 October 2012 for promotional T-shirts for volunteers at the Campus Clean-up on 23 October, timed with Make a Difference Day. Committee chairman Mallory Chaney said dark grey shirts with the SGA logo would read something like "Tops making a difference" and would give students an incentive to turn out. Executive vice president Keyana Boka called the twice-yearly clean-up an important tradition, one the Herald traced back to WKU president Henry Cherry.

Herald, 12 Oct 2012 - "SGA bill funds Campus Clean-up"

17 October 2012
#

SGA adopts a new voting system and holds the South Campus food court resolution

At its eighth meeting SGA implemented a new voting system and gave a first read to an organizational aid bill covering the Student Veteran's Alliance ($175), the WKU chapter of the American Society for Microbiology ($150) and the Academic Competition Club ($275). A resolution supporting a food court at South Campus, due for a second read, was instead held in new business.

Herald, 17 Oct 2012

22 October 2012Tradition
#

Picnic with the president in the Ransdells' back garden

Gary and Julie Ransdell hosted SGA's executive board, student senators and the WKU Administrative Council for a cookout at their home on 22 October 2012, business casual. Ransdell said he had come up with the idea after months of talks with SGA president Cory Dodds about connecting his office to SGA. Dodds said the picnic had been running for several years and was an SGA tradition.

Herald, 17 Oct 2012 - "Picnic with the president a SGA tradition"

13 November 2012
#

SGA worked to restart a student government for the Glasgow campus

SGA officials traveled to WKU's Glasgow campus to help restart its dormant student representative body and connect it to main campus. Dodds said Glasgow students had no mechanism for their voices to be heard except contacting main-campus SGA. Roy Ratliff, nominated interim president of the Glasgow student body, said its top priority was establishing a writing center, for which the group had set aside $2,700.

Herald, 13 Nov 2012

14 November 2012
#

Retention official addressed the senate and got no feedback

Speaker of the Senate Christopher Costa opened the floor to Joelle Carter, assistant vice president for retention and student services, who asked senators for ideas on keeping students enrolled through to graduation. No senators offered feedback during the meeting, and Carter encouraged members to contact her later. SGA's current budget was circulated at the start of the meeting, showing the remaining balance and the money spent.

Herald, 14 Nov 2012

30 November 2012
#

Glasgow campus ratifies its own student government constitution

Glasgow's Associated Student Body ratified its constitution on Nov. 16 by unanimous vote with no abstentions. Interim president Roy Ratliff said the goal was to give SGA a presence on the regional campus and give regional students a vote in student government. The Glasgow body adopted the same bylaws as SGA and began selecting committees for the spring.

Herald, 30 Nov 2012

30 November 2012Service
#

iPad lending programme opened with the Educational Resources Center

SGA and the Educational Resources Center launched an iPad-lending programme giving students, staff and faculty access to tablets loaded with test preparation applications such as MCAT materials. ERC coordinator Roxanne Spencer said SGA had approached the centre wanting the iPads available for a pilot project. Executive vice president Keyana Boka said SGA had bought the iPads the previous year but could not start lending until they were secured against tampering.

Herald, 30 Nov 2012 - "SGA, ERC collaborate for iPad program"

5 December 2012
#

Provost pitches value-added grading to SGA at final fall meeting

Provost Gordon Emslie and University Senate member Guy Jordan addressed SGA on a proposed value-added grading system under which students in the top third of B and C grades would receive an extra quality point and a plus designation. Emslie said an earlier plus-minus proposal had taken several years to develop "only to be dashed at the last minute." Jordan argued the value-added version worked because faculty gave something up and students did not.

Herald, 5 Dec 2012

5 December 2012Tradition
#

Dine with Decision Makers moved to the Kentucky Museum

SGA held Dine with Decision Makers at the Kentucky Museum on 5 December 2012, seating officials from across Kentucky and WKU administrators at tables matched to students by interest or field. President Cory Dodds opened the evening and executive vice president Keyana Boka said the prayer. Wadsworth, Ohio senior Sarah Brazier gave the keynote, arguing that higher education built a productive economy and an informed citizenry rather than only advancing individuals.

Herald, 6 Dec 2012 - "Dine with Decision Makers"

8 February 2013
#

Senate rejects the value-added grading resolution in a close vote

The value-added grading resolution failed on a close vote, disappointing President Cory Dodds, who called it a tangible benefit that increased student competitiveness and said it was "a real shame that our senate didn't see that." Provost Gordon Emslie had introduced the system to SGA and the University Senate in fall 2012. Some senate members opposed the change.

Herald, 8 Feb 2013

8 February 2013Service
#

Safe Rides purple line returned for the spring

SGA's Safe Rides service, known on campus as the purple line, continued into spring 2013 after autumn 2012 became the first semester WKU ran a fixed-route late-night bus rather than a taxi contract. President Cory Dodds said the consistency of set pick-up points and times was why it worked where Provide-A-Ride had not. Administrative vice president Cain Alvey said Provide-A-Ride had been an unreliable taxi service by comparison.

Herald, 8 Feb 2013 - "SGA's safe rides program will continue for the spring semester"

12 February 2013Service
#

ID voucher programme halved the cost of a replacement card

SGA's ID voucher programme, running since the middle of the autumn 2012 semester, let students pay $10 rather than $20 for their first replacement student ID. The bill behind it allocated $800 to part-cover 80 cards. Administrative vice president Cain Alvey said the idea came from his job as a resident assistant in McCormack Hall, and that SGA covered only the first loss so as to teach responsibility and spread the benefit. Vouchers were collected from the SGA office in Cravens Library room 13.

#

Eight Study Away scholarships funded as transcript vouchers ran on

The senate passed a bill on 12 February 2013 allocating $800 for eight $100 Study Away scholarships covering the spring and summer 2013 programmes. SGA reported that 30 of the semester's transcript vouchers had been used, with 70 left. Campus improvements chair Mallory Chaney set the spring Campus Safety Walk for 26 February.

$800 in eight $100 scholarships

15 February 2013
#

Senate passed the teaching assistant evaluation resolution

On 5 February the senate passed a resolution creating teaching assistant evaluations, a program President Cory Dodds had campaigned on and developed with his executive officers before pitching it to Provost Gordon Emslie. Nicki Seay, chair of the Legislative Research Committee, wrote the measure, which aimed to make TA evaluation uniform across departments. Dodds said the idea had been worked on for many years. The resolution next needed review by the Academic Quality Committee and approval by the University Senate.

Herald, 15 Feb 2013

19 February 2013
#

Senate tabled Hannah Garland's plus-minus grading resolution

Academic Affairs chair Hannah Garland's resolution to add plus and minus grades to As, Bs, Cs and Ds was tabled at the Tuesday meeting after public relations chair Mac Mullins argued for a more formal proposal and more student input first, and most of the senate agreed. Garland said the system would make GPAs more exact and might make WKU more competitive. President Cory Dodds said the executive board had discussed putting the question directly to students on the spring ballot. A similar plan had drawn SGA resistance when the provost proposed it in 2007.

Herald, 26 Feb 2013Herald, 21 Feb 2013

19 February 2013
#

Judicial Council reviewed and approved the constitutional package before the senate voted

Resolution 5-13-S, first read on 19 February 2013 and read a second time on 26 February, records in its whereas clauses that the proposed amendments had been reviewed and approved by the Judicial Council under clause 4.5.9 of the constitution. Seth Church is listed among the resolution's contacts, though the document gives no title for him there, and it is not established that he already held the chief justiceship on this date. The resolution referred the amendments to the student body for ratification on the spring 2013 ballot.

Resolution 5-13-S (data/legislation/2012-13/r5-13-s.pdf)

26 February 2013
#

Senate amended the constitution, adding regional-campus seats

After two to three years of executive discussion, the senate passed most of a package of constitutional amendments, replacing class-year senate seats with college-based representation and guaranteeing seats for regional campuses such as Glasgow and Owensboro. A bylaw that would have made SGA members permanently ineligible for SGA-sponsored scholarships was stripped out and did not pass.

Herald, 1 Mar 2013

March 2013
#

Provost Emslie cancelled the bi-term proposal SGA had opposed

Provost Gordon Emslie announced at the Feb. 21 University Senate meeting that he was dropping his bi-term emphasis proposal and had no plans to revive it, citing input from students and faculty. Both SGA and the University Senate had passed resolutions against it. Dodds said the revocation was in the student body's best interest, saying "I strongly opposed it."

Herald, 1 Mar 2013

March 2013
#

Constitution overhaul passes but senate strips the scholarship conflict rule

SGA adopted a package of constitutional and bylaw amendments with one exception: the senate rejected the bylaw that would have made serving SGA members ineligible for SGA-sponsored scholarships. Administrative Vice President Cain Alvey said executives had been discussing changes for two to three years and had gone through the constitution article by article, first presenting the changes on Feb. 19. Alvey said an amendment guaranteeing representation for regional campuses such as Glasgow and Owensboro was the one he liked most.

Herald, 1 Mar 2013

March 2013
#

Four SGA leaders publicly accuse the senate of rejecting accountability

President Cory Dodds, Travis Taylor, Brittany Crowley and Drew Mitchell wrote a commentary attacking the senate for rejecting the amendment that would have barred serving SGA members from SGA-sponsored scholarships and grants. They wrote that 11 SGA members had received such scholarships or grants since the spring of 2011. The authors said this amounted to a breach of student trust and a conflict of interest.

Herald, 1 Mar 2013

19 March 2013
#

SGA passed a resolution opposing the $150 study abroad application fee

The senate passed a resolution opposing a new $150 application fee for Study Abroad and Global Learning, due to begin with summer 2013 programs, arguing it could put program costs out of reach. Executive Vice President Keyana Boka said SGA took a formal stance because the fee had come up so suddenly, and the University Senate passed a similar resolution seeking to delay the fee until fall 2013. A separate amendment proposed by Dodds, to make SGA members ineligible for SGA-awarded study abroad scholarships, failed. Dodds said 11 of the 125 such scholarships and grants awarded that year had gone to SGA members, which he called a conflict of interest.

Herald, 19 Mar 2013

27 March 2013
#

Executive candidates debate ahead of spring election

Outgoing President Cory Dodds opened a candidates' debate in Ransdell Hall Auditorium by describing to the audience how much influence the students elected to SGA's executive positions would actually have on WKU's campus, ahead of the election that would choose his successor.

Herald, 29 Mar 2013

4 April 2013
#

Boka, Reeves and Seay won the spring executive elections

Executive vice president Keyana Boka was elected 2013-14 SGA president with 626 votes, 47 percent, over administrative vice president Cain Alvey with 419 votes and Austin Wingate with 300. Mark Reeves won executive vice president with 632 votes, 57 percent, over Brad Cockrel, and Nicki Seay ran unopposed for administrative vice president. Twenty-six student senators were also elected.

Herald, 4 Apr 2013

9 April 2013
#

Judicial Council disqualified president-elect Boka, 3-2

In an emergency meeting, SGA's Judicial Council voted 3-2 to disqualify president-elect Keyana Boka after runner-up and outgoing administrative vice president Cain Alvey complained that a self-promotional email had gone to Honors College students at Boka's request, sent by Honors College graduate assistant Destiny Savage, violating the election code. The disqualification would have made Alvey, who had placed second in the April 4 vote, the incoming president; Boka said she would appeal.

Herald, 9 Apr 2013

9 April 2013
#

Regents committee approves SGA's teaching assistant evaluation policy

SGA announced that the Board of Regents had unanimously approved the teaching assistant evaluation resolution at its March 29 committee meeting, with the full board to take it up on April 26. President Gary Ransdell said he had recommended approval and that there was little discussion because students and faculty were in agreement. The policy would require departments to provide a way for students to evaluate teaching assistants.

Herald, 9 Apr 2013

16 April 2013
#

Judicial Council declined to contest Bailey's reinstatement of Boka

In an emergency meeting, the Judicial Council unanimously decided not to challenge Bailey's memorandum reinstating Boka, saying it did not believe it had the power to do so, while expressing official disapproval. Chief Justice Seth Church called the intervention "a serious infringement on our autonomy" and said it set a bad precedent. Boka said the council had conflicts of interest and needed to be held accountable by a neutral party in the administration.

Herald, 16 Apr 2013

16 April 2013
#

Costa stepped down as speaker; Settles elected to succeed him

Christopher Costa did not seek another term as speaker of the senate, and senators elected Paige Settles to replace him. The same meeting confirmed presidential appointments Roy Ratliff as senator-at-large and Jackie Stewart as associate justice, with Chief of Staff Travis Taylor and public relations chair Mac Mullins giving reports.

Herald, 17 Apr 2013Herald, 23 Apr 2013

25 April 2013
#

Senate opposed automatic athletic-fee increases ahead of C-USA move

The senate passed a resolution against the annual automatic increase in the student athletic fee, arguing the revenue-sharing WKU would gain on joining Conference USA that July would outweigh the roughly $40,000 the university lost annually in the Sun Belt. Executive vice president-elect Mark Reeves, who introduced it, said the resolution set a precedent against automatic fee increases generally.

Herald, 26 Apr 2013

26 April 2013
#

SGA votes to end the automatic annual athletic fee increase

The senate passed a resolution supporting an end to the automatic annual increase in the student athletic fee once WKU joined Conference USA on July 1, 2014. Senator and executive vice president-elect Mark Reeves, who initiated it, argued that increased revenues made the automatic increase unnecessary. The resolution stated that WKU athletics paid $80,000 in the Sun Belt Conference but received only $40,000 back in revenue sharing.

Herald, 26 Apr 2013

10 May 2013
#

Budget cuts strip SGA's automatic inflation increase

University budget cuts announced that week removed the automatic increases that SGA, the Campus Activities Board and leadership programs normally received under the Higher Education Price Index, saving the university $8,000. Charley Pride, director of Student Activities and SGA's adviser, said no money was taken away, which was better than losing money. Incoming President Keyana Boka said the loss could affect new programs SGA had wanted to start.

Herald, 10 May 2013

4 July 2013
#

Former officer defends SGA's stance as administrators rename DUC anyway

Former Executive Vice President Kendrick Bryan wrote after the Administrative Council decided the building would be called Downing Student Union. He wrote that former President Cory Dodds had correctly told campus officials students preferred Downing University Center, noting the student assembly had voted against the change in 2011 when asked to do so by administrators. Bryan wrote that student opinion had not changed since that vote.

Herald, 4 Jul 2013

What this is

A year-by-year record of student government at Western Kentucky University, from the ratification of the Associated Students constitution in April 1966 to the present. Every entry names the source it came from.

Corrections

The names here start with the plaque in the SGA Chambers, and the plaque is known to be wrong in places. Where the archive disagrees with it, this site follows the archive and records the change on the corrections page. If you served, or you know that a year here is wrong, the project wants to hear from you: people who held an office can be given the keys to their own year on the contribute page.

What it rests on

Everything here comes from what is publicly available online: the digitised Herald and SGA's own papers on TopSCHOLAR, the Talisman yearbooks, wkuherald.com, the university's news pages and the Wayback Machine. That is a fraction of what happened. Student government has met most weeks for sixty years, and the greater part of it was never written down, or was written down on paper that has never been scanned. A thin year here is a thin record, not a quiet one.
