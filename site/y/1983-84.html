1984-85

Two-year plate. Confirmed by name in Herald headlines across his whole term, from the April 1983 win through the April 1985 retrospective on his presidency ending.

Jack Smith first ran for the Associated Student Government presidency in spring 1982, drawing repeated endorsement letters in the Herald, and though Margaret Ragan won that race, Smith and Lonnie Sears took awards as the year closed. He returned as a candidate in March 1983, when 68 students filed for ASG positions and his supporters again filled the letters pages.

Smith won the presidency of the Associated Student Government in the April 12, 1983 election, in which students also approved constitutional revisions. His victory came after roughly 500 students had voted in the primary the week before.

His presidency was colorful and contentious: the fall brought a discount card controversy, letters accusing him of partying while committees waited and others defending him, and a December cartoon that he wrote in to protest. In April 1984 the Herald caught him impersonating a bunny as his ASG ran a low-turnout poll on beer and a campus pub.

Smith's plaque term runs two years, 1983-85, and the Herald's own account of his exit backs that up: its April 11, 1985 issue reported him ending his term as ASG president in an article headlined 'Jack Smith Ends Term as Associated Student Government President,' alongside a companion piece crediting his enthusiasm with reviving interest in the organization.

Smith's 1983 campaign drew an unusual run of supporting letters in the Herald. The issue of 7 April 1983 carried two, from Dorothy Metzmeler and Sarah Hein, following one from Tony Johnson the week before; the same issue reported that about 500 students had voted in the ASG primary.

Jamie Morton reported on 14 April 1983 that Jack Smith had won the presidency of the Associated Student Government. The same issue reported that students had also voted in favor of the ASG constitutional revisions worked out that spring.

His first term drew heavy comment. In December 1983 the Herald reported that ASG had voided its own procedures even as it saw growth in membership and took up fourteen proposals, and the same issue carried Stanley Reagan's letter headed "Jack Smith, Associated Student Government Thrown Away" along with another Lou Bloss cartoon on ASG. He filed for re-election in February 1984 and stayed in office through a second year, ending his term in April 1985.

The year, in order24

12 April 1983
#

ASG election day: candidates court votes for the top offices

The Herald urged students to the polls for the Associated Student Government election. Beyond the presidential contest, candidates for ASG's other top offices - including Bob Shults, Laura McClellan, Traci Turner, Kim Binkley and Allan Kujala - each drew endorsement letters in the paper; the Herald index does not specify which office each sought.

Herald 58:54, 12 Apr 1983 PDF

23 August 1983
#

Escort service returns; discount card floated

The Herald's opening issue reported that the student escort service would resume and that ASG might sponsor a discount card. Filing for freshman elections was set to begin September 19.

Herald 59:1, 23 Aug 1983 PDF

30 August 1983
#

Smith opens with a "year of action"

Steve Paul reported that the Associated Student Government was planning for a year of action. The story ran in the third issue of the fall semester, a week after the Herald first reported that ASG might sponsor a discount card.

Herald 59:3, 30 Aug 1983 PDF

September 1983
#

Discount card plan draws fire

ASG voted to study the discount card after the Herald editorialized that the organization should govern, not market cards. A week later the paper reported ASG would offer members a commission on card sales, prompting letters calling the plan cheap shots and old tricks.

Herald 59:4, 1 Sep 1983 PDF

8 September 1983
#

ASG members to take a commission on discount cards

Steve Paul reported that the Associated Student Government would offer its members a commission on the discount cards. The same issue carried two critical columns aimed at ASG, Timi Flener's "Cheap Shots" and Connie Hoffman's "And Old Tricks."

Herald 59:5, 8 Sep 1983 PDF

4 October 1983Service
#

Local merchants waited for refunds on the discount cards

Steve Paul reported that local merchants were waiting for refunds over the student discount cards. The card had been ASG's flagship service project of the autumn and had drawn a Herald editorial in September saying the organisation should govern rather than market cards. By November ASG had given up on the outside company and printed the cards itself.

College Heights Herald 59:12, 4 October 1983

13 October 1983
#

ASG asks for cable television in dorm rooms

Steve Paul reported that the Associated Student Government wanted cable in dormitory rooms. The same issue ran a Lou Bloss editorial cartoon on an ASG penalty and a letter from Carl Davis disagreeing with an ASG proposal.

Herald 59:15, 13 Oct 1983 PDF

27 October 1983
#

ASG passes incomplete-grade deadline proposal

The Associated Student Government passed its proposal on the deadline for incomplete grades, after tabling it the week before. The grade issue had occupied ASG since September.

Herald 59:19, 27 Oct 1983 PDF

3 November 1983
#

Budget falls short as the grade plan passes

Craig Dezern and Steve Paul reported that the ASG budget would not cover its planned projects. The same issue reported that ASG had approved its grade plan, the change to the grading scale that ran through most of the year.

Herald 59:21, 3 Nov 1983 PDF

8 November 1983
#

An A-minus was floated for the grade plan

Steve Paul reported that an A-minus might be added to the Associated Student Government's grade plan, and the Herald's editorial page argued in the same issue that a new grade scale must include one. The plan had been approved five days earlier, and by 8 December a group had organised against it.

College Heights Herald 59:22, 8 November 1983 PDF

10 November 1983
#

ASG takes up a university day-care center

Barry Rose reported that a university day-care center would be studied by the Associated Student Government. It joined study hall research and cable television among the service questions ASG picked up that fall.

Herald 59:23, 10 Nov 1983 PDF

10 November 1983Service
#

ASG prints the discount card itself

After an agreement collapsed with University Services Associates of St. Mary's, Missouri, ASG voted to print the student discount cards itself, and they were finally handed out in November through the Student Affairs and Public Relations committees working with Interhall Council. Nine businesses appeared on the card, among them the seven merchants who had paid company president George Wheaton for the original card and never got a refund. President Jack Smith said the businesses would have to recover their own money: "They got what they paid for. They got their discount card."

6 December 1983
#

ASG voids its own procedures amid growth

The Herald reported that ASG voided its own procedures, while also noting growth in membership and a congress weighing 14 proposals. The editorial page mocked the body with a cartoon, and Jack Smith wrote in to say the cartoon aggravated him.

Herald 59:27, 6 Dec 1983 PDF

8 December 1983
#

A group opposed the grade plan, and false proposals were said to risk impeachment

Steve Paul reported that a group had come out against ASG's grade plan, five weeks after the congress approved it. The same issue carried a second Paul piece reporting that writing false proposals could mean impeachment, in a semester in which the Herald had already reported ASG voiding its own procedures.

College Heights Herald 59:28, 8 December 1983 PDF

12 January 1984
#

Vice president William Chandler quits

Victoria Malmer reported that Associated Student Government vice president William Chandler had quit. Within a week the Herald reported that three people had been nominated for ASG positions.

Herald 59:29, 12 Jan 1984 PDF

26 January 1984
#

ASG urged to take up student rights

Barry Rose reported that ASG had been encouraged to look at student rights, and the Herald's editorial page argued in the same issue that the student rights policy needed re-examining. A week later Mark Edelen reported that ASG would examine rights. The issue also reported that a day-care centre was possible in the autumn, a project ASG had taken up in November.

College Heights Herald 59:33, 26 January 1984 PDF

9 February 1984
#

ASG announced a poll on beer at athletic events

Mark Edelen reported that ASG would poll students on whether they wanted beer sold at games. This is the announcement of the poll, not its result. By 16 February the paper was reporting that an NCAA rule might prevent alcohol sales at ballgames and by 23 February that the NCAA and the Sun Belt would allow them. The poll itself went ahead on general election day in April, by then framed around beer and a campus pub, and low turnout threatened a revote.

College Heights Herald 59:37, 9 February 1984 PDF

14 February 1984
#

Student legislature may rejoin ASG

The Kentucky Student Legislature delegation was reported possibly rejoining the Associated Student Government. Letter writers sparred the same day over whether the ASG stereotype was deserved.

Herald 59:38, 14 Feb 1984 PDF

16 February 1984
#

ASG wanted evaluation changes but opposed achievement tests

Mark Edelen reported that the Associated Student Government wanted changes to faculty evaluation while opposing achievement tests. The same issue carried a Herald editorial arguing that linking the Kentucky Intercollegiate State Legislature to ASG would be a mistake, and a letter from Vincent Lewis praising ASG. The legislature delayed its move the following week.

College Heights Herald 59:39, 16 February 1984 PDF

23 February 1984
#

The top ASG officers file for re-election

Mark Edelen reported that the top Associated Student Government officers were seeking re-election. The same issue reported that legislators were upset with ASG and that students were being asked to gripe, and it carried further responses to the alcohol poll plan.

Herald 59:41, 23 Feb 1984 PDF

20 March 1984
#

Top four ASG offices decided without runoffs

The top four Associated Student Government spots were decided, the Herald reported, as ASG emphasized representation. About 450 students had voted in the primary the week before.

Herald 59:45, 20 Mar 1984 PDF

3 April 1984
#

ASG polls students on beer and a campus pub

ASG began a student poll on the beer and campus pub issue on general election day. The Herald's editorial page argued the economics of the campus pub plan were faulty.

Herald 59:49, 3 Apr 1984 PDF

10 April 1984
#

Low turnout may force a beer poll revote

Low turnout threatened to spur a revote in the ASG beer poll. The same issue reported president Jack Smith impersonating a bunny for the season.

Herald 59:51, 10 Apr 1984 PDF

24 April 1984
#

ASG, the centre board and Interhall Council found their duties overlapped

Mark Edelen reported that the Associated Student Government, the University Center Board and the Interhall Council overlapped in what they did. The ground had been divided between them five years earlier, when the regents moved campus entertainment and the money that paid for it from ASG to the centre board.

College Heights Herald 59:55, 24 April 1984 PDF

What this is

A year-by-year record of student government at Western Kentucky University, from the ratification of the Associated Students constitution in April 1966 to the present. Every entry names the source it came from.

Corrections

The names here start with the plaque in the SGA Chambers, and the plaque is known to be wrong in places. Where the archive disagrees with it, this site follows the archive and records the change on the corrections page. If you served, or you know that a year here is wrong, the project wants to hear from you through the Student Government Association office.

What it rests on

Everything here comes from what is publicly available online: the digitised Herald and SGA's own papers on TopSCHOLAR, the Talisman yearbooks, wkuherald.com, the university's news pages and the Wayback Machine. That is a fraction of what happened. Student government has met most weeks for sixty years, and the greater part of it was never written down, or was written down on paper that has never been scanned. A thin year here is a thin record, not a quiet one.

Text on this site is the work of the project. Photographs, documents and legislation are reproduced from Western Kentucky University's own open archives and from the College Heights Herald and the Talisman; each item names the collection, issue and page it came from and links to the original.
