Robert Evans president and student regent, 1994-95

Office
President and student regent
Term recorded here
1994-95
Place in the line
the 28th president and the 26th student regent
Standing of the record
As recorded on the plaque. Not yet corroborated in the archive.

Running for president in April 1994, Evans was profiled by the Herald as the candidate who urged discussion of the issues. He faced Scott Sivley in a contested race; on election day, 19 April, the Herald reported Sivley had been warned by SGA for violating the organization's campaign policies, alongside an opinion piece urging a vote for "Rob Evans, Tara Higdon" and another arguing that "Sacrificing Student Government Association Means Saving Needed Programs." The same issue's editorial pages told students "The Choice Is Yours" in the SGA vote.

He won, and the Herald introduced the incoming leadership with "Rob Evans & Tara Higdon Ready to Lead Students." Higdon, elected alongside him, would succeed him as president a year later.

The SGA's 2001 list of presidents records Robert Evans of Owensboro as the 28th president, serving 1994-95, and marks him as having also served as student regent. The Board of Regents' quarterly meeting of 15 August 1994 swore him in alongside Joy Gramling and Cornelius Martin, and carried resolutions of appreciation for outgoing officers Donald Smith - his predecessor as SGA president and student regent - and Monnie Hankins. The Board's quarterly meeting of 22 September 1995 - the start of the following academic year - swore in Tara Higdon as his successor on the board, marking the end of his regency.

After his year leading the WKU student body, Rob Evans kept a statewide role: in April 1996 the Herald profiled him under the headline 'Rob Evans Representing Students from Across State,' describing his seat on the Kentucky Council on Higher Education, the body that then oversaw the state's public universities.

Evans defeats Sivley for the SGA presidency

Rob Evans faced Scott Sivley at the polls on election day; the Herald reported the same day that Sivley had been warned by SGA for violating the organization's campaign policies. Evans and running mate Tara Higdon won.

Herald 69:52 [51], 19 Apr 1994, "Student Government Association Elections: Scott Sivley vs. Rob Evans at Polls Today"

Campus assesses storm damage

The book exchange takes on the bookstore

SGA ran a book exchange to let students buy and sell textbooks among themselves rather than through the College Heights Bookstore. The Herald headlined it as SGA helping students avoid the bookstore blues.

#

Bill 94-4-F asked every member to talk to 25 students a week

Bill 94-4-F, dated 4 October 1994, proposed that each SGA representative speak with twenty-five students each week about the association's work. It is one of a run of internal-outreach measures from the Evans administration held in SGA's own records on TopSCHOLAR.

TopSCHOLAR - Bill 94-4-F, Physical Contact

Resolution asks for grades before the "W" deadline

SGA passed Resolution 94-11-F, asking that students be given a grade before the end of the withdrawal ("W") period so they could make an informed decision about dropping a class.

SGA Resolution 94-11-F, Examination Prior to end of "W" PeriodRead it on this site

Two bills bought flags for Diddle Arena and the Kentucky Library

On 1 November 1994 the congress took up two companion bills: Bill 94-7-F requested the purchase of WKU flags for Diddle Arena and the Kentucky Library and Museum, and Bill 94-6-F requested Kentucky state flags for the same two buildings. Flags recur in SGA's records through the decade, reappearing in the 1995-96 and 1996-97 minutes.

8 November 1994Programme
#

Bill 94-9-F establishes a series of public forums

Lena Sweeten's bill committed SGA to hold public forums at least three times a semester so students could raise concerns directly. It passed on second reading on 22 November 1994 and set the pattern for the forum programme SGA ran for the next decade.

TopSCHOLAR - Bill 94-9-F, Public Forums

Two resolutions backed the band council's transport problems

On 8 November 1994 the congress passed a pair of resolutions for the WKU Band Council. Resolution 94-17-F asked for a shuttle service from Diddle Arena to Van Meter for public concerts, and Resolution 94-16-F supported a van to move band instruments across campus for football games.

TopSCHOLAR - Resolution 94-17-F, Support for WKU Band Council Public Concert Shuttle Service

#

SGA backed free housing for resident assistants

Resolution 94-9-F, dated 1 November 1994, asked the university for an increased housing subsidy for resident assistants. The Herald reported the position the following week under Mitchell Quarles's byline.

Herald 70:22, 10 Nov 1994 - Quarles, "Student Government Association Endorses Free Resident Assistant Housing"

15 November 1994Tradition
#

Big Red Days and 500 red towels

Jason Young's Bill 94-10-F created Big Red Days, asking the whole student body to wear red on days Western played a men's or women's home basketball game. It directed SGA members to hand out 500 red towels costing $1.00 each at the games, and was written to revive the spirit of the E. A. Diddle era.

500 towels at $1.00 each

A non-circulating textbook library proposed

Resolution 94-18-F, dated 15 November 1994, asked the university to create a non-circulating textbook library so students could consult required texts without buying them. It sat alongside the book exchange the same administration had run in October.

TopSCHOLAR - Resolution 94-18-F, Non-Circulating Textbook Library

22 November 1994
#

Resolution 94-20-F sought to stop undergraduates grading papers

Resolution 94-20-F, dated 22 November 1994, asked that undergraduate teaching assistants be disallowed from grading papers and proctoring tests. It closed a fall session in which the congress also asked for faculty evaluations in both semesters, under Resolution 94-14-F of 8 November.

TopSCHOLAR - Resolution 94-20-F, Discontinuing Undergraduate Student Teaching Assistants Participation in Grading Process

24 January 1995
#

Security phone tops SGA's spring to-do list

The Herald reported a new security phone, a shuttle stop, and the memorial trees on the Student Government Association's agenda, under "New Security Phone, Shuttle Stop, Trees Lead to Student Government's To Do List." The same issue reported a mystery caller had been scaring students with stalking and harassment, the incident that would shape the spring campaign two months later.

Herald 70:32, 24 Jan 1995, "New Security Phone, Shuttle Stop, Trees Lead to Student Government's To Do List"

31 January 1995Programme
#

WKU: Helping Us Help You links students to local employers

Bill 95-1-S created a partnership between SGA and Bowling Green businesses to increase part-time employment for WKU students. Businesses with 15 or more employees were measured twice a year on the share of WKU students they hired, and the winner was thanked with a half-page Herald advertisement and a sign in Downing University Center.

TopSCHOLAR - Bill 95-1-S, WKU: Helping Us Help You

A painted crosswalk and six trash cans

Two resolutions dated 31 January 1995 dealt with the ground between the parking structure and Smith Stadium. Resolution 95-3-S proposed a painted crosswalk to the parking garage and Resolution 95-4-S requested six trash cans along Big Red Way.

TopSCHOLAR - Resolution 95-3-S, Dogwood Avenue Crosswalk

Nearly 150 workers turn out for the clean-up

The February 1995 clean-up drew close to 150 volunteers, a reversal of the two-person turnout of the previous December. The Herald reported the count five days after the event began.

Herald 70:35, 28 Feb 1995 - Gagliardi, "Cleanup Draws Nearly 150 Workers"

7 March 1995Programme
#

Organizational Aid pays out $2,500 to thirteen groups

Bill 95-2-S disbursed the $2,500 budgeted for Organizational Aid after the committee spent more than eight hours across four meetings. Recipients included Kappa Alpha Psi and Delta Sigma Theta at $250 each, Habitat for Humanity at $225, and the Topperettes at $175.

$2,500 total

TopSCHOLAR - Bill 95-2-S, Organizational Aid Recipients

#

Faculty or Staff Member of the Month and Year awards created

Bill 95-3-S established monthly and annual awards for a faculty or staff member, chosen by SGA. The monthly winner received a certificate and the yearly winner a plaque handed out at the end-of-year banquet.

TopSCHOLAR - Bill 95-3-S, Faculty and Staff Recognition

Campus security shapes the spring campaign

The Herald reported that being stalked had influenced candidate Tara Higdon, putting campus security at the center of the spring election coverage.

Herald 70:51, 11 Apr 1995

#

Resolution 95-6-S asks for an annual Sibling's Day

Carlene and Darlene Lodmell's resolution requested a Sibling's Day each spring, arguing that siblings under 18 had no way to visit campus without a parent while Parents' Day covered the autumn. The resolution asked that it be timed to coincide with Coming Home activities.

TopSCHOLAR - Resolution 95-6-S, Sibling's Day

A roommate selection guide for freshmen

Resolution 95-7-S, dated 11 April 1995, proposed creating a freshman roommate selection guide. It was among the last measures of Rob Evans's year, passed in the same week as the Sibling's Day resolution.

TopSCHOLAR - Resolution 95-7-S, Roommate Selection

The last clean-up of the year

SGA held its final campus clean-up of 1994-95 on 13 April. The Herald announced it the same morning.

Herald 70:44, 13 Apr 1995 - "Final Campus Cleanup Held Today"

20 April 1995
#

Tara Higdon moved up as turnout moved down

The Herald reported the spring election under the headline 'Tara Higdon Moves Up; Voting Down,' recording both the choice of the next president and a drop in voter participation. The election closed out the 1994-95 administration.

Herald 70:54, 20 Apr 1995

25 April 1995
#

The Herald called visibility the key to SGA's success

As the year ended, the Herald ran an editorial package on the association, pairing a Stacy Curtis cartoon of student government in a glass box with the editorial 'Visibility Key to Student Government Association Success.' It was a summing-up of a year in which the paper pressed SGA to be seen doing its work.

Herald 70:55, 25 Apr 1995

27 April 1995
#

A new beginning

The Herald's transition piece, "Tara Higdon Sees New Beginning," marked the handover from Robert Evans to his elected successor at the end of the academic year.

Herald 70:56, 27 Apr 1995

What this is

A year-by-year record of student government at Western Kentucky University, from the ratification of the Associated Students constitution in April 1966 to the present. Every entry names the source it came from.

Corrections

The names here start with the plaque in the SGA Chambers, and the plaque is known to be wrong in places. Where the archive disagrees with it, this site follows the archive and records the change on the corrections page. If you served, or you know that a year here is wrong, the project wants to hear from you: people who held an office can be given the keys to their own year on the contribute page.

What it rests on

Everything here comes from what is publicly available online: the digitised Herald and SGA's own papers on TopSCHOLAR, the Talisman yearbooks, wkuherald.com, the university's news pages and the Wayback Machine. That is a fraction of what happened. Student government has met most weeks for sixty years, and the greater part of it was never written down, or was written down on paper that has never been scanned. A thin year here is a thin record, not a quiet one.

Reuse

Text on this site is the work of the project. Photographs, documents and legislation are reproduced from Western Kentucky University's own open archives and from the College Heights Herald and the Talisman; each item names the collection, issue and page it came from and links to the original.
