Standing of the record
Moved here from the year the plaque gives. The correction and its evidence are set out below.

The chambers plaque carries two consecutive plates for this year: "Janet 'Nicki' Seay" and "Nicki Taylor." Herald coverage shows one continuous officeholder, not a succession: she opened the year as "Nicki Seay" (Herald, 28 Aug and 25 Sep 2014), was already being called "Nicki Taylor" by the very next issue on 15 Oct 2014, and was still SGA president under that name through the 2 Apr 2015 election that chose her successor - no election, resignation or hand-off for a second officeholder appears anywhere in that span. Treated here as a single president who changed her surname partway through the term.

Nicki Seay had been SGA's Legislative Research Chair in 2012-13 and ran successfully for administrative vice president in the spring 2013 election. She took office as SGA president in August 2014, a Crofton senior, alongside Administrative Vice President Liz Koehler and Executive Vice President Nolan Miles, and named student involvement, retention and graduation her priorities. Her administration opened the year by expanding SGA's summer and winter need-based scholarship program from $6,000 to $21,000.

By the middle of October 2014 the Herald was referring to the same sitting president as Nicki Taylor; no election or resignation separates the two names in the paper's coverage. As Taylor, she presided over a fall senate that voted down a nonsectarian prayer resolution, saw its smoke-free campus resolution vetoed by the Executive Cabinet and sustained on a failed override vote, and passed a resolution backing changes to the campus alcohol policy. She addressed WKU's December 2014 commencement as SGA president, telling graduates, "You have the rest of your life to look forward to."

In the spring semester the senate under Taylor passed a limited-smoking-campus resolution and a resolution backing a $10 million parking garage funded by a new student fee; President Gary Ransdell responded formally to both. She also sat, by virtue of the office, on the WKU Board of Regents, and abstained from an Executive Council vote on the smoking measure because of that seat. Her term ended with the 2 April 2015 election of Jay Todd Richey as her successor.

The year, in order41

28 August 2014
#

SGA opened the year with a $21,000 scholarship program

SGA began the year under new president Nicki Seay, a Crofton senior, working alongside Administrative Vice President Liz Koehler and Executive Vice President Nolan Miles. The association expanded its summer and winter term need-based scholarship program from the previous year's $6,000 to $21,000, funded in part by an anonymous donation. Seay named student involvement, retention and graduation her first priorities.

Herald, 28 Aug 2014

2 September 2014campus
#

Downing Student Union celebrated its community opening

WKU held a ceremonial community opening for Downing Student Union after a two-year, $49 million renovation; the building had opened to students the week before. Students paid $36 million of the cost through a $70-per-semester fee, and the renovation began as an SGA initiative in 2011. SGA President Nicki Seay attended alongside President Gary Ransdell and other officials.

Herald, 2 Sep 2014

3 September 2014
#

Five new committee chairs take over at first fall meeting

SGA's first meeting of 2014-15 opened in new senate chambers with the induction of eight new members and new committee chairs: Josh Knight for Academic Affairs, Kasey Glasgow for Student Affairs, James Line for Public Relations, Paige Settles for Legislative Research and Tyler Scaff for Campus Improvement. President Nicki Seay said she was looking forward to the year's leadership.

Herald, 3 Sep 2014

9 September 2014Service
#

Preston Center memberships cut in half for sixty part-time students

The senate passed Funding for the Preston Membership Voucher Program, allocating $1,050 so that 60 part-time students could buy a semester membership to the Preston Center at half price. The semester fee for part-time students was $35, and a voucher brought it down to $17.50. It was one of two voucher bills passed that night.

$1,050 for 60 half-price memberships

Herald, 11 Sep 2014

9 September 2014Service
#

Fifty ID vouchers, restricted to students who had not lost a card

After suspending its bylaws to take the bill the same night, the senate passed Funding for the Identification Vouchers Program, allocating $500 to cover half the $20 cost of a WKU ID card for 50 students. The voucher was available only to students who had not previously lost an ID card.

$500 covering half the cost of 50 cards

Herald, 11 Sep 2014

9 September 2014
#

Senate passed a $138,500 budget for the year

The SGA budget for the year was passed at $138,500 at the same meeting that swore in three new senators and approved the Preston and identification voucher programmes. Seats remained open and election applications were due 15 September.

$138,500

Herald, 11 Sep 2014

16 September 2014Service
#

Transcript vouchers handed out first come, first served

Bill 3-14-F was the only bill to pass that night, allocating $500 to a programme covering the $7 cost of an official transcript request for 60 students, two vouchers to a student. Academic Affairs Committee Chair Josh Knight, who wrote the bill, said $7 might not seem much but some students could not carry the extra expense, and that SGA would consider adding vouchers if demand required it.

$500 for 60 vouchers at $7 each

Herald, 17 Sep 2014

23 September 2014Programme
#

A thirty-nine dollar sticker survey asked students what to fix

Bill 4-14-F allocated about $39 from legislative discretionary funds to the Campus Improvements Committee for flip charts and multi-coloured dots, to be used in a walk-up survey asking students what they wanted improved on campus. Author Elizabeth McGrew, a Henderson graduate student, said online surveys took too much of students' time and drew fewer responses than a visible one would.

about $39 for flip charts and dot stickers

Herald, 25 Sep 2014

25 September 2014
#

Fall election drew 742 voters and seated 17 senators

SGA announced its fall election results around 12:20 a.m. in the Senate Chamber of Downing Student Union, before a crowd of about 15 people. There were 742 voters and 17 senators elected, including three freshman senators led by Emily Pride with 281 votes. President Nicki Seay called the election very competitive and said not everyone who ran won a seat. The senators-elect were sworn in at the 30 September meeting.

Herald, 25 Sep 2014

30 September 2014Programme
#

Study Away scholarships funded at $1,200 for a second year

Bill 6-14-F passed with no debate, allocating $1,200 from Academic Affairs Committee funding for additional scholarships for students applying to Study Away, the programme for travel within the United States rather than abroad. Committee chair Joshua Knight said the same amount had been given the previous year and had been used up, so the committee thought it fair to repeat it.

$1,200, the same as the year before

October 2014
#

Commuter focus group brings parking fixes to the senate

Campus Improvements Committee Chair Tyler Scaff reported that a commuter focus group had proposed solutions to campus parking problems, including adding a function to the iWKU app that would list each lot with the number of spaces available. A South Campus park and ride was also proposed. Scaff said the committee was working on other projects including an SGA garden plot at the Office of Sustainability.

Herald, 1 Oct 2014

15 October 2014
#

Senate defeated the nonsectarian prayer policy resolution

After more than 20 minutes of debate the senate defeated Resolution 4-14-F, which would have supported adopting the University Senate's nonsectarian public prayer policy for university-sponsored events. Chief of Staff Seth Church explained that a nonsectarian prayer differs from a sectarian one referring to a specific deity. President Nicki Taylor said the senate was split three ways, among nonsectarian prayer, sectarian prayer and no prayer at all, and that the division sank the resolution. The same night senators passed Bill 9-14-F allocating $250 in organizational aid to the Phi Gamma Delta fraternity.

Herald, 15 Oct 2014

21 October 2014
#

Senate asked WKU to let unused meal swipes roll over

Resolution 5-14-F passed with no debate, supporting an amendment to university dining policy so that meal swipes left at the end of a week could carry forward as meal plans or meal plan dollars rather than being forfeited on Sunday. Author William Berry said students paid for those swipes and should not lose them. He said Aramark was running a feasibility test on whether meal plan prices would have to rise to make a rollover work.

Herald, 22 Oct 2014

22 October 2014
#

SGA funds an emergency food program for students with frozen accounts

Bill 10-14-F passed without debate, allocating $500 from legislative discretionary funds toward the $3,600 Emergency Food Pilot Program. The program gave the first 50 students who could show their university account was locked 10 free meals through Fresh Food Company waivers. Author William Berry said it would let those students eat for a few days while they worked on paying their bills.

Herald, 22 Oct 2014

29 October 2014
#

Glasgow rewrites its constitution after struggling to keep members

Amendments to Glasgow's SGA constitution received their first read on the main campus. Chief of Staff Seth Church said Glasgow's constitution had been modeled on the main campus document and that the regional campus had struggled with student involvement and retaining SGA members, so it was decided to change the constitution drastically. Glasgow's SGA had already voted to approve the amendments, leaving the main campus senate to ratify them.

Herald, 29 Oct 2014

30 October 2014
#

President establishes a Cultural and Diversity Affairs Committee

SGA created a Cultural and Diversity Affairs Committee chaired by Seth Church, who developed the idea for a leadership practicum focused on diversity within SGA and on campus. Church said he collaborated with President Nicki Taylor to create it. Taylor said making SGA more diverse had been an ongoing conversation since her first year and that establishing ad hoc committees was a power she held as president.

Herald, 30 Oct 2014

11 November 2014
#

Senate adopted amendments to the Glasgow campus constitution

Resolution 6-14-F was first read on 28 October 2014 and passed on 11 November. It stated that the Glasgow Student Body, incorporated as a regional affiliate two years earlier, had been unable to raise enough student interest to fill and maintain an executive cabinet, student senate and judicial council. Seth Church wrote it, with GSBA president Samantha Johnson listed as a contact.

SGA Resolution 6-14-F

12 November 2014
#

Senate introduces resolution over being left out of the Gatton housing decision

SGA senator Cole McDowell introduced a resolution responding to Housing and Residence Life's decision to move Gatton Academy students into Bates-Runner Hall, pushing current Bates residents into Gilbert Hall, without consulting SGA first. The resolution was postponed to the following week's meeting to await the outcome of a joint SGA, Gatton and Housing meeting called by Campus Improvements Committee Chair Tyler Scaff. McDowell said the resolution was not a judgment on the decision itself, but a statement that senators had not been given the chance to represent students' views on it.

Herald, 12 Nov 2014

20 November 2014
#

Executive Cabinet vetoed the smoke-free campus resolution hours after it passed

The senate passed Resolution 11-14-F, supporting a smoke-free campus, on a 14-10 vote, and the SGA Executive Cabinet vetoed it after the meeting. Chief of Staff Seth Church argued a ban was unfair to students required to live on campus for two years, while author William Berry said he left the location of any smoking areas to the administration and that the ban infringed no student's rights. Senator Haley Jones countered that smoking is legal and that students over 18 can buy tobacco. The policy would not have covered other tobacco products or electronic cigarettes, and senators kept the power to override the veto with a two-thirds vote.

Herald, 20 Nov 2014

4 December 2014
#

Senate failed to override the smoking veto and passed an alcohol resolution

At the final meeting of the fall semester, which ran two and a half hours, the senate failed to overturn the Executive Cabinet's veto of the smoke-free campus resolution. President Nicki Taylor read the cabinet's official veto statement, which cited enforcement, housing requirements and practicality. The senate then passed Resolution 13-14-F, supporting amendments to WKU's alcohol policy and Student Code of Conduct to let students 21 and older purchase and possess alcohol on campus. Co-author Jody Dahmer called the existing policy ineffective and hard to enforce fairly.

Herald, 4 Dec 2014

14 December 2014
#

President Taylor addressed December graduates

SGA President Nicki Taylor spoke at WKU's December commencement, telling graduates to celebrate what they had accomplished: "You have the rest of your life to look forward to." She had opened the year in August under her previous surname, Seay, and was going by Taylor in Herald coverage by mid-October.

Herald, 14 Dec 2014

30 January 2015Service
#

Student Legal Services Clinic opened with an SGA resolution behind it

WKU's Student Legal Services Clinic was set to open on Friday 30 January in Tate Page Hall room 252, after four years of on-and-off preparation by paralegal studies professor Julie Shadoan. It was open to all students including part-time, transfer, international and regional campus students, drew on about twelve volunteer attorneys, and charged $30 an hour against roughly $300 an hour for an average attorney. SGA President Nicki Taylor said SGA had passed a resolution supporting the clinic and the creation of a $4 student fee to pay for an in-house attorney.

$30 an hour against about $300 for an average attorney

Herald, 29 Jan 2015

3 February 2015
#

Limited smoking campus resolution passed 13-11

SGA passed Resolution 1-15-S, supporting a limited smoking campus, by a 13-11 vote. The resolution would restrict tobacco use to 39 designated areas that the Administrative Council would gradually phase out over three years, while leaving electronic cigarettes and smokeless tobacco unrestricted. Co-author Seth Church said it was roughly the fifth version of the resolution since the smoking debate began the previous semester.

Herald, 3 Feb 2015

3 February 2015
#

SGA backs limiting smoking to 39 areas and phasing them out

The senate voted on Resolution 1-15-S supporting a limited smoking campus, restricting tobacco use to 39 designated areas. The resolution called for the Administrative Council to create a plan reducing those areas and gradually phasing them out over three years, and would not have limited electronic cigarettes or smokeless tobacco. The existing policy allowed smoking anywhere on campus at least 30 feet from buildings.

Herald, 3 Feb 2015

4 February 2015
#

President Taylor abstained on smoking vote as a sitting regent

The Bowling Green Daily News reported that Nicki Taylor, in addition to serving as SGA president, sat on the WKU Board of Regents, and abstained from an Executive Council vote on the smoking-campus proposal because of that seat.

Bowling Green Daily News, 4 Feb 2015

10 February 2015
#

SGA pursues new student fees; Ransdell calls even $4 difficult

SGA said it would pursue further fee resolutions after passing one the previous semester supporting a $4 per year fee for the Student Legal Services Clinic, and had begun asking students about a roughly $30 fee to fund an estimated $10 million parking structure. President Nicki Taylor said the ideas grew out of student interest in conversation with President Gary Ransdell. Ransdell said approving even a $4 fee would be difficult and that he would be reluctant to take it to the Board of Regents.

Herald, 10 Feb 2015

3 March 2015
#

SGA backed a $10 million parking garage funded by a $30 per semester tuition rise

SGA passed a resolution supporting construction of a $10 million parking structure on part of Creason Lot, funded by a $30-per-semester tuition increase. Authors Zach Jones, Brian Chism and Nathan Cherry estimated the roughly 500-car garage would take about 18 months to build, and the resolution suggested a temporary gravel lot on the site of Tate Page Hall after its anticipated demolition. President Nicki Taylor said the structure had been before the parking and transportation committee for the whole academic year. Chief of Staff Seth Church objected that the plan was rushed and left out the regional campuses.

Herald, 3 Mar 2015

3 March 2015
#

Bylaw amendment required roll-call voting and posted minutes

Resolution 3-15-S, first read 24 February 2015 and passed 3 March, added Bylaw 2.7 requiring every vote in any branch to be taken by roll call or electronic voting, recorded in the minutes, and posted by the Director of Information Technology or the Secretary of the Senate within one week. The text set the amendment to take effect on 17 March 2015. Zach Jones and Seth Church wrote it.

SGA Resolution 3-15-S, Resolution to Adopt an Amendment to the Bylaws to Increase Accountability

4 March 2015
#

Ransdell answered four SGA resolutions in an email to faculty and staff

President Gary Ransdell emailed faculty and staff his official response to four SGA resolutions, on limited smoking, amending the alcohol policy and Student Code of Conduct for students over 21, a new parking structure and a student fee for WKU Student Legal Services. On smoking he announced a six-person ad hoc committee of students, faculty and staff to recommend how to implement a limited smoking campus by the end of 2017. On alcohol he said a restaurant facility would be built in the Garrett Conference Center where beer, and possibly wine, could be served in the evenings. He said the parking and legal services fees would need Board of Regents and Council on Postsecondary Education approval.

Herald, 4 Mar 2015

18 March 2015
#

Breast milk expression resolution passes unanimously without debate

Resolution 4-15-S, supporting creation and maintenance of private areas on campus where students, faculty and staff could express breast milk, passed unanimously and without debate. Chief of Staff Seth Church, who wrote it, said a non-traditional student who had just had her third child brought the problem to him and found the existing unlocked room in a department hall uncomfortable. Church said he wanted no fewer than three permanent spaces.

Herald, 18 Mar 2015

24 March 2015Programme
#

Five hundred dollars to promote the Community Farmers Market

Bill 4-15-S, Funding to Support the Community Farmers Market, passed unanimously and without debate, allocating $500 from legislative discretionary funds to the Office of Sustainability to promote the market to students. Co-author Brian Chism said the point was to let students know what food options were available to them.

$500 from legislative discretionary funds

Herald, 25 Mar 2015

2 April 2015
#

Jay Todd Richey won the SGA presidency, 874 votes to 524

Jay Todd Richey defeated senator Brian Chism for SGA president, 63 percent to 37 percent, or 874 votes to 524, with results announced about 20 minutes after TopNet polls closed at 11:59 p.m. Wednesday. Nolan Miles ran unopposed for a second term as executive vice president, and Liz Koehler won the administrative vice president race with 66 percent over J. William Berry. Twenty of 34 senatorial candidates won seats.

Herald, 2 Apr 2015

2 April 2015
#

Campaign spending caps range from $350 for president to $50 for senators

Under the spring 2014 election packet, SGA presidential candidates could not spend more than $350, executive vice president candidates $300, administrative vice president candidates $250 and senatorial candidates $50. President Nicki Taylor said she ran unopposed and did not spend a cent, but pointed to the contested 2013 race between Cain Alvey and Keyana Boka. Taylor said Alvey spent every dollar of his $350 limit.

Herald, 2 Apr 2015

16 April 2015
#

An election result challenged and upheld

Anonymous accusations alleged Jay Todd Richey had breached election codes on poster placement. The judicial council warned him against future violations and confirmed he had rightfully won.

Herald, April 2015

22 April 2015
#

Two hours of debate over an African Student Union reimbursement

Bill 8-15-S, allocating $500 to the WKU African Student Union as reimbursement for attending a conference at the University of Kentucky, passed 12 to four with many senators abstaining, after a meeting that ran two hours and ended with fewer senators present. Acting president Henry Nottinson said the reimbursement would lessen the burden on members who had paid out of pocket. He said the group did not have enough money to keep going.

Herald, 22 Apr 2015

23 April 2015
#

Former senator publishes a letter alleging corruption in organizational aid

Jody Dahmer, who had joined SGA as a freshman, published a letter to the editor arguing that a precedent set during the past session had damaged the organizational aid process. He described the standing system under which organizations could receive up to $500 per academic year, reviewed by the administrative vice president and senators under existing bylaws with forms posted on the SGA website. Dahmer wrote that organizations met the committee outside the senate and that reports were then forwarded and voted on quickly.

Herald, 23 Apr 2015

28 April 2015
#

SGA publishes open letter defending its election process

After a competitive 2015 executive and senate election drew heavy Herald coverage and produced appeals over campaign posters, SGA published an open letter as a letter to the editor thanking the paper for its attention, defending the Judicial Affairs Council's handling of the appeals as fair and transparent, and announcing a coming "rebranding period" meant to improve diversity, transparency and public understanding of SGA's role. Director of Public Relations Sawyer Coffey was listed as the contact for questions.

Herald, 28 Apr 2015

29 April 2015
#

SGA funds a $825.95 surveillance camera for the DSU ATMs

Bill 10-15-S allocated $825.95 to buy and install a surveillance video camera on the ground floor of the Downing Student Union near the ATMs, passing 15-5 after debate. Author J. William Berry said a camera would be far cheaper than relocating the ATMs to an area with more foot traffic. Campus improvements committee chair Tyler Scaff questioned the plan, and Berry noted DSU closes at midnight.

Herald, 29 Apr 2015

5 May 2015
#

Both attempts to change discretionary funding died at the final meeting

At the last meeting of the spring the senate took up two competing approaches to Legislative Discretionary Aid. Resolution 11-15-S by Senator Jody Dahmer, which would have stopped SGA giving discretionary money directly to individual students and organisations and pushed them to the Organizational Aid Committee, failed 11-7 for want of a two-thirds majority; Dahmer argued that without a system, senators could end up promising kickbacks for votes, while Senator Josh Knight opposed any blanket ban on recognising special cases. Bill 15-15-S, which would have split the remaining discretionary money between five organisations that had missed the organizational aid deadline, tied 7-7 and Speaker of the Senate Jay Todd Richey broke the tie against it.

Herald, 6 May 2015

27 May 2015
#

Richey posts the SGA budget publicly and says he has no figures

President Jay Todd Richey used social media to be, in his words, "very transparent with the WKU Student Government Association budget." SGA had not yet received its budget for the coming fiscal year, and the Executive Cabinet controlled the budget over the summer. Richey said he had not been given a concrete amount of how much money remained from that year.

Herald, 27 May 2015

What this is

A year-by-year record of student government at Western Kentucky University, from the ratification of the Associated Students constitution in April 1966 to the present. Every entry names the source it came from.

Corrections

The names here start with the plaque in the SGA Chambers, and the plaque is known to be wrong in places. Where the archive disagrees with it, this site follows the archive and records the change on the corrections page. If you served, or you know that a year here is wrong, the project wants to hear from you: people who held an office can be given the keys to their own year on the contribute page.

What it rests on

Everything here comes from what is publicly available online: the digitised Herald and SGA's own papers on TopSCHOLAR, the Talisman yearbooks, wkuherald.com, the university's news pages and the Wayback Machine. That is a fraction of what happened. Student government has met most weeks for sixty years, and the greater part of it was never written down, or was written down on paper that has never been scanned. A thin year here is a thin record, not a quiet one.

Text on this site is the work of the project. Photographs, documents and legislation are reproduced from Western Kentucky University's own open archives and from the College Heights Herald and the Talisman; each item names the collection, issue and page it came from and links to the original.
