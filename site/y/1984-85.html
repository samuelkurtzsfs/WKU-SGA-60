1983-84

Two-year plate. Either re-elected or the plaque compresses two terms.

Smith presided over a productive opening to the 1984-85 year. By the third week of September the Herald reported that four ASG proposals had been made reality, and the congress had begun studying a student book exchange, which became the signature project of his final year.

By mid-year the record was mixed. The book exchange venture appeared likely to take a summer pause, and the organization was weighing whether to fight cuts to education funding.

His term ended in April 1985. The Herald covered his exit with a news story headlined that Smith was ending his term as ASG president, an editorial crediting his enthusiasm with reviving interest in the organization, and an editorial cartoon. The SGA plaque records him on a two-year 1983-85 plate.

Jack Smith first won the presidency of the Associated Student Government in a race the College Heights Herald reported under the headline "Jack Smith Wins Presidency of Associated Student Government," in the paper's fifty-eighth volume. That win put him in the office he would go on to hold across more than one term.

Smith and the Herald did not always get along. One issue in the paper's fifty-ninth volume indexed an item headed "Jack Smith, Associated Student Government Thrown Away" together with a letter written by Smith himself under the title "Cartoon Aggravates," showing that he answered the paper's cartoonists in print rather than letting the criticism stand.

His time in office closed in April 1985, when Mack Humphreys wrote up Smith ending his term as Associated Student Government president. The Herald ran a second piece in the same issue crediting Smith's enthusiasm with reviving interest in ASG, and Kevin Knapp marked the departure with an editorial cartoon on Smith and the organisation.

The year, in order12

20 September 1984
#

Four proposals made reality in the third week of class

The Herald reported that four Associated Student Government proposals had already been carried out, and that the congress was beginning a study of a student book exchange. The exchange went on to become the running project of the year.

Herald 60:7, 20 Sep 1984 PDF

27 September 1984
#

The grade scale proposal is denied

ASG's proposal for a new grade scale was denied, one week after the congress's early run of successes. It was the first setback of the fall reported in the Herald's coverage.

Herald 60:9, 27 Sep 1984 PDF

18 October 1984
#

ASG wants less general education

The congress went on record wanting a reduction in general education requirements. It was an early instance of the body intervening in curriculum policy, three years before the fights over the General Education Task Force.

Herald 60:15, 18 Oct 1984 PDF

8 November 1984
#

LaMont Jones drops out of a revote

LaMont Jones withdrew from an ASG revote, and the same issue carried an editorial cartoon on the organization. A disputed election requiring a revote marked the low point of the fall semester.

Herald 60:19, 8 Nov 1984 PDF

31 January 1985
#

Education cuts loom and the book exchange stalls

ASG weighed a fight against state education cuts, while its book exchange venture looked likely to pause for the summer. Both stories ran in the same late-January issue.

Herald 60:34, 31 Jan 1985 PDF

14 February 1985
#

Apathy as an editorial cartoon

'Voter Apathy Can be Solved' ran alongside a cartoon on election apathy and a report that a 24-hour open house was under discussion.

Herald 60:38, 14 Feb 1985 PDF

28 February 1985
#

Four file for congress president

A fourth candidate filed for ASG congress president and a debate was set, as the spring filing period opened. The same issue carried letters both applauding an ASG bill and requesting its support.

Herald 60:42, 28 Feb 1985 PDF

26 March 1985campus
#

ASG candidates debated ahead of the spring election

Mack Humphreys covered a debate in which Associated Student Government candidates aired their views before the spring vote. The same issue of the Herald carried student letters backing Greg Elder, Chris Watkins and Mitchell McKinney, showing that the race to succeed Jack Smith was contested.

Herald 60:46, 26 Mar 1985

28 March 1985
#

McKinney and Watkins take the primary

Mitchell McKinney and Chris Watkins rode their experience to victory in the ASG primary. The congress also passed a resolution on grade point averages the same week.

Herald 60:47, 28 Mar 1985 PDF

5 April 1985
#

A reader disputed the Herald's editorial on ASG

The Herald printed a letter from Luis Dickson taking issue with an editorial the paper had run about Associated Student Government. The letter appeared in the middle of the spring campaign season.

Herald 60:50, 5 Apr 1985

11 April 1985
#

Jack Smith ends his term

The Herald marked the end of Jack Smith's presidency with a news story, an editorial crediting his enthusiasm with reviving interest in ASG, and an editorial cartoon. Mitchell McKinney had been elected the new president the week before.

Herald 60:51, 11 Apr 1985 PDF

11 April 1985campus
#

Jack Smith ended his term as ASG president

Mack Humphreys reported on Jack Smith finishing his run as Associated Student Government president. A companion article in the same issue said Smith's enthusiasm had revived student interest in ASG, and Kevin Knapp drew an editorial cartoon on Smith and the organisation.

Herald 60:51, 11 Apr 1985

What this is

A year-by-year record of student government at Western Kentucky University, from the ratification of the Associated Students constitution in April 1966 to the present. Every entry names the source it came from.

Corrections

The names here start with the plaque in the SGA Chambers, and the plaque is known to be wrong in places. Where the archive disagrees with it, this site follows the archive and records the change on the corrections page. If you served, or you know that a year here is wrong, the project wants to hear from you through the Student Government Association office.

Reuse

Text on this site is the work of the project. Photographs, documents and legislation are reproduced from Western Kentucky University's own open archives and from the College Heights Herald and the Talisman; each item names the collection, issue and page it came from and links to the original.
