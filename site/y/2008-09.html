Johnathon Boles president and student regent, 2008-09

Confirmed against Herald election coverage. Boles led the "Red Towel Party" ticket - with Theodore Harden, Reagan Gilley, Skylar Johnson, Kayla Shelton and Kevin Smiley also on the March 2008 candidate slate - in the spring 2008 SGA election. After the vote, Skylar Jordan filed a complaint against the Red Towel Party; the SGA council took no action on it on 22 April 2008, letting the result stand. In office for the February 2008 Frankfort rally, after which roughly fifty senators each filed a written reflection - the richest first-person source in the collection. The Herald's 2013 retrospective records that Boles resigned in January 2009.

Johnathon Boles ran for SGA president in spring 2008 on the "Red Towel Party" ticket, a slate that also carried Theodore Harden, Reagan Gilley, Skylar Johnson, Kayla Shelton and Kevin Smiley as candidates for other executive and senate offices. After Boles's ticket won, Skylar Jordan filed a complaint against the Red Towel Party; the SGA council took no action on it on 22 April 2008, and the result stood. Boles, a junior from Elizabethtown, served as SGA president and student regent for 2008-09. In November 2008, Gov. Steve Beshear appointed him and fellow SGA member Kevin Smiley to the student committee of the governor's College Access Work Group on college affordability, and Boles traveled to Frankfort for its first meeting, where he raised the burden of textbook costs. "I'm honored the governor chose me to represent WKU," he said.

In December 2008, Boles led SGA through its first mid-year budget cut, after a state shortfall required the university to trim about 4 percent. He and Administrative Vice President Reagan Gilley cut $4,100 from SGA's $110,000 budget while protecting scholarships and Provide-A-Ride, and Boles addressed the senate about the cuts, saying he would keep fighting for the affordability of higher education. He also worked to arrange charter buses to a Feb. 3 rally in Frankfort against cuts to higher education.

Boles resigned on Jan. 30, 2009, for health reasons, midway through his term. Executive Vice President Kayla Shelton took his place as president but not as student regent, and the student body went 26 days without a regent until Reagan Gilley won a special election on Feb. 25. Gilley said he planned to contact Boles the day after his win to get up to date on Board of Regents business.

"That's what they decided, so I support it," Shelton said, adding that her main focus remained serving the student body.

Reagan Gilley student regent, 2008-09

Elected student regent in a special election held 25-26 February 2009, beating Nate Eaton 477 to 224 of 701 votes cast. Then the administrative vice president, Gilley took the Board seat that Johnathon Boles vacated when he resigned the presidency on 30 January 2009; Kayla Shelton succeeded Boles as president but did not take the regent seat, so for the rest of that year the two offices were held by two people. The Board of Regents minutes of 24 April 2009 record the constitutional oath being administered to him as student regent. The split closed at the regular summer handover: the Board's roll call of 31 July 2009 lists Kevin Smiley as student regent.

Reagan Gilley was a Pineville senior and the Student Government Association's administrative vice president when he won the special election for student regent held 25-26 February 2009, filling the seat left empty for 26 days after President Johnathon Boles resigned on 30 January for health reasons. He beat Fairdale senior Nate Eaton, the speaker of the senate, 477 votes to 224 of the 701 cast, and Chief Justice Corey Bewley announced the verified result at 12:17 a.m. Thursday in the SGA office. Gilley had campaigned on short notice through a Facebook group, announcements in classes and a YouTube video built around the Rick Astley song "Never Gonna Give You Up," and after the count he handed Eaton a plaque he had bought at Kroger that day. He said he planned to contact Boles the next day to catch up on Board of Regents business and then to contact Faculty Regent Patricia Minter, and that he wished more students had voted. "I am very relieved but excited for things to come," he said.

Gilley took the constitutional oath of office from Board Chair Lois Gray at the second quarterly meeting on 24 April 2009, where President Gary Ransdell presented him with the regent's pin, and he answered the roll call as student regent. He seconded Larry Zielke's motion to approve the naming of designated facilities and rooms, and seconded Yevette Haskins's motion to accept the 2008-09 second quarter statement of revenues and expenditures for filing. On the 2009-10 tuition and fees schedule, which carried a 4 percent increase, Gilley asked to amend the motion to strike the proposed per credit hour fee on course loads above 15 hours; the amended motion was seconded by Zielke and carried unanimously. He then moved to add a drop/add fee of $50 per transaction to the schedule, which Zielke seconded and which also carried unanimously, and afterwards told the Board that tuition increases were a major concern of the student body and that "for the record, I meant to vote in opposition to the tuition increase." During a long exchange over personnel actions and campus morale, in which the faculty and staff regents described resignations and low morale, Gilley said that on behalf of the student body there was concern about fewer staff being available for classes. The minutes list the Board's next meetings as a special budget meeting on 19 June 2009 and the third quarterly meeting on 31 July 2009.

His time on the Board was over by the summer. The minutes of the special budget meeting of 19 June 2009 record the constitutional oath of office being administered to Kevin Smiley as student regent, again by Chair Lois Gray and with the regent's pin presented by President Ransdell, and the roll call for that meeting lists Smiley rather than Gilley. Gilley's name does not appear in those minutes at all, so the quarterly meeting of 24 April 2009 was the only Board meeting that fell within his tenure, and the record of his service as student regent runs to that single day of votes. Gilley's name does not appear in those minutes, so the handover had happened by 19 June 2009, earlier than the 31 July roll call previously recorded here.

4 September 2008campus
#

Shots fired outside Pearce-Ford Tower put dorms on lockdown

Campus police responded to a report of shots fired at about 3 a.m. Saturday in the Pearce-Ford Tower parking lot, where a witness said a man got out of a vehicle, fired a gun and drove away. Police found shell casings but no damage, no one was hurt, and all dorms went into lockdown after the incident.

Herald, 4 Sep 2008

9 September 2008
#

Senate resolved in support of an emergency communications system

Resolution 01-08-F was a resolution in support of a crisis management system for the university. It came five days after shots were reported outside Pearce-Ford Tower and six weeks before the October campus lockdown.

SGA Resolution 01-08-F, 9 September 2008

16 September 2008Concert
#

A Nappy Roots concert came before the senate

The senate minutes of 16 September 2008 record a Nappy Roots concert on the agenda alongside football games, Western Wednesdays and appointments, and the minutes of 23 September record it again together with the campus safety walk and Buy-a-Book. The archive shows the concert being arranged; it does not record whether it took place or what it cost.

SGA senate minutes, 16 September 2008

18 September 2008
#

Fall senate election drew above-average turnout

SGA seated nine new student senators after a two-day fall election in which 18 candidates competed. Seven seats had been open, four reserved for freshmen, and two more opened when two senators resigned the Monday before the vote. A total of 687 students voted, which President Johnathon Boles said was higher than the average for a fall election.

Herald, 18 Sep 2008

13 November 2008
#

Boles and Smiley appointed to governor's college affordability group

Gov. Steve Beshear appointed SGA President Johnathon Boles and SGA member Kevin Smiley to the student committee of his College Access Work Group, which was charged with recommending ways to make Kentucky higher education more affordable. The two traveled to Frankfort for the committee's first meeting, where they raised the impact of textbook costs, which Boles put at an average of $800 a year.

Daily News, 13 Nov 2008

19 November 2008
#

Senate voted to launch a Textbook Subsidy Program

The SGA senate voted to contribute $500 to start a Textbook Subsidy Program, an effort developed with the University Bookstore, which pledged another $500, to build an endowment that would lower textbook costs for students. The senate also created a program board of nearly 20 members, including Boles, bookstore representatives and members from each college, to work out funding and distribution.

Daily News, 19 Nov 2008

4 December 2008
#

SGA made its first mid-year budget cut

Facing a state shortfall that required the university to cut about 4 percent of its budget, SGA cut $4,100 from its $110,000 budget, the first time it had cut its budget in the middle of an academic year. Boles and Administrative Vice President Reagan Gilley chose to cut a $2,600 surplus from the fall retreat and $1,500 from organizational aid, protecting scholarships and Provide-A-Ride. Boles said he was working to get charter buses to send students to a Feb. 3 rally in Frankfort against higher education budget cuts.

Herald, 4 Dec 2008

29 January 2009campus
#

Ice storm hit campus, injuring a student and closing regional campuses

A winter storm coated campus in ice during the week of Jan. 27, part of the second-largest power outage recorded in Kentucky. A falling tree limb struck a sophomore near Grise Hall, who was treated and released, and WKU canceled classes at its Elizabethtown, Owensboro, Glasgow, Fort Knox and Radcliff campuses as well as evening classes on the main campus. AT&T cell outages kept officials from reaching students by text message.

Herald, 29 Jan 2009

12 February 2009
#

Judicial Council ordered a special student regent election over executive dissent

After Boles' resignation left the student regent seat empty, the SGA Judicial Council voted 2-1 on Feb. 10 to hold a special election on Feb. 24 and 25. Chief Justice Lisa Kappler cast the dissenting vote, calling the constitution's requirement a gray area, and Executive Vice President Skylar Jordan presented a letter arguing the election was unnecessary. President Kayla Shelton said she supported the council's decision.

Herald, 12 Feb 2009

12 February 2009
#

Chief Justice Lisa Kappler resigned

SGA Chief Justice Lisa Kappler resigned Thursday night in a letter sent to President Kayla Shelton and student activities director Charley Pride, saying she wanted to spend more time on school and family. Shelton said she hoped the senate would vote on a new chief justice at the next meeting because the special election for student regent was two weeks away.

Herald, 12 Feb 2009

24 February 2009Other
#

Senate refused an extra $82.27 for Reduce Your Use water bottles

A bill passed on 18 November 2008 had given Housing and Residence Life $650 to buy 420 water bottles as prizes for the Reduce Your Use contest, a competition between dorms to cut energy use and increase recycling, won by Minton Hall. When the bottles came in $82.27 over the quote, the senate voted nine to thirteen on 24 February 2009 against covering the difference. Administrative vice president Reagan Gilley said he had not wanted to take the money from the SGA budget without asking the senate.

$650 granted; a further $82.27 refused

Herald, 26 Feb 2009 - "SGA denies money for HRL to purchase more water bottles" (Wayback)

26 February 2009
#

Reagan Gilley won the special student regent election

Administrative Vice President Reagan Gilley, a Pineville senior, defeated Speaker of the Senate Nate Eaton 477 votes to 224 in the special election for student regent, with 701 students voting. The seat had been vacant for 26 days after former President Johnathon Boles resigned on Jan. 30 for health reasons; Executive Vice President Kayla Shelton had taken his place as president but not as regent.

Herald, 26 Feb 2009

31 March 2009Programme
#

Organizational aid and $2,000 for The Come Up

At its meeting on 31 March 2009 the senate passed two bills awarding $9,029.04 in organizational aid to 21 student organizations. A third bill gave $2,000 to the Office of Diversity Programs for The Come Up, a programme to help black men build skills in academics, health and etiquette. The senate voted down a resolution urging WKU to enforce its existing campus smoking policy.

$9,029.04 in organizational aid to 21 groups; $2,000 to The Come Up

Herald, 31 Mar 2009 - "SGA awards org aid, announces another presidential candidate" (Wayback)

7 April 2009Programme
#

SGA put $500 into WKU's herb garden

On 7 April 2009 the senate passed a bill supporting WKU's herb garden, on the path between Downing University Center and South Lawn, giving the office of sustainability $500 to buy more varieties of plant. Recycling coordinator Cristin Lanham said the garden had been planted two years earlier so students, staff and faculty could pick herbs free, and that Fresh Food Company chefs sometimes cut from it. The garden was used to teach a workshop at the Campus Community Partnerships for Sustainability Conference that month.

$500 to the office of sustainability

College Heights Herald 84:44, 9 Apr 2009 - "SGA hopes chefs can reap what they sow" (TopSCHOLAR)

16 April 2009
#

Kevin Smiley won the SGA presidential election

The Herald reported Kevin Smiley's win in the spring SGA presidential election under the headline "All Smiles, Kevin Smiley Wins Student Government Association Election," ending Kayla Shelton's term as the president who had finished out Johnathon Boles's 2008-09 year. Smiley took office as president and student regent for 2009-10.

College Heights Herald 84:46, 16 Apr 2009

23 April 2009
#

SGA negotiated removal of proposed $30 per-credit-hour fee

SGA and university officials agreed to strike a proposed $30 fee for every credit hour over 15 from the Board of Regents agenda, replacing it with a proposal to raise the add/drop fee from $20 to $50. SGA canceled a planned protest after the compromise, and Student Regent Reagan Gilley called the outcome "really a win-win for everyone."

Herald, 23 Apr 2009

What this is

A year-by-year record of student government at Western Kentucky University, from the ratification of the Associated Students constitution in April 1966 to the present. Every entry names the source it came from.

Corrections

The names here start with the plaque in the SGA Chambers, and the plaque is known to be wrong in places. Where the archive disagrees with it, this site follows the archive and records the change on the corrections page. If you served, or you know that a year here is wrong, the project wants to hear from you: people who held an office can be given the keys to their own year on the contribute page.

What it rests on

Everything here comes from what is publicly available online: the digitised Herald and SGA's own papers on TopSCHOLAR, the Talisman yearbooks, wkuherald.com, the university's news pages and the Wayback Machine. That is a fraction of what happened. Student government has met most weeks for sixty years, and the greater part of it was never written down, or was written down on paper that has never been scanned. A thin year here is a thin record, not a quiet one.

Reuse

Text on this site is the work of the project. Photographs, documents and legislation are reproduced from Western Kentucky University's own open archives and from the College Heights Herald and the Talisman; each item names the collection, issue and page it came from and links to the original.
