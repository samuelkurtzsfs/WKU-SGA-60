Johnathon Boles president, 2008-09

Johnathon Boles ran for SGA president in spring 2008 on the "Red Towel Party" ticket, a slate that also carried Theodore Harden, Reagan Gilley, Skylar Johnson, Kayla Shelton and Kevin Smiley as candidates for other executive and senate offices. After Boles's ticket won, Skylar Jordan filed a complaint against the Red Towel Party; the SGA council took no action on it on 22 April 2008, and the result stood. Boles, a junior from Elizabethtown, served as SGA president and student regent for 2008-09. In November 2008, Gov. Steve Beshear appointed him and fellow SGA member Kevin Smiley to the student committee of the governor's College Access Work Group on college affordability, and Boles traveled to Frankfort for its first meeting, where he raised the burden of textbook costs. "I'm honored the governor chose me to represent WKU," he said.

In December 2008, Boles led SGA through its first mid-year budget cut, after a state shortfall required the university to trim about 4 percent. He and Administrative Vice President Reagan Gilley cut $4,100 from SGA's $110,000 budget while protecting scholarships and Provide-A-Ride, and Boles addressed the senate about the cuts, saying he would keep fighting for the affordability of higher education. He also worked to arrange charter buses to a Feb. 3 rally in Frankfort against cuts to higher education.

Boles resigned on Jan. 30, 2009, for health reasons, midway through his term. Executive Vice President Kayla Shelton took his place as president but not as student regent, and the student body went 26 days without a regent until Reagan Gilley won a special election on Feb. 25. Gilley said he planned to contact Boles the day after his win to get up to date on Board of Regents business.

Plaque plate reads "2009" and had been filed under 2009-10, overlapping Kevin Smiley there; moved to 2008-09. Confirmed: the Herald of April 2009 describes her as the sitting Student Government Association president, having taken office after Johnathon Boles resigned, and previously senator, student affairs chair, speaker of the senate and executive vice president.

Kayla Shelton ran on Johnathon Boles's "Red Towel Party" ticket in the spring 2008 SGA election and served as executive vice president for 2008-09. When Boles resigned as president and student regent on 30 Jan. 2009 for health reasons, Shelton succeeded him as president but not as student regent, leaving the regent seat vacant.

As president, Shelton backed the SGA Judicial Council's 2-1 decision to hold a special student regent election rather than leave the seat vacant, over the dissent of Executive Vice President Skylar Jordan, saying "That's what they decided, so I support it" and that her main focus remained serving the student body. She also accepted Chief Justice Lisa Kappler's resignation in mid-February, which came with the regent election two weeks away. Administrative Vice President Reagan Gilley won that special election on 25 Feb. 2009, taking over the regent's seat while Shelton continued as president through the rest of the term.

Shelton presided over the senate through the spring, including a Feb. 24 meeting where senators debated and rejected, 13 votes to 9, a bill to cover an extra $82.27 for water bottles SGA had earlier agreed to fund as prizes for a Housing and Residence Life energy-saving contest. Shelton said she thought it was great that the senate debated something it felt passionate about rather than casting "just a general yes or no."

Shelton did not continue as president into 2009-10; Kevin Smiley won the regular spring election for that term on 16 April 2009.

Kayla Shelton, who had been executive vice president, became SGA president after Johnathon Boles resigned, serving out the rest of the 2008-09 year. She inherited a dispute over whether to hold a special election for the vacant student regent seat, and when the Judicial Council voted 2-1 in February to hold one despite objections from other executives, she backed the decision. "That's what they decided, so I support it," Shelton said, adding that her main focus remained serving the student body.

The year, in order11

4 September 2008campus
#

Shots fired outside Pearce-Ford Tower put dorms on lockdown

Campus police responded to a report of shots fired at about 3 a.m. Saturday in the Pearce-Ford Tower parking lot, where a witness said a man got out of a vehicle, fired a gun and drove away. Police found shell casings but no damage, no one was hurt, and all dorms went into lockdown after the incident.

Herald, 4 Sep 2008

18 September 2008
#

Fall senate election drew above-average turnout

SGA seated nine new student senators after a two-day fall election in which 18 candidates competed. Seven seats had been open, four reserved for freshmen, and two more opened when two senators resigned the Monday before the vote. A total of 687 students voted, which President Johnathon Boles said was higher than the average for a fall election.

Herald, 18 Sep 2008

13 November 2008
#

Boles and Smiley appointed to governor's college affordability group

Gov. Steve Beshear appointed SGA President Johnathon Boles and SGA member Kevin Smiley to the student committee of his College Access Work Group, which was charged with recommending ways to make Kentucky higher education more affordable. The two traveled to Frankfort for the committee's first meeting, where they raised the impact of textbook costs, which Boles put at an average of $800 a year.

Daily News, 13 Nov 2008

19 November 2008
#

Senate voted to launch a Textbook Subsidy Program

The SGA senate voted to contribute $500 to start a Textbook Subsidy Program, an effort developed with the University Bookstore, which pledged another $500, to build an endowment that would lower textbook costs for students. The senate also created a program board of nearly 20 members, including Boles, bookstore representatives and members from each college, to work out funding and distribution.

Daily News, 19 Nov 2008

4 December 2008
#

SGA made its first mid-year budget cut

Facing a state shortfall that required the university to cut about 4 percent of its budget, SGA cut $4,100 from its $110,000 budget, the first time it had cut its budget in the middle of an academic year. Boles and Administrative Vice President Reagan Gilley chose to cut a $2,600 surplus from the fall retreat and $1,500 from organizational aid, protecting scholarships and Provide-A-Ride. Boles said he was working to get charter buses to send students to a Feb. 3 rally in Frankfort against higher education budget cuts.

Herald, 4 Dec 2008

29 January 2009campus
#

Ice storm hit campus, injuring a student and closing regional campuses

A winter storm coated campus in ice during the week of Jan. 27, part of the second-largest power outage recorded in Kentucky. A falling tree limb struck a sophomore near Grise Hall, who was treated and released, and WKU canceled classes at its Elizabethtown, Owensboro, Glasgow, Fort Knox and Radcliff campuses as well as evening classes on the main campus. AT&T cell outages kept officials from reaching students by text message.

Herald, 29 Jan 2009

12 February 2009
#

Judicial Council ordered a special student regent election over executive dissent

After Boles' resignation left the student regent seat empty, the SGA Judicial Council voted 2-1 on Feb. 10 to hold a special election on Feb. 24 and 25. Chief Justice Lisa Kappler cast the dissenting vote, calling the constitution's requirement a gray area, and Executive Vice President Skylar Jordan presented a letter arguing the election was unnecessary. President Kayla Shelton said she supported the council's decision.

Herald, 12 Feb 2009

12 February 2009
#

Chief Justice Lisa Kappler resigned

SGA Chief Justice Lisa Kappler resigned Thursday night in a letter sent to President Kayla Shelton and student activities director Charley Pride, saying she wanted to spend more time on school and family. Shelton said she hoped the senate would vote on a new chief justice at the next meeting because the special election for student regent was two weeks away.

Herald, 12 Feb 2009

26 February 2009
#

Reagan Gilley won the special student regent election

Administrative Vice President Reagan Gilley, a Pineville senior, defeated Speaker of the Senate Nate Eaton 477 votes to 224 in the special election for student regent, with 701 students voting. The seat had been vacant for 26 days after former President Johnathon Boles resigned on Jan. 30 for health reasons; Executive Vice President Kayla Shelton had taken his place as president but not as regent.

Herald, 26 Feb 2009

16 April 2009
#

Kevin Smiley won the SGA presidential election

The Herald reported Kevin Smiley's win in the spring SGA presidential election under the headline "All Smiles, Kevin Smiley Wins Student Government Association Election," ending Kayla Shelton's term as the president who had finished out Johnathon Boles's 2008-09 year. Smiley took office as president and student regent for 2009-10.

College Heights Herald 84:46, 16 Apr 2009

23 April 2009
#

SGA negotiated removal of proposed $30 per-credit-hour fee

SGA and university officials agreed to strike a proposed $30 fee for every credit hour over 15 from the Board of Regents agenda, replacing it with a proposal to raise the add/drop fee from $20 to $50. SGA canceled a planned protest after the compromise, and Student Regent Reagan Gilley called the outcome "really a win-win for everyone."

Herald, 23 Apr 2009

What this is

A year-by-year record of student government at Western Kentucky University, from the ratification of the Associated Students constitution in April 1966 to the present. Every entry names the source it came from.

Corrections

The names here start with the plaque in the SGA Chambers, and the plaque is known to be wrong in places. Where the archive disagrees with it, this site follows the archive and records the change on the corrections page. If you served, or you know that a year here is wrong, the project wants to hear from you through the Student Government Association office.

Text on this site is the work of the project. Photographs, documents and legislation are reproduced from Western Kentucky University's own open archives and from the College Heights Herald and the Talisman; each item names the collection, issue and page it came from and links to the original.
