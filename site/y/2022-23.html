Cole Bornefeld president, 2022-23

Office
President
Term recorded here
2022-23
Standing of the record
As recorded on the plaque. Not yet corroborated in the archive.

Elected April 2022 with 49% of 1,448 votes. In February 2023 the Speaker of the Senate brought a censure case against him over anti-transgender posts he had liked on Instagram; the Judicial Council voted unanimously against censure, and a Title IX report seeking his removal was filed two days later. Last name currently on the plaque.

Cole Bornefeld came to the presidency by an unusually fast route. He transferred to WKU from Volunteer State Community College as a junior, emailed then-President Matthew Wininger during his first week on campus, and was appointed a senator-at-large at their first meeting. He had not planned to run for president, but after senators urged him to, he formed a ticket the week of the filing deadline and won. He campaigned on programs he backed as a senator, including the Share-A-Swipe meal donation program and growing SGA's scholarship budget, which rose by $3,500 during his presidency. Adjusting to the office, he said it felt like "drinking out of a fire hose," and he sought advice from former student body presidents weekly.

Bornefeld opened the 2022-23 year with an executive retreat on Aug. 29 at the Cliff Todd Center, where his team set three priorities: a one-stop resource guide for students, a push to represent WKU's five regional campuses, which he said was "an area that I really don't think SGA has ever touched," and financial assistance for students as the year's underlying goal. He had met with the director of regional campuses the week before to start that work.

His spring semester was dominated by fallout from anti-LGBTQ Instagram posts he had liked. After the judicial council unanimously declined to censure him, the Queer Student Union removed SGA meetings from its list of campus safe spaces and said it would not associate with SGA until a public apology was released. Addressing the senate on Feb. 21, Bornefeld, who also served as student regent, said he "always commits to loving thy neighbor" but could not commit to always agreeing, tying his position to his Christian values and offering to meet any student over coffee or breakfast to discuss disagreements.

30 August 2022
#

Executive retreat set the year's goals

SGA's executive team held a formal retreat dinner on Aug. 29 at the Cliff Todd Center to set its agenda for the year. President Cole Bornefeld said the group was building a one-stop resource guide for students and trying to promote SGA at WKU's regional campuses in Elizabethtown, Fort Knox, Glasgow, Owensboro and Somerset. Speaker Julie Mishchuk said the underlying goal of the year was financial assistance for students.

Executive retreat sets goals on regional campuses and student resources

SGA's executive team met at the Cliff Todd Center to set goals for the semester, including a student resource guide and better representation for WKU's regional campuses in Elizabethtown, Fort Knox, Glasgow, Owensboro and Somerset. President Cole Bornefeld said he had met with regional campuses director Derick Strode and called it an area SGA had never touched. Speaker of the Senate Julie Mishchuk said financial assistance for students was the underlying goal.

31 August 2022campus
#

WKU dedicated the Commons at Helm Library

WKU formally dedicated the $35 million Commons at Helm Library, a renovation funded through a partnership with Aramark, and President Timothy Caboni announced early plans for a similar makeover of Cherry Hall. SGA President Cole Bornefeld spoke at the ceremony, saying the building had been full of excitement and community since it opened.

September 2022
#

First meeting filled senate and cabinet vacancies

At its first meeting of the year on Aug. 30, the senate approved all of President Bornefeld's nominees, including Juan Thomas, Will Hemenover and Sydney Denney as senators-at-large, Aniya Johnson as director of information technology, Donte' Reed as director of enrollment and student experience, and Isaac Kind and Garrett Baum to the judicial council. Administrative Vice President Sam Kurtz spoke on the budget and Chief Justice Holden Schroeder swore in the new members.

#

Committee chairs installed; vice president serves on governor's higher education committee

Six committee chairs were installed, including Olivia Feck on student mental health and wellbeing, Elizabeth DeLozier on legislative operations and Ahatali Tahiri on diversity, equity and inclusion. Bornefeld commended Executive Vice President Garrison Reed for his work on Gov. Andy Beshear's higher education committee. Provost Bud Fischer briefed senators on the Quality Enhancement Plan tied to WKU's 2025 accreditation reaffirmation submission.

14 September 2022campus
#

SGA presses administrators on Big Red Backpack opt-out default

Assistant Vice President Jennifer Tougas took SGA feedback on the Big Red Backpack course materials program, which SGA discussions had prompted. Asked why the program defaulted to opt-out, Tougas acknowledged that "schools that use the opt-in approach are far more successful" but said WKU chose opt-out to keep the per-credit-hour cost low. Senators also raised missing textbooks in student orders, and President Timothy Caboni appeared to commend SGA for its role in creating the program.

21 September 2022
#

Fall election turnout doubled; Reed appointed to state council

Fall 2022 election results announced at midnight seated Rush Robinson, Callison Padgett and Rhett Helland as freshman senators, plus Neel Patel for Gatton Academy, Shy'Ante Williams as graduate senator and Salvador Leon Golib as international student senator. President Bornefeld said the vote total rose from 398 the previous fall to 805. SGA also announced that Vice President Garrison Reed had been appointed student representative on Kentucky's Council on Postsecondary Education, with a term running through June 30, 2023.

Senate sponsors Bowling Green Pride and announces a student veteran scholarship

#

Executives report on a campus listening tour as enrollment chief briefs senate

Bornefeld said he, Garrison Reed and Sam Kurtz had been holding a listening tour to increase SGA's accessibility to students, citing feedback from the Panhellenic Council. Ethan Logan, vice president of enrollment, told senators WKU's retention rate stood at 76.66% and discussed reaching students who do not speak English as a first language. 20 October 2022campus
#

Appeals court revives part of former SGA president's suit against WKU

The Sixth Circuit vacated summary judgment on the pre-February 2018 Title IX student-on-student harassment claims of Loandria "Andi" Dahmer, SGA president in 2017-18 and a former student regent, and vacated qualified immunity for SGA faculty adviser Charley Pride. The court upheld dismissal of her remaining claims, including a retaliation claim over President Timothy Caboni declining to write a Rhodes Scholarship recommendation. University spokesperson Jace Lux said WKU would renew its motion for summary judgment on the remaining issue.

25 January 2023
#

Senate approved five nominations and reviewed a shrinking budget

The senate unanimously confirmed five nominations, including Adan Canizalez as chair of student enrollment and experience and Miles Huff, a track athlete, to the judicial council. Administrative Vice President Sam Kurtz urged senators to sponsor more resolutions, which use university funds, because only about $39,460.76 remained of SGA's $100,000 annual budget for bills. Speaker Julie Mishchuk floated a constitutional amendment requiring every senator to sponsor at least one bill or resolution.

Vice president urges resolutions over spending bills as budget dwindles

Administrative Vice President Sam Kurtz pushed senators to sponsor more resolutions because of the shrinking SGA budget. Five nominations were confirmed unanimously, including Adan Canizalez as chair of student enrollment and experience and Miles Huff to the Judicial Council, filling the vacancy left by Holden Schroeder. February 2023
#

Voucher bills funded dental clinic, ID center and Preston Center

The senate unanimously passed Bill 28-23-S, putting $500 toward free teeth cleanings at the WKU Dental Health Clinic after voucher use rose 660 percent in the fall, and Bill 27-23-S, allocating $2,000 in discount vouchers split 60/30/10 among the Preston Center, the ID center and the dental hygiene clinic. A $260 bill funding a poetry mic night with the Talisman also passed with one nay vote. Former SGA President Matthew Wininger opened the meeting as guest speaker.

Senate funds dental, ID and Preston Center vouchers over budget objections

15 February 2023
#

Narcan resolution aimed to put overdose spray in residence halls

SGA unanimously passed Resolution 2-23-S on Feb. 7 supporting placement of Narcan, an opioid overdose reversal spray, in WKU residence halls. SGA had planned to spend about $1,000 of its own budget, but a University of Kentucky harm reduction specialist helped secure the product through a government grant instead. Housing and Residence Life planned to install the spray in AED-style boxes across its 15 dorms by Aug. 1 to allow time for staff training.

15 February 2023campus
#

Senate passes Narcan resolution; implementation delayed to August

Resolution 2-23-S, supporting Narcan in WKU residence halls, passed unanimously on Feb. 7 with support from five of seven committees. SGA had planned to spend about $1,000 but obtained the product through a government grant arranged with a University of Kentucky harm reduction specialist, so the resolution became a statement of support. Housing and Residence Life asked to delay implementation to Aug. 1 for training, and director of housing operations Lana Kunkel said no overdose incidents had occurred in a WKU residence hall.

17 February 2023
#

The Speaker takes the President to the Judicial Council

Speaker of the Senate Yulia 'Julie' Mishchuk requested a censure hearing against President Cole Bornefeld over anti-transgender posts he had liked on Instagram. Members of the Queer Student Union and both vice presidents attended. The Judicial Council voted unanimously against censure.

19 February 2023
#

A Title IX report seeks the President's removal

Filed with Student Conduct. Under the constitution, removing an executive who will not resign requires written endorsement from at least 20% of the Senate, a wait of at least fourteen days and a two-thirds vote of the full Senate sitting as a court.

Herald, March 2023

22 February 2023
#

Menstrual product legislation passed as QSU protested

At its Feb. 21 meeting SGA passed a resolution supporting menstrual product dispensers on the first floor of every academic building, a $200 bill to distribute 35 menstrual cups at tabling events, and $100 for a community relations survey. Queer Student Union president Alistar Flowers announced QSU had removed SGA meetings from its list of campus safe spaces after the judicial council declined to censure President Bornefeld, and said QSU would not associate with SGA until a public apology was released.

Herald, 22 Feb 2023

22 February 2023campus
#

Queer Student Union removes SGA from its safe space list

QSU President Alistar Flowers told the senate the organization would no longer list SGA as a safe space and would not associate with it until a public apology was released over the president's Instagram activity. Flowers questioned the fairness of the Judicial Council because most members are nominated by the president. Bornefeld responded in his officer report that he "always commits to loving thy neighbor" but cannot always agree, and the senate passed legislation funding menstrual product dispensers, a community relations survey and Sexual Assault Prevention Month t-shirts.

2 March 2023campus
#

Title IX complaint against SGA president seeks his removal

A Title IX report filed Feb. 19 accused President Cole Bornefeld, described in the report as student regent and representative of the student body, of gender-based harassment over four Turning Point USA Instagram posts he liked. The complaint sought his removal, a public statement from WKU and a public apology; the complainant's identity was redacted from records released to the Herald. Spokesperson Jace Lux said WKU does not comment on Student Conduct complaints, and the Herald noted removal would require impeachment endorsed by 20% of the senate and a two-thirds vote.

Herald, 2 Mar 2023

22 March 2023
#

Election reform bill failed 19-15

Bill 39-23-S, which would have moved the transfer, non-traditional and first-generation senate seats to the fall election and required a two-thirds senate vote to convert vacant seats to at-large seats, failed 19-15, short of the two-thirds needed. Sponsor Neel Patel called the growing reliance on presidential appointments a dangerous practice for a government organization. The same meeting unanimously funded the April 3 Afro-Latinidad cultural event and rejected funding for Dance Big Red.

Herald, 22 Mar 2023

22 March 2023campus
#

Election reform bill fails 19-15; Dance Big Red funding rejected

Bill 39-23-S, which would have moved transfer, non-traditional and first-generation senate seats to the fall election and required a two-thirds senate vote before a vacant seat could become an at-large seat, failed 19-15 because it did not reach the required two-thirds. Gatton Academy Senator Neel Patel said reliance on the president to convert vacancies was a dangerous practice for a government organization. Bill 41-23-S, funding Dance Big Red, also failed, while Bill 40-23-S funding an Afro-Latinidad event passed unanimously.

Herald, 22 Mar 2023

13 April 2023
#

Senate merged committees and funded Mental Health Awareness Week

At the April 11 meeting the senate passed Bill 44-23-S, a constitutional amendment merging the Community Relations and Outreach committees starting in 2023-24, which Speaker Mishchuk said would create a stronger committee amid low membership. Senators also unanimously funded a slate of Mental Health Awareness Week events for April 23 through 28, including an Out of the Darkness suicide prevention walk, and passed a $62 terrarium tabling bill. President Bornefeld reported 44 applicants for the upcoming SGA elections.

13 April 2023campus
#

Constitutional amendment merges two committees for the next school year

Bill 44-23-S, a constitutional amendment merging the Community Relations committee with Outreach, passed and takes effect in the 2023-24 school year. Speaker Julie Mishchuk said the committees did much of the same work and that low membership meant a merger would strengthen the remaining committees. Bornefeld reported 44 applicants for the election cycle then underway, and bills funding terrariums and Mental Health Awareness Week events also passed.

19 April 2023campus
#

Kurtz ticket elected unopposed

Sam Kurtz, the outgoing administrative vice president, was elected student body president on a ticket that ran unopposed, with Salvador León as administrative vice president and Annalise Finch as executive vice president. Fourteen at-large senators and college, class, first-generation and Gatton seats were also filled. Results were read shortly after midnight in the DSU senate chambers by Chief Justice Justin Goins.

What this is

A year-by-year record of student government at Western Kentucky University, from the ratification of the Associated Students constitution in April 1966 to the present. Every entry names the source it came from.

Corrections

The names here start with the plaque in the SGA Chambers, and the plaque is known to be wrong in places. Where the archive disagrees with it, this site follows the archive and records the change on the corrections page. If you served, or you know that a year here is wrong, the project wants to hear from you through the Student Government Association office.

Reuse

Text on this site is the work of the project. Photographs, documents and legislation are reproduced from Western Kentucky University's own open archives and from the College Heights Herald and the Talisman; each item names the collection, issue and page it came from and links to the original.
