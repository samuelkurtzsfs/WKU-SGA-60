Billy Stephens president and student regent, 2011-12

Office
President and student regent
Term recorded here
2011-12
Standing of the record
As recorded on the plaque. Not yet corroborated in the archive.

Named as SGA president throughout the year's Herald coverage, from his April 2011 election through spring 2012 budget and personnel stories.

Billy Stephens, a junior from Hawesville, was elected SGA president in the spring 2011 election, announced just after midnight on April 7. He defeated Diego Leal Ambriz, a senior from Monterrey, Mexico, with 56 percent of the 1,066 total votes. Outgoing president Colton Jessie called him "a fantastic candidate for the job," and Kendrick Bryan and Devon Hilderbrandt were elected unopposed as his executive and administrative vice presidents.

Stephens opened his term in August 2011 with a revamped public relations campaign built around asking students what SGA could do for them. "I want PR to be visible on campus," he said, complaining that new students often did not hear about SGA until the second semester. The campaign aimed to raise turnout in the fall senate elections, produce contested races and fill all 36 senate seats.

The fall brought open conflict: the senate voted down, 19-8, a resolution to rename Downing University Center that campus improvements chair Keyana Boka had written, the Herald editorial board told Stephens to focus on his own platform promises instead, two senators were dismissed for absences, and SGA was surprised by a $15,000 bill for the prior administration's unpaid Provide-A-Ride service. Chief of Staff Katie Stillwell resigned before the spring semester was out; Stephens replaced her with Eric Smiley, who had held the same post two years earlier under his brother's administration. The senate passed a spring budget of $50,026 in February 2012, down from $54,000 in fall spending.

The year, in order33

6 September 2011
#

Senate reconvenes, appointments confirmed, tobacco vote postponed

At the semester's first senate meeting, held after the previous week's session was canceled, senators postponed a vote on the tobacco-free campus resolution so new members could review it first. President Billy Stephens made all of his executive-branch, judicial-committee and committee-chair appointments in a single blanket vote that passed unanimously, and Speaker of the Senate Kaylee Egerer told senators to keep generating new ideas for their committees.

Herald, 7 Sep 2011

14 September 2011
#

SGA ties tobacco ban support to ending two-year housing rule

The SGA senate passed a tobacco-free campus resolution that supported a ban only if the requirement for students to live on campus for two years was eliminated. Speaker of the Senate Kaylee Egerer said SGA backed the ban "if and only if" the housing requirement went. President Gary Ransdell had said the Board of Regents would not consider a tobacco-free campus until the University Senate, Staff Council and SGA each passed their own resolutions.

Herald, 14 Sep 2011

16 September 2011
#

SGA passes tobacco-free campus resolution with housing condition

The SGA senate voted unanimously for a resolution supporting a campus-wide tobacco ban, but only if WKU dropped its requirement that students live on campus for two years. President Gary Ransdell had said the Board of Regents would not consider a ban until SGA, the University Senate and Staff Council all passed resolutions. SGA was the only body to attach the housing stipulation.

Herald, 16 Sep 2011

21 September 2011
#

SGA waives its own process to oppose faculty regent term limits

SGA passed a resolution recommending that the Faculty Regent seat on the Board of Regents carry no term limit, following a University Senate recommendation the week before. The senate waived the usual two-week process of a first reading followed by a vote the next week. A bill funding the Cage the Elephant concert received its first read at the same meeting.

Herald, 21 Sep 2011

23 September 2011
#

Fall senate election fills 11 seats with 977 votes cast

SGA announced its fall senator election results just after midnight on Thursday, with 11 new senators elected including Christopher Costa, Corey Johnson and Mallory Treece. Twenty students ran for the seats and 977 students voted. President Billy Stephens said he was happy with the turnout for a fall election and that he got the contested race he wanted.

Herald, 23 Sep 2011

23 September 2011
#

Fall senate election draws 977 voters for 11 seats

Results of the fall senate election were announced just after midnight, with 11 senators elected from a field of 20 candidates. A total of 977 students voted. Those elected were Kelley Boothe, Hannah Garland, Corey Johnson, Elizabeth Wininger, Mallory Chaney, Mallory Treece, Kevin Asbery, Christopher Costa, Jordan Campbell, Nolan Miles and Ross Nowland.

Herald, 23 Sep 2011

30 September 2011
#

SGA to lend iPads through WKU's Educational Resource Center

SGA partnered with the Educational Resource Center in Gary Ransdell Hall to provide two first-generation iPads for the ERC to load textbooks onto and lend out to students at no cost, part of President Billy Stephens' push to make the organization more visibly useful to students.

Herald, 30 Sep 2011

30 September 2011
#

SGA spent $8,000 on Cage the Elephant concert, approving part of it afterward

SGA co-sponsored the Sept. 21 Cage the Elephant concert with the Campus Activities Board, splitting the costs. President Billy Stephens said the executive cabinet took $5,000 from executive discretionary money, which does not require senate approval, while a bill allocating a further $3,000 in senate funding passed on the Tuesday after the concert had already taken place. Stephens said there had been no rush for the funding because CAB had already covered the expenses up front.

Herald, 30 Sep 2011

5 October 2011
#

Ransdell tells SGA the two-year housing requirement cannot be dropped

In his first visit to SGA that semester, President Gary Ransdell told senators that eliminating the two-year on-campus housing requirement did not work "from a business standpoint." He said debts remained to be paid on dormitory renovations, so a certain number of students had to live in the dorms. SGA had been the only campus governing body to make removal of the housing requirement a condition of its tobacco ban resolution.

Herald, 5 Oct 2011

26 October 2011
#

Organizational aid bill passes for five student groups

SGA passed an organizational aid funding bill covering Black Men at Western, Women in Transition, the Western Kentucky Pre-Dental Society, Students in Free Enterprise and the Kentucky Public Health Association student chapter. A second aid bill received its first read, proposing $1,000 from the organizational aid fund for the Black Student Alliance and the WKU Habitat for Humanity campus chapter. Senators also discussed continuing work on Provide-A-Ride.

Herald, 26 Oct 2011

28 October 2011
#

Speaker says she never expects Regents to adopt SGA's tobacco ban version

Speaker of the Senate Kaylee Egerer said she did not expect the Board of Regents ever to enact SGA's version of the tobacco ban because the housing requirement was such a key factor. She said linking the two was necessary because requiring tobacco users to live on a tobacco-free campus was "infringing on students' rights." Egerer called the proposed ban "totally unfair and unrealistic."

Herald, 28 Oct 2011

9 November 2011
#

Senate defeats DUC name change resolution 19-8

A resolution to rename Downing University Center as Downing Student Union, written by campus improvements chair Keyana Boka, was voted down 19-8 with one abstention after a lengthy open debate. Executive Vice President Kendrick Bryan suggested putting the question on the spring ballot, but senators noted architects working on the DUC renovation would need an answer sooner.

Herald, 9 Nov 2011

9 November 2011
#

Senate votes down renaming Downing University Center 19-8

A resolution that would have changed Downing University Center's name to Downing Student Union was defeated 19-8, with one senator abstaining. A motion to table the resolution was also voted down. Executive Vice President Kendrick Bryan had suggested putting the question on the spring ballot as was done in 2006, but it was noted that architects would likely need an answer sooner.

Herald, 9 Nov 2011

15 November 2011
#

Herald editorial tells SGA to drop renaming and keep its promises

The Herald editorial board wrote that SGA should focus on student issues rather than renaming a building honoring former president Dero Downing, who had recently died. It said Stephens had barely addressed his platform promises, including a dead day before finals and an improved Provide-a-Ride service, which had seen no visible progress since SGA cut ties with Franklin Taxi.

Herald, 15 Nov 2011

15 November 2011
#

Two senators removed for absences; ten faced judicial review

Ten of SGA's 36 senators were called before the judicial board during the fall semester for excessive absences from senate and committee meetings. Two of them, Kevin Adams and Kevin Preston, were dismissed from the senate after failing to attend their judicial hearings, which the council interpreted as intent to resign. The rest received warnings, and Stephens said he would appoint replacements to the two empty seats.

Herald, 15 Nov 2011

15 November 2011
#

Ten of 36 senators face judicial review; two dismissed for absences

Ten of SGA's 36 senators were called before the judicial board that semester for excessive absences from committee and full senate meetings. Kevin Adams and Kevin Preston were dismissed after failing to attend their judicial hearings; Justice Chris Jankowski said each had between eight and 10 absences. Chief Justice David Spalding said the council interpreted not appearing at a review as intent to resign.

Herald, 15 Nov 2011

18 November 2011
#

SGA pays surprise $15,000 bill for previous year's Provide-A-Ride

SGA paid Parking Services a $15,000 retroactive payment for the prior year's Provide-A-Ride service after Parking Services found no record of the previous administration's payment. The money was split evenly between the organizational aid and student scholarship funds, $7,500 from each. Stephens said he was "shocked" by the bill, while 2010-11 president Colton Jessie insisted his administration had budgeted the money.

Herald, 18 Nov 2011

18 November 2011
#

SGA pays $15,000 retroactive Provide-A-Ride bill from aid and scholarship funds

SGA paid Parking Services a $15,000 retroactive payment for the previous year's Provide-A-Ride service, taking $7,500 each from the organizational aid and student scholarship funds. President Billy Stephens said he was "shocked" to be notified of the payment the week before, and that Parking Services had not received the money from the previous administration. Because the SGA budget does not roll over between years, no prior-year funds were available to cover it.

Herald, 18 Nov 2011

30 November 2011
#

SGA funds extended library hours and $2,500 in organizational aid

SGA passed a bill allocating $600 to Helm-Cravens Library to stay open until 2 a.m. from Sunday through Thursday of finals week, an annual practice. A second bill allocated $2,500 total to the Student Affairs Graduate Association, MBA Student Association, Topper Well, WKU Eco-Club, WKU Black Graduate Student Association and Kappa Alpha Psi. Three scholarship allocation bills received first reads.

Herald, 30 Nov 2011

6 February 2012
#

SGA cancels $1,175 bus to Frankfort rally after eight sign up

SGA canceled the bus it had planned to run to the Rally for Higher Education in Frankfort, arranging carpools instead. As of that Friday only eight people had confirmed they were attending, and the bus would have cost $1,175, about $150 per student. The Tuesday night meeting, previously canceled for the rally, was reinstated.

Herald, 6 Feb 2012

7 February 2012
#

Herald editorial says SGA should and could do more

A Herald editorial said that after two short meetings and no new plans on the agenda, SGA appeared not to be on the ball, and urged it to do more before the semester ended. The editorial singled out the loss of the Provide-A-Ride service and the failure to find a replacement provider despite an offer from Parking and Transportation Services. SGA had said it would work toward a new service but admitted it was not feasible before the semester ended.

Herald, 7 Feb 2012

21 February 2012
#

Free speech resolution on student handbook delayed four weeks

A resolution supporting removal of the "External Computer Use and Ethics" section from the student handbook, which its backers said restricted freedom of speech, was scheduled for a second read. Its author, senator Christopher Costa, pushed it back four weeks to do additional research. The section warned that student posts on sites such as Facebook could be seen by university officials and that communications deemed inappropriate might lead to discipline.

Herald, 21 Feb 2012

24 February 2012
#

SGA approves $50,026 spring budget after $54,000 fall spending

The senate approved a spring 2012 budget of $50,026, divided among operations, public relations, executive programming and senate programming, with $10,500 set aside for organizational aid and $12,000 for scholarships. Chief of Staff Katie Stillwell told senators SGA had spent about $54,000 in the fall, $15,000 of it the retroactive Provide-A-Ride payment.

Herald, 24 Feb 2012

24 February 2012
#

SGA approves $50,026 spring budget after $54,000 fall semester

SGA approved a spring 2012 budget of $50,026, of which $43,476.17 remained. Chief of Staff Katie Stillwell said the organization had spent roughly $54,000 the previous fall, $15,000 of it the retroactive Provide-A-Ride payment. The budget was divided into operations, public relations, executive programming and senate programming, with $5,500 in stipends already spent.

Herald, 24 Feb 2012

29 February 2012
#

Senator removed for absences is reappointed by the president

Louisville sophomore Kevin Adams, removed from the senate the previous semester for excessive absences, returned through a presidential appointment by President Billy Stephens. Adams said he was less busy and had more time for SGA. Stephens said Adams had overbooked himself and that the two were good friends who had stayed close after the removal.

Herald, 29 Feb 2012

22 March 2012
#

Chief of staff resigns over grades; predecessor takes the job back

Chief of Staff Katie Stillwell announced her resignation at the Tuesday senate meeting, having officially resigned March 16. She said she stepped down because her grades were slipping ahead of a May graduation. President Billy Stephens named former chief of staff Eric Smiley as her replacement quickly enough that she did not need to serve out her two weeks' notice.

Herald, 22 Mar 2012

26 March 2012
#

Spring election postponed a day by TopNet failure

The spring election, set to run from March 27 to 28, was pushed back to March 28-29 because of campus-wide problems with TopNet. Director of Information Technology Cory Dodds said the election would slip another week if repairs were not finished in time. The executive cabinet made the decision with Charley Pride, director of student activities and organizations.

Herald, 26 Mar 2012

28 March 2012
#

Chief of Staff Eric Smiley approved after Stillwell's resignation

The senate approved President Billy Stephens' choice of Eric Smiley as chief of staff after Katie Stillwell resigned the post; Smiley had served as chief of staff two years earlier under his brother Kevin Smiley's administration. The same meeting passed five bills, including two Organizational Aid grants of $500 each to six campus groups (the Chinese Students and Scholars Association, Geology Club, Chinese Music Club, Relay for Life, International Club and Baptist Campus Ministry), $500 toward the April 19 International Night, $398 reimbursing two Kindle Fires raffled at the WKU Student Research Conference, and $500 toward Big Red's trip to the 2012 Mascot Nationals. Senators also passed a resolution for a second campus cleanup on April 17 and moved $3,000 from general senate funds to Organizational Aid so SGA could spend down its budget before the semester's end; announcements noted that spring executive elections would open at 12:01 a.m. the next day.

Herald, 28 Mar 2012

30 March 2012
#

Dodds elected SGA president for 2012-13; Boka and Alvey win uncontested VP races

Cory Dodds was elected SGA president for 2012-13 with 44 percent of the vote, over senator Corey Johnson with 35 percent and former senator Austin Wingate with 21 percent. Sophomore Keyana Boka was elected executive vice president and Cain Alvey administrative vice president; both vice-presidential races were uncontested.

Herald, 30 Mar 2012

20 April 2012
#

SGA gave about $34,000 in organizational aid to roughly 50 groups

Administrative Vice President Devon Hilderbrandt said SGA distributed around $34,000 in organizational aid over the year, helping about 50 organizations. Groups applied online, met with the organizational aid committee, and could receive a maximum of $500 each. Senator Corey Johnson, who served on the committee, said the committee had been strict about what it funded.

Herald, 20 Apr 2012

25 April 2012
#

Transgender inclusion resolution tabled after debate

A resolution by senator Kevin Asbery supporting policies more inclusive of transgender and gender-nonconforming students was tabled after debate. The resolution would have encouraged the university and its departments to revise policies and stated that WKU lacked adequate gender-neutral facilities, including housing and restrooms. Asbery said the issue had recently been brought to his attention.

Herald, 25 Apr 2012

2 May 2012
#

Transgender resolution passes 18-5 at final meeting of the year

At the last meeting of the year the senate passed the transgender and gender-nonconforming support resolution 18-5, with four senators abstaining. The resolution encouraged WKU to create gender-neutral housing and restrooms in all university buildings and to revise policies to be more inclusive. Students who were not SGA members attended specifically to watch the vote, and debate covered student safety, cost and whether separate housing would isolate students.

Herald, 2 May 2012

7 May 2012
#

Safe ride program finalized to replace Provide-A-Ride

President-elect Cory Dodds said SGA had finalized a partnership with Parking and Transportation to run a safe ride program in the fall, after a year without one. SGA's share would cost $15,000 for the school year, the same as under Provide-A-Ride. The earlier service had been a $30,000 contract split with Parking Services until original provider Franklin Taxi ceased its affiliation with WKU.

Herald, 7 May 2012

What this is

A year-by-year record of student government at Western Kentucky University, from the ratification of the Associated Students constitution in April 1966 to the present. Every entry names the source it came from.

Corrections

The names here start with the plaque in the SGA Chambers, and the plaque is known to be wrong in places. Where the archive disagrees with it, this site follows the archive and records the change on the corrections page. If you served, or you know that a year here is wrong, the project wants to hear from you through the Student Government Association office.

Text on this site is the work of the project. Photographs, documents and legislation are reproduced from Western Kentucky University's own open archives and from the College Heights Herald and the Talisman; each item names the collection, issue and page it came from and links to the original.
