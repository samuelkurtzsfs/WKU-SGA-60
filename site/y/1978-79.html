Steven Thornton president and student regent, 1978-79

Office
President and student regent
Term recorded here
1978-79
Place in the line
the 13th president and the 11th student regent
Standing of the record
Confirmed in the sources below.

Contested the ASG presidency against Ed Johnson in the 6 April 1978 election, campaigning on his experience; Johnson's contest of the result was denied by the Judicial Council on 18 April 1978 (see 1977-78 events, the outgoing Moore administration's year). This replaces a prior citation to 'Herald 54:54, 12 Apr 1979' - that issue is the following year's primary, does not name Thornton, and has been moved to 1979-80.

Thornton beat Ed Johnson for the Associated Student Government presidency in the 6 April 1978 election, campaigning on his experience; Johnson contested the result, and the ASG Judicial Council denied his appeal on 18 April 1978 after a long debate.

In office that fall, Thornton's ASG tabled an open house bill rather than act on it, the Herald reported on 28 September 1978. By January 1979 the government faced a fight to keep control of student activities funding and was pressing for a student seat on a state-level council, both reported in the same 25 January 1979 issue.

In March 1979 the Herald reported Thornton and fellow WKU student Terri Craig elected to the executive council of the Student Government Association of Kentucky, a state-level body - the kind of state seat ASG had been pressing for two months earlier. In the last full week of his term, Thornton's ASG approved resolutions on campus housing and parking policy, reported 26 April 1979, the same week voting closed in the election for his successor. The archive does not otherwise show how the January activities-funding fight was resolved, and no further Herald coverage of the Thornton administration has been found before his successor, Jamie Hargrove, was profiled as the new ASG head at the start of the 1979-80 year.

The organization

Everyone the record shows in office this year. Each name goes to their own page, with every office they held and everything the archive says they did.

The executive

Administrative Vice PresidentDavid Young

Elected unopposed in April 1978, having been a representative from Ogden College that year; he told the Herald his main goal as an executive officer would be to begin a book exchange. In spring 1979 he explained the new constitution's creation of 24 separate races for the 24 representative-at-large seats on ASG congress as a way to make candidates compete head to head.

1979 Talisman, ASG, pp. 288-289
TreasurerSteve Shipp

One of the four executive officers elected unopposed in the April 1978 election; the Herald's results report names him treasurer.

Herald 53:55, 11 Apr 1978
SecretaryAlice Wicks

Elected unopposed in April 1978. She told the Herald before the vote that she wanted to make students more aware of ASG's functions and that she was joining ASG because she wanted to get more involved in student government.

Herald 53:54, 6 Apr 1978
Activities Vice PresidentDavid Carwell

Elected unopposed in April 1978 and put in charge of concerts, saying he wanted more mini-concerts and fewer big shows. In the autumn he said the Indianapolis firm Sunshine Promotions met Western's three tests for an outside promoter, but shortly before the 3 November Player and Exile concert it emerged that the contract had never been signed, Sunshine having refused a $100,000 performance bond; it later posted a letter of credit instead. He also chaired ASG's lecture and concert committees. On 7 December 1978 the Herald reported colleagues' claims that he was neglecting the duties of an office that carried $800 a year, that he had missed five ASG meetings, and that lecture committee members and a Herald reporter could not reach him; president Steve Thornton said one more missed meeting would take him before the executive council to explain, and Carwell replied that much of his work was done on the telephone from home and defended his record. This archive found no later report of an executive council hearing, of any removal, or of a successor in the office.

Herald 54:30, 7 Dec 1978

The senate

Chairman, Faculty Evaluation CommitteeVictor Jackson

Jackson chaired the committee behind the student evaluation of faculty that ASG approved, a form called the Purdue Cafeteria System in which teachers pick questions from a list of several hundred around a common core. He said only the core questions would be made public because of printing costs, and put the cost at about $6,500 in the first year and about $5,000 a year after; the evaluations were scheduled to begin in the fall 1979 semester. He had earlier been the only black student running in the campus election for the Board of Regents' presidential screening committee.

1979 Talisman, ASG, pp. 288-289
Judicial Council ChairmanBob Moore

Nominated and accepted as Judicial Council chairman at ASG's first meeting of the year, 29 August 1978. This is Robert "Bob" Moore, ASG president 1977-78. The Herald reported 7 September 1978 that the former president was ruled ineligible for an Associated Student Government job; by the 12 September 1978 meeting Thornton announced an opening for Judicial Council chairman and Congress confirmed Steve Wilson in the position, 30 in favour with 3 abstaining. Neither source explicitly ties the two events, but the dates align and no other ASG post for Moore is recorded that year.

The year, in order30

1978Concert
#

John Prine sells out Van Meter and ASG still loses $2,000

John Prine came to Western in the autumn of 1978 and did what no other musical act had done in a long time, playing to a capacity house. But Van Meter Auditorium holds only slightly more than 1,000, and even with a sellout and tickets priced higher than any previous mini-concert, ASG lost about $2,000 on the show. His five-man band ran through 25 songs, among them "Spanish Pipedream," "Dear Abby," "The Bottomless Lake" and "Sam Stone."

ASG lost about $2,000

#

Roll-call votes made public; Moore ruled ineligible

ASG decided its roll-call votes would be made public, the Herald reported. The same issue reported that former president Bob Moore was ineligible for an Associated Student Government job.

7 September 1978Programme
#

ASG hands its concerts to an outside promoter

The Board of Regents gave ASG authority to seek outside help booking concerts, and ASG contracted Sunshine Promotions of Indianapolis, producers of a Rolling Stones show in Lexington that summer and of Summer Jam II in Louisville. The firm had booked more than 150 concerts; for its part of the bargain ASG was to receive 13 percent of the net profits. President Steve Thornton said at least three major concerts were being planned for the year.

ASG to take 13 percent of net profits

Downing, who had led WKU since 1969, announced he would step down effective 31 December 1978. Vice President John Minton took over as interim president the following January while the Board of Regents searched for a successor.

26 October 1978Concert
#

ASG's promoter had not signed a contract days before the Player and Exile concert

ASG said in the early autumn of 1978 that it would sign a contract with Sunshine Promotions, an Indianapolis concert firm, and its activities vice president David Carwell said the company met the three requirements for an outside promoter: an office within 500 miles of Western, at least 75 major concerts promoted, and $2 million grossed on them. Eight days before the Player and Exile concert of 3 November, the Herald reported that Western had still not signed, because the firm would not post the $100,000 performance bond the university required to guarantee that all the bills would be paid. Sunshine's Sybesma said Indiana insurance companies wanted 100 per cent collateral and a $2,000 service fee for the bond, and said his company might instead put up money before each concert. Sunshine later posted an irrevocable letter of credit that Western could cash if the concert bills went unpaid.

$100,000 performance bond in dispute

College Heights Herald 54:19, 26 Oct 1978; 1979 Talisman, Associated Student Government section PDF

3 November 1978Concert
#

Player and Exile play the Homecoming concert

Player and Exile played Diddle Arena on Homecoming night to 4,296 people. Player, whose hits were "This Time I'm In It For Love" and "Baby Come Back," failed to get the crowd on its feet; Exile, from Richmond, skipped its sound check and played through poor sound but had the arena standing. After $24,000 in production costs and Western's 13 percent cut of a $25,694.29 after-tax gross, the Talisman recorded that there was little financial success to speak of.

$25,694.29 after-tax gross; $24,000 production costs; Western took 13 percent

Talisman 1979, concerts section

9 November 1978Programme
#

The Herald tells ASG to quit the concert business

An unsigned Herald editorial said it was time ASG got out of the concert business, arguing that concert promotion had taken most of the organisation's time and effort and that ASG could not even hire a promoter competently. It cited excessive university demands, poor communication with Sunshine Promotions and the unsigned contract behind the Homecoming show. The same issue carried a reader's letter defending the concert as good.

College Heights Herald, Vol. 54, No. 23, 9 November 1978 PDF

Joe Cocker drops out; Wet Willie takes his place

Joe Cocker, who had been announced to appear with the Outlaws at Diddle Arena on 5 December, cancelled his entire tour, and Sunshine Promotions signed the southern rock band Wet Willie in his place. The same meeting heard that Eleanor Smeal, president of the National Organization for Women, would speak on 7 December in Van Meter Auditorium.

The Outlaws cancel after 242 tickets sell

Reaction to the advertising for the Outlaws and Wet Willie was so poor that only 242 tickets had been sold by the week of the show. 18 January 1979Lecture
#

Eleanor Smeal's lecture keeps slipping

Activities vice president David Carwell told ASG's first meeting of the semester that the university was still trying to reschedule a lecture by Eleanor Smeal, president of the National Organization for Women, for February or March; she had been announced for 7 December and had not come. He set out what ASG was paying the three speakers it had signed: $4,000 for Dick Gregory, $2,000 for Princeton professor Gerard O'Neill and $2,000 for Congresswoman Shirley Chisholm, plus expenses, with all the lectures free. Carwell added that tickets for the Heart concert at Diddle Arena on 15 February would go on sale on 22 January at $7 in advance and $8 on the day.

Gregory $4,000; O'Neill $2,000; Chisholm $2,000; Heart tickets $7 advance, $8 day of show

15 February 1979Concert
#

Heart at Diddle Arena

Heart played Diddle Arena on 15 February with Ann Wilson opening on "High Times" and her sister Nancy on guitar, running through "Heartless," "Straight On," "Magazine," "Mistral Wind," "Dog and Butterfly," "Hijinx," "Crazy on You" and "Barracuda." Sunshine Promotions had signed the band as its first major break for Western, but only 5,821 came, a little more than half of capacity. Western made about $5,200 from the show, the one clear financial success of the concert year.

27 March 1979Programme
#

Forum '79 cut to one night

ASG's gubernatorial candidates' discussion panel, scheduled for two nights, was shortened to one after the university orchestra needed Van Meter Auditorium on the Monday. President Steve Thornton said the surviving session would run at 7:30 p.m. on the Tuesday, with Democrats Carroll Hubbard, Ralph Ed Graves and George Atkins appearing and Lieutenant Governor Thelma Stovall sending a representative. Ray White was the only Republican candidate willing to attend; Terry McBrayer and Louie Nunn stayed away.

College Heights Herald, Vol. 54, No. 48, 27 March 1979 PDF

27 March 1979
#

Thornton and Craig win seats on Kentucky's student government council

The Herald reported two Western students, ASG president Steven Thornton and Terri Craig, elected to the executive council of the Student Government Association of Kentucky, a state-level student body - the kind of state seat ASG had pressed for in January. The same issue editorialized that ASG should make its teacher evaluations public.

Herald 54:48, 27 Mar 1979 PDF

31 March 1979Other
#

The Regents take concerts and lectures away from ASG

The Board of Regents approved a rebuilt University Center Board on 31 March 1979 and funded it with $80,000 for programming. From the autumn its committees of students and administrators would run the lectures and concerts Associated Student Government had been running. ASG kept three seats, held by its president, its activities vice president and one member of congress, alongside representatives of the Interfraternity and Panhellenic councils, United Black Students, the men's and women's residence hall councils, two other students and three faculty members. The university programs coordinator, Tim Nemeth, said the new board would involve more student participation, and then put its maximum at ten students working on lectures and concerts against the fifty involved under ASG.

$80,000 to the University Center Board for programming

1979 Talisman, University Center Board

2 April 1979Lecture
#

Gerald O'Neill on space colonies

Gerald O'Neill, a physics professor at Princeton and author of "The High Frontier", told 100 people in the Center Theater in his lecture of 2 April 1979 that space colonies and solar power stations were possible with current technology, and that electricity from solar cells could be sent back to Earth as low energy microwaves. He said plans for space colonies existed but President Carter had abolished the NASA office exploring space colonisation, and that he was concerned about the militarisation of space because Carter had signed a bill to develop a particle beam weapon to be placed in space. The same yearbook records that lectures and concerts had been run by Associated Student Government until the Board of Regents voted on 31 March 1979 to hand that programming to a new University Center Board from the following autumn.

100 in the audience

1979 Talisman, Lectures p. 97 and University Center Board p. 81

17 April 1979
#

Voting held for Thornton's successor; candidates lean on experience

Students voted in the ASG general election to choose Thornton's successor, with the Herald reporting the presidential candidates relying on their experience. The Herald ran candidate endorsements the same day, including United Black Students' backing of Jamie Hargrove, and pieces from outgoing president Steve Thornton and former president Bob Moore.

Herald 54:55, 17 Apr 1979 PDF

18 April 1979Lecture
#

Shirley Chisholm closed the Black Awareness Symposium

Congresswoman Shirley Chisholm cancelled her February lecture, which is why the former Black Awareness Week was stretched across the spring semester as a symposium, and her rescheduled talk on 18 April closed it. She spoke on America's unfulfilled legacies before a crowd of about 250, predominantly black, who shouted and chanted in response, describing herself as a people's politician who could not be manipulated and saying she was there to tell people what they did not want to hear. The same yearbook records that lectures and concerts had been run by Associated Student Government until a reorganised University Center Board took them over in the autumn of 1979.

about 250 attended

19 April 1979
#

Jamie Hargrove easily wins the presidency

The Herald reported that Jamie Hargrove easily won the Associated Student Government presidency, defeating David Young 1,087-535 on a turnout of 1,725 votes, up from 1,097 in the 1978 election. In the other executive races reported the same day, Steve Fuller defeated Tom Jecker 973-574 for administrative vice president, Dean Bates defeated Ben Bratcher 785-761 for activities vice president - the closest of the four races - Betty Thompson defeated Lynn Driver 1,052-517 for secretary, and Terri Craig defeated Darell Pierce 937-623 for treasurer.

What this is

A year-by-year record of student government at Western Kentucky University, from the ratification of the Associated Students constitution in April 1966 to the present. Every entry names the source it came from.

Corrections

The names here start with the plaque in the SGA Chambers, and the plaque is known to be wrong in places. Where the archive disagrees with it, this site follows the archive and records the change on the corrections page. If you served, or you know that a year here is wrong, the project wants to hear from you: people who held an office can be given the keys to their own year on the contribute page.

What it rests on

Everything here comes from what is publicly available online: the digitised Herald and SGA's own papers on TopSCHOLAR, the Talisman yearbooks, wkuherald.com, the university's news pages and the Wayback Machine. That is a fraction of what happened. Student government has met most weeks for sixty years, and the greater part of it was never written down, or was written down on paper that has never been scanned. A thin year here is a thin record, not a quiet one.

Text on this site is the work of the project. Photographs, documents and legislation are reproduced from Western Kentucky University's own open archives and from the College Heights Herald and the Talisman; each item names the collection, issue and page it came from and links to the original.
