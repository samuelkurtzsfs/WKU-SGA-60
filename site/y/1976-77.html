Standing of the record
Moved here from the year the plaque gives. The correction and its evidence are set out below.

The plaque reads "Christy Mollozzi". Every contemporaneous source calls her Christy Vogt: she bylined Herald pieces as Vogt through 1976-77, the paper covered her as ASG president under that name, and SGA's own roster of former presidents, archived in September 2001, lists "Christy Vogt, Louisville, 1976-77" and stars her as a student regent. This site follows the sources. Vogt was administrative vice president in 1975-76 before winning the presidency. As with Coates and Lich, and Seay and Taylor, the plaque's later surname is most likely a married name, but no source found states the connection. The Herald's 9 April 1976 headline "Christy Vogt Wins Election" and her own "Winner Says Thanks" the same issue confirm the 1976-77 ASG presidency was won under the name Christy Vogt; her byline runs through the term (campaign endorsement 26 Mar 1976, "Urges Involvement" 12 Apr 1977, the closing letter to Congress 26 Apr 1977). The plaque records her as Christy Mollozzi. WKU's own archive ties the two names together in multiple places written by the university itself, not by this project: a WKU Archives research notecard indexed as "Mollozzi, Christy Vogt," and Christy Mollozzi appearing in the President's Office finding aids for the Meredith and Ransdell administrations in connection with Marketing & Promotions, meaning she had a further career on the WKU staff. "Christy Vogt Mollozzi" follows the ordinary American convention of a married name with the maiden name kept as a middle name. No single source states outright "formerly Christy Vogt," so this is an inference from a consistent name pattern repeated across independent WKU records over three decades, not a documented fact - flagged here rather than asserted as certain.

Christy Vogt entered the spring 1976 Associated Student Government campaign with an endorsement from Herald columnist John Butchko, in an issue whose front section also covered the start of impeachment hearings against congress member Gerard Faulk and an editorial warning the university's new Faculty Senate away from ASG's own troubles.

Vogt and Bruce Smith survived the 2 April primary as the Herald reported 527 ballots cast, then Vogt won the presidency outright, the paper reporting the result on 9 April 1976 under the headline "Christy Vogt Wins Election," with her own note of thanks to voters in the same issue - the same week Faulk was acquitted.

In office for 1976-77, Vogt's congress created a constitutional revision committee within its first month (Bill 2, 7 September 1976) and kept revision on its agenda all year. It asked the university for a minimum wage policy for student employees (Resolution, 26 October 1976), recommended preregistration for all students, and opposed a proposed tuition increase (Bill 9, 8 March 1977) while creating a Complaint Committee to field student grievances the same meeting. A resolution on discrimination failed in February 1977 with fourteen members abstaining. The fall Seals and Crofts concert lost the organization $3,800, a recurring sore point ASG had wrestled with under Vogt's predecessors as well.

Vogt wrote her own case for the office in the Herald - "Urges Involvement," 12 April 1977, days before Bob Moore won the presidency to succeed her by a 26-vote margin. She closed her term on 26 April 1977 with a letter to Congress thanking its members and recommending four items to the administration: constitutional revision, extended hours for student buildings, open visitation, and advance registration - the same four causes her congress had pushed all year.

WKU's own archive later indexes her, alongside her maiden name, as Christy Vogt Mollozzi, and separately as Christy Mollozzi in President's Office records tied to Marketing & Promotions - evidence, though not a documented statement, that she went on to a career on the university's own staff.

The organization

Everyone the record shows in office this year. Each name goes to their own page, with every office they held and everything the archive says they did.

The executive

Administrative Vice PresidentTom Blair

Elected on 6 and 7 April 1976 by 570 votes to John David Evans's 552, one of the closest races that spring. The 1977 Talisman lists him with the year's other officers as already having ASG experience. At the 6 April 1976 congress meeting, before the vote on Gerard Faulk, he moved to drop the impeachment proceedings without a vote on removal, and withdrew the motion at the urging of several congressmen. He ran for president the following April and lost to Bob Moore, 562 to 536.

Herald 51:53, 9 Apr 1976
TreasurerDavid Payne

The 1977 Talisman records Payne and Rick Kelley as the two officers beginning second terms in 1976-77. Note for the editor: years.json separately carries a David Payne who succeeded Marcel Bush as ASG president in January 1982, filed under 1981-82 (not 1982-83) with year_confidence "corrected" - Board of Regents minutes of 30 January 1982 already confirm that identification, so he is not simply an unresolved name. Whether the 1981-82 president is the same person as this 1970s ASG treasurer is not established by any source found so far, and this archive should not merge them without one that links them.

1977 Talisman, ASG, pp. 50-51
SecretaryPam Keown

Elected on 6 and 7 April 1976 with 731 votes to Sally Chenault's 501. The 1977 Talisman lists her among the officers who came into the year with ASG experience.

Herald 51:53, 9 Apr 1976
Activities Vice PresidentRick Kelley

Re-elected on 6 and 7 April 1976 over Robert Tackett, 705 to 496, for a second term running the concert programme. He presided over the 6 April 1976 congress meeting in the absence of president Steve Henry. The 1977 Talisman describes him taking acts to the concert selection committee for approval and working with assistant dean of student affairs Ron Beck; the Seals and Crofts homecoming concert lost about $3,800. His surname is printed both Kelley and Kelly in these volumes and the spelling is not settled.

1977 Talisman, ASG, pp. 50-51

The senate

Chairman, Judicial CouncilGerard Faulk

Faulk was acquitted of incompetence charges by Congress on 6 April 1976 and the Herald reported that he would conclude a two-year term in office during the 1976-77 ASG session. No later report of a successor was found in this archive.

Herald 51:53, 9 Apr 1976
ASG Rules and Elections ChairmanMike Pearson

Pearson ran the April 1977 general election and told the Herald that no one had been turned away from the polls, against complaints about long voting lines.

Herald 52:53, 15 Apr 1977

The year, in order29

30 June 1976Other
#

ASG and the Center Board publish the 1976-77 events schedule

The summer Herald carried the University Center Board and ASG schedule of events for 1976-77. By late August the Herald was reporting two lectures and two concerts on ASG's entertainment slate and that ASG was seeking an appearance by Chicago.

College Heights Herald, 30 Jun 1976 PDF

7 September 1976
#

ASG creates constitutional revision committee

The Associated Student Government congress passed Bill 2, creating a committee charged with revising the organization's constitution. Constitutional revision remained on the ASG agenda for the rest of the school year and appeared again among proposed amendments and a spring referendum bill.

SGA Records (UA66), Bill 2 - Constitutional Revision Committee, 7 Sep 1976

10 September 1976Concert
#

Lori Wolfson opens the mini-concert series

ASG continued its mini-concert series with the acoustic guitarist Lori Wolfson in Van Meter Auditorium on 10 September 1976. The second, on 22 September, was Jerico Harp, whose Jim Thomas and Tom Schmidt mixed their own material with songs including Dan Fogelberg's 'Wysteria'.

1977 Talisman, Concerts

10 September 1976Service
#

400 books sold in the ASG and Veterans on Campus co-op

The Herald reported that 400 books had been sold in the book co-op ASG ran with Veterans on Campus. The exchange had been advertised in the first issue of the year.

400 books sold

College Heights Herald 52:5, 10 Sep 1976 PDF

15 September 1976Lecture
#

David Frye fills Van Meter with impersonations of Nixon

The comedian David Frye kept a capacity crowd in Van Meter Hall laughing in mid-September 1976 with impersonations of political figures and film stars, Richard Nixon foremost among them. In January the comedian Robert Klein warmed about 700 people in the Garrett Conference Center ballroom in arctic weather.

Klein drew about 700

1977 Talisman, Lectures

24 September 1976Other
#

Students petition for their Maranatha concert money back

Students petitioned ASG for the return of a concert payment connected with the Maranatha Christian Center. ASG voted to refund the Maranatha money on 1 October, and by 8 October the Herald reported the Judicial Council had dropped the ASG-Maranatha case.

College Heights Herald 52:9, 24 Sep 1976 PDF

8 October 1976Lecture
#

Bob Woodward cancels and comes six months late

Carl Bernstein was booked to open the lecture season but contracted pneumonia, and his partner Bob Woodward was announced as a substitute before cancelling his Western and University of Kentucky dates for work. Promoters paid the advertising costs for the cancelled lecture, but someone in the ASG office drew a bull's-eye on Woodward's publicity poster; he finally appeared in early April 1977.

College Heights Herald 52:13, 8 Oct 1976 PDF

15 October 1976Concert
#

Chicago grosses $53,092 and becomes Western's most successful concert

Chicago appeared at Diddle Arena on 15 October 1976, drew 10,450 people and grossed $53,092. The 1977 Talisman called it Western's most financially successful concert. The show cost $25,000 and was promoted by Reggae Ltd of St Louis with ASG, the first privately promoted concert at Western; about 5,000 advance tickets were sold, another record, and Ron Beck called it one of the best the university had ever had.

grossed $53,092; attendance 10,450; act cost $25,000

1977 Talisman, Concerts

26 October 1976
#

ASG asks WKU for a student minimum wage

The congress adopted Resolution 13, proposing that Western institute a minimum wage policy for all student employees. It was part of a heavy fall docket that also included resolutions on parking, laundry services, non-smoking areas and residence hall change machines.

SGA Records (UA66), Resolution 13 - Minimum Wage, 26 Oct 1976

November 1976Lecture
#

Clive Barnes, John Sears and Vincent Bugliosi on the lecture series

The lecture season carried Clive Barnes of the New York Times in early November on 'The Plight of the Theatre Today', John Sears, Nixon's 1968 campaign manager and later Reagan's, in late November, and Vincent Bugliosi, the Manson prosecutor and author of 'Helter Skelter', in early December. Members of the ASG lecture committee, David Carwell, Ricky Sheppard and Mike Seiler, hosted the visiting speakers.

1977 Talisman, Lectures

6 November 1976Concert
#

Seals and Crofts play Homecoming and lose $3,800

Jim Seals and Dash Crofts returned to Western for the Homecoming concert on Saturday 6 November 1976, with the Cate Brothers opening. About 5,200 attended and the concert lost roughly $3,800; Ron Beck blamed the Saturday date, saying everyone was worn out from the rest of Homecoming day. Afterwards the pair held a fireside chat with about 80 students about their Baha'i faith.

lost about $3,800; attendance about 5,200

College Heights Herald 52:23, 9 Nov 1976 PDF

6 November 1976Tradition
#

ASG Homecoming dance with the Back Stabbers and Those Perfections

For those who wanted livelier Homecoming entertainment, ASG sponsored a dance in the Garrett Conference Center Ballroom featuring the Back Stabbers and Those Perfections. It ran the same weekend as the Seals and Crofts concert.

1977 Talisman, Homecoming

9 November 1976
#

Seals and Crofts concert loses ASG $3,800

The Herald reported that the Seals and Crofts concert set the Associated Student Government back $3,800. Concert finances would remain a recurring sore point for ASG through the rest of the decade.

Herald 52:21, 9 Nov 1976 PDF

15 November 1976Concert
#

Barry Manilow plays free to 7,000 and cuts his set short

Barry Manilow gave the only free major concert of the semester on Monday 15 November 1976 before 7,000 people. The show cost $23,000, with ticket sales subtracting $10,100 from that; a heckler on the first row put Manilow in a bad mood, so he dropped his commercial set and cut the show by fifteen minutes.

cost $23,000, less $10,100 in ticket sales; attendance 7,000

23 November 1976
#

ASG recommends preregistration for all students

The Herald reported that ASG would recommend preregistration for all students. Advance registration was one of the four items president Christy Vogt later carried into her end-of-year recommendations to the administration.

Herald 52:25, 23 Nov 1976 PDF

ASG considers cutting off the Volunteer Bureau

The Herald reported that ASG might discontinue funding for the Student Volunteer Bureau. A week later an editorial argued the bureau should have ASG's support; ASG kept funding it for another year before stopping in May 1978.

Bill calls for ASG to evaluate itself

Bill 6 proposed that the Associated Student Government conduct a self-evaluation "for the purposes of transparency and improvement."

25 February 1977
#

Discrimination resolution fails as 14 abstain

Fourteen members abstained as the Associated Student Government rejected a resolution on discrimination, the Herald reported. The same issue noted that filing for ASG posts would begin the following Monday.

Herald 52:43, 25 Feb 1977 PDF

8 March 1977
#

ASG passes bill opposing tuition increase

The congress approved Bill 9, formally opposing a proposed tuition increase. The same March meeting produced Bill 8, which created a Complaint Committee to handle student grievances.

SGA Records (UA66), Bill 9 - Opposition to Tuition Increase, 8 Mar 1977

23 March 1977Concert
#

Rufus and Wild Cherry draw 6,500 to a loud spring show

Rufus and Wild Cherry headlined the first major spring concert on 23 March 1977, opened by Fresh, whom Ron Beck signed for $500 because they were a favourite of Wild Cherry. About 6,500 attended, roughly 4,000 of them students, and Chaka Khan sang on her birthday; Beck said students told him the concert was too loud and that Rufus did not perform to their expectations.

attendance about 6,500; opening act Fresh cost $500

College Heights Herald 52:47, 25 Mar 1977 PDF

April 1977
#

Four candidates open the race to succeed Vogt

The Herald previewed a crowded ASG election: Tom Blair, Kirt Grubbs, Mark McChesney and Bob Moore all sought the presidency, with voting set for a Tuesday primary. Three candidates ran for the administrative vice presidency and four for the activities post in the same election.

Herald 52:49, 1 Apr 1977 PDF

5 April 1977
#

Bill 11 sends the constitutional revision to a referendum

Congress had created a Constitutional Revision Committee by Bill 2 on 7 September 1976. On 5 April 1977 it passed Bill 11, to hold a referendum on the proposed constitutional revisions.

SGA Bill 11 - Forward Constitutional Revision, 5 Apr 1977

12 April 1977
#

Vogt urges involvement as her term closes

With her term nearly over, president Christy Vogt wrote "Urges Involvement" for the Herald, days before Bob Moore's election to succeed her.

Herald 52:52, 12 Apr 1977 PDF

15 April 1977
#

Bob Moore elected ASG president by 26 votes

One of the narrowest margins in the record. The same issue carries the outgoing president congratulating voters, the faculty asking for a say in hiring, and Western fighting a beer licence for a local pizzeria.

Herald 52:53, 15 Apr 1977 PDF

26 April 1977
#

Vogt closes the congress with four recommendations

President Christy Vogt sent a letter closing the congress and thanking ASG members for their work. She left four items to recommend to the administration: constitutional revision, extended hours for student buildings, open visitation and advanced registration.

SGA Records (UA66), Christy Vogt Letter, 26 Apr 1977

27 April 1977Concert
#

Jimmy Buffett and the Coral Reefer Band closed the concert year

The Herald announced on 25 March 1977 that a Jimmy Buffett concert was set for 27 April. The 1977 Talisman records that Buffett and the Coral Reefer Band played Diddle Arena that night, drawing three encores after a ninety-minute set from a small crowd, with highlights from 'Changes in Latitudes, Changes in Attitudes' and tunes from 'Livin' and Dyin' in 3/4 Time', 'A-1-A' and 'Havana Daydreamin''. The concert lost money, and the yearbook counts Buffett among the acts brought to Western in 1976-77 by the ASG concert programme run by activities vice president Rick Kelley.

College Heights Herald 52:47, 25 Mar 1977 PDF

Student discount card covers 29 merchants

The Herald reported that the student discount card offered reductions from 29 merchants. The same issue carried Rick Kelley's review of a year in which Chicago and Barry Manilow headlined the concerts and a prosecutor and a reporter were among the speakers.

29 participating merchants

What this is

A year-by-year record of student government at Western Kentucky University, from the ratification of the Associated Students constitution in April 1966 to the present. Every entry names the source it came from.

Corrections

The names here start with the plaque in the SGA Chambers, and the plaque is known to be wrong in places. Where the archive disagrees with it, this site follows the archive and records the change on the corrections page. If you served, or you know that a year here is wrong, the project wants to hear from you: people who held an office can be given the keys to their own year on the contribute page.

What it rests on

Everything here comes from what is publicly available online: the digitised Herald and SGA's own papers on TopSCHOLAR, the Talisman yearbooks, wkuherald.com, the university's news pages and the Wayback Machine. That is a fraction of what happened. Student government has met most weeks for sixty years, and the greater part of it was never written down, or was written down on paper that has never been scanned. A thin year here is a thin record, not a quiet one.

Text on this site is the work of the project. Photographs, documents and legislation are reproduced from Western Kentucky University's own open archives and from the College Heights Herald and the Talisman; each item names the collection, issue and page it came from and links to the original.
