Will Harris
president · plaque: 2019-20
Will Harris: From Glasgow. Sworn in as student regent 2 August 2019. His term ran into the March 2020 campus shutdown.

Will Harris — the term

Harris won the student body presidency in April 2019 at the head of the WGK ticket with Garrett Edmonds and Kenan Mujkanovic, defeating Asha McWilliams. After the results were announced at SGA's election party, he said he looked forward to "making a positive impact for all students here at WKU" and to collaborating with the opposing ticket. He took office at SGA's annual banquet and presided over his first meeting the following Tuesday.

The defining controversy of his fall semester was Resolution 4-19-F, the senate's push to punish Alpha Xi Delta after a video showed members singing a racial slur. Harris abstained when his executive board vetoed the resolution 4-0, explaining that the discrimination claim would have to come from the Student Code of Conduct and that no violation had been noted by the Office of Student Conduct. When senators suggested his position as a knight of Alpha Xi Delta motivated him, he rejected the charge and said he wanted to reach an amicable outcome with the resolution's authors.

By his second semester the senate was passing a busier legislative slate: an expanded Go With the Flow menstrual-hygiene program (Bill 1-20-S, 24-0-0, 11 February 2020) and a $5,000 hammock initiative for the First Year Village (Bill 3-20-S, 23-6, 3 March 2020) that Edmonds said was meant to make SGA known for more than "scantrons and greenbooks." Harris led a senate delegation to the Rally for Higher Education in Frankfort that same day. Eight days later WKU suspended in-person classes over COVID-19; Harris kept his cabinet meeting by Zoom through the spring, donated the year's unspent budget to the WKU Opportunity Fund and Emergency Relief Fund, and postponed SGA's spring elections into the fall.

What happened, in order

2019-04-17

Harris-led WGK ticket wins SGA executive election

The WGK ticket of Will Harris, Garrett Edmonds and Kenan Mujkanovic won the spring SGA elections, with Harris defeating Asha McWilliams for student body president. Edmonds was re-elected to a second term as executive vice president and Mujkanovic defeated Symone Whalin for administrative vice president. Results were announced at SGA's election party, with the new officers set to take office at the annual banquet; the Herald's results story reported no turnout figures.

Herald, 17 Apr 2019 ↗
2019-09-03

First meeting of the year confirms five executive appointments

At the first meeting of the eighteenth Senate, presided over by Speaker Nathan Terrell, the body confirmed all five of President Will Harris's executive-branch nominees: Aubrey Kelley as chief of staff (16-1, with Anthony Survance dissenting), Ashlynn Evans as director of public relations (12-5), Abbey Norvell as director of academic and student affairs (unanimous), Paul Brosky as director of information technology (14-2), and Josh Zaczek as Legislative Research Committee chair (unanimous). Appointees outlined plans including wider use of counseling center fee vouchers and a revival of the need-based Earn-a-Computer program.

SGA Senate minutes, 3 Sep 2019 ↗
2019-09-10

Senate blocks budget over special projects fund

The senate debated the year's proposed budget, which included a $6,500 special projects fund for executive branch initiatives such as a sand volleyball court and hammock space in the First Year Village green space. Senators including Anthony Survance and Symone Whalin objected that the fund cut into scholarship money; the senate voted 10-4 on an amendment moving $3,000 into scholarships and $3,500 into senate discretionary funds. Rather than accept the budget as amended, the executive branch pulled it from the agenda, forcing a vote the following week.

SGA Senate minutes, 10 Sep 2019 ↗
2019-09-19

Budget passes unanimously with spending stipulations

All 13 senators approved the yearly budget after a compromise added stipulations restricting the special projects fund to the First Year Village plans and redistributing any money unspent by March 3 back to discretionary and voucher funds. The budget's passage let Bills 1-19-F (Greeks Go Green) and 2-19-F (funding for Bowling Green Pride) pass unanimously. The senate also amended its election codes to halve campaign spending maximums, which had been $300 for presidential candidates, $125 for vice presidential candidates and $50 for senate candidates.

Herald, 19 Sep 2019 ↗
2019-10-24

Senate passes Resolution 4-19-F against Alpha Xi Delta

The senate voted 24-7 to pass Resolution 4-19-F, authored by senators Symone Whalin and Anthony Survance, supporting disciplinary action up to suspension from Greek affairs against the Epsilon Kappa chapter of Alpha Xi Delta. The resolution followed an August Twitter video showing AXiD members singing a racial slur in a rap song, and the authors said administrative silence signaled the university did not care about minority students. Before passage the senate amended the title to drop a call for outright removal from campus.

Herald, 24 Oct 2019 ↗
2019-10-30

Executive board veto of Resolution 4-19-F upheld

The executive board vetoed Resolution 4-19-F by a 4-0 vote with two abstentions, and the senate confirmed the veto at its Tuesday meeting. President Will Harris, one of the abstainers, said the resolution's discrimination claim would have to come from the Student Code of Conduct and no violation had been noted by the Office of Student Conduct, while co-author Anthony Survance accused the executive branch of dragging the issue out. Senators also raised Harris's connection to Alpha Xi Delta as one of its knights, which he rejected as grounds for bias.

Herald, 30 Oct 2019 ↗
2019-11-12

Senate fills last two committee chairs, senators join December climate walkout

By its ninth meeting the senate had filled its last two vacant committee chairs, naming Jillian Kenney to head the Sustainability committee and Hope Wells to head SAVES (Standing Against Violence and Ending Self-harm). Senator Jay Wells asked ten senators to join the Sunrise Movement's 6 December climate walkout and proposed an ad hoc labor committee to test-run the following semester.

2020-02-11

Go With the Flow feminine hygiene bill passes unanimously

Bill 1-20-S, funding the Go With the Flow program that supplies feminine hygiene products in campus restrooms, passed the senate 24-0-0 with 24 senators present. The bill, sponsored by Campus Improvements chair Matt Barr, doubled the program's budget from $588, and a floor amendment from Senator Symone Whalin raised the final amount to $1,300 from senate discretionary funds. Products were to be placed in Grise Hall, DSU, Preston Center, Gary Ransdell Hall and Snell Hall, including gender neutral bathrooms.

Herald, 12 Feb 2020 ↗ read it on this site ↗
2020-03-03

Senate approves $5,000 hammocks project

The senate passed Bill 3-20-S 23-6, allocating $5,000 from the special projects fund to Facilities Management to begin planning hammock poles across campus, after the sand volleyball court proposed earlier in the year was deemed too expensive. Brian Kuster, head of student life and enrollment experience, spoke in favor but raised concerns about tree bark damage from hammocking; the Residence Hall Association's president also spoke in favor. Senators Anthony Survance and Josh Zaczek spoke against the bill, arguing the money would be better spent on food assistance and that the plan lacked a timeline or budget detail. Executive Vice President Garrett Edmonds said he and Harris ran so SGA would be known for more than "scantrons and greenbooks."

Herald, 4 Mar 2020 ↗ read it on this site ↗
2020-03-03

Senate passes four more bills the same night as the hammocks vote

The meeting that approved the hammocks initiative also passed Bill 4-20-S, organizational aid funding for eight student groups including WKU HOLAS and Iota Phi Theta (15-0-0-2), and Bill 5-20-S, funding the Healthy Days Fair (15-0-0-2). Senators introduced Bill 6-20-S, an honor society membership reimbursement fund, and Bill 7-20-S, an Educational Disparities Scholarship, for a vote the following week; earlier in the meeting a guest speaker pitched a $1,000 LGBTQ+ scholarship bill still in drafting.

2020-03-11campus

WKU extends spring break, moves classes online amid COVID-19

WKU announced it would extend spring break by one week and transition to "alternative delivery formats" for classes amid growing coronavirus concerns, with President Tim Caboni recommending that students who could safely return home do so. The announcement came after the University of Kentucky and University of Louisville suspended in-person classes. In-person instruction never resumed that semester, upending the rest of SGA's spring calendar.

Herald, 11 Mar 2020 ↗
2020-04-20

Spring elections postponed until fall as SGA moves to Zoom

With campus closed by COVID-19, SGA postponed its spring elections until at least September so voting could happen in person, and added an "emergency clause" constitutional amendment allowing cabinet terms to be extended until the following election with judicial oversight. The cabinet continued meeting over Zoom every Tuesday. Harris said that after scholarships were awarded, the remainder of SGA's budget would be donated to the WKU Opportunity Fund and Emergency Relief Fund, and SGA gave its remaining food bank goods to the Office of Sustainability for redistribution.

Herald, 20 Apr 2020 ↗
