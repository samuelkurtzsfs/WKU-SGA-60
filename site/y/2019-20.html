Will Harris president and student regent, 2019-20

Office
President and student regent
Term recorded here
2019-20
Place in the line
the 54th president and the 51st student regent
Standing of the record
As recorded on the plaque. Not yet corroborated in the archive.

From Glasgow. Sworn in as student regent 2 August 2019. His term ran into the March 2020 campus shutdown. His term ran past its normal end. SGA voted on 20 April 2020 to postpone the spring election to the autumn because the campus had closed for COVID-19, and Harris stayed in office under that postponement: he was still president when the senate met for the first time since April on 15 September 2020, and gave the election update at the meeting of 22 September. Garrett Edmonds was elected on 29 September and took over in October. The extension is not a second term and Harris is counted once, for 2019-20.

Harris won the student body presidency in April 2019 at the head of the WGK ticket with Garrett Edmonds and Kenan Mujkanovic, defeating Asha McWilliams. After the results were announced at SGA's election party, he said he looked forward to "making a positive impact for all students here at WKU" and to collaborating with the opposing ticket. He took office at SGA's annual banquet and presided over his first meeting the following Tuesday.

The defining controversy of his fall semester was Resolution 4-19-F, the senate's push to punish Alpha Xi Delta after a video showed members singing a racial slur. Harris abstained when his executive board vetoed the resolution 4-0, explaining that the discrimination claim would have to come from the Student Code of Conduct and that no violation had been noted by the Office of Student Conduct. When senators suggested his position as a knight of Alpha Xi Delta motivated him, he rejected the charge and said he wanted to reach an amicable outcome with the resolution's authors.

The year, in order31

17 April 2019
#

Harris-led WGK ticket wins SGA executive election

The WGK ticket of Will Harris, Garrett Edmonds and Kenan Mujkanovic won the spring SGA elections, with Harris defeating Asha McWilliams for student body president. Edmonds was re-elected to a second term as executive vice president and Mujkanovic defeated Symone Whalin for administrative vice president. Results were announced at SGA's election party, with the new officers set to take office at the annual banquet; the Herald's results story reported no turnout figures.

Herald, 17 Apr 2019

4 September 2019
#

First meeting of the year confirms five executive appointments

At its first official meeting of the semester, the senate confirmed all five of President Will Harris's executive branch nominees: Aubrey Kelley as chief of staff, Ashlynn Evans as chief of public relations, Abbey Norvell as director of academic and student affairs, Paul Brosky as director of information technology and Josh Zaczek as Legislative Research Committee chair. Appointees outlined plans including wider use of counseling center fee vouchers and a revival of the need-based Earn-a-Computer program.

Herald, 4 Sep 2019

10 September 2019Programme
#

Greeks Go Green sustainability contest funded at $75

Bill 1-19-F allocated $75 from senate discretionary funding for prizes in Greeks Go Green, a competition between WKU's Greek organisations to run the most sustainable chapter house by logging environmentally friendly actions for points. The contest ran from 20 September 2019 through April, with winners announced at the Greek Week convocation. The bill passed its second reading on 10 September 2019.

#

SGA sponsored the third Bowling Green Pride Festival

Bill 2-19-F allocated $500 from senate discretionary funding to sponsor the Bowling Green Pride Festival held on 5 October 2019 at Circus Square Park by the Bowling Green Fairness group. The money bought a Silver Sponsorship, putting SGA's name and logo on the festival t-shirt and website and securing tabling space for the Committee for Diversity and Inclusion. The bill's authors, senators Conner Hounshell and Mark Clark, wrote that funding was necessary because LGBTQ+ students were not legally protected in Bowling Green for want of a Fairness ordinance, and noted this was the third such festival and that SGA had sponsored every one before it. The posted form records a first reading on 3 September 2019, a second reading on 10 September and a passing vote.

11 September 2019
#

Senate blocks budget over special projects fund

The senate refused to pass the year's budget over a $6,500 special projects fund the executive branch wanted for a sand volleyball court and hammock space at the First Year Village green space. Senators including Anthony Survance objected that the allocation cut into scholarship money, with Survance calling the motion "slimy", and the senate voted to keep $3,000 in its discretionary fund and $35,000 in scholarships. The executive branch pulled the budget, forcing a week's wait before a revised version could be voted on.

Herald, 11 Sep 2019

11 September 2019
#

SGA deadlocks over budget, executive branch pulls the bill

The senate and executive branch failed to pass a budget at their second meeting, splitting over a $6,500 special projects allocation for a sand volleyball court and hammock spaces at the First Year Village. Senator Anthony Survance called the motion "slimy" and said it cut money reserved for scholarships; the senate voted to keep $3,000 in its own discretionary fund and $35,000 in scholarships, and the executive branch withdrew the budget. Rules required a full week before a revised budget could be voted on.

Herald, 11 Sep 2019

19 September 2019
#

Budget passed with spending stipulations and campaign spending caps halved

All 13 senators approved the yearly budget after a three-part compromise confined the special projects fund to the First Year Village plans and redistributed any money unspent by March 3 back to discretionary and voucher funds. Its passage let Bill 1-19-F, Greeks Go Green, and Bill 2-19-F, funding for Bowling Green Pride, pass unanimously. The senate also amended its election codes to halve campaign spending maximums, which had been $300 for presidential candidates, $125 for vice presidential candidates and $50 for senate candidates. Executive Vice President Garrett Edmonds objected, calling SGA turnout awful and estimating 1,500 students voted the previous year, while Senator Symone Whalin said poorer students were less likely to run for executive office.

Herald, 19 Sep 2019

24 September 2019
#

Judicial Council filled out with four nominations

The senate confirmed four nominees to the SGA Judicial Council at the 24 September 2019 meeting, filling the body out ahead of that week's senate elections. President Will Harris had interviewed freshman Jason Herlick, junior Derek Collins, sophomore Turner Reynolds and freshman Holden Schroeder before the meeting, and all four brought experience from other student government organisations. Herlick was confirmed unanimously after Senator Anthony Survance asked whether he had read SGA's constitution and rules. Collins, who headed WKU's Students Demand Action chapter, spoke about the council's duty to hold the association to account.

Herald, 25 Sep 2019 - "SGA judicial council 'fully functional' following four nominations"

October 2019
#

Council defined a Senate majority as a majority of those present

At its first meeting of the year the council had to decide whether a majority in the Senate chamber was measured against those present or against the full membership. Chief Justice Isaac Keller argued that a senator who is not present abstains and loses the right to vote. No justice objected, and the council ruled unanimously that a simple majority is based on senators present.

Judicial Council minutes, 1 Oct 2019

2 October 2019
#

Senate votes 17-15 to cancel a meeting for a sorority philanthropy event

A motion by Senator Hope Wells to cancel the Oct. 8 senate meeting for Kappa Delta's Shenanigans passed 17-15 after debate. Senator Anthony Survance argued that given recent controversies with Greek organizations SGA should not signal endorsement, while Senator Brigid Stakelum said the reason was practical, that quorum might not be met. Newly elected senators were sworn in at the same meeting by Chief Justice Isaac Keller.

Herald, 2 Oct 2019

8 October 2019
#

Two SGA members organize protest against Alpha Xi Delta

Senators Symone Whalin and Anthony Survance organized a protest at the Shenanigans philanthropy event in response to a Twitter video showing WKU Alpha Xi Delta members singing a racial slur. Survance said the vote to cancel the senate meeting for the event helped prompt the protest, and the pair created a Facebook group titled "Demand Alpha Xi Delta's Removal." Whalin said administrative inaction signaled that minority students were not a priority.

Herald, 8 Oct 2019

15 October 2019
#

Diversity scholarship bill postponed over a Title IX conflict

Discussion of Bill 4-19-F, which would have created a diversity scholarship, was postponed at the 15 October 2019 meeting because the bill conflicted with Title IX, and no date was set for taking it up again while the Legislative Research Committee worked through it. At the same meeting Senator Symone Whalin announced a campus survey on the use of racial slurs and tried to show a clip from a slur-awareness video, which failed on technical grounds and was held over. Harris told senators he and Executive Vice President Garrett Edmonds had met administrators about the special projects fund and come away with costings.

Herald, 16 Oct 2019 - "Nominations confirmed, racial slur discussion continues at SGA meeting"

24 October 2019
#

Senate passed Resolution 4-19-F seeking discipline against Alpha Xi Delta, 24-7

The senate voted 24-7 for Resolution 4-19-F, authored by senators Symone Whalin and Anthony Survance, supporting disciplinary action up to suspension from Greek affairs against the Epsilon Kappa chapter of Alpha Xi Delta. The resolution followed an August Twitter video showing chapter members singing a racial slur in a rap song, and the authors said administrative silence signaled the university did not care about minority students, wanting more than "an article from the Herald or an email from Bob Skipper." Before passage a motion struck the call for removal from campus out of the title and a friendly amendment substituted suspension from Greek affairs. Senator Mary Mayo said it was not SGA's job to act as a disciplinary committee, and the resolution was to be submitted to the Faculty Senate.

Herald, 24 Oct 2019

29 October 2019
#

Council ruled the Alpha Xi Delta resolution's clause constitutional 7-0

Asked by Chief of Staff Conner Hounshell to rule on the discrimination clause in Resolution 4-19-F, the council heard President Will Harris argue that defining discrimination was a Title IX matter rather than SGA's. All seven justices found the whereas clause constitutional, noting vagueness in section 1.1 and that SGA already awarded scholarships on protected characteristics. The ruling was made pending university information, and the council said a university finding of illegality would change it.

Judicial Council minutes, 29 Oct 2019

30 October 2019
#

Executive board vetoed Resolution 4-19-F 4-0 and the senate sustained the veto

The executive board vetoed Resolution 4-19-F, the Alpha Xi Delta resolution, by 4 votes to 0 with two of its six members abstaining, and the senate confirmed the veto at its Tuesday meeting. President Will Harris, one of the abstainers, said the discrimination claim the resolution invoked would have to come from the Student Code of Conduct and that the Office of Student Conduct had noted no violation. Co-author Anthony Survance accused the executive branch of dragging the issue out, and Legislative Research Chair Josh Zaczek disputed the reasoning. Senators raised Harris's standing as an Alpha Xi Delta knight, which he rejected as grounds for bias.

Herald, 30 Oct 2019

5 November 2019Programme
#

Study Away scholarships renewed for a ninth year

Bill 5-19-F allocated $1,500 from the scholarship budget to WKU's Office of Study Away for the 2019-20 year, continuing a funding partnership the bill records as having run since 2011. Study Away sends WKU students to placements elsewhere in the United States rather than abroad, and the office had approached SGA to keep the arrangement going. The bill was authored by Administrative Vice President Kenan Mujkanovic, sponsored by Josh Zaczek of the Legislative Research Committee, and passed on 5 November 2019.

$1,500 from scholarships

7 November 2019
#

Chief justice tells senate the AXiD resolution would be symbolic; student urges labor committee

Chief Justice Isaac Keller told senators the resolution would be purely symbolic because SGA cannot direct WKU to defund an organization on grounds of discrimination, and suggested supporters take the matter to the Title IX office or the Kentucky Human Rights Commission. Harris said no student had filed a formal discrimination charge. Former senator Jay Wells addressed the meeting urging SGA to form a labor committee, citing cut student worker hours and the CAPE committee's recommendation to suspend 101 academic programs.

Executive Vice President Garrett Edmonds reported First Year Village construction was on schedule and said a remaining building near the old library site would be torn down if incoming student numbers did not require the beds.

Etiquette dinner for the Glasgow campus

Five hundred dollars voted to the 19th Healthy Days Fair

Bill 5-20-S proposed allocating $500 from senate discretionary funding to WellU towards the Healthy Days Fair set for Wednesday 25 March 2020, the nineteenth annual student health fair, in return for SGA's logo on the event's t-shirts, video boards, social media and email. 3 March 2020Programme
#

A scholarship aimed at counties with the fewest graduates

Bill 7-20-S proposed allocating $1,000 from senate discretionary funding to create a scholarship for students with financial need from Kentucky counties with low college graduation rates or a low share of residents holding bachelor's degrees. The bill argued those educational disparities track with lower incomes and higher unemployment and poverty, and directed the Director and Committee of Academic and Student Affairs to build an application identifying qualifying counties from data sources such as the Kentucky Council on Postsecondary Education. The award was to be made in the spring 2020 semester. The posted bill records a first reading on 3 March 2020 and no second reading, so the archive cannot say it passed.

4 March 2020
#

Senate approved a $5,000 hammock project after the volleyball court was dropped

The senate passed Bill 3-20-S, allocating $5,000 from the special projects fund to begin planning hammock poles across campus, after the sand volleyball court proposed earlier in the year was judged too expensive. Planned locations included spots near most residence halls and the Valley, and Executive Vice President Garrett Edmonds said he and Harris had run so SGA would be known for more than scantrons and greenbooks. Legislative Research Chair Josh Zaczek criticized having to vote without seeing budget numbers, said he had met friction getting figures from Edmonds and wanted assurance the money would be spent inclusively. Residence Hall Association President Emily Whitney backed the project and noted RHA's own budget cuts, and the senate kept the power to halt spending during planning.

11 March 2020
#

WKU extends spring break, moves classes online amid COVID-19

WKU announced it would extend spring break by one week and transition to "alternative delivery formats" for classes amid growing coronavirus concerns, with President Tim Caboni recommending that students who could safely return home do so. The announcement came after the University of Kentucky and University of Louisville suspended in-person classes. In-person instruction never resumed that semester, upending the rest of SGA's spring calendar.

Herald, 11 Mar 2020

14 April 2020
#

Bill 8-20-S puts emergency clauses to the Senate

With campus closed by COVID-19, Bill 8-20-S had its first reading on 14 April 2020. Authored by Dawson McCoun and MJ Mayo, it provided that on a two-thirds Senate vote together with a majority of the Executive Cabinet and a majority of the Judicial Council, officers and senators could extend their terms until a new election was held, the president could extend the student regent position under KRS 164.321, and the rescheduled election would be held within three weeks of the start of the fall 2020 semester. The clause as later printed in the constitution allows four weeks and adds virtual Senate meetings.

SGA Bill 8-20-S

20 April 2020
#

Elections postponed, and a clause written to keep the officers in place

With the campus closed by COVID-19, SGA postponed its spring elections until at least September so that voting could happen in person, and passed an emergency clause amending the constitution to let cabinet terms run on until the following election under judicial oversight. Executive vice president Garrett Edmonds explained the amendment. President Will Harris said that once scholarships were awarded the remainder of SGA's budget would go to the WKU Opportunity Fund and the Emergency Relief Fund, and the organisation gave what was left in its food bank to the Office of Sustainability to redistribute. The cabinet kept meeting over Zoom every Tuesday. The clause is why the year that followed had no handover in the spring: Harris stayed in office through the summer and was still president when the senate met for the first time since April on 15 September, and Edmonds was not elected to succeed him until 29 September.

Herald, 20 Apr 2020 - SGA spring elections postponed until fall

5 May 2020Service
#

A thousand dollars to the food pantry after the campus closed

Bill 9-20-S proposed donating $1,000 to the WKU Food Recovery Network for the Office of Sustainability food pantry, drawn as organisational aid with the organisational aid budget expanded by $1,000 from legislative discretionary funds. The bill records that the pantry's usual influx of donated food had dried up under COVID-19 while it had served over 150 students, faculty, staff, alumni and community members in the five weeks since the university closed, and lists what the money would buy: spaghetti sauce, juice, canned meat, macaroni and cheese, soup, condiments, rice bowls and other easily prepared meals. It was read first on 28 April 2020 and a second time on 5 May 2020; the posted form carries no vote line.

What this is

A year-by-year record of student government at Western Kentucky University, from the ratification of the Associated Students constitution in April 1966 to the present. Every entry names the source it came from.

Corrections

The names here start with the plaque in the SGA Chambers, and the plaque is known to be wrong in places. Where the archive disagrees with it, this site follows the archive and records the change on the corrections page. If you served, or you know that a year here is wrong, the project wants to hear from you: people who held an office can be given the keys to their own year on the contribute page.

What it rests on

Everything here comes from what is publicly available online: the digitised Herald and SGA's own papers on TopSCHOLAR, the Talisman yearbooks, wkuherald.com, the university's news pages and the Wayback Machine. That is a fraction of what happened. Student government has met most weeks for sixty years, and the greater part of it was never written down, or was written down on paper that has never been scanned. A thin year here is a thin record, not a quiet one.

Reuse

Text on this site is the work of the project. Photographs, documents and legislation are reproduced from Western Kentucky University's own open archives and from the College Heights Herald and the Talisman; each item names the collection, issue and page it came from and links to the original.
