David Payne: Plaque reads 1982; this site originally filed him under 1982-83. Board of Regents minutes (30 Jan 1982) and Herald coverage place him as SGA president succeeding Marcel Bush, who resigned 14 Jan 1982, filling the balance of the 1981-82 term. Margaret Ragan, elected president in April 1982, led the separate 1982-83 term - the two are not the same office-year.

Marcell Bush — the term

Marcel Bush won the Associated Student Government presidency in April 1981, defeating David Sturgeon after a campaign the Herald said saw candidates "playing big time politics." Close races elsewhere on the ballot prompted recounts.

In office that fall, ASG raised its own campaign spending limit and vetoed a plan to pay an education lobbyist, days after rallying publicly for higher education funding. Bush later listed the fall rally as one achievement of his term.

Bush resigned the presidency on or shortly before 14 January 1982, reported the same week as the retrospective on his term. David Payne succeeded him; Board of Regents minutes from 30 January 1982 name Payne the new SGA president.

David Payne — the term

David Payne first appears in the indexed record in February 1981 with a Herald letter titled 'Apathy Erased.' By October 1981, before he held the presidency, he was writing on the Associated Student Government's behalf, declaring that ASG was rallying for higher education as budget cuts loomed.

Payne became ASG's president after Marcel Bush resigned on 14 January 1982; Board of Regents minutes from the January 30 meeting name him the new SGA president. In early February the Herald reported that Payne wanted another adviser for ASG, and its editorial page attacked his judgment, while a report the next week said Payne considered certain resolutions misdirected.

Later that month, letter writers to the Herald argued that Payne had been made a victim in the affair; the indexed headline does not state what precipitated it. His name does not appear in the indexed headlines of the April 1982 election that chose Margaret Ragan for the 1982-83 term, nor in the 1982-83 issues examined - consistent with his presidency ending with Bush's original term.
