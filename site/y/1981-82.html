Marcel Bush president and student regent, 1981-82

Office
President and student regent
Term recorded here
1981-82
Place in the line
the 16th president and the 14th student regent
Standing of the record
Moved here from the year the plaque gives. The correction and its evidence are set out below.

The plaque reads "Marcell Bush" with two Ls. Herald bylines spell it Marcel throughout, and SGA's own roster of former presidents, archived in September 2001, lists "Marcel Bush, Lexington, 1981-82" with a footnote recording that he resigned in January 1982 and that administrative vice president David Payne filled the position. This site follows the sources. The roster stars Bush as a student regent and does not star Payne, which is why the Board seat went to a special election in February 1982 rather than passing with the presidency. Herald bylines consistently spell this "Marcel Bush" (one L); the plaque and this site list "Marcell Bush" - spelling unverified. Resigned the ASG presidency 14 January 1982; David Payne succeeded him for the balance of the term.

Marcel Bush won the Associated Student Government presidency in April 1981, defeating David Sturgeon after a campaign the Herald said saw candidates "playing big time politics." Close races elsewhere on the ballot prompted recounts.

In office that fall, ASG raised its own campaign spending limit and vetoed a plan to pay an education lobbyist, days after rallying publicly for higher education funding. Bush later listed the fall rally as one achievement of his term.

Bush resigned the presidency on or shortly before 14 January 1982, reported the same week as the retrospective on his term. David Payne succeeded him; Board of Regents minutes from 30 January 1982 name Payne the new SGA president.

Standing of the record
Moved here from the year the plaque gives. The correction and its evidence are set out below.

Plaque reads 1982; this site originally filed him under 1982-83. Board of Regents minutes (30 Jan 1982) and Herald coverage place him as SGA president succeeding Marcel Bush, who resigned 14 Jan 1982, filling the balance of the 1981-82 term. Margaret Ragan, elected president in April 1982, led the separate 1982-83 term - the two are not the same office-year. The Herald of 14 January 1982 photographed Payne, then Associated Student Government vice president, announcing the resignation of president and student regent Marcell Bush, whom he succeeded.

David Payne first appears in the indexed record in February 1981 with a Herald letter titled 'Apathy Erased.' By October 1981, before he held the presidency, he was writing on the Associated Student Government's behalf, declaring that ASG was rallying for higher education as budget cuts loomed.

Payne became ASG's president after Marcel Bush resigned on 14 January 1982; Board of Regents minutes from the January 30 meeting name him the new SGA president. In early February the Herald reported that Payne wanted another adviser for ASG, and its editorial page attacked his judgment, while a report the next week said Payne considered certain resolutions misdirected.

Later that month, letter writers to the Herald argued that Payne had been made a victim in the affair; the indexed headline does not state what precipitated it. His name does not appear in the indexed headlines of the April 1982 election that chose Margaret Ragan for the 1982-83 term, nor in the 1982-83 issues examined - consistent with his presidency ending with Bush's original term.

Sandra Norfleet student regent, 1981-82

Standing of the record
Moved here from the year the plaque gives. The correction and its evidence are set out below.

Student regent, not president. The plaque reads 1982 and this site had filed her under 1982-83; the Herald puts the whole term inside 1981-82. Five students sought the seat in what the paper called the first campus-wide election for it, the vote of 9 February 1982 produced no majority, and Norfleet won the runoff on 16 February. On 15 April 1982 the Herald ran 'Student Regent's 2-month Term Nears End'. Who held the seat earlier in the year, before that first election, is not established in the archive.

No candidate won a majority on February 9, forcing a runoff, which Norfleet won on February 16. The Herald reported her election as student regent in its February 18 issue.

Her initial term was short: by mid-April 1982 the Herald noted that the student regent's 2-month term was nearing its end. She is listed among the 1982-83 student leaders, and in February 1983 the Herald's editorial page was still weighing the office, insisting the student regent must represent everyone.

Norfleet reached the student regent seat through a crowded contest. The Herald reported on 2 February 1982 that five students were seeking the student regent spot, and two days later Kevin Francke ran a piece in which the candidates set out their qualities.

Kevin Francke reported on 18 February 1982 that Sandra Norfleet had been elected student regent. The same issue carried his story on a group that would speak on the Associated Student Government's behalf.

Her time in the seat was short. By mid-April Francke was reporting that the student regent's two-month term was nearing its end, in an issue that also covered voided ASG election results and a joke resolution that nearly passed the congress.

27 August 1981
#

Bush opened his term saying students should understand ASG

Ellen Banahan's interview with the new president, Marcel Bush, ran in the second issue of the autumn under the line that he wanted students to understand the Associated Student Government. The same issue reported that campus police were offering a night escort service, a year before ASG and Interhall Council started a volunteer one of their own.

College Heights Herald 57:2, 27 August 1981 PDF

3 September 1981
#

ASG announces its goals for the year

The Associated Student Government announced its goals for the coming year. Within two weeks its executives were defending their actions in the Herald, whose editorial page complained that petty politics plagued the organization.

Herald 57:4, 3 Sep 1981 PDF

10 September 1981Service
#

The discount cards that never existed

ASG announced it would file complaints with the Better Business Bureaus of Georgia and Texas against University Press Inc. of Lewisville, Texas, and Atlanta, which had taken $275 from each of at least four Bowling Green merchants for ASG-endorsed student discount cards that were never produced. A man calling himself Louis F. Cordoba had told Gary Smith of the Bouquet Shoppe on the U.S. 31-W By-Pass that then-president Steve Fuller had recommended his shop as an advertiser, and Smith wrote a $275 cheque on 18 March. The cards were supposed to have been handed out at registration for the autumn.

$275 taken from each of at least four merchants

College Heights Herald, Vol. 57, No. 5, 10 September 1981 PDF

10 September 1981
#

ASG submitted its budget

Ellen Banahan reported that ASG had submitted its budget for the year. It ran in the same issue as her first report that the organisation could not find the student discount cards it had ordered, and in a week the Herald headlined as the beginning of budget pleas across the university.

College Heights Herald 57:5, 10 September 1981 PDF

17 September 1981
#

ASG executives defended their actions, and a former president was honoured

Ellen Banahan reported that the ASG executives had defended their actions, in the same issue that reported the campaign spending limit might be raised and that Steve Thornton, the 1978 ASG president, had been honoured. The defence came five days before the Herald's editorial page complained that petty politics plagued the organisation.

College Heights Herald 57:7, 17 September 1981 PDF

22 September 1981

ASG asked for a book drop away from the library

Resolution 81-4, dated 22 September 1981, requested a book drop in a central location away from the library, reviving a request ASG had first made in November 1979 for a drop in the Downing University Center. Resolution 81-3, on parking spaces, carries the same date.

SGA Resolution 81-4, Book Drop

24 September 1981

Campaign spending limit raised

ASG voted to increase its campaign spending limit. The change followed a week of debate over whether the cap should rise.

Herald 57:9, 24 Sep 1981 PDF

29 September 1981Service
#

14,000 cards, a dead phone line and a returned letter

President Marcel Bush had spent three weeks trying to reach the company printing 14,000 ASG discount cards: the Texas number was disconnected, an answering service pushed her to an Atlanta number nobody answered, and a letter to the Texas address came back from the post office. On the day complaints were being filed, company representative Phil Bewley finally telephoned to say the cards had shipped from New York and that printing problems had caused the delay. The Herald noted that until two years earlier ASG had printed discount cards at its own expense as a free student service, charging merchants nothing, and had dropped it when costs became prohibitive.

14,000 cards; merchants paid $275 for a listing

College Heights Herald, Vol. 57, No. 10, 29 September 1981 PDF

October 1981Service
#

Discount cards arrive, but do not go out

The ASG discount cards reached campus the day before the Herald's report of 1 October 1981. There were 14,000 of them and they offered students discounts at 21 listed merchants, valid until 1 September 1982. They were not handed out: Bush had been busy with other projects and passed the job to the communications committee, whose chairwoman, Laura Simms, was still working on a plan.

ASG considered barring freshmen from bringing cars to campus

Ellen Banahan reported that ASG was considering a plan to bar freshmen cars from campus. Parking ran through the session's legislation as well: Resolution 81-3 of 22 September dealt with parking spaces and Resolution 81-5 of 13 October with parking privileges.

College Heights Herald 57:17, 22 October 1981 PDF

Tommy Newton reported that the ASG president had appointed an adviser. The question did not close with the appointment: after Bush resigned, his successor David Payne told the Herald in February that ASG wanted a second one.

A resolution on students' right to privacy

Resolution 81-6, dated 24 November 1981, concerned students' right to privacy, a year after the congress's long fight over dormitory room inspections and open house visitor lists. Resolution 81-9, on safety strips, carries the same date.

SGA Resolution 81-6, Privacy

14 January 1982
#

ASG president Marcel Bush resigns

Associated Student Government president Marcel Bush resigned. The same issue reported him listing the fall's higher-education rally as one achievement of his term.

Herald 57:31, 14 Jan 1982 PDF

26 January 1982Service
#

The discount cards left few happy

Kevin Francke's report on the student discount card scheme ran under the line that it had left few happy, and the same issue carried a second Francke piece reporting that many businesses were unhappy too. It closed a saga that had run since September: ASG could not find the cards, then found the printer's Texas telephone disconnected and its letter returned, then received 14,000 cards it did not hand out for weeks.

College Heights Herald 57:34, 26 January 1982

26 January 1982
#

David Payne's appeal failed and a regent election was called

Kevin Francke reported that David Payne's appeal had failed and that an election for the student regent seat had been set. Payne had succeeded to the ASG presidency after Marcel Bush's resignation, and the Board of Regents minutes of 30 January 1982 name him as president. Under the previous arrangement the ASG president also sat as student regent, as Steve Fuller had. The seat instead went to a campus-wide vote and Sandra Norfleet won the runoff in February. The Herald index records the appeal and its failure but not the grounds on which it was made.

College Heights Herald 57:34, 26 January 1982

ASG appointed a council to settle its internal disputes

The Associated Student Government appointed a council to decide disputes inside the organization. The Herald report, by Kevin Francke, ran two weeks after his coverage of the resignation of ASG president Marcel Bush. The same issue reported that the regents bill, changing how the student regent was chosen, had passed the state House.

23 March 1982

ASG asked for a grace week before finals

Resolution 81-12, dated 23 March 1982, requested a grace week between the last week of classes and finals week. It is the earliest of the calendar demands ASG returned to through the decade, which by 1985 included a fall mid-semester break and a week-long Thanksgiving.

The Herald argued a smaller ASG would be more effective

The Herald's editorial page argued that a smaller Associated Student Government would increase its effectiveness. It ran in the issue reporting Margaret Ragan's win in the repeated general election, and beside a letter applauding the University Center Board.

Marsupial Awards for ASG and the Herald, and a letter calling ASG a joke

Robert Carter's Marsupial Awards column handed prizes to both the Associated Student Government and the Herald, in the same issue that printed a letter from M. A. Baker headed that ASG was a joke. Baker had been the subject of a Herald feature in February 1981 describing him as fighting the system inside ASG. The pair closed a session that began with the paper complaining of petty politics and ended with the presidential election being run twice.

What this is

A year-by-year record of student government at Western Kentucky University, from the ratification of the Associated Students constitution in April 1966 to the present. Every entry names the source it came from.

Corrections

The names here start with the plaque in the SGA Chambers, and the plaque is known to be wrong in places. Where the archive disagrees with it, this site follows the archive and records the change on the corrections page. If you served, or you know that a year here is wrong, the project wants to hear from you through the Student Government Association office.

What it rests on

Everything here comes from what is publicly available online: the digitised Herald and SGA's own papers on TopSCHOLAR, the Talisman yearbooks, wkuherald.com, the university's news pages and the Wayback Machine. That is a fraction of what happened. Student government has met most weeks for sixty years, and the greater part of it was never written down, or was written down on paper that has never been scanned. A thin year here is a thin record, not a quiet one.

Text on this site is the work of the project. Photographs, documents and legislation are reproduced from Western Kentucky University's own open archives and from the College Heights Herald and the Talisman; each item names the collection, issue and page it came from and links to the original.
