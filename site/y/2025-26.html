Rush Robinson president and student regent, 2025-26

Office
President and student regent
Term recorded here
2025-26
Place in the line
the 59th president and the 56th student regent
Standing of the record
As recorded on the plaque. Not yet corroborated in the archive.

Elected 13-15 April 2025 on a turnout of 966. Told the Faculty Senate to 'take a field trip' to see the state of WKU's residence halls. Handed his successor the red suit jacket on 28 April 2026.

Robinson, a senior from Benton, Kentucky triple-majoring in psychology, philosophy and sociology and a member of Sigma Phi Epsilon and Omicron Delta Kappa, won the SGA presidency in the 13-15 April 2025 general election on a turnout of 966, running with Savanna Kurtz for vice president and Gabriel Jerdon for chief financial officer. He also succeeded Sam Kurtz as WKU's student regent; the Board of Regents administered his oath of office on 8 August 2025, the same meeting at which it named the new Gordon Ford College of Business building Amy and David Chandler Hall and elected Jan West chair, Doris Thomas vice chair and Melissa Dennison secretary for 2025-26.

Robinson ran on turning SGA away from national politics and toward direct student advocacy, saying the organization had sometimes echoed national political talking points and that he wanted it to act as "an advocate and resource for students" who could be pointed to the right contact in the administration. His cabinet - Kurtz as vice president, Jerdon as chief financial officer, Maggie Yelton as chief of staff, and Preston Jenkins, reappointed chief communications officer on 22 April 2025 and confirmed by the Senate - ran a legislative program heavy on small, direct-benefit measures: vouchers for CPR certification, dental cleanings, transcripts and identification cards, equipment to borrow such as umbrellas, calculators and hammocks, and a resolution urging accessible restroom stalls in Grise Hall. Jenkins also organized a student-painted mural for a column in the SGA chambers. In March 2026 SGA drew on its projects fund to host a baseball-game tailgate, a response to Athletics Director Todd Stewart's call for more student engagement. The Senate closed its final meeting on 14 April 2026 having passed 56 bills for the year, the most since 2018-19, with Jenkins also reporting that a new university Artificial Intelligence Committee was weighing bulk AI-product subscriptions for students.

Robinson's most sustained policy push was on student mental health, working with Mental Health and Wellbeing Committee chair Veronica Butler on a year-long program backed by data from social work professor Jay Gabbard, who told SGA that 44 percent of WKU students reported depression symptoms and 10 percent had considered suicide in the past year. SGA co-organized Mental Health Week with the WKU Counseling Center and the campus NAMI chapter, ran a Toppers Talk About It awareness campaign, and had student recommendations - including relocating counseling services from Potter Hall to the Downing Student Union - reviewed by the Counseling Center Review Committee before forwarding them to administrators, though Butler said it was unclear whether they would be enacted. The push closed with an Out of Darkness suicide-remembrance walk on 18 April 2026, and successor Caden Lucas said the focus would continue "in a sustained and meaningful way."

At the Senate's first meeting under his successor, Robinson opened by telling the mostly-new chamber they looked untainted by SGA's usual troubles, then handed president-elect Caden Lucas the red suit jacket passed president to president - the same jacket Sam Kurtz had given him the year before. His term as student regent outlasted his SGA presidency: at the Board of Regents' 5 June 2026 meeting, his last as regent, he cast the lone vote against a $204, 1.69 percent tuition increase, saying the board had "to do whatever we can to not raise the tuition" to preserve affordability. Afterward he planned to remain at WKU for an Ed.S. in school psychology, aiming for a career as a school psychologist in a school district.

2 September 2025
#

Caboni tells SGA all community bathrooms will be phased out

President Timothy Caboni told the Senate that WKU intends to eliminate community showers and bathrooms as residence halls are replaced, with two to four people sharing each facility. He said the Student Life Foundation had announced Hilltopper Hall would come down the following summer and that Hilltopper, McCormick and Rodes Harlin halls would all be replaced by fall 2030. He said the university had to reach a point where its offerings created a competitive advantage.

SGA sets up meal swipe donations through Swipe it Forward

Chief of Staff Maggie Yelton told the Herald Editorial Board that Swipe it Forward would let students donate up to three meal swipes a semester from traditional meal plans and let students in need request them through a food assistance form. October 2025
#

General counsel tells SGA that House Bill 4 binds its spending

WKU General Counsel Andrea Anderson told the Senate that the WKU Pride Center would become a student-run organization, losing its status as a university-related initiative and its Downing Student Union office, under House Bill 4. Anderson said the statute, passed March 28 and enforced by the Attorney General's office and a committee formed by Provost Bud Fischer, applies to SGA because SGA receives university money for events and bills. She said SGA could still fund organizations and events if they were content neutral, and that HB4 counts social media and digital content as a resource.

Herald, 1 Oct 2025

Senate funds 78 organizations and strikes a food pantry bill

Bill 10-25-F allocated organizational aid to registered student organizations graded on timeliness, allocation quality and financial dependence. #

A hundred pumpkins on South Lawn for Hope Harbor

SGA's Action and Opportunity Committee held a campus-wide pumpkin carving contest on South Lawn on Thursday 23 October 2025 from 3 to 5 p.m. as a fundraiser for Hope Harbor. Bill 4-25-F, tabled at its second reading on 9 September before passing at a third reading on 16 September, allocated $320: $250 for a pallet of 100 to 150 pumpkins from Hodge Family Farms, stored in the DSU basement until the day, and a $70 Boo Basket of a heated weighted blanket, candles and donated gifts as the prize. Entry was $10 for up to two carvers, paid straight to the Hope Harbor GoFundMe, and judging was to be done blind by faculty. The Alpha Chi chapter of Alpha Omicron Pi and the Chi Eta chapter of Phi Gamma Delta partnered on the event, covering carving materials and promotion.

28 October 2025Programme
#

Toppers Talk About It, a week built around a faculty-student panel

A bill passed on 28 October 2025 set up a Mental Health Awareness Week for 3 to 7 November, with a different event each day, among them a day of writing positive affirmations in chalk on the pavements and a day of writing positive messages on sticky notes for other students. The week ended with Flipping the Script, a two-part panel at which faculty from the WKU Counseling Center and the Student Accessibility Resource Center answered questions submitted by students, and then turned the format round so the faculty questioned a group of selected student leaders. The bill was written by Veronica Butler, who told the Herald she wanted the week to raise awareness, reduce stigma and create a supportive campus culture.

Herald, 28 Oct 2025 - "SGA focuses on mental health awareness"

4 November 2025
#

Senate amends food pantry bill from $123 to $750 after SNAP cuts

Senator Amelia Tucker introduced a bill to help the WKU Food Pantry in response to cuts to the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program, which the article reported has served more than 645,000 people in Kentucky. Tucker handed out 24 slips of paper, six marked with a star, to illustrate that one in four people aged 18 to 26 experience hunger during the year. The bill was introduced at $123 and successive amendments raised it to $750, including $500 drawn from emergency assistance funding.

#

Judicial Council heard a censure case against a committee chair and dismissed it 7-0

The Judicial Council heard a censure case against Senator Jade Ismail, chair of the Action and Opportunity Committee, on 12 November 2025. The complaints included one from members of Alpha Omicron Pi alleging she had treated them poorly when they volunteered at the 23 October pumpkin carving fundraiser she ran, an allegation that she had underspent the sums bills had allocated, which Chief Justice Blake Graham described as undercutting senators' ability to use legislative discretionary funds, and an allegation that she had emailed registered student organisations about a bill before it passed. The council voted 7-0 not to censure, treating most of it as poor communication, and Associate Justice Hannah Hash urged her to keep writing bills.

F1rstGen staff briefed the senate as it passed a bike rack resolution

#

Parking director tells SGA bus fuel costs have doubled

Director of Parking and Transportation Ginny Griffin told the final meeting of the 25th Senate that the monthly diesel bill for WKU buses, normally seven to eight thousand dollars, had reached 14 thousand the previous month. She said service was assured for the rest of the year but transit would have to find money elsewhere if oil prices held. Griffin also said permits would be ordered for the lots serving Regents and Normal halls when they open the following spring.

18 April 2026
#

Year-long mental health push closed with the Out of Darkness Walk

Robinson and Mental Health and Wellbeing Committee chair Veronica Butler spent the year pressing WKU on student mental health, citing data from social work professor Jay Gabbard that 44 percent of students reported depression symptoms and 10 percent had considered suicide in the past year. SGA co-ran Mental Health Week with the WKU Counseling Center and campus NAMI chapter, launched a Toppers Talk About It campaign, and forwarded student recommendations - including moving counseling services out of Potter Hall - through the Counseling Center Review Committee. The year closed with an Out of Darkness suicide-remembrance walk on April 18; incoming president Caden Lucas said the focus would continue.

#

Lucas voices concern over proposed tuition increase

WKU released a proposed 2026-27 budget that would raise tuition by $210 to $6,246 per semester within a $405.1 million operating plan. Lucas, identified as SGA president and soon-to-be student regent, said he was "generally concerned" by a proposal that would raise the cost of attending WKU. He said students deserved both a high-quality education and an affordable way to achieve it.

5 June 2026
#

Regents raise tuition over the student regent's lone no vote

The Board of Regents approved a $204 tuition increase, of 1.69 per cent, taking tuition to $6,240 a semester, the sixth consecutive yearly rise, alongside a 5 per cent housing increase and a 2 per cent meal plan increase within a $405 million operating budget. Every regent voted for it except outgoing SGA president Rush Robinson, who said the board had to do everything it could to preserve affordability. It was his last meeting as student regent before Caden Lucas took the seat. The board also suspended several majors, kinesiology and geological sciences among them, for reworking and mergers.

What this is

A year-by-year record of student government at Western Kentucky University, from the ratification of the Associated Students constitution in April 1966 to the present. Every entry names the source it came from.

Corrections

The names here start with the plaque in the SGA Chambers, and the plaque is known to be wrong in places. Where the archive disagrees with it, this site follows the archive and records the change on the corrections page. If you served, or you know that a year here is wrong, the project wants to hear from you through the Student Government Association office.

What it rests on

Everything here comes from what is publicly available online: the digitised Herald and SGA's own papers on TopSCHOLAR, the Talisman yearbooks, wkuherald.com, the university's news pages and the Wayback Machine. That is a fraction of what happened. Student government has met most weeks for sixty years, and the greater part of it was never written down, or was written down on paper that has never been scanned. A thin year here is a thin record, not a quiet one.
