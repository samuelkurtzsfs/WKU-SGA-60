Rush Robinson — the term

Robinson, a senior from Benton, Kentucky triple-majoring in psychology, philosophy and sociology and a member of Sigma Phi Epsilon and Omicron Delta Kappa, won the SGA presidency in the 13-15 April 2025 general election on a turnout of 966, running with Savanna Kurtz for vice president and Gabriel Jerdon for chief financial officer. He also succeeded Sam Kurtz as WKU's student regent; the Board of Regents administered his oath of office on 8 August 2025, the same meeting at which it named the new Gordon Ford College of Business building Amy and David Chandler Hall and elected Jan West chair, Doris Thomas vice chair and Melissa Dennison secretary for 2025-26.

Robinson ran on turning SGA away from national politics and toward direct student advocacy, saying the organization had sometimes echoed national political talking points and that he wanted it to act as "an advocate and resource for students" who could be pointed to the right contact in the administration. His cabinet - Kurtz as vice president, Jerdon as chief financial officer, Maggie Yelton as chief of staff, and Preston Jenkins, reappointed chief communications officer on 22 April 2025 and confirmed by the Senate - ran a legislative program heavy on small, direct-benefit measures: vouchers for CPR certification, dental cleanings, transcripts and identification cards, equipment to borrow such as umbrellas, calculators and hammocks, and a resolution urging accessible restroom stalls in Grise Hall. Jenkins also organized a student-painted mural for a column in the SGA chambers. In March 2026 SGA drew on its projects fund to host a baseball-game tailgate, a response to Athletics Director Todd Stewart's call for more student engagement. The Senate closed its final meeting on 14 April 2026 having passed 56 bills for the year, the most since 2018-19, with Jenkins also reporting that a new university Artificial Intelligence Committee was weighing bulk AI-product subscriptions for students.

Robinson's most sustained policy push was on student mental health, working with Mental Health and Wellbeing Committee chair Veronica Butler on a year-long program backed by data from social work professor Jay Gabbard, who told SGA that 44 percent of WKU students reported depression symptoms and 10 percent had considered suicide in the past year. SGA co-organized Mental Health Week with the WKU Counseling Center and the campus NAMI chapter, ran a Toppers Talk About It awareness campaign, and had student recommendations - including relocating counseling services from Potter Hall to the Downing Student Union - reviewed by the Counseling Center Review Committee before forwarding them to administrators, though Butler said it was unclear whether they would be enacted. The push closed with an Out of Darkness suicide-remembrance walk on 18 April 2026, and successor Caden Lucas said the focus would continue "in a sustained and meaningful way."

At the Senate's first meeting under his successor, Robinson opened by telling the mostly-new chamber they looked untainted by SGA's usual troubles, then handed president-elect Caden Lucas the red suit jacket passed president to president - the same jacket Sam Kurtz had given him the year before. His term as student regent outlasted his SGA presidency: at the Board of Regents' 5 June 2026 meeting, his last as regent, he cast the lone vote against a $204, 1.69 percent tuition increase, saying the board had "to do whatever we can to not raise the tuition" to preserve affordability. Afterward he planned to remain at WKU for an Ed.S. in school psychology, aiming for a career as a school psychologist in a school district.

2026-04-02

Senate learns the Gilbane housing partnership cleared the legislature

At its Thursday meeting SGA senators learned that the Kentucky Legislature had approved WKU's public-private partnership with developer Gilbane, which the Board of Regents had provisionally approved 11 December 2025 to design and build a new residence hall replacing Douglas Keen and Hugh Poland Halls. "WKU is now in the clear to start building the new residence halls," said Senator Jakob Barker, who with Senator Ethan Vietze also sponsored Resolution 7-26-S, backing a crosswalk at Alumni Avenue and Kentucky Street for residents of the 1355 Kentucky Street apartments who Barker's resolution said had to "step into oncoming traffic" to reach the Alumni Square parking garage. The same meeting passed legislation funding textbooks, exam supplies and lab goggles and authorizing a student-experience survey.

2026-04-14

25th Senate closes the year having passed 56 bills, the most since 2018-19

The 25th Senate's final meeting passed its last three bills of the year - funding an NPHC preview day, the Office of Sustainability's food pantry, and massage-therapy vouchers for finals week, the latter two amended to spend down the year's remaining $71.17 budget - bringing the year's total to 56 pieces of legislation. Chief Communications Officer Preston Jenkins reported that WKU's new Artificial Intelligence Committee, which he sat on, was in early talks about bulk subscriptions to AI products such as ChatGPT Pro for all students and a possible introductory AI course for freshmen. Guest speakers included a Parking and Transportation representative, who said the bus system's diesel bill had roughly doubled amid rising fuel prices. The Herald identified her as "Ginny Griffin"; the official senate minutes give her name as "Jenny Griffith" - unresolved which is correct.

2026-04-18

Year-long mental health push closed with the Out of Darkness Walk

Robinson and Mental Health and Wellbeing Committee chair Veronica Butler spent the year pressing WKU on student mental health, citing data from social work professor Jay Gabbard that 44 percent of students reported depression symptoms and 10 percent had considered suicide in the past year. SGA co-ran Mental Health Week with the WKU Counseling Center and campus NAMI chapter, launched a Toppers Talk About It campaign, and forwarded student recommendations - including moving counseling services out of Potter Hall - through the Counseling Center Review Committee. The year closed with an Out of Darkness suicide-remembrance walk on April 18; incoming president Caden Lucas said the focus would continue.
