Scott Whitehouse president and student regent, 1988-89

Office
President and student regent
Term recorded here
1988-89
Place in the line
the 22nd president and the 20th student regent
Standing of the record
Confirmed in the sources below.

ASG filed a formal reaction to the General Education Task Force proposal in 1988. Confirmed twice: the Herald of 7 April 1988 reported 'Whitehouse winner in presidential election', and coverage through the year names him as president.

Whitehouse won the March 1988 ASG presidential primary alongside Shannon Ragland, emerging from a field of seven candidates in a year when only two of 32 congress positions were contested.

He won the presidency on 7 April 1988, in an election the Herald's own editorial pages said had been marred by oversight and closed minds, and amid coverage of votes for a write-in candidate that the congress was told it could not ignore.

The disputed election shadowed his term into the fall: Bruce Cambron, who had demanded a new election in April, took his complaint to university president Thomas Meredith in October 1988, weeks after the congress banned future write-in campaigns. His year also produced ASG's formal written reaction to the General Education Task Force proposal.

Scott Whitehouse came through the Associated Student Government primaries in the spring of 1988. Dorren Klausnitzer reported that Whitehouse and Shannon Ragland were the winners, in an issue that also covered what a losing campaign cost candidates in money and in feeling.

By the middle of his year in office the Herald had settled on a running characterisation of him. Rebecca Fullen's feature called him the "Peddling President" who sold Associated Student Government to students, and the same issue reported that university president Thomas Meredith had come to speak to the organisation.

The following week the paper printed "Scott Whitehouse Selling Good Government" together with a John Chattin editorial cartoon drawing him as a salesman. The salesman image was the Herald's shorthand for a president whose main task was convincing students that student government mattered.

1988
#

A formal reaction to the General Education Task Force

ASG filed a written response to a proposed overhaul of general education requirements - one of the earliest surviving examples of student government intervening in curriculum rather than campus life.

13 September 1988Programme

Vote America ran through the first month of the term

Vote America is in the minutes of 13, 20 and 27 September 1988, in a presidential election autumn. The 20 September meeting set it beside a lecture series, International Day, College Awareness Day and the Student Alumni Association; the 27 September meeting set it beside committee service and Panhellenic activities. The drive ran before the mock presidential election ASG staged with the College Republicans in October.

ASG meeting minutes, 13 September 1988

15 September 1988
#

Write-in campaigns banned

ASG approved a ban on future write-in campaigns, five months after write-in votes had thrown its own presidential election into dispute. An editorial the next issue said the block showed ASG was not listening.

A lecture series, College Awareness Day and the Student Alumni Association

The minutes of 20 September 1988 record a lecture series, College Awareness Day and the Student Alumni Association among the congress's business, together with a meeting with the Council for Higher Education, a visit from the University of Louisville student government, International Day and constitutional amendments. The minutes name what was planned rather than what happened.

ASG meeting minutes, 20 September 1988

The election appeal continues into the fall

Rebecca Fullen reported that Bruce Cambron was still appealing the spring presidential election, five months after the result. The same issue announced the freshman election for the following Tuesday.

Turnout in the ASG freshman election was reported as "pretty good." The same issue said the organization was working to make the importance of voting register with students.

Bruce Cambron, who had called for a new election in April, talked to university president Thomas Meredith about the ASG election. The spring's disputed result was still alive six months on.

International Day ran through the autumn of 1988 as it had in 1986: the minutes of 20 September, 11 October and 18 October all record it. The 11 October meeting took it alongside homecoming, the student alumni association and the member of the week awards.

#

Congress changes its committees

Rebecca Fullen reported that the Associated Student Government had made committee changes. The same issue reported Dan Knowles winning the freshman election. The surviving index preserves only the headlines, so which committees changed is not recorded here.

Herald 64:15, 13 Oct 1988, "Associated Student Government Makes Committee Changes"

20 October 1988Service
#

ASG sets up a Wednesday afternoon hotline

Student government set up a hotline for complaints and suggestions about the organisation. From Wednesday 26 October the ASG secretary was to answer the office line, 745-4354, from 1 to 4 p.m. every Wednesday, referring anything she could not answer to an executive officer. Public relations vice president Amos Gott said students could call at any time during the week but did not, and that with a set time people were more apt to call.

College Heights Herald 64:17, 20 Oct 1988 PDF

20 October 1988
#

A congress seat goes to a run-off

Rebecca Fullen reported that Lori Easton had won an Associated Student Government run-off race.

Herald 64:17, 20 Oct 1988, "Lori Easton Wins Associated Student Government Run Off Race"

25 October 1988Programme
#

Mock presidential election with the College Republicans

ASG co-sponsored a mock presidential election with the College Republicans on Tuesday 25 October 1988, from 10:30 a.m. to 3 p.m. in the university center, with student identification cards required to vote. Public relations vice president Amos Gott said before it that the aim was to see how Western felt as a community compared with Bowling Green and with the national response to the candidates. The Herald of 27 October reported that George Bush won the mock race.

ASG Awareness Week

Bids taken for the shuttle shelters ASG had asked for

Having asked in Resolutions 88-2-F and 88-3-F of 1 November 1988 for shelters and benches at the Big Red shuttle stops, the congress followed the job through the spring. 14 February 1989

A student athletic promotions committee proposed

Resolution 89-2-S, dated 14 February 1989, proposed creating a student athletic promotions committee to push attendance at athletic events up. The minutes of the following week, 21 February, record a basketball game among the congress's business.

16 February 1989
#

ASG recommended an extra study day before exams

Associated Student Government recommended adding an extra study day before exams to the university calendar. Diane Tsimekles reported the recommendation in the Herald's 16 February issue. The proposal followed January efforts to find a way to print teacher evaluations.

21 February 1989Film
#

A Sunday matinee at the campus theatre

Resolution 89-4-S, dated 21 February 1989, asked for a Sunday matinee to be added at the campus theatre. Two more resolutions carried the same date, 89-5-S asking that the library stay open until 10 p.m. on Fridays and Saturdays, and 89-6-S asking that men's intramural basketball and volleyball be played at weekends. The minutes of 21 March 1989 record a Hell No, We Won't Go weekend, and those of 28 March 1989 a Stay and Play Weekend, alongside the ASG banquet and the shuttle shelter construction. More coed dorms and a later spring break

The Herald of 23 March 1989 reported that Associated Student Government wanted more coed dormitories and a later spring break. The two requests are in the legislation as Resolution 89-7-S of 20 March, moving spring break to the third week of March, and Resolution 89-8-S of 21 March, converting a residence hall without air conditioning to coed housing.

The minutes of 28 March 1989 record the installation of Thomas Meredith as Western's president among the congress's business, alongside the shuttle shelter construction, the ASG banquet and the Stay and Play Weekend. Meredith addressed an ASG meeting in January, and returned repeatedly over the following five years.

Amos Gott edges Lynn Groemling

Amos Gott narrowly defeated Lynn Groemling in the ASG presidential race. The campaign season had drawn complaints from candidates about the brief primary and an editorial warning that name recognition should not decide the race.

What this is

A year-by-year record of student government at Western Kentucky University, from the ratification of the Associated Students constitution in April 1966 to the present. Every entry names the source it came from.

Corrections

The names here start with the plaque in the SGA Chambers, and the plaque is known to be wrong in places. Where the archive disagrees with it, this site follows the archive and records the change on the corrections page. If you served, or you know that a year here is wrong, the project wants to hear from you: people who held an office can be given the keys to their own year on the contribute page.

What it rests on

Everything here comes from what is publicly available online: the digitised Herald and SGA's own papers on TopSCHOLAR, the Talisman yearbooks, wkuherald.com, the university's news pages and the Wayback Machine. That is a fraction of what happened. Student government has met most weeks for sixty years, and the greater part of it was never written down, or was written down on paper that has never been scanned. A thin year here is a thin record, not a quiet one.

Reuse

Text on this site is the work of the project. Photographs, documents and legislation are reproduced from Western Kentucky University's own open archives and from the College Heights Herald and the Talisman; each item names the collection, issue and page it came from and links to the original.
