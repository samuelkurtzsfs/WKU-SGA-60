Amanda Coates president and student regent, 1999-00

Office
President and student regent
Term recorded here
1999-00
Place in the line
the 33rd president and the 31st student regent
On the plaque
1999-2000
Standing of the record
Moved here from the year the plaque gives. The correction and its evidence are set out below.

The plaque reads "Amanda Lich". No source from her SGA year uses that surname; the Herald has her winning the presidency in a recount on 15 April 1999, covers her as president through the autumn, and ran her year-end reflection on 25 April 2000, all as Amanda Coates. The two names are now confirmed as one person, from two sittings of the Kentucky Council on Postsecondary Education: its minutes of 13 November 2000 introduce the incoming holder of the council's statewide student seat as Amanda Coates, "a graduate of Western Kentucky University", and its minutes of 30 July 2001, recording Governor Patton's appointment of her successor, name the outgoing member "Amanda Coates Lich." The first carries the Coates of Western into the seat and the second carries the surname Lich out of it. This site does not otherwise report what became of her after her SGA service, in keeping with its rule against publishing a living person's post-office life absent a source tying it directly to that service or a notable public career.

Contemporaneous records give her name as Amanda J. Coates. The SGA's own 2001 list of presidents records Amanda J. Coates of Brownsville as the 33rd president, serving 1999-2000, and marks her as having also served as student regent. The organization's website during and just after her term consistently refers to "President Amanda Coates."

She won the presidency in a contested April 1999 race against Will Jones, in results so close the Coates-Martin ticket demanded a recount: the initial 616-611 tally over Jones and running mate Kara Yeckering narrowed to 614-611 on the recount. A write-in ticket, Dwight Campbell and Kerri Richardson, took 145 votes. The same election saw a bitter down-ballot fight for vice president of finance, where Joe Matheis beat Ryan Morrison 700-688 only after SGA's judicial council reviewed - and let stand - his victory over a campaign-flier violation, and a Herald letter to the editor questioned SGA's handling of an investigation into racially charged fliers that had targeted Jones, the first Black SGA presidential candidate in years. The Herald's pre-election issue had separately reported SGA questioning an election-eve pizza party Jones held, and a debate over dropping the rule requiring a year of SGA service before running for office.

Her signature project was publishing faculty evaluations. From February 2000 she led meetings with the university president, the Faculty Senate chair and the senate's executive committee, and in April she co-authored Resolution 00-2-S with Adam Howard asking the Faculty Senate to approve a mandatory published evaluation beginning that fall.

At the end of her term the SGA website opened with the words "What SGA has done for you," showcasing three things: the faculty evaluations project, the fight over the Vice President of Student Affairs position and the Provide-A-Ride service. It is as close to a self-written record of her administration's priorities as survives.

As her term closed, the Herald ran an exit interview, "Amanda Coates Reflects on Year as President," in its April 25, 2000 issue. The election to succeed her, held in late March 2000, was contested between Cassie Martin and Duan Wright; Martin won, and by the following issue the Herald was asking the incoming president to "stand up on tough issues" while confirming that the faculty evaluations Coates had championed would be conducted and published starting that fall.

The Herald introduced the incoming 1999-2000 president on April 22, 1999 under the headline 'New Student Government Association Leader Workaholic.' The paper's index records the new leader's name as Amanda Coates, and she took office in the aftermath of a spring election that SGA's Judicial Council had overturned two days earlier.

Her congress spent the fall of 1999 building institutions: it created an Information Technology Director and website guidelines, established a senior awards banquet and hall of distinguished seniors, launched the toll-free Topline activity line, and publicly opposed an administrative move in Student Affairs while asking that Vice President Jerry Wilder be retained.

Her public service continued past her SGA year at the state level. Kentucky's Council on Postsecondary Education carries a statewide student member, and the council's minutes of 13 November 2000 record a district judge swearing her in, introducing her as Amanda Coates, a graduate of Western Kentucky University then studying public policy at the University of Kentucky. The council's minutes of 30 July 2001 record Governor Paul Patton appointing Christopher J. Pace to replace the outgoing student member, named there as "Amanda Coates Lich." Those two records together are what tie the Coates who led WKU's SGA in 1999-2000 to the surname the SGA Chambers plaque uses for the same year.

The year, in order35

8 April 1999
#

Jones and Coates square off amid a pizza-party dispute

The Herald's pre-election issue covered the presidential race between Will Jones and Amanda Coates, alongside a report that SGA was questioning an election-eve pizza party Jones had held, a debate over eliminating the rule requiring a year of SGA experience to run for office, and a heated treasurer's race between Ryan Morrison and Joe Matheis.

Herald 74:49, 8 Apr 1999

15 April 1999
#

A turbulent election puts Amanda Coates in office

The Herald's coverage of the spring SGA election was headlined "Turbulent Elections Complete"; the same issue carried an opinion piece, "Student Government Association Offers No Leadership," challenging the incoming administration to prove itself.

Herald 74:51, 15 Apr 1999Read it on this site

15 April 1999
#

Matheis survives a flier scandal to win vice president of finance

Joe Matheis defeated Ryan Morrison 700-688 for vice president of finance in the campaign's most contentious race. Two hours after the initial result, SGA's judicial council placed the race under review after fliers Matheis had distributed turned up left on classroom desks and in computer labs overnight, violating election code; the council warned Matheis could lose the seat if he had not made a good-faith effort to collect them. The council let the result stand the next day, and Morrison filed a complaint with the judicial council disputing that decision, saying fliers were still on desks in Garrett and the fine arts center.

Herald 74:51, 15 Apr 1999Read it on this site

15 April 1999
#

A letter to the editor questions an SGA probe of racially charged campaign fliers

A Herald letter to the editor reported that fliers reading "Vote for Will Jones in Racism," written in black ink, had circulated in dorms during the campaign, and that SGA opened an investigation into whether they were racially motivated. The letter's author named Jones - described as the first Black candidate for SGA president in years - as a suspect despite his denial of any involvement, and questioned whether the investigation was following proper procedure rather than targeting him by assumption. No source found reports how, or whether, the investigation was resolved.

Herald 74:51, 15 Apr 1999Read it on this site

14 September 1999Programme
#

Professional development workshops for upperclassmen

Bill 99-2-F committed SGA to run a series of free professional development workshops with the Career Services Center and Housing and Residence Life across both semesters of 1999-2000. They were aimed at sophomores, juniors and seniors preparing to move from study into work.

TopSCHOLAR - Bill 99-2-F, Professional Development Workshops

15 September 1999Programme
#

An autumn campus clean-up on 18 October 1999

Bill 99-3-F established a campus-wide clean-up at 3 p.m. on 18 October 1999, the first held in an autumn term. Other campus organisations could take part and count the time as community service, and the date was chosen because alumni would be on campus all that week.

TopSCHOLAR - Bill 99-3-F, Campus Clean Up

15 September 1999
#

Bill 99-10-F sought to create an SGA Information Technology Director

SGA took up Bill 99-10-F, to create the SGA position of Information Technology Director. The SGA records date the bill 15 September 1999 through 5 October 1999 and do not say whether it passed, so the archive does not show that the post was in fact filled. The same month produced a bill amending the constitution to include website guidelines.

Meet Your Dean Reception, 11 November 1999

SGA gives away its recycling trailer

#

SGA endorsed ending Flip Friday

A fall resolution endorsed the schedule proposed by the Class Schedule Review Committee to do away with Flip Friday. It was the association's formal word on one of the era's odder scheduling experiments.

SGA Records: Resolution 99-F, 11 Nov 1999

18 November 1999
#

Keep our voice: a rally at Wetherby

About fifty students gathered outside the Wetherby Administration Building to urge the university to retain both the Vice President of Student Affairs and the Dean of Student Life positions, chanting "It's not your choice. Keep our voice." The SGA's own account said president Ransdell appreciated how the students conducted themselves and would keep an open mind.

WKU SGA site, V.P. of Student Affairs Update (archived 2000)

30 November 1999
#

VarsityBooks.com came to the SGA website

Bill 99-17-F had SGA join VarsityBooks.com and post a link from its website, and a same-day bill extended library hours during finals week. The dot-com partnership marked the first e-commerce deal in the digitised SGA record.

SGA Records: Bill 99-17-F, 30 Nov 1999

Website guidelines written into the constitution

A bill dated 6 December 1999 amended the SGA constitution to include guidelines for the association's website. It is filed in SGA's records without a number, as Bill 99- -F.

TopSCHOLAR - Bill 99- -F, Web Page Outline

2000
#

Provide-A-Ride in full operation

During this year SGA ran Provide-A-Ride as a free service giving safe and sober rides anywhere in the Bowling Green and campus area on Thursday and Friday nights from 11 p.m. to 2 a.m. A fifteen-passenger blue van answered calls within about fifteen minutes, and all a rider needed was a valid Western ID.

WKU SGA site, Provide-A-Ride (archived 2000)

SGA offers a shelter for shuttle riders

SGA moved in February 2000 to give Big Red Shuttle riders a sheltered stop. The project was reported through the year and cost $7,000 by the time it was built.

Herald 75:32, 10 Feb 2000 - Hall, "Student Government Association Offers Shelter for Shuttle Riders"

22 February 2000
#

The faculty evaluations campaign opens

President Amanda Coates opened a campaign to publish faculty evaluations as a tool for students choosing professors, starting with a February 22 meeting with the university president and the Faculty Senate chair. The SGA published a step-by-step timeline running through a March 23 presentation to the full Faculty Senate, saying it did not want to win the tool at the expense of unnecessarily upsetting faculty.

WKU SGA site, Faculty Evaluations (archived 2000)

Resolution 00-2-S: publish the evaluations

Resolution 00-2-S, authored by Amanda J. Coates and Adam Howard and sponsored by the Legislative Research Committee, asked the Faculty Senate to approve a mandatory SGA-sponsored faculty evaluation to be published beginning in fall 2000. What this is

A year-by-year record of student government at Western Kentucky University, from the ratification of the Associated Students constitution in April 1966 to the present. Every entry names the source it came from.

Corrections

The names here start with the plaque in the SGA Chambers, and the plaque is known to be wrong in places. Where the archive disagrees with it, this site follows the archive and records the change on the corrections page. If you served, or you know that a year here is wrong, the project wants to hear from you: people who held an office can be given the keys to their own year on the contribute page.

What it rests on

Everything here comes from what is publicly available online: the digitised Herald and SGA's own papers on TopSCHOLAR, the Talisman yearbooks, wkuherald.com, the university's news pages and the Wayback Machine. That is a fraction of what happened. Student government has met most weeks for sixty years, and the greater part of it was never written down, or was written down on paper that has never been scanned. A thin year here is a thin record, not a quiet one.

Reuse

Text on this site is the work of the project. Photographs, documents and legislation are reproduced from Western Kentucky University's own open archives and from the College Heights Herald and the Talisman; each item names the collection, issue and page it came from and links to the original.
