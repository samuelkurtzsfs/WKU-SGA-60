Sam Kurtz — the term

Sam Kurtz, then the outgoing administrative vice president, was elected student body president in results announced early on April 19, 2023 in the Downing Student Union senate chambers, after voting opened on TopNet on April 17. His KFL ticket, with Annalise Finch as executive vice president and Salvador León as administrative vice president, ran unopposed, and Chief Justice Justin Goins read the results. In a statement, Kurtz said the ticket would "listen to students' concerns, represent their voices, lead with integrity, and promote transparency."

At the 23rd Senate's first fall meeting on Aug. 29, Kurtz nominated Ashley Ranger, Anna Kate Hardin, Ellen Henderson, Morgan Gammons and Garrison Reed to the Judicial Council from what he called a record number of applicants, and named seven committee heads, including Rush Robinson for Campus Improvements and Sustainability and Karley Solorzano for Diversity, Equity and Inclusion. The Senate's first bill of the year, passed unanimously that September, funded the SGA Pink Walk breast-cancer event. That November the senate backed moving DSU fire drills from 5 a.m. to 2 a.m. and funded a Boys and Girls Club toy drive. Speaker of the Senate Sydney Denney wrote or co-wrote several of the year's bills, including a resolution capping student parking fees at $50, a $700 fund to help students pay parking fines, and voucher funding for the Dental Hygiene Center and ID Center. In February 2024 Kurtz and Chief of Staff Donté Reed rolled out a 450-voucher Uber giveaway aimed at student safety and outreach, followed in March by a need-based scholarship awarding Uber vouchers and a Community Builder Excellence Scholarship for incoming Bowling Green-area freshmen; that month the senate also planned a Frankfort trip for the Rally for Higher Education.

The year's fight was internal. In February 2024 the Judicial Council voted 6-0 to censure Administrative Vice President Salvador León after a hearing brought by Denney and the executive cabinet, which alleged León had spent SGA funds - including a $425.25 Preston Center deposit for Neurodiversity Acceptance Week - before the Senate passed the funding bill, in violation of the constitution's clause 2.5.3 requiring the administrative vice president to expend funds only as the Senate authorizes. Chief Justice Isaac King said the cabinet, excluding León, had asked for the censure after León spent the funds despite Kurtz's condition that he wait for Bill 22-23-F to pass, and after he pushed PR Director Preston Romanov to promote the week on SGA social media ahead of the vote, effectively forcing the Senate's hand. León countered that text messages showed Kurtz had signed off on the spending in advance; the council found the spending improper but recommended no discipline beyond the censure itself.

In January 2024 Chief of Staff Lindsey Kelly and enrollment director Donte Reed both resigned, with Reed moving up to chief of staff. That April, Kurtz ran for a second term with Reed as his executive vice president candidate and junior senator Ethan Taylor for administrative vice president; the ticket again ran unopposed on a ballot of more than 60 legislative candidates, and the three were re-elected as the 24th Senate's 34 members were announced the night of April 16-17, 2024. Before that, SGA partnered with WKU Red Wave for a final Red Wave tailgate on April 9, with a student-DJ contest winner earning a $500 scholarship. The 23rd Senate held its last meeting on April 16 with no legislation on the agenda, and Kurtz thanked senators in his final report for what the group had accomplished that year.

What happened, in order

2023-08-30

23rd Senate seats Judicial Council and committee heads

At its first meeting of the fall semester, SGA approved President Sam Kurtz's nominations of Ashley Ranger, Anna Kate Hardin, Ellen Henderson, Morgan Gammons and Garrison Reed to the 2023-2024 Judicial Council after what Kurtz called a record number of applicants. Kurtz also announced seven committee heads, including Rush Robinson for Campus Improvements and Sustainability and Karley Solorzano for Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion. The new appointees took the oath of office at the meeting.

Herald, 30 Aug 2023 ↗
2023-09-20

First bill of the year funds the Pink Walk

The 23rd Senate passed its first piece of legislation, Bill 1-23-F, at its fifth meeting. The bill allocated $500 from SGA's scholarship budget for the SGA Pink Walk, an October breast cancer awareness event for students and the Bowling Green community. It passed unanimously with 28 votes.

Herald, 20 Sep 2023 ↗
2023-09-27

Fall election winners sworn in

SGA announced its fall election results on Sept. 20 over an Instagram livestream, electing Savanna Kurtz, Malick Ibrahim and Chloe Ralston as freshman senators, ShyAntye'e Williams as graduate senator, Sim Guan Zhou as international senator and Livi Ray as Gatton Academy senator. Five of the six were sworn in at the following Tuesday meeting. Sophomore senator Rush Robinson received the year's first Senator of the Month award.

Herald, 27 Sep 2023 ↗
2023-11-14

Senate backs a later fire-drill time, funds a toy drive

At the 23rd Senate's 11th meeting, senators unanimously passed Resolution 6-23-F, backing a change to DSU fire-drill timing from 5 a.m. to 2 a.m. after the Mental Health and Wellness Committee argued the earlier hour caused sleep problems and anxiety for students on early schedules or with accommodations. They also unanimously passed Bill 12-23-F, allocating $200 from the legislative discretionary fund for a Boys and Girls Club of Bowling Green toy drive, and swore in newly elected ISEC senator Ashlee Potter.

Herald, 15 Nov 2023 ↗
2023-11-28

The 23rd Senate corrects its own governing documents

Three bills from the Legislative Operations Committee: fix references to a 'University Senate' that had since split into separate Faculty and Staff senates, reconcile bylaws language about expelling senators with a constitution that only provides for impeachment, and update committee names that had drifted.

Herald, Nov 2023 ↗
2024-01-23

Spring committee heads sworn in

At the spring semester's first meeting, Kurtz appointed Madison Parker Payne as Outreach Committee chair and Sophia Bryant as Mental Health and Well-Being Committee chair; both were approved without contested debate. Executive Vice President Annalise Finch appointed Jaden Marshall to chair the Graduate Advisory Council campus committee and Chloe Ralston to chair the Colonnade General Education committee. Kurtz urged senators to spend down the senate's still-large discretionary budget before the semester's end.

Herald, 31 Jan 2024 ↗
2024-01-24

Midyear resignations reshuffle the executive cabinet

President Sam Kurtz announced the resignations of Chief of Staff Lindsey Kelly and of Donte Reed as director of enrollment and student experience. The senate approved Kurtz's nominations of Reed as the new chief of staff and Sarah Vincent as director of enrollment and student activities. Kurtz also filled vacant transfer/non-traditional, sophomore and graduate senate seats with Sawyer Edmonds, Gabe Jerdon and Elizabeth Gannon.

Herald, 24 Jan 2024 ↗
2024-02-06

Cabinet requests censure hearing against León

Kurtz told the senate the Executive Cabinet had filed a formal report seeking a Judicial Council censure hearing against Administrative Vice President Salvador León. Chief Justice Isaac King said the cabinet's complaint was that León spent project funds before President Kurtz or EVP Finch consented, that he had done so after Kurtz conditioned permission on Bill 22-23-F first passing, and that he pushed PR Director Preston Romanov to promote Neurodiversity Week on SGA social media before the funding bill passed, effectively forcing the Senate's hand. The Executive Cabinet, excluding León, asked for a censure; the hearing was set for Feb. 7.

Herald, 6 Feb 2024 ↗
2024-02-09

Judicial council censures Salvador León

The SGA judicial council voted 6-0 to censure Administrative Vice President Salvador León after a Feb. 7 hearing on allegations that he spent funds, including a $425.25 Preston Center deposit for Neurodiversity Acceptance Week, before the senate passed the funding bill. León argued text messages showed President Sam Kurtz had accepted the spending, while Kurtz said he asked León multiple times not to spend before the bill passed. The council recommended no further disciplinary action beyond the censure.

Herald, 9 Feb 2024 ↗
2024-02-15

Uber voucher program and parking fee measures

President Sam Kurtz and Chief of Staff Donte Reed announced a program of 450 Uber vouchers worth $10 each, distributed through social media giveaways, meeting attendance and SGA events. The senate also passed Resolution 18-23-S supporting a cap on student parking fees at $50 and Bill 19-23-S allocating $700 to help financially vulnerable students pay parking fines, along with $1,045 in Dental Hygiene Center and ID Center vouchers.

Herald, 15 Feb 2024 ↗
2024-03-05

Rally trip to Frankfort, new voucher and cleanup plans

Kurtz previewed a senate trip to Frankfort for the Rally for Higher Education, where SGA planned to meet state Rep. Kevin Jackson and other Kentucky campus governments, and urged senators to seek re-election to preserve institutional knowledge. Chief of Staff Donté Reed introduced a need-based scholarship awarding four Uber vouchers and the Community Builder Excellence Scholarship for incoming Bowling Green-area freshmen. Campus Improvements and Sustainability chair Rush Robinson moved Earth Day from April 22 to April 19 and set a tentative Bowling Green cleanup for March 28.

Herald, 6 Mar 2024 ↗
2024-03-13

Funding for DEI Week and Spring Fling

At its 20th meeting the senate funded SGA Diversity, Equity and Inclusion Week, four days of themed tabling from March 25 to 28 covering mental health, campus demographics, sexual health and deaf awareness. A $450 bill for the SGA Spring Fling, whose proceeds went to the Kids Cancer Alliance, passed 28-2 after debate among senators over the amount being spent. A $280 bill funded a Hope Harbor Denim Day event for Sexual Assault Prevention Month.

Herald, 13 Mar 2024 ↗
2024-04-09

SGA closed the year with a Red Wave tailgate

SGA partnered with WKU Red Wave for the school year's last tailgate before a baseball game against Louisville, featuring winners of a "Battle of the DJs" social-media poll; sophomore Wilson Patterson won the $500 top scholarship prize as "DJ WLZN." Kurtz called the crowd to "savor one last tailgate of the 2023-2024 school year," with SGA grilling food for attendees.

Herald, 8 Apr 2024 ↗
2024-04-16

Spring general election opened with over 60 candidates

Voting for the spring 2024 general election opened on TopNet with more than 60 students on the ballot for legislative seats. Kurtz, Chief of Staff Donté Reed and junior senator Ethan Taylor ran unopposed for student body president, executive vice president and administrative vice president respectively; the legislative council ballot included three senior, three junior and three sophomore senator seats, six college seats, an ISEC senator, a first-generation senator, a transfer/non-traditional senator and 14 senator-at-large seats.

Herald, 15 Apr 2024 ↗
2024-04-17

23rd Senate holds its final meeting

The 23rd Senate met for the last time with no legislation on the agenda, spending the meeting on senators' final remarks. Kurtz thanked the senate and said in his final report, "We've done a lot of great things this year." Executive Vice President Annie Finch and Administrative Vice President Salvador León also gave closing reports before the 24th Senate's first meeting on April 23.

Herald, 17 Apr 2024 ↗
2024-04-17

Kurtz re-elected unopposed to a second term

Running with Chief of Staff Donté Reed for executive vice president and junior senator Ethan Taylor for administrative vice president, Kurtz was re-elected without opposition as the 24th Senate's 34 members were announced the same night. More than 60 students had appeared on the ballot for the spring 2024 general election.

Herald, 17 Apr 2024 ↗
