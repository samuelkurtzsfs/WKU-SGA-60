James Hargrove president and student regent, 1979-80

Office
President and student regent

Place in the line
the 14th president and the 12th student regent
Standing of the record
Moved here from the year the plaque gives. The correction and its evidence are set out below.

The plaque reads "James Hargroave". The Herald called him Jamie Hargrove throughout 1979-80, and SGA's own roster of former presidents, archived in September 2001, lists "James Earl Hargrove, Milton" and stars him as a student regent. Two independent sources against the plaque's spelling, so this site follows them. Surname spelling on the plaque is HARGROAVE. The Herald, in a 30 August 1979 profile calling him ASG Head at the start of the year, spells the name 'Jamie Hargrove.' Flagging the discrepancy rather than silently correcting the plaque spelling, per project rule.

Hargrove, a Milton junior, won the ASG presidency in the 17 April 1979 general election, defeating David Young 1,087-535 on a turnout of 1,725 votes. The same day's balloting elected the rest of his executive slate: Steve Fuller administrative vice president, Dean Bates activities vice president, Betty Thompson secretary and Terri Craig treasurer. All five were sworn in at the outgoing Congress's 24 April meeting, where Congress waived its rules so that Hargrove's first act as president, a resolution asking that Helm-Cravens Library stay open longer, could pass unanimously the same night. By August 30, 1979, at the start of the school year, the Herald profiled him - spelled 'Jamie Hargrove' in the byline - under the headline that he believed students were interested.

Beyond the executive roster now on record, the archive does not yet show in detail what Hargrove's administration passed or fought over during 1979-80, beyond the record's general sweep of the year. Further Herald issues from the fall and spring semester have not yet been checked.

24 senators this year.

A constitutional amendment this year replaced the 24 representatives-at-large with 24 seats split three ways: eight on-campus, eight off-campus and eight general-representative. The Herald covered the change as it moved through Congress that September (see the 13 and 20 September 1979 events, above); the 1980 Talisman's year-end account, p. 274, is the only source found naming the new three-way split itself. No roster of who held the new seats has been located.

The year, in order37

12 April 1979
#

830 students vote in the ASG primary; the Herald runs a defence of not voting

The Associated Student Government's spring primary drew 830 voters and put the open-house policy on the ballot. The same issue carried a piece justifying student apathy in ASG voting - the paper had moved from scolding non-voters to explaining them. This primary elected the officers who took office for 1979-80; it was previously misfiled under 1978-79.

Herald 54:54, 12 Apr 1979 PDF

24 April 1979
#

New ASG officers sworn in; Hargrove's first resolution passed

At its 24 April 1979 meeting, ASG's outgoing 1978-79 Congress passed four housing and parking resolutions and then swore in the newly elected 1979-80 officers: president Jamie Hargrove, administrative vice president Steve Fuller, activities vice president Dean Bates, secretary Betty Thompson and treasurer Terri Craig. Congress then voted to waive its rules and passed unanimously the new administration's first resolution, written by Hargrove, asking that Helm-Cravens Library stay open longer.

ASG head Jamie Hargrove profiled at year's start

The Herald profiled the Associated Student Government head under the headline that he believed students were interested, in the same issue that introduced the new president's wife, Tommie Zacharias, to campus.

30 August 1979
#

Hargrove opens the year saying students are interested

Associated Student Government head Jamie Hargrove told the Herald he believed students were interested in their government as the year opened. A week later ASG heard its 1979-80 budget and discussed changing its delegate policy.

Herald 55:2, 30 Aug 1979 PDF

13 September 1979
#

ASG weighs expanding hall representation

The Associated Student Government considered expanding residence hall representation in its congress, in the same issue reporting the Board of Regents set to consider a two-year budget request.

Herald 55:5, 13 Sep 1979 PDF

20 September 1979
#

Seats bill passes after revision under pressure

A bill on Associated Student Government seats passed after being revised under pressure, expanding on earlier talk of broader residence hall representation. The Herald called the representation bill a small step in the right direction.

Herald 55:7, 20 Sep 1979 PDF

25 September 1979
#

ASG asked the state to seat a student on the higher education council

Resolution 79-6, dated 25 September 1979, called for a student representative to be included on the Kentucky Council on Higher Education. It is the earliest dated piece of legislation held for the 1979-80 session in SGA's own archive, and it set the tone of a year in which the organisation, stripped of its entertainment budget, turned to academic and state-level questions.

SGA Resolution 79-6, Student on Council of Higher Education

4 October 1979Programme
#

ASG announced it would run student opinion polls

The Herald of 4 October 1979 carried a notice that Associated Student Government opinion polls were planned. This is the announcement, not a report of any poll: it proves what was set up, not what came back. The polls became the signature project of Jamie Hargrove's term, run by a committee under Kevin Kinne, and by the following September the Herald was reporting the first results.

College Heights Herald 55:11, 4 October 1979 PDF

18 October 1979
#

ASG proposed cutting special benefits for athletes

Ken Morris reported that an ASG proposal would reduce special benefits for athletes. The same issue carried the report by Morris and Tim Fish that favouritism had been alleged in the plans for the Homecoming dance, and news that Western had been denied construction funds.

College Heights Herald 55:15, 18 October 1979 PDF

25 October 1979
#

The Homecoming edition ran a history of student government

Tom Beshear wrote a feature for the Homecoming edition under the line that student government had grown as the university expanded. It ran in an issue given over to campus history, alongside pieces on Hugh Poland, on how the Second World War left Western with few men, on the naming of the Herald and the Talisman, and on the old Fort Bridge.

College Heights Herald, Homecoming edition, 25 October 1979 PDF

November 1979
#

ASG rejects move to close Diddle Hall

The Associated Student Government rejected a move to close Diddle Hall dormitory. The vote came amid a fall of housing debates that later included a push to make Keen Hall a coed dorm.

Herald 55:19, 1 Nov 1979 PDF

November 1979
#

A $250 grant to a fraternity drew charges of favouritism

Low attendance at the Saturday night Homecoming dance left the sponsoring fraternity, Sigma Chi, out of pocket, and the University Center Board gave the group $250. The Talisman reported that the payment prompted allegations of favouritism because ASG president Jamie Hargrove sat on the centre board and was a former Sigma Chi president. The Herald had already reported on 18 October that favouritism was alleged in the plans for the dance. The yearbook records the allegation only: it names no complainant, reports no finding, and no resolution of the matter appears in the digitised index.

$250 from the University Center Board

A resignation was clarified in the Herald

Lisa Hunn wrote to the Herald under the heading that a resignation from the Associated Student Government had been clarified. The digitised index records the letter and its subject but names nobody, so who resigned and why is not established here.

College Heights Herald 55:20, 6 November 1979 PDF

13 November 1979
#

Resolution 79-10 seeks staff for scholarship administration

The Associated Student Government passed Resolution 79-10, urging the university to assign staff to administer academic scholarships.

SGA Resolution 79-10, Academic Scholarship Expansion

13 November 1979
#

A library book drop for Downing University Center

Resolution 79-12, dated 13 November 1979, asked for a library book drop to be installed in the Downing University Center so students would not have to walk to the library to return books. It was one of four resolutions filed that night, with the scholarship staffing resolution 79-10 and the part-time activity fee resolution 79-11. ASG asked again two years later, in Resolution 81-4 of September 1981.

SGA Resolution 79-12, Book Drop in Downing University Center

13 November 1979
#

ASG asked for an ice skating rink

Resolution 79-13, dated 13 November 1979, asked the university to build a skating rink. SGA's archive holds the document and describes it in a single line, and the Herald of 7 February 1980 carried an item on an ice skating rink. Nothing in the record says the rink was ever built.

SGA Resolution 79-13, Ice Skating Rink

15 November 1979
#

ASG asked that Keen Hall be made a coed dorm

Karen Owen reported that ASG wanted Keen Hall turned into a coed dormitory. The request is Resolution 79-9, dated 30 October 1979 in SGA's own archive under the title Keen Hall Conversion. It came in the same autumn in which the congress refused to back closing Diddle Hall, and coed housing did not reach the Board of Regents until February 1984.

College Heights Herald 55:23, 15 November 1979 PDF

29 November 1979
#

ASG proposes an activity fee for part-time students

ASG proposed a part-time student activity fee. The proposal came as the Board of Regents weighed budget requests for the coming biennium.

Herald 55:25, 29 Nov 1979 PDF

1980Programme
#

ASG replaces concerts with opinion polls and a radio phone-in

Stripped of entertainment, ASG spent the year on student opinion polls, a phone-in programme on the campus station WKYU where President Jamie Hargrove answered questions, and expanded publicity for its own elections. Hargrove said the transfer of activities to the University Center Board had cost ASG between $60,000 and $70,000 of budget but no real power. Poll committee chairman Kevin Kinne said the university administration was showing a lot of support for the polls.

$60,000 to $70,000 of activities budget lost to the Center Board

Talisman 1980, "Concerting student opinion"

1980
#

Hargrove went to the White House to meet President Carter

ASG president Jamie Hargrove was invited, with about 250 other student leaders, to the White House to discuss foreign and domestic issues with President Jimmy Carter. Hargrove told the Talisman that draft registration and energy policy were the main subjects of his meetings with Carter and some of his senior advisers, and that the purpose of the meeting was to explain why the administration was taking the actions it was taking. The yearbook does not give the date of the visit.

1980 Talisman, pp. 274-275, "Concerting student opinion"

1980
#

ASG surveyed 28 universities on open house hours

ASG surveyed 28 institutions comparable to Western and found that Western's open house policy gave the fewest visitation hours per semester of any of them. The Herald reported the finding on 27 September 1979 under the line that Western came last in a 28-college housing poll. ASG also published the results of the previous spring's open house referendum, which showed a majority of students surveyed wanted weekly open houses. The policy finally adopted came from the student affairs office rather than from ASG.

1980 Talisman, pp. 274-275, "Concerting student opinion"

17 January 1980
#

Hargrove said ASG had laid foundations

Michele Wood reported Jamie Hargrove's assessment at the halfway point of his term, that the Associated Student Government had laid foundations. The same issue reported that open house hours had been increased to 31 a week, the policy question ASG had spent the autumn surveying other universities about.

College Heights Herald 55:30, 17 January 1980 PDF

31 January 1980Programme
#

ASG asks the bookstore to break even on books

ASG introduced a bill asking the College Heights Bookstore to adopt a break-even policy on book sales, arguing that the store had a monopoly on textbooks and that the College Heights Foundation which runs it is a non-profit organisation. Congress was to vote on it the following week. At the same meeting congress formed an ad hoc committee on university food services.

College Heights Herald, Vol. 55, No. 34, 31 January 1980 PDF

5 February 1980
#

ASG backed the Uniform Landlord Tenant Reform Act

Resolution 79-16, dated 5 February 1980 and filed under the name Ultra Extension Support, put ASG behind the Uniform Landlord Tenant Reform Act. Michele Wood reported ASG's support for the tenants' rights bill in the Herald of 7 February, in an issue that also carried a letter from Marion Tabor crediting ASG for its survey work. Off-campus housing stayed on the agenda into the next session, when Resolution 80-34 set out the duties of ASG's off-campus representatives in landlord and tenant disputes.

SGA Resolution 79-16, Ultra Extension Support

21 February 1980
#

ASG considers moving its spring elections

The Associated Student Government considered changing the date of its spring elections. The discussion took place against campus anger over a proposed tuition increase.

Herald 55:40, 21 Feb 1980 PDF

26 February 1980
#

ASG moved to create a vice president for public affairs

Resolution 79-18, dated 26 February 1980, supported amending the ASG constitution to add a vice president of public affairs. It failed within a fortnight: on 13 March Michele Wood reported that the proposal for a new congress position had been rejected. The office was still unsettled the following autumn, when Resolution 80-14 of 21 October 1980 carried the same title again.

SGA Resolution 79-18, Public Affairs Vice President

4 March 1980
#

A bill would have changed the activities officer's duties

Michele Wood reported a bill to change the duties of the ASG activities officer, and two days later the Herald reported the activities office ready for change. The office was the last remnant of the entertainment function the Board of Regents had moved to the University Center Board the previous spring, taking between $60,000 and $70,000 of ASG's budget with it.

College Heights Herald 55:43, 4 March 1980 PDF

6 March 1980
#

The student seat on the state council was refused

Michele Wood reported that a student seat on the Council on Higher Education had been denied. It was the answer to Resolution 79-6, which ASG had passed on 25 September 1979 asking for exactly that. The same issue reported that few tickets remained for the NCAA tournament.

College Heights Herald 55:44, 6 March 1980 PDF

April 1980Programme
#

ASG printed a candidate pamphlet for the spring election

After the primary, ASG distributed pamphlets carrying a photograph of each executive office candidate and a short statement written by the candidate on their qualifications and plans. President Jamie Hargrove said ASG was moving away from leaving publicity to the candidates and treating turnout as its own responsibility. Rules and elections committee chairman Tim Irons said voters had stayed away in the past because ASG had not told them where the voting tables were or how to use them. The Talisman does not give the exact date the pamphlets went out; the primary was on 17 April and the general election on 24 April.

1980 Talisman, pp. 274-275, "Concerting student opinion"

8 April 1980
#

ASG wrote rules for campaign spending, and for withdrawals

Resolution 79-22, dated 8 April 1980, dealt with campaign budgets for ASG elections, and the same night's Resolution 79-23 addressed withdrawal periods and deficiency reports. The spending cap became a live argument the following year, when the congress voted in September 1981 to raise it.

SGA Resolution 79-22, Candidate Expenditures

10 April 1980
#

ASG proposed a longer period for dropping classes

Michele Wood reported that ASG had proposed lengthening the time students had to drop a class. It ran in the same issue that named Steve Fuller, David Rue and Tim Irons as the three candidates for the presidency.

College Heights Herald 55:51, 10 April 1980 PDF

15 April 1980
#

The Herald called the centre board seat a burden on ASG

The Herald's editorial page argued that the University Center Board position was a burden for the Associated Student Government, and Roland Gibbons drew a cartoon of the centre board against ASG in the same issue. ASG had lost campus entertainment to the centre board the previous spring but its president still sat on it, which was what had made the Homecoming dance grant so awkward.

College Heights Herald 55:52, 15 April 1980 PDF

17 April 1980
#

Fuller and Irons win the presidential primary

Steve Fuller and Tim Irons emerged as victors in the ASG presidential primary, from a field of three that also included David Rue. Candidates had been certified earlier in the month.

Herald 55:53, 17 Apr 1980 PDF

24 April 1980
#

Steve Fuller elected ASG president

Steve Fuller, Mark Wilson and Greg Zoeller were elected to the top Associated Student Government jobs. The Herald wrote that persistence had paid off in Fuller's campaign, while runner-up Tim Irons looked to the future after losing.

Herald 55:55, 24 Apr 1980 PDF

May 1980
#

New ASG officers and members sworn in

The new Associated Student Government officers and members were sworn in. ASG and the Interhall Council were also conducting student polls as the year closed.

Herald 55:57, 1 May 1980 PDF

6 May 1980
#

The first resolution against room inspections

Resolution 80-1, dated 6 May 1980, was a resolution to stop room inspections. It opens the fight that dominated the next academic year, in which ASG twice voted down bills to abolish the inspections, the Kentucky Civil Liberties Union took the question towards a federal court, and the congress instead approved stricter rules for entering rooms.

SGA Resolution 80-1, Permission for Room Inspection

What this is

A year-by-year record of student government at Western Kentucky University, from the ratification of the Associated Students constitution in April 1966 to the present. Every entry names the source it came from.

Corrections

The names here start with the plaque in the SGA Chambers, and the plaque is known to be wrong in places. Where the archive disagrees with it, this site follows the archive and records the change on the corrections page. If you served, or you know that a year here is wrong, the project wants to hear from you: people who held an office can be given the keys to their own year on the contribute page.

What it rests on

Everything here comes from what is publicly available online: the digitised Herald and SGA's own papers on TopSCHOLAR, the Talisman yearbooks, wkuherald.com, the university's news pages and the Wayback Machine. That is a fraction of what happened. Student government has met most weeks for sixty years, and the greater part of it was never written down, or was written down on paper that has never been scanned. A thin year here is a thin record, not a quiet one.

Reuse

Text on this site is the work of the project. Photographs, documents and legislation are reproduced from Western Kentucky University's own open archives and from the College Heights Herald and the Talisman; each item names the collection, issue and page it came from and links to the original.
