Katie Dawson
president · plaque: 2005-06
Katie Dawson: Named in April 2004 Herald coverage as incoming executive vice president, so she came up through the cabinet. Confirmed as a 2005 presidential candidate in College Heights Herald election coverage, and the paper's own election-night story names her as the winner.

Katie Dawson — the term

Dawson served as both SGA president and student regent during Western's centennial year. When the university launched its 14-month centennial celebration on Nov. 19, 2005, she spoke at the dedication of Centennial Plaza, offering her own meaning for the school slogan: "Find what you love and carry it with you your whole life through."

Her spring semester was dominated by a proposed $46-per-semester fee for three campus construction projects. At a student forum with President Gary Ransdell she suggested exempting the next year's seniors, who would graduate before any project was finished; the Board of Regents rejected the exemption 10-1 at its April 7 meeting and approved the fee 9-1. Dawson cast the lone abstention, saying that although she supported the fee, she could not vote for it with so many seniors counting on the exemption.

Her term ended at Western's 159th commencement on May 13, 2006, where the Radcliff native graduated with a degree in corporate and organizational communication and addressed her 1,797 fellow undergraduates as SGA president. She charged the graduates to have courage, endurance, strength, patience and character, saying success was knowing "when to throw in the towel and when to wave it around and cheer."

The Senate

25 senators this year.

All 25 senate candidates on the March 2005 ballot ran unopposed and were elected. A senator surnamed Johnson was reported elected speaker of the senate in December 2005, but no source found gives a first name, so the office is left unfilled here rather than guessed.

What happened, in order

2005-01-01

The plus/minus grading fight

An Institutional Research report on plus and minus grading, a protest broadside, a post-winter-term student survey and an SGA handbook all survive - one of the few academic-policy campaigns documented from research through to public agitation.

2005-11-20campus

WKU launched its centennial celebration

Western kicked off a 14-month celebration of its 1906 founding on Saturday, Nov. 19, dedicating three new campus landmarks including Centennial Plaza. SGA President Katie Dawson spoke at the plaza dedication, offering her own meaning of the school slogan "The Spirit Makes the Master."

2005-12-08

SGA closed the fall with 10 pieces of legislation

The Herald reported that SGA passed 10 pieces of legislation in the fall semester, fewer than in previous fall semesters. Its legislation brought bikes, a cancer documentary and iPods to Western. The same issue reported that a senator named Johnson had been elected the new speaker of the senate.

2006-02-01

SGA took up a proposed $45 construction fee

At its first regular meeting of the spring semester, SGA discussed a proposed $45-per-semester fee to pay for a campus-wide wireless network, a new health center and renovations to the Ivan Wilson Center for Fine Arts. President Katie Dawson supported the fee, and Western's chief financial officer estimated it would raise about $1.4 million a year.

Daily News, 1 Feb 2006 ↗
2006-02-08

Ransdell asked SGA for a resolution backing the fee

WKU President Gary Ransdell came to the SGA meeting and urged senators to pass a resolution supporting the proposed $45 fee, which the Board of Regents would vote on April 7. Dawson sat on that board as student regent, and SGA officers said a supporting resolution was likely within a few weeks.

Daily News, 8 Feb 2006 ↗
2006-03-03

Executive vice president aired an internal rift

Executive Vice President Mason Stevenson read a letter at the SGA meeting saying some members trying to protect their political interests had "thwarted" SGA's mission to improve Western. The letter outlined his concerns about relations between the executive and legislative branches.

Herald, 3 Mar 2006 (via Wayback) ↗
2006-03-16

Senate put $2,000 toward SGA's 40th anniversary party

Two bills devoting $2,000 to a party for SGA's 40th anniversary passed unanimously at the group's Tuesday meeting. The Herald reported the organization planned a campus-wide party to mark four decades of student government at Western.

Herald, 16 Mar 2006 (via Wayback) ↗
2006-04-08

Regents approved the fee; Dawson's senior exemption failed

The Board of Regents voted 9-1 to approve the $46-per-semester construction fee, with Dawson abstaining, and rejected 10-1 her proposal to exempt next year's seniors, an idea she had raised at a student forum the week before. Dawson said that although she supported the fee, she could not vote for it with so many seniors counting on the exemption.

Daily News, 8 Apr 2006 ↗
2006-04-13

Rob Watkins elected SGA president

Bowling Green senior Robert Watkins won the online election for SGA president after spending more than a week seeking supporters who would vote on TopNet. He defeated Berea junior Kara Ratliff, and the results were announced Tuesday at a crowded Revolution 91.

Herald, 13 Apr 2006 (via Wayback) ↗
2006-04-20

Executive board issued its first veto of the year

After the Tuesday senate meeting, the SGA executive board vetoed a bill that would have financed community- and campus-oriented projects through civic engagement grants, the first time it exercised its veto power that year. The Herald reported the grants were "a reality for only about 30 minutes" and that time restraints halted an appeal of the veto.

Herald, 20 Apr 2006 (via Wayback) ↗
2006-04-20

Herald editorial attacked SGA misspending

A Herald editorial titled "SGA misspending" said members should not have to be "motivated by a giveaway to show up for meetings." It noted that the opinion page had devoted considerable space in recent weeks to both positive and negative aspects of SGA, which it said could and should be an important organization on campus.
