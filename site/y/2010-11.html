Standing of the record
As recorded on the plaque. Not yet corroborated in the archive.

Named as SGA president throughout the year's Herald coverage, including a dedicated year-end profile.

Jessie opened the 2010-11 year having spent the summer preparing with his executive board, which attended the Kentucky Leadership Academy at Barren River State Park alongside SGAs from Kentucky's other public universities. His early priorities included building a closer relationship with international students, whom SGA hosted at a dinner during orientation, planning a study away program for students to study elsewhere in the country, and setting up a forum for South Campus concerns. "We are trying to reach out to South Campus," he said, and he and Executive Vice President Kendrick Bryan reallocated the budget to fund scholarship and study abroad programs.

By the end of his term, SGA had given out more scholarship money than ever, with the scholarship fund raised by $8,000 and organizational aid increased to $40,000, and the senate had passed first-of-their-kind bills reimbursing CLEP exam fees and buying test prep books for libraries. Jessie called the DUC renovation "one of the biggest things SGA has done in a while," and Ransdell credited him with taking the initiative to start the renovation talks. A senior, Jessie handed the office to Billy Stephens and left for Chicago after graduation to work for Teach for America.

The year, in order22

10 September 2010Service
#

SGA listed its signature services, including Western Wednesdays

In a commentary published on 10 September 2010, executive vice president Kendrick Bryan set out SGA's standing services: organizational aid of more than $40,000 a year with up to $500 per group, Provide-A-Ride, study abroad scholarships and scholar development grants, free blue books and Scantron forms in the SGA office at DUC 130, and Western Wednesdays, a discount for students wearing WKU apparel honoured by thirteen restaurants. SGA was operating on a budget of $110,000.

$110,000 budget; more than $40,000 a year in organizational aid

Herald, 10 Sep 2010 - "LETTER TO THE EDITOR: SGA kick-starts new academic year at WKU"

10 September 2010Programme
#

The events the 2009-10 senate had helped pay for

The same commentary listed what the student senate had sponsored during 2009-10: FIJIs Across America, the American Collegiate Intramural Sports regional flag football tournament, the Wilson Family Endowment Fund for Injured Student Athletes, the mural near Schneider Hall, the Miss Black Western Pageant, the Rally for Higher Education and the Interfaith Dialogue on Earth Care. It is the clearest surviving inventory of a single year's SGA co-sponsorships from this period.

Herald, 10 Sep 2010 - "LETTER TO THE EDITOR: SGA kick-starts new academic year at WKU"

14 September 2010
#

SGA raised scholarship budget to $20,000

After running out of scholarship money the previous year, SGA increased its scholarship budget from $12,000 to $20,000 and lowered general senate funding from $20,000 to $12,000 to cover the change. Study abroad awards were capped at $500 for a semester trip and $250 for a winter or summer term so more students could receive money. SGA also finalized a new study away scholarship for travel inside the country and set aside $400 for it in the winter term.

Herald, 14 Sep 2010

14 September 2010Other
#

SGA paid marketing students $885 for koozies and bottle openers

WKU's American Marketing Association partnered with SGA to market the organisation after AMA president Currie Martin, a former senator, argued that exposure was SGA's weakness. The senate passed a bill giving AMA $885 to buy 500 koozies and 500 keychain bottle openers carrying the SGA logo and the Provide-A-Ride phone number. President Colton Jessie called it a win for both sides, giving marketing students a real client.

$885 for 500 koozies and 500 keychain bottle openers

Herald, 21 Sep 2010 - "Marketing group, SGA partner up"

23 September 2010
#

Twelve senators elected in fall election with 847 voters

Twelve students won seats in the fall senate election, in which 847 total voters cast ballots on TopNet. Keyana Boka, Natalie Broderick and Eppiphanie Benton were elected freshman senators, running unopposed, while 25 students competed for the at-large seats. Winners included Diego Leal Ambriz, who would run for president that spring.

Herald, 23 Sep 2010

19 October 2010Tradition
#

Campus Clean-up drew about 35 volunteers

Campus improvements committee head Kaylee Egerer told the senate on 19 October 2010 that around 35 people had turned out to help at that day's Campus Clean-up. President Colton Jessie had announced the week before that the clean-up would carry recycling bags as well as trash bags for the first time.

Herald, 19 Oct 2010 - "SGA: Safety Walk to take place next week"

22 October 2010Service
#

Provide-A-Ride carried more than 9,200 passengers in a year

The service's 2010 report showed more than 9,200 passengers used Provide-A-Ride during 2009-10, with about 40 per cent picked up from bars and 46 per cent from off-campus residences. More than 10,300 people had used it in 2007-08. No figures survived for 2008-09 because a fire at the Yellow Cab Company building destroyed the handwritten logs.

jointly paid for by SGA and Parking and Transportation Services

Herald, 22 Oct 2010 - "Bars, off-campus residences top Provide-A-Ride pickups"

November 2010Service
#

Campus safety walk found nearly 30 lights out

SGA's campus safety walk took place in the week before 10 November 2010. Reporting to the Senate, executive vice president Kendrick Bryan said participants had found nearly 30 lights that were out around campus.

College Heights Herald, 10 Nov 2010 - Stunson, "SGA invites regents to Dine with Decision Makers"

2 November 2010Service
#

Free test forms switched to recycled Apperson sheets

SGA replaced the free Scantron answer forms it handed out in its office with 4,000 Apperson forms made from 100 per cent recycled material. Administrative vice president Wade Pierce said the 4,000 Apperson forms cost $197 against $300 for just 1,000 Scantrons, and that they still worked in the university's machines.

$197 for 4,000 forms, against $300 for 1,000 Scantrons

Herald, 2 Nov 2010 - "SGA switches to 'greener' test materials"

12 November 2010Programme
#

Focus Fridays emailed the Greek presidents every week

SGA began Focus Fridays on 12 November 2010, sending Greek chapter presidents a weekly email with notes from the previous senate meeting and current business. Public relations chair Ann Blair Thornton said SGA had lacked relations with Greeks, who were often the most involved students on campus. Bryan called it the largest digital presence SGA had ever had with Greek organisations.

Herald, 19 Nov 2010 - "SGA starts Focus Fridays with Greeks"

19 November 2010
#

Ransdell brought $49 million DUC renovation plan to SGA

President Gary Ransdell and head architect Jeff Stivers told SGA at its Nov. 16 meeting that officials were moving ahead on a plan to completely renovate Downing University Center at a cost of up to $49 million. Students would pay about $140 extra per year in fees for up to 20 years, and Ransdell promised students would be involved in every decision. SGA had to pass resolutions on the design and fees before the Board of Regents could approve the project.

Herald, 19 Nov 2010

7 December 2010
#

SGA funds extended library hours for finals week

The senate approved $600 to keep the library open until 2 a.m. during finals week, covering extra staff time and lighting. President Colton Jessie said continuing the service was never in question, calling finals "an important time for students."

Herald, 7 Dec 2010

7 December 2010Tradition
#

Second Dine with Decision Makers drew about fifty students

SGA held Dine with Decision Makers on 7 December 2010 in the Cupola Room, where Kentucky Secretary of State Trey Grayson and more than a dozen other officials shared tables with students. Bryan said about 50 students attended. Administrative vice president Wade Pierce, seated at Grayson's table, said the casual conversation showed him the secretary of state in a new light.

Herald, 8 Dec 2010 - "Students get to know decision makers at SGA event"

10 December 2010
#

Senate tabled DUC resolution over lack of student input

Senators tabled the bill supporting the DUC renovation at the Dec. 7 meeting, saying they had not received enough student input just three weeks after learning of the project. Jessie backed the delay, saying he wanted to hear students' voices before moving forward, even though it pushed SGA's presentation to the Board of Regents from January 21 to April 22. Campus Improvements Chair Kaylee Egerer said many students did not yet know they would pay $140 a year for the project.

Herald, 10 Dec 2010

4 February 2011Service
#

Provide-A-Ride outgrew its taxi contractor

By February 2011 President Colton Jessie said Provide-A-Ride had become too big for Franklin Taxi to run and students were waiting hours for a car. Between 31 August and 13 November 2010 the service carried 6,111 passengers on 1,189 calls, more than two-thirds of the whole previous year's total in three and a half months. Provide-A-Ride had carried 9,200 passengers on 1,772 calls across 2009-10.

Herald, 4 Feb 2011 - "WKU's Provide-A-Ride growing too large for current provider"

11 February 2011
#

Herald reported Greeks held 40 percent of SGA seats

The Herald reported that 21 of SGA's 50 members belonged to Greek organizations, more than 40 percent, while less than 8 percent of the student body was in a fraternity or sorority. Sixteen of the 21 were fraternity men, and two fraternities, Sigma Chi and Lambda Chi Alpha, accounted for nine of them. Officers defended the imbalance, and the story noted SGA's operating budget for 2010-11 was $121,335, drawn from tuition.

Herald, 11 Feb 2011

22 February 2011
#

SGA unanimously passed DUC renovation resolution

SGA unanimously passed the resolution supporting DUC renovations at its Feb. 22 meeting, the bill's second reading. Project costs were capped at $49,128,545, with a student fee of up to $70 per semester for 20 years and $1.5 million per year from WKU. The resolution, authored by Kaylee Egerer, asked for assurance of a 24-hour study space and called for construction to begin as soon as possible while interest rates were low.

Herald, 22 Feb 2011

March 2011
#

SGA members joined Rally for Higher Education in Frankfort

SGA members traveled to Frankfort on March 1 for the annual Rally for Higher Education, where SGA secretary Katie Stillwell was the keynote speaker representing students statewide. Her speech urged legislators to protect money already set aside for higher education. Jessie, the previous year's keynote speaker, joined chief of staff Charlie Harris and other state SGA officers for a lunch with Gov. Steve Beshear.

Herald, 1 Mar 2011

April 2011
#

Administrative Vice President Wade Pierce to hand post to fraternity brother

Pierce, who chaired the organizational aid committee, said he would be succeeded as administrative vice president by Devon Hilderbrandt, his committee co-chair and his "little brother" in Lambda Chi Alpha. Hilderbrandt ran unopposed in that spring's election.

Herald, 1 Apr 2011

7 April 2011
#

Stephens won presidency with 56 percent of 1,066 votes

Billy Stephens, a junior from Hawesville, defeated senior Diego Leal Ambriz for SGA president, taking 56 percent of the 1,066 total votes in results announced just after midnight on April 7. Kendrick Bryan was re-elected executive vice president and Devon Hilderbrandt won administrative vice president, both unopposed. All 35 senate candidates were elected to the 36 open seats, each needing just one vote to win.

Herald, 7 Apr 2011

12 April 2011Concert
#

Senate endorsed the Topper Talent Benefit Concert and funded One Planet Day

At its meeting on 12 April 2011 the senate approved $500 to buy reusable water bottles for One Planet Day, the renamed Earth Day observance, and passed a resolution endorsing Buck for Luck and the Topper Talent Benefit Concert, both run by Hilltoppers for a Cure and Relay for Life. Another $250 went to the National College Dodgeball Association national tournament. The last study abroad scholarship bill of the year gave $6,700 to 23 students.

$500 for water bottles, $250 for the dodgeball tournament, $6,700 in study abroad awards

Herald, 13 Apr 2011 - "SGA Notes: Money allotted for One Planet Day"

6 May 2011
#

Senate tabled tobacco-free campus resolution

After nearly 45 minutes of debate, the senate tabled a resolution supporting a tobacco-free campus until the next semester. The resolution rested on a survey of more than 400 students in which 47.82 percent supported a ban, but senators argued the survey was biased because tombstones displaying the dangers of smoking had been laid outside DUC the day it was given. The campus improvements committee then said it would not support a ban until the two-year on-campus living requirement was removed.

Herald, 6 May 2011

What this is

A year-by-year record of student government at Western Kentucky University, from the ratification of the Associated Students constitution in April 1966 to the present. Every entry names the source it came from.

Corrections

The names here start with the plaque in the SGA Chambers, and the plaque is known to be wrong in places. Where the archive disagrees with it, this site follows the archive and records the change on the corrections page. If you served, or you know that a year here is wrong, the project wants to hear from you: people who held an office can be given the keys to their own year on the contribute page.

What it rests on

Everything here comes from what is publicly available online: the digitised Herald and SGA's own papers on TopSCHOLAR, the Talisman yearbooks, wkuherald.com, the university's news pages and the Wayback Machine. That is a fraction of what happened. Student government has met most weeks for sixty years, and the greater part of it was never written down, or was written down on paper that has never been scanned. A thin year here is a thin record, not a quiet one.

Reuse

Text on this site is the work of the project. Photographs, documents and legislation are reproduced from Western Kentucky University's own open archives and from the College Heights Herald and the Talisman; each item names the collection, issue and page it came from and links to the original.
