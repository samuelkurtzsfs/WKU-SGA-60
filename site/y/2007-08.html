2006-07

Confirmed against primary Board of Regents records: a resolution read into the 18 Jul 2008 board minutes states she served as the board's student member from 27 Apr 2007 to 18 Jul 2008, and the same minutes record Johnathon Boles taking the oath to replace her that day. Also confirmed in Herald coverage of the 24 Apr 2008 Board of Regents meeting, where she is named 'Student Government Association President Jeanne Johnson.'

Jeanne Lynn Johnson, a Marion senior and Kappa Delta member, had served as SGA's president pro tempore after Rob Watkins's November 2006 resignation and won the February 2007 special election for the vacant student regent seat with 688 votes. She became SGA president and student regent for 2007-08 in her own right, sworn onto the Board of Regents on April 27, 2007, and installed as president at SGA's May 2007 banquet.

That fall her administration reported record interest in the senate election, 25 candidates for 20 seats, and she and Executive Vice President Johnathon Boles helped organize a mock gubernatorial election that drew 127 student voters. She pushed a senate resolution asking the state for more funding after Western received only 54 percent of its recommended budget increase, and organized Walk Out Western, an Oct. 25 class walkout protesting state budget cuts that President Gary Ransdell said he could not condone.

As student regent, Johnson voted against the scale of the board's 9 percent tuition increase at the April 24, 2008 quarterly meeting, seconding Louisville regent Larry Zielke's motion to cap the increase at 6 percent and push the General Assembly for more funding instead; the motion died with only two votes. At the same meeting she argued the $370,000 budgeted for the Honors College should be cut, saying the college "only benefits 900 students" and other students should not have to pay for it; Ransdell defended it as part of the university's strategic plan.

In the spring, as her administration ran the election to choose her successor, Johnson defended the one-week campaign window written into election codes she said were inherited from past administrations, warning that longer campaigns could hurt candidates' schoolwork and that "too much PR hurts." She pointed to new turnout efforts her administration tried, including holding the debate during SGA week and adding candidate profiles and questionnaires to the SGA website.

Johnathon Boles won the March 2008 election and was installed as SGA president at the spring banquet, but Johnson kept the Board of Regents seat until the board's next quarterly meeting; a resolution read there on July 18, 2008 thanked her for "able leadership, faithful service, and dedication" and Boles took the oath as student regent the same day.

The year, in order25

13 September 2007
#

Fall senate election drew 25 candidates for 20 seats

SGA reported record interest in its fall senate election, with 25 students running for 20 available seats, the most applications President Jeanne Johnson said she had ever seen. Elections were set for Sept. 19 and 20 through TopNet, with an informational table in Downing University Center on Sept. 18. Public relations director Liz Goddard said more competition would produce harder workers.

Herald, 13 Sep 2007

2 October 2007
#

SGA co-sponsored mock gubernatorial election

SGA and the Office of the Secretary of State co-sponsored a mock gubernatorial election at Centennial Mall as part of Secretary of State Trey Grayson's Civic Literacy Initiative of Kentucky. The 127 students who voted chose challenger Steve Beshear over incumbent Gov. Ernie Fletcher, 74 to 50. President Jeanne Johnson and Executive Vice President Johnathon Boles called the event a success.

Herald, 2 Oct 2007

9 October 2007Programme
#

SGA held an autumn cookout

The senate minutes of 9 and 16 October 2007 both record a cookout being organised, alongside Walk-Out Western. The minutes name the event but record nothing about how it went.

SGA senate minutes, 9 October 2007

18 October 2007
#

Resolution demanded more state funding for Western

At its Oct. 9 meeting SGA passed a resolution asking the state government to appropriate more money to Western, which had received 54 percent of the funding the Council on Postsecondary Education recommended for fiscal year 2008. President Jeanne Johnson argued that tuition increases to cover the shortfall hurt students. The resolution stated Western needed the full recommended amount to meet its 2020 educational goals.

Herald, 18 Oct 2007

18 October 2007
#

SGA organized Walk Out Western budget protest

SGA sponsored Walk Out Western, asking students to leave class at 12:20 p.m. on Oct. 25 for a rally at the Colonnade to protest state budget cuts, with empty seats meant as a message to Frankfort legislators. President Gary Ransdell said he could not condone students walking out of class and doubted a single-campus protest would affect state funding. Johnson said other state universities declined to join but were watching how it worked at Western.

Herald, 18 Oct 2007

25 October 2007
#

Committee proposed $1,995 educational video subscription

SGA's Academic Affairs Committee, chaired by Drew Eclov, prepared legislation to buy a roughly $1,995 subscription to United Streaming, a Discovery Channel-owned site with about 45,000 educational video clips that Eclov called a version of YouTube for higher education. The committee planned to survey students and professors before bringing it to a vote. SGA also scheduled a vote on a resolution placing $1,000 in a field trip fund to support learning outside the classroom.

Herald, 25 Oct 2007

November 2007
#

Athletics director asked SGA for help on football attendance

Athletics Director Wood Selig came to the SGA meeting to ask senators for ideas on getting more students to football games as Western moved to the Football Bowl Subdivision, which required an average attendance of 15,000 to keep bowl eligibility. Senators suggested advertising on Western's website and Facebook rather than email, and later game times. Executive Vice President Johnathon Boles, who sat on a faculty advisory committee for athletics, arranged the consultation.

Herald, 1 Nov 2007

Senate worked on an end-of-semester study day

An end-of-semester study day appears in the senate minutes of 9 October and 6 November 2007 and in the executive council minutes of the same date, alongside homecoming and Walk-Out Western. It returned in the spring, appearing in the minutes of 1 April 2008.

SGA senate minutes, 6 November 2007

13 November 2007Service
#

SGA planned a finals week Oasis and a holiday dinner

The executive council minutes of 13 November 2007 record the finals week oasis being planned along with cafeteria hours and computer labs. The senate minutes of 27 November carry it forward together with a holiday dinner, and those of 4 December record the oasis again. The following April the senate voted $250 for a Spring Oasis, then in its second year.

SGA executive council minutes, 13 November 2007

15 November 2007
#

SGA planned Listen Up Legislators lobbying campaign

SGA began laying groundwork for Listen Up Legislators, a spring lobbying campaign in which senators would meet legislators one-on-one in Frankfort to push for university funding, after the Council on Postsecondary Education recommended a $174 million increase for public universities. Chief of staff Skylar Jordan said the traditional February rally had not worked, pointing to Western receiving only 54 percent of the recommended increase. Other state student governments expressed interest in coordinating lobbying dates.

Herald, 15 Nov 2007

31 January 2008
#

Spring session opened with ONE Campus bill

At its first spring meeting on Jan. 29, SGA introduced legislation by Senators Matt Vaughan and Doug Tate asking the senate to recognize Western as a ONE Campus, part of the national campaign against global AIDS and extreme poverty. SGA also released its spring schedule, including Listen Up Legislators on Feb. 14, the Frankfort rally for higher education on Feb. 20 and the annual banquet on April 29. In the semester's opening handout, Executive Vice President Johnathon Boles pointed members to the constitution's purpose of serving the student body equally.

Herald, 31 Jan 2008

Gatton Academy students asked for a seat in SGA

The executive cabinet minutes of 24 March 2008 record a discussion of Gatton Academy students participating in SGA, alongside plus/minus grading, the Hall of Distinguished Seniors and a block party. The Herald of 27 March carried an article headed that the Gatton Academy of Math and Science wanted representation too.

SGA executive cabinet minutes, 24 March 2008

27 March 2008
#

The Herald turned its opinion page on SGA during the election

The Herald of 27 March 2008 ran an editorial cartoon depicting SGA as clowns, an editorial saying the organisation needed to change its election process, and rival opinion pieces from candidates Johnathon Boles and from Theodore Harden, Kevin Smiley and Reagan Gilley. On 8 April a further cartoon and an editorial headed that SGA needed an overhaul followed.

Herald 83:42, 27 March 2008 (article index)

April 2008
#

Spring election closed amid low-turnout criticism

Voting for three executive seats and 28 senate seats ended after a one-week campaign that candidates criticized as part of SGA's low-involvement problem; turnout had been 1,424 the year before and 1,376 the year before that. Red Towel Party presidential candidate Tedde Harden promised to rework what he called outdated election codes, while opponent Johnathon Boles agreed campaign time should be extended. President Jeanne Johnson said her administration inherited the codes and had tried new turnout methods, including extra credit for attending the debate.

Herald, 1 Apr 2008

8 April 2008
#

Skylar Jordan filed a complaint against the Red Towel Party

The Herald of 8 April 2008 reported that Skylar Jordan had filed a complaint against the Red Towel Party, the slate that had campaigned to energise SGA. Two days later the paper reported the Judicial Council was addressing both a tie and the campaign complaint, and that SGA would have to elect a new Speaker of the Senate. The archive does not record how the council ruled.

Herald 83:45, 8 April 2008 (article index)

8 April 2008Tradition
#

Spring campus clean-up rescheduled to Thursday

The Herald of 8 April 2008 reported that SGA had rescheduled the campus clean-up for Thursday. The senate minutes of 25 March and 1 April both record the clean-up being organised.

Herald 83:45, 8 April 2008 (article index)

10 April 2008
#

Western won the national ONE Campus Challenge

The Herald of 10 April 2008 reported that Western had won the ONE Campus Challenge, the national campaign against extreme poverty and global AIDS. SGA had introduced legislation asking the senate to recognise Western as a ONE Campus at its first spring meeting on 29 January, and the Herald of 24 April reported ONE leaders advocating an education bill in Washington.

Herald 83:46, 10 April 2008 (article index)

17 April 2008campus
#

Ransdell announced 9 percent tuition increase

President Gary Ransdell announced at campus forums that he would recommend a 9 percent tuition increase for 2008-09, abandoning a plan to hold increases at 6 percent. The change followed a state budget crisis in which Gov. Steve Beshear ordered a 3 percent cut to postsecondary education in December 2007 and proposed an additional 12 percent, later reduced by the General Assembly to 3 percent. Ransdell said the swing from expected state revenue was about $11 million to $12 million.

Herald, 17 Apr 2008

17 April 2008Other
#

Senator pushed WKU to join the Ruckus free music service

Kevin Smiley, senator and chair of the student affairs committee, wrote legislation asking administrators to apply for membership of Ruckus, a download service that let anyone with a .edu address stream music and films free from a company-supplied server. The senate was due to consider it on 22 April 2008. Smiley said the software was already available to students but could not run on WKU computers until the university joined, and that installing the server would cost the university nothing.

College Heights Herald 83:48, 17 Apr 2008 - "SGA bill pushes for free music" (TopSCHOLAR)

22 April 2008Service
#

Spring Oasis gave out exam supplies during finals week

On 22 April 2008 the senate voted $250 for Spring Oasis, then in its second year, a programme meant to relieve the cost of finals week. The money bought scantrons, exam books, pencils, pens and highlighters. SGA allocated a further $500 to provide refreshments for students in the library.

College Heights Herald 83:50, 24 Apr 2008 - "SGA helps to relieve college expenses during finals week" (TopSCHOLAR) PDF

24 April 2008
#

Student regent Johnson opposed scale of 9 percent tuition increase

The Board of Regents approved a 9 percent tuition increase for 2008-09 and finalized $2.5 million in cuts to the 2007-08 budget. Louisville regent Larry Zielke moved to cap the increase at 6 percent and push the General Assembly for more funding instead; SGA President and student regent Jeanne Johnson seconded the motion, but it died with only two votes. Johnson also argued the $370,000 budgeted for the Honors College should be reduced, saying the college benefited only 900 students and other students should not have to pay for it.

Herald 83:51, 29 Apr 2008 - "Regents approve 9 percent tuition increase, finalize 07-08 budget cuts"

The Ruckus free music bill was shelved over IT concerns

The Herald of 24 April 2008 reported that the Ruckus bill had been shelved because of information technology concerns, a week after the senate was due to consider it.

Herald 83:50, 24 April 2008 (article index) PDF

24 April 2008
#

Senate rejected a diploma proposal and backed the SEAT

The Herald of 24 April 2008 reported that SGA had rejected a diploma proposal and that it was in favour of the Student Engagement Activities Transcript, the record of learning outside the classroom that SGA was still promoting to students in 2009. The same issue reported Helm-Cravens Library staying open later during finals week.

Herald 83:50, 24 April 2008 (article index) PDF

18 July 2008
#

Johnson's regent term ended as Boles took the board oath

The Board of Regents read a resolution thanking Jeanne Johnson for her service as the board's student member from April 27, 2007 to July 18, 2008, calling her term one of "able leadership, faithful service, and dedication." Johnathon Boles, who had already been installed as SGA president that spring, took the oath of office as student regent the same day, formally completing the transition between administrations.

What this is

A year-by-year record of student government at Western Kentucky University, from the ratification of the Associated Students constitution in April 1966 to the present. Every entry names the source it came from.

Corrections

The names here start with the plaque in the SGA Chambers, and the plaque is known to be wrong in places. Where the archive disagrees with it, this site follows the archive and records the change on the corrections page. If you served, or you know that a year here is wrong, the project wants to hear from you: people who held an office can be given the keys to their own year on the contribute page.

What it rests on

Everything here comes from what is publicly available online: the digitised Herald and SGA's own papers on TopSCHOLAR, the Talisman yearbooks, wkuherald.com, the university's news pages and the Wayback Machine. That is a fraction of what happened. Student government has met most weeks for sixty years, and the greater part of it was never written down, or was written down on paper that has never been scanned. A thin year here is a thin record, not a quiet one.

Reuse

Text on this site is the work of the project. Photographs, documents and legislation are reproduced from Western Kentucky University's own open archives and from the College Heights Herald and the Talisman; each item names the collection, issue and page it came from and links to the original.
