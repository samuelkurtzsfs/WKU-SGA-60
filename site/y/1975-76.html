Standing of the record
Confirmed in the sources below.

Likely the Steve Henry who later served as Lieutenant Governor of Kentucky (1995-2003). Wikipedia's biography of Stephen L. Henry (b. 8 Oct 1953) states he was elected WKU Student Body President "in his second attempt, after losing a close election the preceding year to Jeff Costello," and served on the WKU Board of Regents as a Kentucky resident while president - matching this record's own Herald sourcing almost exactly: Jeffrey Consolo (not "Costello", likely a Wikipedia transcription of the same name) won the presidency in spring 1974, and the Herald's 30 Apr 1976 wrap-up of Henry's term specifically describes him balancing the dual jobs of student regent and ASG president. The match in name, university, era, and this specific narrative beat is treated as verification, but the identification rests on a secondary source (Wikipedia), not a WKU archive document that states it outright - flagged here rather than treated as fully closed.

Steve Henry appeared in ASG coverage as early as September 1972, when the Herald reported his view that the Associated Student Government had lost respect.

Henry won the spring 1974 election on a ticket with Jeff Consolo, and resigned his post running the student discount program after the victory.

As president in 1975-76 Henry moved in November to change ASG's concert proposal after a money-losing fall season, while a student group urged support for his energy awareness plan. At year's end the Herald summed up his tenure with a piece on how the role had required balancing the twin jobs of student regent and ASG president, which he held at the same time.

A Wikipedia biography of Stephen L. Henry, later the 52nd Lieutenant Governor of Kentucky (1995-2003), describes an orthopedic surgeon and WKU alumnus who was elected Student Body President on his second attempt after an earlier loss, and who sat on the WKU Board of Regents as president - a career arc that lines up point for point with this record's own Herald sourcing, though no WKU archive document has yet been found that states the identification directly.

The organization

Everyone the record shows in office this year. Each name goes to their own page, with every office they held and everything the archive says they did.

The executive

Administrative Vice PresidentChristy Vogt

Vogt was ASG administrative vice president in 1975-76. She ran the book exchange ASG co-sponsored with Veterans on Campus from 12 to 26 January 1976, reporting that the two groups had covered expenses by 21 January on sales of over 600 books at a margin of 25 cents a book. She said the executive officers were closer than in any past administration she had witnessed.

1976 Talisman, ASG
TreasurerDavid Payne

Payne was ASG treasurer in 1975-76. Congress member Marc Levy brought charges against him for spending before budget approval had been given, and Levy appeared twice before the Judicial Council. Payne argued the constitution provided for such spending and the council ruled in his favour. Note for the editor: years.json carries a David Payne under 1982-83 as an unresolved name. These may or may not be the same person and this archive should not merge them without a source that links them.

1976 Talisman, ASG
SecretaryNancy Wilk

Wilk was ASG secretary in 1975-76 and kept the attendance roll the yearbook printed in full, effective 6 April 1976. She explained the rule: a warning after four absences and automatic removal from Congress after two more. The printed roll names some fifty Congress members and marks Mitchell Deep as resigned.

1976 Talisman, ASG
Activities Vice PresidentRick Kelley

Kelley was ASG activities vice president in 1975-76. He introduced a bill in October creating a student activities committee, whose members were chosen in December from open applications and which ran three subcommittees for concerts, lectures and special events and publicity. The volume credits the year's concerts with turning a profit of around $4,300 for ASG. His surname is printed both Kelley and Kelly in the same volume; the spelling is not settled.

1976 Talisman, ASG

The senate

Chairman, Judicial CouncilGerard Faulk

Six congressmen moved on 16 March 1976 to impeach Faulk over his handling of two cases sent to the Judicial Council, one on Tom Blair's allegation that President Steve Henry had obstructed an advisory intramurals committee and one on Marc Levy's misuse-of-funds charge against treasurer David Payne. Congress acquitted him on 6 April 1976. The Herald reported his was a two-year term that would run into the 1976-77 session.

Herald 51:53, 9 Apr 1976
Chairman, Student Discount ServiceJane Anne Coverdale

ASG's own Student Insight supplement credited Coverdale and her committee with contracting 29 local discounts by the end of December 1975; the 1976 discount cards were then mailed to all Western students.

Student Insight, supplement to the Herald, 27 Apr 1976
Co-chair, ASG Housing CommitteeSusan Hurley

Hurley co-chaired the housing committee that visited the campus laundry, surveyed students and drafted the laundry resolution Congress passed and sent to the university committee President Dero Downing had appointed.

Student Insight, supplement to the Herald, 27 Apr 1976
Co-chair, ASG Housing CommitteeScott Taylor

Taylor co-chaired the housing committee with Susan Hurley on the laundry resolution. He is separately listed in the same supplement's Congress roster as a representative-at-large.

Student Insight, supplement to the Herald, 27 Apr 1976

The year, in order37

26 August 1975Other
#

Regents add $16,000 to the entertainment budget and buy a second free concert

The Board of Regents passed a $16,000 increase in the ASG entertainment budget in August 1975. President Steve Henry said the increase allowed a second free concert and that he had never known one year to have two free concerts, much less two in a semester.

$16,000 increase in the ASG entertainment budget

College Heights Herald 51:1, 26 Aug 1975 PDF

September 1975Concert
#

Gove, Alex Harvey and Bottom and Company welcome students back

Two free concerts opened the fall semester to welcome students back, featuring Gove, Alex Harvey and Bottom and Company at the fine arts centre outdoor theatre. Harvey picked a dulcimer with a feather during his set.

1976 Talisman, Concerts

9 September 1975Lecture
#

Charles Berlitz opens the ASG lecture series on the Bermuda Triangle

Charles Berlitz, author of The Bermuda Triangle, opened the Associated Student Government lecture series in Van Meter Auditorium on 9 September 1975. The Herald carried the advance notice that morning; the 1976 Talisman reported a standing-room-only audience and recorded that Berlitz said more than 100 planes and ships and 1,000 people had vanished in the region since 1945, and that he ran through theories from heavy magnetisation to alien beings.

College Heights Herald 51:4, 9 Sep 1975 PDF1976 Talisman, p. 52

11 September 1975Concert
#

ASG sponsors LAMB, a messianic music duo

On 11 September 1975 ASG sponsored LAMB, a duo specialising in messianic music, in Van Meter Auditorium. The Talisman noted the concert was well attended, perhaps because many students had writing assignments for MC 272 classes that required it.

1976 Talisman, Concerts

17 September 1975Concert
#

Loggins and Messina open the season and lose $7,000

Kenny Loggins and Jim Messina played the first major concert of the year in Diddle Arena on Wednesday 17 September 1975, with the Amazing Rhythm Aces also on the bill. The crowd of 4,300 was too small and ASG lost about $7,000; activities vice president Rick Kelley blamed a lack of student body support.

lost about $7,000; attendance 4,300

19 September 1975
#

Big concert, big loss

An ASG concert drew 4,300 people but lost $7,000, the Herald reported, opening a money argument that ran through the fall.

Herald 51:7, 19 Sep 1975 PDF

23 September 1975Lecture
#

Jules Bergman speaks on '2000 A.D. - The Way it Will Be'

ABC science editor Jules Bergman came to campus on 23 September 1975 for the first guest of the University Lecture Series, which the Talisman notes was also sponsored by ASG. Poor weather hurt the crowd; Bergman said man was adaptable enough to fly to the moon six times but could not endure much longer what he had created for himself on earth.

30 October 1975Concert
#

Doug Kershaw takes three encores in Homecoming week

ASG brought the Cajun fiddler Doug Kershaw for the Thursday night of Homecoming week, 30 October 1975. About 700 people saw him jump into the crowd to lead 'Jambalaya' and land in the lap of a startled coed in the fourth row; he took three encores and closed with 'Louisiana Man'.

1976 Talisman, Concerts

31 October 1975Lecture
#

Pat Paulsen returns for a free 'Lecture in Humor'

Pat Paulsen entertained a Diddle Arena crowd on Homecoming eve, 31 October 1975, at a free show sponsored by ASG. Presenting 'A Lecture in Humor', he traced the history of comedy from prehistoric times to the future and said he had found out you cannot speak your mind and expect to get elected.

1976 Talisman, Lectures

November 1975Concert
#

Linda Ronstadt's Homecoming concert nets ASG $4,300

Linda Ronstadt played the Homecoming concert in Diddle Arena on Saturday 1 November 1975, opening with "Colorado", with Goose Creek Symphony before her and Andrew Gold as her backup group. The 1976 Talisman put the crowd at about 6,500 and the profit to ASG at $4,300, and reported that activities vice-president Rick Kelley and ASG president Steve Henry rated it among the most successful concerts in years - successful enough, the yearbook said, to guarantee a free Spinners and Wet Willie concert for students on 30 January.

netted $4,300 for ASG; attendance about 6,500, though the same yearbook's opening section gives 6,300

25 November 1975
#

A budget remedy and a book exchange

The Herald weighed whether a new ASG budget offered a remedy, and reported a spring semester book exchange to be co-sponsored by ASG and Veterans on Campus. President Steve Henry moved to change the concert proposal the same week.

2 December 1975Other
#

ASG creates a student activities committee for concerts, lectures and publicity

To settle the dispute over booking, ASG adopted a Student Activities Committee, put into operation on 2 December 1975 and chaired by Rick Kelley. From 63 applicants, 17 members were chosen across three subcommittees, seven for concerts, five for lectures and five for publicity, each with alternates.

10 December 1975Concert

ASG sponsored Natalie Cole in Van Meter

The Herald reported on 9 December 1975 that Associated Student Government would sponsor a concert by the jazz vocalist Natalie Cole. The 1976 Talisman records that Cole, daughter of the late Nat King Cole, performed in Van Meter Auditorium on 10 December to an estimated crowd of 325, singing 'This Will Be', 'Killing Me Softly With His Song', 'Honky Tonk Women' and 'You Are the Sunshine of My Life'.

1976Other
#

ASG sets up a Student Activities Committee to choose the acts

After a semester of argument over who should decide what came to campus, ASG adopted a Student Activities Committee to bring more student opinion into the booking and publicising of concerts and lecturers. Seventeen members were chosen from 63 applicants and divided into concert, lecture and publicity sub-committees, with alternates for each. The dispute behind it had begun when an assistant dean of student affairs argued that any programme the university presented ought to have educational value; students wrote to the Herald against him.

1976 Talisman, Concerts and student activities

12 January 1976Service
#

ASG and Veterans on Campus run a book exchange against the bookstore

To give students an alternative to the College Heights Bookstore, ASG co-sponsored a book exchange with Veterans on Campus from 12 to 26 January 1976. Administrative vice president Christy Vogt said the two groups had covered expenses by 21 January through the sale of over 600 books, with ASG and VOC making 25 cents on each book sold.

over 600 books sold; 25 cents profit per book

1976 Talisman, ASG

26 January 1976Service
#

A second campus check-cashing facility opens after an ASG survey

An ASG survey at fall registration found that the most significant desire of students was a new check-cashing facility. A second campus check-cashing point opened in the customer service area of the bookstore on 26 January 1976 for nine hours a week, after the original proposal for a full-service campus bank branch was judged impractical by area bankers.

1976 Talisman, ASG

30 January 1976Concert
#

The Spinners and Wet Willie drew 7,500 to a free concert

The Spinners and Wet Willie played free to students on 30 January 1976 before a crowd of 7,500, the second straight major ASG promotion to make money. It was the first concert the new Student Activities Committee helped bring about. The Spinners worked impressions of the Ink Spots, the Mills Brothers, Tom Jones, the Miracles and Elvis Presley into the set, donned wigs for the Supremes, and came back for two encores.

free to students; crowd of 7,500

6 February 1976Lecture
#

George Wallace is fought over in committee and never appears

President Steve Henry reported a 99 per cent chance of getting George Wallace, then seeking the Democratic presidential nomination, to speak in April 1976. A subcommittee of ASG's Student Activities Committee voted 4-2 with one abstention on 6 February to strike Wallace from the list of lecturers on the grounds that the arrangements had been made improperly, ASG Congress overruled the resolution, and by 13 February the Herald reported the lecture was uncertain; no appearance is recorded.

College Heights Herald 51:38, 6 Feb 1976 PDF

12 February 1976Lecture
#

George Allen lectures on success and ambition

George Allen, head coach of the Washington Redskins, spoke in the Garrett Conference Center on 12 February 1976 in one of the spring ASG-sponsored lectures. He showed a film, answered questions on professional football and advised those facing a tight job market that the last thing to talk about when looking for a job is money.

College Heights Herald 51:40, 13 Feb 1976 PDF

24 February 1976Lecture
#

Chris Miller of National Lampoon draws 1,100 and a rebuke from the dean

Chris Miller of National Lampoon drew about 1,100 people to the Garrett Conference Center on 24 February 1976 with a lecture titled 'Toe Sucking in Albania, or An Evening of Erotic Laughter'. Three days later the Herald reported that dean of student affairs Charles Keown had criticised ASG's taste in sponsoring the lecture.

attendance about 1,100

26 March 1976
#

Faulk impeachment hearings open as campaign begins

The Associated Student Government began impeachment hearings against congress member Gerard Faulk the same week candidates for the spring election, including Christy Vogt, were set to speak to students. An editorial the same issue held up Associated Student Government's troubles as a cautionary example for the university's newly forming Faculty Senate.

Herald 51:49, 26 Mar 1976 PDF

The Herald reported the Associated Student Government presidential primary under the headline "Bruce Smith, Christy Vogt Survive as 527 Cast Ballots," the two advancing to the 9 April runoff that Vogt went on to win.

The Herald reported Christy Vogt's win in the Associated Student Government presidential election under the headline "Christy Vogt Wins Election," with Vogt's own "Winner Says Thanks" running in the same issue. 15 April 1976Service
#

Laundry improvements take effect

From 15 April 1976 the university installed more washers and dryers, an air-conditioned study area and a laundry pick-up service, with dry cleaning by a local firm in the proposal. Steve Henry said he hoped the facilities, which ASG had proposed the previous October, would be long-lasting.

1976 Talisman, ASG

23 April 1976Concert
#

James Taylor plays a free concert to close the year

The Herald of 20 April 1976 billed James Taylor for a free concert that Friday, and the issue of 23 April told readers the performance would be that night. The next issue, 27 April, carried Tip Shanklin's review under the headline "Evening with James Taylor Well Spent", so the concert took place. The 1976 Talisman listed James Taylor among the year's concerts but, before the event, recorded only that he was scheduled to give a free concert in April.

27 April 1976
#

Herald supplement recaps a year of ASG wins

A special supplement to the Herald, paid for and written by the Associated Student Government, summed up the outgoing Henry administration's year: women's hours abolished with ASG's backing, better intramural facilities credited to ASG resolutions, a new ASG discount card program, a second campus site for cashing checks, and a Veterans on Campus book exchange. The supplement also printed the year's roster of congress members and its bills and resolutions, and carried Christy Vogt's own piece on laundry facility improvement.

Student Insight (Herald supplement), 27 Apr 1976 PDF

27 April 1976Service
#

ASG's own supplement counts the discount cards and the book exchange

In the Student Insight supplement it paid for and wrote itself, ASG set out its services for the year: the discount cards it sponsored, the book exchange run with Veterans on Campus which it called a success, and its work on laundry facilities and intramural facilities. Christy Vogt wrote the piece on laundry improvements and Elaine Boeckman the piece on the abolition of women's hours.

Student Insight, 27 Apr 1976 PDF

30 April 1976
#

Final meeting approves a budget

The congress approved a tentative budget at the final ASG meeting of the year, and the Herald assessed how Steve Henry's year had required balancing his twin jobs as student regent and ASG president.

What this is

A year-by-year record of student government at Western Kentucky University, from the ratification of the Associated Students constitution in April 1966 to the present. Every entry names the source it came from.

Corrections

The names here start with the plaque in the SGA Chambers, and the plaque is known to be wrong in places. Where the archive disagrees with it, this site follows the archive and records the change on the corrections page. If you served, or you know that a year here is wrong, the project wants to hear from you through the Student Government Association office.

What it rests on

Everything here comes from what is publicly available online: the digitised Herald and SGA's own papers on TopSCHOLAR, the Talisman yearbooks, wkuherald.com, the university's news pages and the Wayback Machine. That is a fraction of what happened. Student government has met most weeks for sixty years, and the greater part of it was never written down, or was written down on paper that has never been scanned. A thin year here is a thin record, not a quiet one.
