Reed Morgan role unresolved, 1968-69

Single-year plate. Sits between Menser and Straeffer, whose election the Herald reported on 9 May 1968 - so Morgan likely finished a partial term or held the seat between elections. The only Reed Morgan found in the archive is a different, earlier role: chair of the organizing committee that received President Kelly Thompson's approval letter for the Associated Students constitution, 1 April 1966 - two years before this plaque entry. No Herald or WKU Timeline coverage connects a Reed Morgan to a 1968-69 SGA office. Left unresolved rather than moved, since the 1966 role (committee chair, pre-election) is not the same office the plaque implies. Re-checked 4 Aug 2026: fresh TopSCHOLAR searches (name plus SGA/regent/student-government terms) turned up only the same 1963-67 WKU Forensics team roster (M. Reed Morgan, debater) and the 1966 constitution-committee material already cited. Nothing new for 1968-69. The portrait on this page was published in the Herald of 7 April 1966, when Morgan was a graduating senior chairing the committee that drafted the constitution; it predates the plaque's 1968 placement by two years, and no source describes him as president.

Reed Morgan chaired the student committee that drew up plans for a campus government in 1965-66. In March 1966, answering critics of the project's pace, Morgan replied in the Herald that the committee was not dragging its feet.

A month before students ratified the constitution, the Herald profiled Morgan as a senior who tripled as Greek editor, debater and government chairman, crediting him as a principal architect of the Associated Students plan. The digitized Herald index shows no student government coverage of Morgan in 1968-69 itself.

Bill Straeffer president, 1968-69

Office
President
Term recorded here
1968-69
Standing of the record
Confirmed in the sources below.

Election win reported in the Herald, 9 May 1968, in the same issue as an editorial headed 'Elections Illustrate Faults, Indifference'. The internal election-results memo (mirrored below) gives his exact margin: 1,732 votes to Charles Whitley's 1,098.

Straeffer ran for the Associated Students presidency against Charles Whitley in the 2 May 1968 election, the organization's second contested presidential race. He won 1,732 votes to Whitley's 1,098. Terry Gilpin was elected vice president in the same election, beating Bob Van Corbach 1,516 to 1,206. Total turnout was 2,894 votes, about 34 percent of the roughly 8,500-student enrollment - a turnout the Herald's coverage of the result treated as a persistent problem, running an editorial in the same issue headed 'Elections Illustrate Faults, Indifference'.

A year later, the Herald's 8 May 1969 issue carried Straeffer and Gilpin's own review of their year in office, headlined 'Associated Students Produces Year of Advancement,' alongside a companion piece, 'Bill Straeffer, Terry Gilpin Review Their Year with Associated Students.' The archive does not yet hold the article text itself, only its headline and byline (Ellen Bennett), so the specifics of what the administration counted as advancement are not yet confirmed.

Bill Straeffer vied with Charles Whitley for the Associated Student Government presidency in the spring 1968 election, the Herald carrying both hopefuls' ideas before the balloting, in a campaign that also saw Terry Gilpin and Bob Van Corbach contest the vice presidency.

Straeffer won the presidency in the May 1968 vote, and in the same issue the Herald reported the new president making plans for 1968-69.

Closing his term in May 1969, Straeffer and vice president Terry Gilpin reviewed their year with the Associated Students for the Herald, which credited the administration with a year of advancement.

Paul Gerard, III student regent, 1968-69

1969-70

Two-year plate reading 1968-70, immediately after William Menser took the first student regent seat in April 1968. Almost certainly the second student regent. Early student regents were non-voting 'associate regents', which may explain the longer term. Not yet confirmed as regent. A Paul Gerard did appear on the same 2 May 1968 ballot that elected Straeffer, winning the Junior Class presidency 461 votes to Mike Collins' 277 - circumstantially consistent (a junior in spring 1968 would be positioned for a campus-wide office the next year) but not proof of identity or of the regent seat specifically. Two people can share a name; this is flagged, not merged. Resolved: the 1969 Talisman captions him "Paul Gerard, the Student Regent" and the Herald of 27 February 1969 explains the mechanism - Kentucky's 1968 law required a student regent at every state campus, and because Associated Students president Bill Straeffer was an out-of-state student, the Congress of Associated Students elected Gerard, a Bowling Green junior and junior class president, to the seat instead. He sat as a non-voting member. Contemporary sources print his name without the suffix the plaque carries.

Paul Gerard first appeared in the Herald's campus politics coverage in April 1968, when the paper reported him battling Mike Collins for a senior class post in that week's election.

By February 1969 Gerard held the student seat on the Board of Regents, and Herald writer Bruce Tucker profiled him as the student regent. He followed William Menser, who in April 1968 had become the first student to sit with the board.

The year, in order10

29 April 1968
#

Straeffer and Whitley vie for the presidency

The Herald's pre-election coverage set out the field: Bill Straeffer against Charles Whitley for president, Terry Gilpin against Bob Van Corbach for vice president, and contested races down to secretary, treasurer, and congressman at large.

Herald 47:28, 29 Apr 1968

2 May 1968
#

Straeffer elected president, 1,732 to 1,098; turnout 34 percent

The Associated Students' internal election-results memo lists the full slate: Bill Straeffer over Charles Whitley for president, Terry Gilpin over Bob Van Corbach for vice president, Becky Cooper over Mary Miller for secretary, Tonii Rizzo over John Combs for treasurer, and John Cobelli and Janie Heathcoat elected congressmen at large over Billy Goodman and Scott Phillips. Total turnout was 2,894 votes, about 34 percent of enrollment.

What this is

A year-by-year record of student government at Western Kentucky University, from the ratification of the Associated Students constitution in April 1966 to the present. Every entry names the source it came from.

Corrections

The names here start with the plaque in the SGA Chambers, and the plaque is known to be wrong in places. Where the archive disagrees with it, this site follows the archive and records the change on the corrections page. If you served, or you know that a year here is wrong, the project wants to hear from you through the Student Government Association office.

Reuse

Text on this site is the work of the project. Photographs, documents and legislation are reproduced from Western Kentucky University's own open archives and from the College Heights Herald and the Talisman; each item names the collection, issue and page it came from and links to the original.
