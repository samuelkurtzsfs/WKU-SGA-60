Reed Morgan role unresolved, 1968-69

On the plaque
1968
Standing of the record
Confirmed in the sources below.

Single-year plate reading 1968, sitting between William Menser and Bill Straeffer. He was not the student body president, and he did not hold the student regent seat. The plate almost certainly honours the man who chaired the committee that wrote the constitution. The case rests on two things the archive can now show positively, rather than on his absence from the record. First, both offices in 1968-69 are occupied by other people, each confirmed from a contemporaneous source. Straeffer won the presidency on 2 May 1968 by 1,732 votes to Charles Whitley's 1,098, a margin recorded in the election-results memo Ron Beck wrote the next day and reported in the Herald of 9 May. Paul Gerard III held the regent seat: the Herald of 27 February 1969 carries Bruce Tucker's profile of him as student regent, inside the academic year in question. There is no vacancy for a third officeholder. Second, he had already graduated. Every appearance of the name in the digitised Herald falls in the spring of 1966, and the issue of 7 April 1966 calls him a senior in as many words: "Senior Triples as Greek Editor, Debater, Government Chairman." A senior in April 1966 left in June. The suggestion that he returned as a graduate student and served in 1968 no longer needs disproving, because the offices he might have returned to were filled. Three later checks agree. The rosters the President's Office kept of the first Congress elected in May 1966 and of the Congress of 1967-68 name every officer and representative, and he is on neither. SGA's own roster of former presidents, archived in September 2001, numbers the office continuously through this period, Haynes first, Menser second, Straeffer third, Zielke fourth, with no gap. The unfiltered index of the digitised collection carries the name once, in 1966, and for 1968-69 that index is close to complete: only nine of the lines covering those two years hit the 300-character truncation cap, so the silence there is real silence and not an artefact. What the plate does record is the founding. Kelly Thompson's letter of 1 April 1966 approving the constitution is addressed to him as chair of the organising committee, with John Lovett as vice chair. His committee wrote the document the students ratified on 26 April 1966 by 1,812 to 726; Jim Haynes was elected to lead the government it created. Why the plate reads 1968 rather than 1966 is not established, and the archive does not guess at it. He is kept here, on the year his plate names, marked as not having held the office, and he is not counted in the line of presidents.

Reed Morgan chaired the student committee that drew up plans for a campus government in 1965-66. In March 1966, answering critics of the project's pace, Morgan replied in the Herald that the committee was not dragging its feet.

A month before students ratified the constitution, the Herald profiled Morgan as a senior who tripled as Greek editor, debater and government chairman, crediting him as a principal architect of the Associated Students plan. The digitized Herald index shows no student government coverage of Morgan in 1968-69 itself.

Election win reported in the Herald, 9 May 1968, in the same issue as an editorial headed 'Elections Illustrate Faults, Indifference'. The internal election-results memo (mirrored below) gives his exact margin: 1,732 votes to Charles Whitley's 1,098.

Straeffer ran for the Associated Students presidency against Charles Whitley in the 2 May 1968 election, the organization's second contested presidential race. He won 1,732 votes to Whitley's 1,098. Terry Gilpin was elected vice president in the same election, beating Bob Van Corbach 1,516 to 1,206. Total turnout was 2,894 votes, about 34 percent of the roughly 8,500-student enrollment - a turnout the Herald's coverage of the result treated as a persistent problem, running an editorial in the same issue headed 'Elections Illustrate Faults, Indifference'.

A year later, the Herald's 8 May 1969 issue carried Straeffer and Gilpin's own review of their year in office, headlined 'Associated Students Produces Year of Advancement,' alongside a companion piece, 'Bill Straeffer, Terry Gilpin Review Their Year with Associated Students.' The archive does not yet hold the article text itself, only its headline and byline (Ellen Bennett), so the specifics of what the administration counted as advancement are not yet confirmed.

Bill Straeffer vied with Charles Whitley for the Associated Student Government presidency in the spring 1968 election, the Herald carrying both hopefuls' ideas before the balloting, in a campaign that also saw Terry Gilpin and Bob Van Corbach contest the vice presidency.

Straeffer won the presidency in the May 1968 vote, and in the same issue the Herald reported the new president making plans for 1968-69.

Closing his term in May 1969, Straeffer and vice president Terry Gilpin reviewed their year with the Associated Students for the Herald, which credited the administration with a year of advancement.

1969-70

Two-year plate reading 1968-70, immediately after William Menser took the first student regent seat in April 1968. Almost certainly the second student regent. Early student regents were non-voting 'associate regents', which may explain the longer term. Confirmed as regent by the Herald of 27 February 1969, which carries Bruce Tucker's profile of him as student regent. A Paul Gerard also appeared on the 2 May 1968 ballot that elected Straeffer, winning the Junior Class presidency 461 votes to Mike Collins' 277 - circumstantially consistent (a junior in spring 1968 would be positioned for a campus-wide office the next year) but not proof of identity or of the regent seat specifically. Two people can share a name; this is flagged, not merged. Resolved: the 1969 Talisman captions him "Paul Gerard, the Student Regent" and the Herald of 27 February 1969 explains the mechanism - Kentucky's 1968 law required a student regent at every state campus, and because Associated Students president Bill Straeffer was an out-of-state student, the Congress of Associated Students elected Gerard, a Bowling Green junior and junior class president, to the seat instead. He sat as a non-voting member. Contemporary sources print his name without the suffix the plaque carries.

Paul Gerard first appeared in the Herald's campus politics coverage in April 1968, when the paper reported him battling Mike Collins for a senior class post in that week's election. A Paul Gerard of Bowling Green appears on the 1967-68 Associated Students committee roster, appointed to the Rules and Elections Committee that fall; nothing on that roster confirms the two are the same person, but the hometown and the timing, a year before this campaign, are circumstantially consistent.

By February 1969 Gerard held the student seat on the Board of Regents, and Herald writer Bruce Tucker profiled him as the student regent. He followed William Menser, who in April 1968 had become the first student to sit with the board.

29 April 1968
#

Straeffer and Whitley vie for the presidency

The Herald's pre-election coverage set out the field: Bill Straeffer against Charles Whitley for president, Terry Gilpin against Bob Van Corbach for vice president, and contested races down to secretary, treasurer, and congressman at large.

Herald 47:28, 29 Apr 1968

2 May 1968
#

Straeffer elected president, 1,732 to 1,098; turnout 34 percent

The Associated Students' internal election-results memo lists the full slate: Bill Straeffer over Charles Whitley for president, Terry Gilpin over Bob Van Corbach for vice president, Becky Cooper over Mary Miller for secretary, Tonii Rizzo over John Combs for treasurer, and John Cobelli and Janie Heathcoat elected congressmen at large over Billy Goodman and Scott Phillips. Total turnout was 2,894 votes, about 34 percent of enrollment.

#

A constitutional amendment on treasurer qualifications reaches the spring ballot

The spring 1968 Associated Students election ballot carried, alongside the cheerleader vote and a student fee question, a constitutional amendment to revise the qualifications for treasurer. The results memo of 3 May 1968 reports the officer races only, so the archive does not record how the amendment fared.

What this is

A year-by-year record of student government at Western Kentucky University, from the ratification of the Associated Students constitution in April 1966 to the present. Every entry names the source it came from.

Corrections

The names here start with the plaque in the SGA Chambers, and the plaque is known to be wrong in places. Where the archive disagrees with it, this site follows the archive and records the change on the corrections page. If you served, or you know that a year here is wrong, the project wants to hear from you: people who held an office can be given the keys to their own year on the contribute page.

What it rests on

Everything here comes from what is publicly available online: the digitised Herald and SGA's own papers on TopSCHOLAR, the Talisman yearbooks, wkuherald.com, the university's news pages and the Wayback Machine. That is a fraction of what happened. Student government has met most weeks for sixty years, and the greater part of it was never written down, or was written down on paper that has never been scanned. A thin year here is a thin record, not a quiet one.

Text on this site is the work of the project. Photographs, documents and legislation are reproduced from Western Kentucky University's own open archives and from the College Heights Herald and the Talisman; each item names the collection, issue and page it came from and links to the original.
