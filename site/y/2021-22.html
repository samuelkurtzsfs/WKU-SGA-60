Matthew Wininger — the term

Wininger ran for student body president in the April 2021 SGA election, with voting held on TopNet April 12-13. In the Herald's ballot guide he said he wanted to make SGA "the most active and vibrant organization on campus." Me'Lon Craighead, who would serve as his executive vice president, ran for that office in the same election.

As president, Wininger opened the fall semester by welcoming new and returning senators to the second meeting of the 21st Senate. He presided over the unanimous confirmation of senators elected the previous semester and of his executive appointments, including Jamison Moorehead as IT director, Alexis Courtenay as public relations director and Parker Raybourne as chief of staff, and the senate adopted new election codes at the same meeting.

In spring 2022 Wininger welcomed senators back from winter break and announced an initiative to create a student resource handbook aimed particularly at underclassmen, encouraging all SGA committees to pitch in on the project. At that same first meeting of 2022, the senate he led responded to the December tornado disaster by unanimously passing a $1,000 bill to restock the Office of Sustainability food pantry.

What happened, in order

2021-08-06

Northeast Hall renamed for Margaret Munday

Student Regent Matthew Wininger was sworn in at the Board of Regents meeting that renamed Northeast Hall for WKU's first African American student to enrol - the first building on campus named after an African American.

WKU News ↗
2021-09-01

SGA opened the fall term with confirmations and new election codes

SGA held its first meeting of the fall semester, the second meeting of the 21st Senate, with President Matthew Wininger welcoming new and returning senators. All senators present were confirmed by unanimous consent and sworn in by Chief Justice Holden Schroeder, along with executive appointments Jamison Moorehead as IT director, Alexis Courtenay as public relations director and Parker Raybourne as chief of staff. New election codes for the next semester also passed with unanimous consent.

Herald, 1 Sep 2021 ↗
2021-09-10

Senate confirmed the year's budget

At the third meeting of the 21st Senate on Sept. 7, SGA passed Bill 1.21f, confirming its budget for the year. Dean of Students Martha Sales spoke on leadership, and Wininger announced a planned donor drive in partnership with at least four other student organizations. Chief of Staff Parker Raybourne announced a Hilltopper Hangout event for Sept. 24.

Herald, 10 Sep 2021 ↗
2021-09-15

New senators seated and freshman election results announced

The senate unanimously voted in four new senate members, two judicial council members and Trib Singh as director of academic and student affairs. Speaker of the Senate Tess Welch introduced committees on legislative operations, student experience, mental health, sustainability, campus improvement, diversity and student outreach. That night the fall election results named Addison Falkner, Sam Kurtz and Jenna Wells freshman senators and Arivumani Srivastava the Gatton Academy senator.

Herald, 15 Sep 2021 ↗
2021-10-06

21st Senate passed its first resolution, on advisor evaluations

At its Oct. 5 meeting SGA passed Resolution 1.21f, addressing the lack of an advisor evaluation system, by a 21-2 vote, the first resolution of the 21st Senate. Bill 2.21f, barring the speaker of the senate from voting on legislation, passed 21-2, and two bills scrubbing outdated 2007-era bylaws passed by unanimous consent. Parking director Jennifer Tougas discussed a possible monthly parking tag subscription and campus bookstore operations.

Herald, 6 Oct 2021 ↗
2021-11-09

ISEC's assistant director urged closer ties with SGA

Lamario Moore, assistant director of the Intercultural Student Engagement Center, told senators that improving the relationship between SGA and ISEC was pivotal and could be as simple as showing up. The senate passed Resolution 2.21f to give a gift basket to veterans at a Veterans Day wreath-laying, Bill 11.21f funding the Chinese club, and after a long debate, Bill 12.21f funding three $250 scholarships at an Advising and Career Development Center job fair.

Herald, 10 Nov 2021 ↗
2021-11-17

CEBS dean visited; predatory-loan resolution stalled on a 17-16 vote

CEBS Dean Corinne Murphy spoke to the senate about the college's programs and the student life cycle. Executive Vice President Me'Lon Craighead announced new senate committee nominations, two of them to the newly created restaurant group committee. Resolution 3.21f, opposing predatory student loans for first-year students and prompted by state Rep. Patti Minter's visit to SGA, split 17-16 and was pushed to the next meeting rather than passing. Bill 13.21f, funding a $350 stress-relief tabling event before finals, passed by a wide margin.

Herald, 18 Nov 2021 ↗
2021-12-01

Resolution condemning critical race theory bans passed 16-15

After weeks of debate, the senate passed Resolution 7.21f, which condemned the banning of critical race theory teachings in postsecondary education ahead of an anticipated bill in the Kentucky legislature. The vote was 16 in favor, 15 against, with two abstentions. Sponsor Olivia Blackmon said the measure was meant to ensure intellectual freedom in the curriculum.

Herald, 1 Dec 2021 ↗
2021-12-12campus

EF-3 tornado struck Bowling Green, killing 11 in Warren County

An EF-3 tornado with 150 mph winds slammed into Bowling Green early on Dec. 11, killing 11 people according to the coroner and destroying homes and businesses across Warren County. Gov. Andy Beshear signed a state of emergency and President Biden signed an emergency declaration. The disaster shaped SGA relief efforts the following semester.

Herald, 12 Dec 2021 ↗
2022-01-18

First 2022 meeting funded tornado-drained food pantry

At its first meeting of 2022, SGA passed Bill 1.22s by unanimous consent, providing $1,000 to restock the WKU Office of Sustainability food pantry, whose food and toiletries had been drained by the tornado recovery effort. Wininger announced an initiative to create a student resource handbook for underclassmen. Speaker Tess Welch announced spring election dates of April 18-19.

Herald, 18 Jan 2022 ↗
2022-02-01

Former state Rep. Debra Maggart spoke on lobbying; Faculty Awards Committee launched

Debra Maggart, a WKU alumna and Tennessee state representative from 2004 to 2012, told the senate that being part of SGA had inspired her political career and urged senators to get involved in issues they cared about. President Matthew Wininger nominated four new senators, all confirmed. Executive Vice President Me'Lon Craighead announced 10 nominations to a newly created Faculty Awards Committee meant to help students identify helpful professors. Bill 4.22s (removing the requirement of an author at LOC meetings) and Bill 5.22s (on when office hours may be collected) both passed by unanimous consent.

Herald, 2 Feb 2022 ↗
2022-02-16

Syllabi clause, crosswalk resolutions and Title IX meetings passed

At its Feb. 15 meeting SGA unanimously passed Resolution 6-22-S to add an SGA-related clause to course syllabi, which the Herald reported would be the first of its kind in SGA history. Three resolutions supported new crosswalks below the Van Meter green space and on Chestnut and Normal streets, and Bill 6-22-S required a committee chair or delegate to meet with the Counseling Center director and Title IX coordinator each semester. The senate also approved a task force for the iWKU app and funding for a financial success workshop.

Herald, 16 Feb 2022 ↗
2022-04-14

Judiciary Council cleared Bornefeld's campaign over student-all email

After several SGA members complained that presidential candidate Cole Bornefeld's student-wide email about the Swipe Out Hunger initiative violated the election code's ban on self-promotion, the Judiciary Council held a roughly hour-long Zoom hearing on April 13 and voted in favor of his campaign. Speaker Tess Welch argued the signed email constituted self-promotion, while a statement from President Wininger, read by Chief Justice Holden Schroeder, said nothing in the code required executive approval for a student-all email. Senators said the initiative saw a record number of donations after the email went out.

Herald, 14 Apr 2022 ↗
2022-04-20

Cole Bornefeld elected with 49% of 1,448 votes

Alexis Courtenay took 40% and Olivia Feck 11%. Sam Kurtz and Garrison Reed were elected to the two vice presidencies.

WKU News ↗
2022-04-20

End-of-year allocations funded scholarships, organizational aid and planetarium donation

Hours before the 2022 election results were announced, the senate passed a slate of funding bills: $8,000 in organizational aid at $500 per applicant, and $15,500 across five scholarship programs including summer class, study abroad, ISEC book and Scholar Level scholarships, plus $500 to Black Student Alliance scholarships for the first time. The senate also approved $325 for the Hardin Planetarium, $500 for a student worker appreciation event, $300 for the CEC Run/Walk for Autism and $570.02 for an e-waste recycling bin on main campus.
