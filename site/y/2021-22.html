Matthew Wininger president and student regent, 2021-22

Office
President and student regent
Term recorded here
2021-22
Place in the line
the 56th president and the 53rd student regent
Standing of the record
As recorded on the plaque. Not yet corroborated in the archive.

Sworn in as student regent 6 August 2021, at the meeting that renamed Northeast Hall for Margaret Munday, WKU's first African American student to enrol.

Wininger ran for student body president in the April 2021 SGA election, with voting held on TopNet April 12-13. In the Herald's ballot guide he said he wanted to make SGA "the most active and vibrant organization on campus." Me'Lon Craighead, who would serve as his executive vice president, ran for that office in the same election.

As president, Wininger opened the fall semester by welcoming new and returning senators to the second meeting of the 21st Senate. He presided over the unanimous confirmation of senators elected the previous semester and of his executive appointments, including Jamison Moorehead as IT director, Alexis Courtenay as public relations director and Parker Raybourne as chief of staff, and the senate adopted new election codes at the same meeting.

In spring 2022 Wininger welcomed senators back from winter break and announced an initiative to create a student resource handbook aimed particularly at underclassmen, encouraging all SGA committees to pitch in on the project. At that same first meeting of 2022, the senate he led responded to the December tornado disaster by unanimously passing a $1,000 bill to restock the Office of Sustainability food pantry.

The year, in order41

12 April 2021
#

Spring voting opened for the senate that would sit in 2021-22

SGA's spring election for the senate that would sit in 2021-22 opened on TopNet on 12 April 2021 and ran two days. The Herald published the ballot: candidates stood for senator-at-large, the class seats, the first-generation seat, the Gatton Academy seat and the college seats, with Avery Wells and Reed Breunig running for administrative vice president and Wells also standing for a senate seat. Candidate statements promised campus safety work across gender, age, sexual orientation and race, and an expanded SGA scholarship programme worked up with WKU Health Education and Promotion. The page has since been marked with the winners, showing Matthew Waninger elected student body president and Me'Lon Craighead executive vice president.

Herald, 12 Apr 2021 - "SGA voting opens today: Who is on the ballot"

#

Northeast Hall renamed for Margaret Munday

Student Regent Matthew Wininger was sworn in at the Board of Regents meeting that renamed Northeast Hall for WKU's first African American student to enrol - the first building on campus named after an African American.

September 2021
#

SGA opened the fall term with confirmations and new election codes

SGA held its first meeting of the fall semester, the second meeting of the 21st Senate, with President Matthew Wininger welcoming new and returning senators. All senators present were confirmed by unanimous consent and sworn in by Chief Justice Holden Schroeder, along with executive appointments Jamison Moorehead as IT director, Alexis Courtenay as public relations director and Parker Raybourne as chief of staff. New election codes for the next semester also passed with unanimous consent.

Herald, 1 Sep 2021

10 September 2021
#

Senate confirmed the year's budget in Bill 1.21f

At the third meeting of the 21st Senate, on 7 September, SGA passed Bill 1.21f, confirming its budget for the year. Dean of Students Martha Sales opened the meeting on leadership, telling senators they had to learn how to lead and pressing them on their purpose as a student body. President Matthew Wininger announced a planned donor drive in partnership with at least four other student organizations, and Chief of Staff Parker Raybourne announced a Hilltopper Hangout event for 24 September.

SGA takes up residence hall mold and DSU food lines

Council ruled the constitution can be amended by ordinary bill

After debate on whether constitutional change required the special amendment committee, the council ruled that the constitution could be amended through bills, which students could write, and that the special committee was reserved for larger amendments. December 2021
#

Resolution condemning critical race theory bans passed 16-15

After weeks of debate and several speeches of affirmation and negation, the senate passed Resolution 7.21f, condemning the banning of critical race theory teaching in postsecondary education. The vote was 16 in favor and 15 against, with two abstentions. Sponsors cited an anticipated bill request in the Kentucky General Assembly, and sponsor Olivia Blackmon said the measure was meant to ensure intellectual freedom in the curriculum rather than politicize classrooms.

Herald, 1 Dec 2021

3 December 2021
#

Council held the constitution supreme over the bylaws on the executive veto

Ruling on a discrepancy between the constitution and the bylaws over which members of the executive cabinet could vote on a veto, the council held that supremacy went to the constitution and that the bylaws had to be amended to match. It took no action on votes already cast but required proper procedure in future. At the same meeting it set a standard of three unexcused committee-meeting absences for senators, effective the following semester, with no action against senators already over the line.

Judicial Council minutes, 3 Dec 2021

17 February 2022
#

SGA approves a meal swipe donation program against food insecurity

SGA approved a program letting students donate excess meal swipes in the 4th, 8th and 12th weeks of each semester, with the WKU Restaurant Group matching one swipe for every four donated. 8 March 2022
#

Judicial Council gets its first written procedural rules

Bill 17-22-S, first read 1 March 2022 and given a second reading on 8 March, supported adoption of the General Procedural Rules of the Judicial Council. The bill stated that the council had previously had no formal written established guidelines or procedures pertaining to its functions and duties. The rules that followed rank as equal to the Legislative Bylaws, set out the council's attendance, censure and judicial review procedure, and provide for the Senate to override a Judicial Council veto by two-thirds of those present and voting.

SGA Bill 17-22-S; WKU SGA General Procedural Rules of the Judicial Council

22 March 2022
#

The seventh standing committee is created

Bill 21-22-S, first read 8 March and given a second reading on 22 March 2022, amended the constitution to establish a Community Relations Committee to advocate for students' needs in Bowling Green, encourage volunteer service and coordinate a yearly voter drive; the bill stated the committee was not meant for pushing a political agenda. Its authors were senators Margaret Glaser, Emily Bunning and Cole Bornefeld, sponsored by the Outreach and Student Experience and Legislative Operations committees. The current constitution still lists the Community Relations Committee last among seven standing committees.

Constitution amended on mental health committee; food insecurity clause added to syllabi

Bill 22-22-S, amending the SGA constitution to redefine the duties of the Student Mental Health and Wellbeing Committee, passed unanimously. Resolution 10-22-S, adding a food insecurity clause to course syllabi pointing students to the meal swipe request form and the Office of Sustainability, also passed unanimously. Bill 36-22-S funding an Easter egg hunt benefiting the Kentucky Veterans Brigade was passed with the amount amended upward from $500 to $750.

12 April 2022Festival
#

Earth Day tie-dye returned after two years without a celebration

Bill 41-22-S allocated $125 from legislative discretionary funds for the Campus Improvements and Sustainability Committee's Earth Day tie-dye tabling event at Centennial Mall on 22 April 2022, from 10 a.m. to 2 p.m. The bill noted that COVID-19 had left WKU without a proper Earth Day celebration for two years, and that the year's theme of sustainable fashion made recycled tie-dye a fitting activity.

12 April 2022Other
#

Hilltopper Hunt raised money for a veterans' food charity

Bill 36-22-S allocated $500 from legislative discretionary funds to Hilltopper Hunt: Egging out Hunger, a donation drive and fundraiser for the Kentucky Veterans Brigade, a charity assisting south central Kentucky veterans facing hunger, homelessness and financial strain. The brigade had opened its doors to the whole Bowling Green community after the December 2021 tornado outbreak. The event doubled as the senior capstone project of four WKU public relations majors.

14 April 2022
#

Judiciary Council cleared Bornefeld's campaign over student-all email

After several SGA members complained that presidential candidate Cole Bornefeld's student-wide email about the Swipe Out Hunger initiative violated the election code's ban on self-promotion (section 3.5), the Judiciary Council held a roughly hour-long Zoom hearing on April 13 and voted in favor of his campaign. Speaker Tess Welch argued the signed email constituted self-promotion, and Administrative Vice President Reed Breunig said the email was not cleared by any member of SGA. A statement from President Wininger, read by Chief Justice Holden Schroeder, said nothing in the code required executive cabinet or PR approval for a student-all email. Senators said the initiative saw a record number of donations after the email went out.

20 April 2022
#

Students fill the 2022-23 class, at-large and college seats

The same April ballot filled the incoming Senate's class, college and at-large seats. Olivia Feck, Matthew Gadd and James Cecil won the senior class seats; Mallory Hardesty, Aniya Johnson and Preston Romanov the junior class seats; and Ethan Taylor, Reed Hensley and Jakob Briggs the sophomore class seats. Ten students were elected senators-at-large, including Antonina Clementi and Ethan Huffaker, and Elizabeth DeLozier won the College of Health and Human Services seat, one of five college seats decided that day.

Herald, 20 Apr 2022

20 April 2022
#

End-of-year allocations funded scholarships, organizational aid and planetarium donation

Hours before the 2022 election results were announced, the senate passed a slate of funding bills: Bill 45-22-S split $15,500 across five scholarship programs unanimously, including summer class, study abroad, ISEC book and Scholar Level scholarships, plus $500 to Black Student Alliance scholarships for the first time; and Bill 42-22-S granted $500 to each organization that applied for fall organizational aid, totaling $8,000. The senate also cut a requested $1,000 Hardin Planetarium donation to $325, a $500 autism walk request to $300, and approved $500 for a student worker appreciation event and $570.02 for an e-waste recycling bin on main campus.

Herald, 20 Apr 2022

25 April 2022Programme
#

SGA's first Mental Health Awareness Week

SGA ran a mental health awareness week from 25 to 29 April 2022, ahead of Mental Health Awareness Month in May, with an event each day. The week opened with tabling at Centennial Mall from 10:30 a.m. to 2 p.m., with raffled gift cards, music and counselling information, and continued with a Zumba class in the Preston Center on 26 April run with GroupX Fitness. Student Mental Health and Wellbeing Committee chair Alex Cissell said the point was to reduce the stigma around mental illness.

What this is

A year-by-year record of student government at Western Kentucky University, from the ratification of the Associated Students constitution in April 1966 to the present. Every entry names the source it came from.

Corrections

The names here start with the plaque in the SGA Chambers, and the plaque is known to be wrong in places. Where the archive disagrees with it, this site follows the archive and records the change on the corrections page. If you served, or you know that a year here is wrong, the project wants to hear from you: people who held an office can be given the keys to their own year on the contribute page.

What it rests on

Everything here comes from what is publicly available online: the digitised Herald and SGA's own papers on TopSCHOLAR, the Talisman yearbooks, wkuherald.com, the university's news pages and the Wayback Machine. That is a fraction of what happened. Student government has met most weeks for sixty years, and the greater part of it was never written down, or was written down on paper that has never been scanned. A thin year here is a thin record, not a quiet one.

Reuse

Text on this site is the work of the project. Photographs, documents and legislation are reproduced from Western Kentucky University's own open archives and from the College Heights Herald and the Talisman; each item names the collection, issue and page it came from and links to the original.
