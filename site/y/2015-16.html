Won amid anonymous complaints that he had breached election codes on poster placement. The Judicial Council warned him but confirmed the result stood.

Richey opened his first term in fall 2015 "leaving behind the Postergate controversy" of the spring election, saying he had "about a hundred things" he wanted to do. His priorities included MyCampusToo, a campaign initiative to bring underrepresented students into SGA, more outreach to international students and the regional campuses, and stronger communication between the executive cabinet and senate. He also oversaw a student competition to name the new pub coming to the Garrett Conference Center, a product of the alcohol-policy change SGA had supported the previous year.

In September he championed the senate's resolution disapproving of how the Confucius Institute building was approved, writing in a letter to the Herald that the contract was signed in China before the Board of Regents voted and that many controversial details were never disclosed. He rejected warnings that the vote made "powerful enemies," saying he hoped it sent a message that "accountability and transparency should always be top priorities" at WKU, while noting he respected Ransdell and would keep working with him.

In April 2016 Richey won reelection with 1,497 of 2,341 presidential votes, becoming a two-term president in a high-turnout election announced after midnight in the Downing Student Union senate chambers. "It's a tremendous honor to be elected the first time," he said, "but a second time is verification of promises kept." His running mate Kate Hart won executive vice president and MyCampusToo chairwoman Hannah Neeper won administrative vice president, keeping his allies in the executive branch for 2016-17. A March 2017 Herald profile noted the win made him WKU SGA's first two-term president since 1988 and one of only three since 1974.

SGA Executive Cabinet minutes, 1 Dec 2015
Executive Vice PresidentNolan Miles

Miles was returned unopposed to a second term as executive vice president in April 2015. He opened the year telling senators that sitting on university committees mattered and promising to revive Dine with Decision Makers, and reported through the autumn on the housing shortage and the Student Life Foundation's building and wiring work. Administrative Vice PresidentLiz Koehler

Koehler was elected administrative vice president in April 2015 with 66 percent of the vote against J. William Berry, and kept the budget through a year in which SGA's money fell by $19,500 after a $15,000 private donation was not repeated. She moved $4,000 out of organizational aid back into scholarships and warned that fewer organisations would be funded; in February 2016 she reported a further 4.5 percent cut worth $1,301.27. At the executive cabinet on 1 December 2015 she moved the $600 that kept the library open longer through finals week, argued against a separate Judicial Council adviser and a code of conduct, and proposed that SGA take up suicide awareness after a student's death.

SGA paid half the fare for a Latino leadership keynote

On 15 September 2015 the senate unanimously passed Bill 4-15-F, allocating $200 from legislative discretionary funds for the seminar "Si, Se Puede: Leadership in the Latino Community." 15 September 2015
#

SGA budget fell by $19,500

SGA's budget dropped $19,500 from the previous year, largely because a $15,000 private donation was not repeated and the university cut another $4,500 in reallocations. Organizational funding took the hardest hit, losing $9,000, and the Academic Affairs and Student Affairs scholarship pools each fell by more than $5,000. Administrative Vice President Liz Koehler reallocated $4,000 from organizational aid back into scholarships to soften the loss. The Herald noted WKU's SGA budget stood at $138,500 against the University of Louisville's $1.2 million.

Herald, 15 Sep 2015

22 September 2015
#

Senate voted 21-4 to disapprove the Confucius Institute approval process

The senate passed Resolution 2-15-F by 21 votes to 4, disapproving of the nontransparent procedure by which the Model Confucius Institute building was authorized, after President Gary Ransdell signed the contract in China in December 2014 and the Board of Regents, including the student regent, voted on it more than a month later without full details disclosed. Several senators argued the institute was an asset to WKU and Bowling Green, while Campus Improvement Chairman Zachary Jones backed the resolution as an expression of student opinion. Richey wrote separately that the vote was not meant to set a precedent beyond ensuring the Board had full information, and that some senators had warned it would make "powerful enemies" for SGA.

Planning began for SGA's fiftieth anniversary

#

SGA cabinet pulls 'Spirit Masters' from pub-naming ballot

SGA's "Dub the Pub" contest to name the new Garrett Conference Center pub narrowed its field from 12 names to 11 on 5 November 2015 after the executive cabinet removed "Spirit Masters," following objections that the name demeaned WKU's student ambassador group and history; Director of Public Relations Sawyer Coffey said the name was pulled "out of respect for the organization." President Jay Todd Richey said the cabinet had compiled the original 12 from 50 names submitted earlier and took responsibility for not catching the problem sooner. The removal, made though Spirit Masters was running in second place, drew a second round of criticism the following week: Senator Chase Coffey said Richey closed the issue without letting senators speak and objected that "Topped Off" had been left on the ballot. Topper Tavern won the final vote with 480 of 2,132 ballots cast.

3 December 2015
#

Judicial Council declined to censure a senator; the chief justice resigned

The Judicial Council voted unanimously not to censure Senator John Winstead, whom it had investigated over an impeach-Ransdell sign at the homecoming game and a Facebook image mocking President Gary Ransdell, finding his conduct protected by the First Amendment. Winstead said the process was "fundamentally flawed" and objected to having been investigated at all. Chief Justice Kelsey Luttrell then announced she would step down at the end of the semester, saying the council had been publicly ridiculed and lacked support from fellow SGA members.

Judicial Council strips funding from physics and art departments

The Judicial Council ruled Bills 8-15-F and 9-15-F unconstitutional under Bylaw 6.1, which stated SGA may allocate funds solely to students, voiding $1,500 for the physics department and up to $759 for the art department. Chief Justice Kelsey Luttrell said money given to departments benefits faculty and staff as well as students. The council encouraged the student League of Sculptors to apply instead for $500 in organizational aid.

#

Senate passed guidelines for WKU's tenth president

The senate approved Resolution 2-16-S, by John Winstead and Richey, outlining what students wanted in WKU's tenth president, which Richey planned to use in his role on the presidential search committee. A clause on the displaced Jonesville community originally asked the next president to offer solutions for how reparations could be made; senators disputed whether reparations or restitution was the right term, changed the wording to restitution, and then removed the clause entirely by majority vote. A sustainability clause seeking a zero-emissions campus was softened to a goal of improving WKU's AASHE STARS rating.

20 April 2016
#

Richey reelected in one of highest-turnout elections

Students reelected Jay Todd Richey as SGA president with 64 percent of the vote over Braxton Powell, in one of the highest-turnout elections in the association's history with 2,442 voters. Kate Hart, an Academic Affairs chairwoman and Richey's running mate, won executive vice president. Richey called the win "verification of promises kept."

Herald, 20 Apr 2016

27 April 2016
#

Final meeting passes eSports, recycling and vegetarian dining resolutions

At its last meeting of the year the senate passed Resolution 3-16-S supporting recycling in the Downing Student Union, Garrett and Tower food courts, Resolution 4-16-S supporting creation of an eSports program and dedicated space, and Resolution 5-16-S supporting vegetarian options on the DSU meal plan. Bill 15-16-S allocated $600 to keep Helm-Cravens Library open until 2 a.m. during finals week. Nathan Cherry was elected speaker of the senate for 2016-17.

Herald, 27 Apr 2016

What this is

A year-by-year record of student government at Western Kentucky University, from the ratification of the Associated Students constitution in April 1966 to the present. Every entry names the source it came from.

Corrections

The names here start with the plaque in the SGA Chambers, and the plaque is known to be wrong in places. Where the archive disagrees with it, this site follows the archive and records the change on the corrections page. If you served, or you know that a year here is wrong, the project wants to hear from you: people who held an office can be given the keys to their own year on the contribute page.

What it rests on

Everything here comes from what is publicly available online: the digitised Herald and SGA's own papers on TopSCHOLAR, the Talisman yearbooks, wkuherald.com, the university's news pages and the Wayback Machine. That is a fraction of what happened. Student government has met most weeks for sixty years, and the greater part of it was never written down, or was written down on paper that has never been scanned. A thin year here is a thin record, not a quiet one.

Reuse

Text on this site is the work of the project. Photographs, documents and legislation are reproduced from Western Kentucky University's own open archives and from the College Heights Herald and the Talisman; each item names the collection, issue and page it came from and links to the original.
