Jay Todd Richey president and student regent, 2015-16

Also on the wall
2016-17

Won amid anonymous complaints that he had breached election codes on poster placement. The Judicial Council warned him but confirmed the result stood.

Richey opened his first term in fall 2015 "leaving behind the Postergate controversy" of the spring election, saying he had "about a hundred things" he wanted to do. His priorities included MyCampusToo, a campaign initiative to bring underrepresented students into SGA, more outreach to international students and the regional campuses, and stronger communication between the executive cabinet and senate. He also oversaw a student competition to name the new pub coming to the Garrett Conference Center, a product of the alcohol-policy change SGA had supported the previous year.

In September he championed the senate's resolution disapproving of how the Confucius Institute building was approved, writing in a letter to the Herald that the contract was signed in China before the Board of Regents voted and that many controversial details were never disclosed. He rejected warnings that the vote made "powerful enemies," saying he hoped it sent a message that "accountability and transparency should always be top priorities" at WKU, while noting he respected Ransdell and would keep working with him.

In April 2016 Richey won reelection with 1,497 of 2,341 presidential votes, becoming a two-term president in a high-turnout election announced after midnight in the Downing Student Union senate chambers. "It's a tremendous honor to be elected the first time," he said, "but a second time is verification of promises kept." His running mate Kate Hart won executive vice president and MyCampusToo chairwoman Hannah Neeper won administrative vice president, keeping his allies in the executive branch for 2016-17. A March 2017 Herald profile noted the win made him WKU SGA's first two-term president since 1988 and one of only three since 1974.

The year, in order26

27 August 2015
#

Richey outlined fall goals, MyCampusToo launch

Opening his first term, President Jay Todd Richey said he wanted more executive-cabinet and senate communication and more outreach to international students and regional campuses, and confirmed his campaign initiative MyCampusToo would launch that semester. Executive Vice President Nolan Miles said he would push senators to sit on university committees and revive the Dine with Decision Makers event.

Herald, 27 Aug 2015

10 September 2015campus
#

SGA tables Confucius Institute resolution, creates sustainability committee

The senate tabled Resolution 1-15-F, which would have concurred with the University Senate in asking President Gary Ransdell to revisit WKU's contract with Hanban, citing uncertainty about the contract terms. At the same meeting Bill 1-15-F recognizing an Elizabethtown/Fort Knox student body association and Bill 2-15-F supporting the MyCampusToo initiative both passed unanimously, as did an executive proposal creating a sustainability committee. President Jay Todd Richey said of the Hanban contract that it was "definitely one sided."

Herald, 10 Sep 2015

15 September 2015
#

SGA budget fell by $19,500

SGA's budget dropped $19,500 from the previous year, largely because a $15,000 private donation was not repeated and the university cut another $4,500 in reallocations. Organizational funding took the hardest hit, losing $9,000, and scholarship pools for Academic Affairs and Student Affairs each fell by more than $5,000. Administrative Vice President Liz Koehler reallocated $4,000 from organizational aid back into scholarships to soften the loss.

Herald, 15 Sep 2015

15 September 2015campus
#

SGA budget falls by $19,500

SGA's budget dropped $19,500 from the prior year after a $15,000 private donation was not repeated and the university cut a further $4,500. Organizational funding took the largest hit, losing $9,000, and Administrative Vice President Liz Koehler moved $4,000 from organizational aid back into scholarships. The Herald noted WKU's SGA budget stood at $138,500 against the University of Louisville's $1.2 million.

Herald, 15 Sep 2015

22 September 2015
#

Senate voted to disapprove Confucius Institute approval process

The Student Senate voted overwhelmingly to pass a resolution disapproving of how the Confucius Institute building project was approved, after President Gary Ransdell signed the contract in China in December 2014 and the Board of Regents, including the Student Regent, voted on it more than a month later without full details disclosed. Richey said the vote was not meant to set a precedent beyond ensuring the Board had full information before acting.

Herald, 24 Sep 2015

24 September 2015campus
#

Senate votes 21-4 to disapprove Confucius Institute building process

The senate passed Resolution 2-15-F disapproving of what it called the nontransparent procedure by which the Model Confucius Institute building was authorized. Several senators argued the institute was an asset to WKU and Bowling Green, while Campus Improvement Chairman Zachary Jones backed the resolution as an expression of student opinion. Richey wrote separately that some senators had warned the vote would make "powerful enemies" for SGA.

Herald, 24 Sep 2015

30 September 2015campus
#

Regents decline to revisit Confucius contract, closing SGA debate

Richey told the senate the Board of Regents had decided at its Sept. 25 meeting not to revisit the contract for the Confucius Institute building, and said he wished to close SGA's debate on the matter. "Just to clarify, it's a done deal, the building will be built," he said. In the same report he told senators state appropriations had fallen from 47 percent of WKU's operating budget in 1998 to 18 percent for 2015-16.

Herald, 30 Sep 2015

12 November 2015campus
#

SGA cabinet pulls 'Spirit Masters' from pub-naming ballot

The executive cabinet removed the name "Spirit Masters" from the Dub the Pub ballot after administrators said it demeaned WKU's student ambassador group, though the name was in second place at the time. Topper Tavern won with 480 of 2,132 votes cast. Senator Chase Coffey said Richey closed the issue without letting senators speak and objected that "Topped Off" had been left on the ballot.

Herald, 12 Nov 2015

18 November 2015
#

Senate backed meningitis vaccine requirement, amended bylaws

The Senate unanimously passed Resolution 6-15-F supporting a meningococcal meningitis vaccination requirement for incoming freshmen in on-campus housing beginning with the class of 2020, and unanimously passed Bill 10-15-F amending SGA's bylaws. Richey reported that his fall listening tours of the regional campuses had gone well and turned up how different those campuses were from main campus.

Herald, 18 Nov 2015

19 November 2015campus
#

Senate backs meningitis vaccine requirement for on-campus freshmen

Resolution 6-15-F, supporting a meningococcal meningitis vaccination requirement for incoming freshmen living in on-campus housing beginning with the class of 2020, passed unanimously. Bill 10-15-F amending the SGA bylaws also passed unanimously. Richey reported back from listening tours at the Glasgow, Owensboro and Elizabethtown/Fort Knox campuses, saying a full-time security officer was Glasgow's top priority.

Herald, 19 Nov 2015

3 December 2015
#

Chief Justice resigned after censure hearing backlash

The Judicial Council unanimously declined to censure senator John Winstead, whom it had investigated over an "impeach Ransdell" sign and a mocking Facebook post about President Ransdell, ruling his actions were protected free speech. Winstead called the fact he was investigated at all "the problem I'm objecting to." Chief Justice Kelsey Luttrell then announced she would step down at the end of the semester, saying the council had been publicly ridiculed and lacked support from fellow SGA members.

Herald, 3 Dec 2015

3 December 2015campus
#

Judicial Council strips funding from physics and art departments

The Judicial Council ruled Bills 8-15-F and 9-15-F unconstitutional under Bylaw 6.1, which stated SGA may allocate funds solely to students, voiding $1,500 for the physics department and up to $759 for the art department. Chief Justice Kelsey Luttrell said money given to departments benefits faculty and staff as well as students. The council encouraged the student League of Sculptors to apply instead for $500 in organizational aid.

Herald, 3 Dec 2015

3 December 2015campus
#

Judicial Council declines to censure senator over 'impeach Ransdell' sign; chief justice resigns

The Judicial Council voted unanimously not to censure Senator John Winstead, who had been investigated over a sign at the homecoming game and a Facebook image mocking President Gary Ransdell, finding his conduct protected by the First Amendment. Chief Justice Kelsey Luttrell resigned effective the end of the semester. Winstead said the process was "fundamentally flawed" and objected to being investigated at all.

Herald, 3 Dec 2015

26 January 2016campus
#

SGA refuses to enforce the campus smoking phaseout

After hearing from a University of Kentucky representative who explained UK pays ambassadors to enforce its tobacco ban and requires them to report violators, SGA declined to assemble the enforcement corps Ransdell's email had described. "SGA has no intention on actually being the group that enforces this," Richey said, adding that he personally disagreed with the ban but would adhere to it. The policy proceeded as voluntary compliance backed by signage.

Herald, 26 Jan 2016

28 January 2016
#

Administration overrode student vote on pub name

Richey announced that administrators had renamed the future Garrett Conference Center pub "Topper Grill and Pub," setting aside the name students chose in SGA's "Dub the Pub" contest, Topper Tavern. Richey said Ransdell wanted to downplay the alcohol theme and called himself "very disappointed," saying the move made SGA question how much the administration listened to students. At the same meeting SGA introduced the RAVE Guardian campus safety app.

Herald, 28 Jan 2016

28 January 2016campus
#

Administration overrides student vote on pub name

Richey announced that administrators had changed the winning pub name from Topper Tavern and Grill to Topper Grill and Pub, a decision he said was made by former Student Affairs Vice President Howard Bailey and reflected Ransdell's wish to downplay the alcohol theme. "I was very disappointed," Richey said, adding that it made SGA question how much the administration listens to students. The same meeting introduced the RAVE Guardian campus safety app.

Herald, 28 Jan 2016

2 February 2016campus
#

Ransdell announced retirement amid state budget cuts

President Gary Ransdell announced he would step down as WKU's ninth president on June 30, 2017, triggering a presidential search. The announcement came the same week new Gov. Matt Bevin ordered an immediate 4.5 percent cut to WKU's state funding and proposed a 9 percent cut, with future funding shifted to a performance-based pool. The changes framed much of SGA's advocacy for the rest of the year.

Herald, 2 Feb 2016

17 February 2016
#

Senate opposed state tuition-freeze bill

The senate approved Resolution 2-16-S opposing Kentucky Senate Bill 75, which would have frozen resident tuition at 2015-16 levels for four years. Richey argued such freezes were "dangerous risks" that could wreck universities and cost students more in the long run. At the same meeting, Administrative Vice President Liz Koehler reported an expected 4.5 percent cut to SGA's own budget, about $1,301.

Herald, 17 Feb 2016

17 February 2016
#

SGA opposes state tuition-freeze bill; Richey says he appointed 42 percent of the senate

Resolution 2-16-S, opposing Kentucky Senate Bill 75's four-year tuition freeze, passed after debate, with Richey arguing such measures "can end up wrecking universities and charging students even more in the long run." Administrative Vice President Liz Koehler reported an expected 4.5 percent cut to SGA's own budget, a loss of $1,301.27. Richey also told senators appointments should be more democratic, noting he had appointed 42 percent of the sitting senate.

Herald, 17 Feb 2016

24 February 2016
#

Senators lobbied legislators in Frankfort

SGA members traveled to Frankfort to lobby against state budget cuts that could slash WKU funding by as much as 9 percent, meeting with eight state officials. Senators pressed for smaller cuts and restoration of need-based aid, citing figures that about 15,000 students were denied need-based financial aid the prior year. Both chambers of the General Assembly recognized SGA for its participation.

Herald, 24 Feb 2016

2 March 2016
#

Ransdell declined SGA's meningitis vaccine mandate

Richey read aloud a letter from Ransdell responding to Resolution 6-15-F, passed the previous semester, which supported requiring meningitis vaccinations for incoming freshmen living on campus starting with the class of 2020. Ransdell wrote that the university would highly recommend vaccination but "stop short of requiring it," citing a $143.77 cost for uninsured students at the Graves-Gilbert Clinic.

Herald, 2 Mar 2016

23 March 2016campus
#

Senate refuses to vote on Judicial Council's new election codes

The senate declined to vote on election codes drafted by the Judicial Council that would have cut senate campaign spending from $50 to $25 and limited candidates to three pieces of campaign material per building, placed only on bulletin boards. Richey, absent from the meeting, called the changes "too much red tape" and over-regulation. Chief Justice Amber Daniel scheduled a separate meeting for senators to propose changes.

Herald, 23 Mar 2016

6 April 2016
#

Senate passed guidelines for WKU's tenth president

The senate approved a resolution by John Winstead and Richey outlining what students wanted in WKU's tenth president, which Richey planned to use in his role on the presidential search committee. After debate, senators amended the sustainability clause to target improving WKU's AASHE STARS rating and voted to remove language about Jonesville "reparations" entirely, after disputing whether reparations or restitution was the right term.

Herald, 6 Apr 2016

6 April 2016campus
#

Senate strips reparations language from presidential-qualities resolution

Resolution 2-16-S, outlining recommended qualities for WKU's tenth president, passed after amendment. A clause on the displaced Jonesville community originally asking the next president to "offer solutions for how reparations can be made" was debated, changed to "restitution," then removed entirely by majority vote. A sustainability clause seeking a zero-emissions campus was also softened to a goal of raising WKU's AASHE STARS rating.

Herald, 6 Apr 2016

20 April 2016
#

Richey reelected in one of highest-turnout elections

Students reelected Jay Todd Richey as SGA president with 64 percent of the vote over Braxton Powell, in one of the highest-turnout elections in the association's history with 2,442 voters. Kate Hart, an Academic Affairs chairwoman and Richey's running mate, won executive vice president. Richey called the win "verification of promises kept."

Herald, 20 Apr 2016

27 April 2016campus
#

Final meeting passes eSports, recycling and vegetarian dining resolutions

At its last meeting of the year the senate passed Resolution 3-16-S supporting recycling in the Downing Student Union, Garrett and Tower food courts, Resolution 4-16-S supporting creation of an eSports program and dedicated space, and Resolution 5-16-S supporting vegetarian options on the DSU meal plan. Bill 15-16-S allocated $600 to keep Helm-Cravens Library open until 2 a.m. during finals week. Nathan Cherry was elected speaker of the senate for 2016-17.

Herald, 27 Apr 2016

What this is

A year-by-year record of student government at Western Kentucky University, from the ratification of the Associated Students constitution in April 1966 to the present. Every entry names the source it came from.

Corrections

The names here start with the plaque in the SGA Chambers, and the plaque is known to be wrong in places. Where the archive disagrees with it, this site follows the archive and records the change on the corrections page. If you served, or you know that a year here is wrong, the project wants to hear from you through the Student Government Association office.
