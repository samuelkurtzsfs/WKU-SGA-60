As recorded on the plaque. Not yet corroborated in the archive.

Single-year plate and the only name attached to 2006 because Watkins resigned partway through the 2006-07 term; no plate covers the full year. Confirmed against Herald election coverage: a Bowling Green senior, he won the SGA presidency in the 13 April 2006 online election, defeating Berea junior Kara Ratliff. He had been elected the first speaker of the senate under the new constitution on 15 April 2004, a result contested 9-8 and then upheld. He resigned as president and student regent on 28 November 2006, per SGA's own senate minutes for that day's regularly scheduled Tuesday meeting, which record the resignation being read into the record; this site previously followed a Herald account giving the date as 30 November, which this run corrects against the primary minutes.

Robert "Rob" Watkins, a Bowling Green student, wrote the SGA constitution adopted in 2004 and was elected the first speaker of the senate under it on 15 April 2004, defeating Brittany Fausey 9 votes to 8 in a result immediately contested over what quorum the new constitution required; the vote was upheld later that month.

Watkins won the SGA presidency in the 13 April 2006 online election, defeating Berea junior Kara Ratliff after more than a week courting TopNet voters, with results announced at a crowded Revolution 91.7. He took office for 2006-07 alongside Administrative Vice President Amanda Allen, who by October was tightening the budget after the prior administration left roughly $24,000 unspent.

Watkins resigned as SGA president and student regent on 28 November 2006, with the resignation read into the senate's own minutes at that day's regularly scheduled Tuesday meeting, after senators had aired concerns about him at an unannounced 10 p.m. meeting the evening before. (A Herald account this site had followed gave the date as 30 November; the primary senate minutes are treated as authoritative here.) A Herald editorial the same week was headlined "SGA actions an embarrassment to Western." The resignation left students without a voting voice on the Board of Regents for at least a month and put junior Jeanne Johnson, already serving as his executive vice president since the 5 September season-opening meeting, in as a fill-in leader for the rest of the term, formally sworn in as president at the 5 December Winter Banquet.

The year, in order12

5 September 2006
#

Fifth SGA opened its year, swore in eight senators

At the first meeting of that year's Senate, called to order with 8 of 18 senators present, eight new senators were sworn in: Chris Anderson, Diane DeRosa-Reynolds, Leah Keys, Amanda Kidd, Suzie Moghaddam, Seth Norman, Tori Theiss and Christa Wilcox. Administrative Vice President Amanda Allen reported $30,000 in organizational aid for the year, and the senate nominated committee heads and Judicial Council members, including Matt Holland as chief justice. The minutes record Jeanne L. Johnson delivering the executive vice president's report.

24 October 2006
#

SGA tightened its budget after leaving $24,000 unspent

The Herald reported that SGA had left about $24,000 of its $115,000 budget unspent at the end of 2005-06, money that returned to Western's general fund. Administrative Vice President Amanda Allen said SGA was cutting money from areas where it went unspent, adding almost $10,000 to organizational aid and $3,000 to the miscellaneous fund. Allen called the previous year a bad budgetary year but also a learning year.

Herald, 24 Oct 2006 (Wayback)

25 October 2006
#

SGA-run referendum showed students favored the I-A football move

In an online referendum SGA arranged alongside the homecoming queen election, 64.6 percent of voting students, 1,095, supported WKU's proposed move to Division I-A football, with 600 against. A total of 2,136 students voted in the poll, and the 1,695 who answered the football question represented about 9 percent of enrollment. SGA President Rob Watkins, who held the student seat on the Board of Regents, said the poll would help him define a position but noted the homecoming tie-in likely drew football-friendly voters.

Daily News, 25 Oct 2006

2 November 2006
#

SGA senate unanimously passed a resolution backing the I-A move

SGA members unanimously passed a resolution saying they supported the move to Division I-A football, following the student referendum result. Watkins, as student regent and SGA president, still would not say what his own position was before the regents' vote.

Herald front page, 5 Nov 2006 (Wayback)

28 November 2006
#

Watkins resigned as SGA president and student regent

SGA's own senate minutes record that president and student regent Rob Watkins's resignation was formally read into the meeting at the senate's regularly scheduled Tuesday session, with senators told Jeanne Johnson would be sworn in as president at the Dec. 5 Winter Banquet. Senators had voiced concerns about Watkins at an unannounced 10 p.m. meeting the evening before. A Herald front-page account (Wayback, 2 Dec 2006) covered the same resignation but gave the date as 30 November; this entry follows the primary senate minutes, which date the reading of the resignation to 28 November, leaving students without representation on Western's governing board for at least a month.

SGA Senate minutes, 28 Nov 2006 (TopSCHOLAR)Read it on this site

28 November 2006
#

Senate passed Books for Africa bill and a printing-quota resolution

At the same Nov. 28 meeting where Watkins's resignation was read into the record, the senate unanimously passed Bill 11-06-F, sponsored by Lucas Humble, to collect textbooks the university bookstore would not buy back and send them to Africa. Senators also unanimously passed Resolution 08-06-F, sponsored by Seth Norman, backing a university study of a computer-lab printing quota after administrators told SGA's Student Affairs committee how many pages some students were printing.

28 November 2006campus
#

Senate heard Douglas Keen Hall was next for renovation

In a University Committees report to the senate, a member reported that the Student Life Foundation had discussed campus housing renovations and that Douglas Keen Hall would be the next residence hall to be renovated, within about three years. The same report covered a Budget Council discussion of rolling several student organizations' budgets, including SGA's, into the university's base budget.

5 December 2006
#

Senate tabled two resolutions for lack of quorum at Winter Banquet meeting

With 16 of 31 senators present, the senate lacked quorum to act on Resolution 09-06-F on vending machine prices and Resolution 10-06-F on special events parking, so both were tabled. Executive Vice President was listed vacant, since Jeanne Johnson had moved into the presidency after Robert Watkins's resignation. The Judicial Council reported meeting on the time requirements for the student regent seat. Winners of the Outstanding Senator and Outstanding Committee Member awards, chosen by secret ballot, were announced later that evening at the Winter Banquet.

27 January 2007
#

Fill-in SGA leader Jeanne Johnson challenged tuition plan at regents meeting

The Board of Regents approved a 7.8 percent tuition increase for 2007-08, bringing tuition and fees to $6,417 a year including the higher athletics fee. Jeanne Johnson, a junior economics and finance major from Marion described as a fill-in for the former SGA president, opposed the size of the increase for online and distance-learning courses, though she had no vote. She said 'We know tuition increases are inevitable' but argued distance learners should not carry that burden.

Daily News, 27 Jan 2007

February 2007
#

Johnson won the student regent election; SGA seated a full senate

Jeanne Johnson, SGA president and Marion junior, won the special election for Western's vacant student regent seat with 688 votes, 41 percent of ballots cast. The same Herald issue reported that SGA began the spring semester with its first full senate attendance since fall 2004.

Herald front page, 3 Feb 2007 (Wayback)

24 April 2007
#

Provost rejected plus/minus grading; Johnson credited SGA's campaign

Provost Barbara Burch told the University Senate she would not implement the plus/minus grading system the senate had passed 36-23 in March. SGA President Jeanne Johnson said she was grateful for the decision, noting SGA had raised awareness and led student protests, and pledged that students would keep fighting if the issue returned. She said 'I'm confident that the students' opinions have been expressed.'

Herald, 24 Apr 2007 (Wayback)

2 May 2007
#

SGA banquet closed the year and swore in 2007-08 officers

At its annual banquet on May 1, SGA presented awards and swore in officers for 2007-08, with Jeanne Lynn Johnson of Marion becoming the new SGA president and student regent and receiving the Dero Downing Award. Johnathon Boles of Elizabethtown became executive vice president, Kayla Shelton speaker of the Student Senate, and Cherieth Lea Lineweaver administrative vice president. Amanda Beth Allen received the Charles A. Keown Award, SGA's highest honor.

WKU News, 2 May 2007 (Wayback)

What this is

A year-by-year record of student government at Western Kentucky University, from the ratification of the Associated Students constitution in April 1966 to the present. Every entry names the source it came from.

Corrections

The names here start with the plaque in the SGA Chambers, and the plaque is known to be wrong in places. Where the archive disagrees with it, this site follows the archive and records the change on the corrections page. If you served, or you know that a year here is wrong, the project wants to hear from you through the Student Government Association office.
