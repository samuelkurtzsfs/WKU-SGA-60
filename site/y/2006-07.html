Single-year plate and the only name attached to 2006 because Watkins resigned partway through the 2006-07 term; no plate covers the full year. Confirmed against Herald election coverage: a Bowling Green senior, he won the SGA presidency in the 13 April 2006 online election, defeating Berea junior Kara Ratliff. He had been elected the first speaker of the senate under the new constitution on 15 April 2004, a result contested 9-8 and then upheld. He resigned as president and student regent on 28 November 2006, per SGA's own senate minutes for that day's regularly scheduled Tuesday meeting, which record the resignation being read into the record; this site previously followed a Herald account giving the date as 30 November, which this run corrects against the primary minutes.

Robert "Rob" Watkins, a Bowling Green student, wrote the SGA constitution adopted in 2004 and was elected the first speaker of the senate under it on 15 April 2004, defeating Brittany Fausey 9 votes to 8 in a result immediately contested over what quorum the new constitution required; the vote was upheld later that month.

Watkins won the SGA presidency in the 13 April 2006 online election, defeating Berea junior Kara Ratliff after more than a week courting TopNet voters, with results announced at a crowded Revolution 91.7. He took office for 2006-07 alongside Administrative Vice President Amanda Allen, who by October was tightening the budget after the prior administration left roughly $24,000 unspent.

Watkins resigned as SGA president and student regent on 28 November 2006, with the resignation read into the senate's own minutes at that day's regularly scheduled Tuesday meeting, after senators had aired concerns about him at an unannounced 10 p.m. meeting the evening before. (A Herald account this site had followed gave the date as 30 November; the primary senate minutes are treated as authoritative here.) A Herald editorial the same week was headlined "SGA actions an embarrassment to Western." The resignation left students without a voting voice on the Board of Regents for at least a month and put junior Jeanne Johnson, already serving as his executive vice president since the 5 September season-opening meeting, in as a fill-in leader for the rest of the term, formally sworn in as president at the 5 December Winter Banquet.

Also on the wall
2007-08

Succeeded to the presidency partway through 2006-07. SGA's senate minutes record Rob Watkins's resignation read into the meeting of 28 November 2006 and Johnson sworn in as president at the Winter Banquet on 5 December, and she then won the special election for the vacant student regent seat in February 2007. The Chambers wall carries her only for 2007-08, the full term she was elected to in her own right, so the plaque understates her service by about five months.

Jeanne Johnson was executive vice president when Rob Watkins resigned the presidency and the student regent seat in November 2006. The senate minutes for 28 November record his resignation being read to the chamber and senators being told she would be sworn in at the Winter Banquet on 5 December; the minutes of that meeting list the executive vice presidency as vacant because she had moved up.

The regent seat did not pass with the presidency, and Western went about two months without a student on its governing board. At the Board of Regents meeting of 26 January 2007 she spoke against the size of a 7.8 per cent tuition increase for online and distance learners without holding a vote, saying tuition increases were inevitable but that distance learners should not carry the burden. She won the special election for the seat the following week with 688 votes, 41 per cent of those cast.

She was elected to a full term in her own right and sworn in as president and student regent at the SGA banquet on 1 May 2007, where she also received the Dero Downing Award.

Fifth SGA opened its year, swore in eight senators

At the first meeting of that year's Senate, called to order with 8 of 18 senators present, eight new senators were sworn in: Chris Anderson, Diane DeRosa-Reynolds, Leah Keys, Amanda Kidd, Suzie Moghaddam, Seth Norman, Tori Theiss and Christa Wilcox. 24 October 2006
#

SGA tightened its budget after leaving $24,000 unspent

The Herald reported that SGA had left about $24,000 of its $115,000 budget unspent at the end of 2005-06, money that returned to Western's general fund. Administrative Vice President Amanda Allen said SGA was cutting money from areas where it went unspent, adding almost $10,000 to organizational aid and $3,000 to the miscellaneous fund. Allen called the previous year a bad budgetary year but also a learning year.

25 October 2006
#

SGA-run referendum showed students favored the I-A football move

In an online referendum SGA arranged alongside the homecoming queen election, 64.6 percent of voting students, 1,095, supported WKU's proposed move to Division I-A football, with 600 against. A total of 2,136 students voted in the poll, and the 1,695 who answered the football question represented about 9 percent of enrollment. SGA President Rob Watkins, who held the student seat on the Board of Regents, said the poll would help him define a position but noted the homecoming tie-in likely drew football-friendly voters.

Daily News, 25 Oct 2006

#

Fill-in SGA leader Jeanne Johnson challenged tuition plan at regents meeting

What this is

A year-by-year record of student government at Western Kentucky University, from the ratification of the Associated Students constitution in April 1966 to the present. Every entry names the source it came from.

Corrections

The names here start with the plaque in the SGA Chambers, and the plaque is known to be wrong in places. Where the archive disagrees with it, this site follows the archive and records the change on the corrections page. If you served, or you know that a year here is wrong, the project wants to hear from you: people who held an office can be given the keys to their own year on the contribute page.

What it rests on

Everything here comes from what is publicly available online: the digitised Herald and SGA's own papers on TopSCHOLAR, the Talisman yearbooks, wkuherald.com, the university's news pages and the Wayback Machine. That is a fraction of what happened. Student government has met most weeks for sixty years, and the greater part of it was never written down, or was written down on paper that has never been scanned. A thin year here is a thin record, not a quiet one.
