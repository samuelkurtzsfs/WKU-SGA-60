Linda Jones president and student regent, 1971-72

Standing of the record
Confirmed in the sources below.

First woman to hold the office. The WKU Timeline dates her election to 6 April 1971, matching the plaque year. The Herald of 28 April 1972 carries a piece headed 'Jones, Linda. Reflects on Term' in the same issue as a strike call over dorm visitation.

Jones was elected the first woman president of the Associated Student Government on 6 April 1971; the Western Alumnus photographed her being sworn in with faculty regent Lowell Harrison by Georgia Bates.

That fall, on 14 September 1971, the Herald reported the Associated Students affiliating with a wider student association, though the article index alone does not identify which body. By 1 February 1972, four years after the student regent seat was created, the Herald was still reporting the seat had no vote.

Through February and March 1972, Jones's Congress pushed a rewritten constitution: it voted section by section on 22 February, when the Herald printed the executive branch's full text, and the Herald reported the executive branch accepted on 29 February. The same 29 February issue reported the Kentucky Civil Liberties Union planning court action connected to Associated Students and The Fly, a separate WKU underground newspaper, and carried Jones's own piece, 'Associated Students Corrects Herald', a public correction of the paper's coverage of the organization. The archive does not yet hold the text of either the KCLU piece or Jones's correction, so the underlying dispute and what she corrected both remain unconfirmed.

Days earlier, the Herald's 25 February 1972 issue ran under the headline 'Chaos Reigns at Associated Students Meeting', alongside a separate piece arguing that student interest in the organization had collapsed and the Associated Students had followed it down.

The constitution went to referendum at the end of March: the Herald's 28 March issue urged students to 'Vote Yes' ahead of a two-day vote, and Associated Students formally endorsed the Kentucky Civil Liberties Union's lawsuit in the same issue. The archive does not yet show the referendum's result or turnout.

Her term closed on 28 April 1972 as a Mass Action Committee called for a strike over dorm visitation and the Associated Students installed its next officers; the same issue reported students had won six votes on a university council, and carried Jones's own reflection on her year in office.

Everyone the record shows in office this year. Each name goes to their own page, with every office they held and everything the archive says they did.

The executive

Vice PresidentReginald Glass

Glass was vice president of Associated Students in 1971-72 and the yearbook calls him the first black vice president of the body and the first black student to hold any high student leadership position at Western. As vice president he assisted the president, sat on the Academic Council as one of two voting student members, and chaired the activities committee; a caption of him signing a speaker contract appears beside the yearbook's note that Dr. Benjamin Spock was A.S.'s first sponsored speaker, though the volume does not explicitly say the two are the same contract. He was a member of Alpha Phi Alpha, which received its national charter on 31 October 1971, and the Herald reported that November that he was named the campus's outstanding Black Greek. By October 1973 he was ASG's representative from the Graduate College and the Herald reported him asking for better minority representation in student government; he resigned that post in protest on 4 December 1973, the Herald reported, and the 1974 Talisman gives his reason as the harm he said the lack of Black and foreign students on ASG and the Academic Council did to the minorities and the University alike.

The Associated Students ran a mock gubernatorial election on 21 October 1971. The 1972 Talisman records that only 1,175 of Western's 11,000 students voted and that they elected the Republican Tom Emberton; the Herald reported the result on 22 October. The year's programme summed up in the yearbook

The 1972 Talisman summarised Linda Jones's year under the heading "A.S. makes great strides in implementing new programs": in entertainment, mini-concerts, a coffee house in West Hall and the Gaslight film series joined several top-name musical groups and lecturers. The same volume records that Associated Students conducted the elections for class officers, Who's Who and Homecoming queen, held the October mock election for governor, ran a symposium at which students could question administrators, sponsored the Homecoming dance, and revived the Kentucky Student Association among the state's student governments, with several meetings held at Western.

1,175 of Western's 11,000 students voted in the 21 October mock election

What this is

A year-by-year record of student government at Western Kentucky University, from the ratification of the Associated Students constitution in April 1966 to the present. Every entry names the source it came from.

Corrections

The names here start with the plaque in the SGA Chambers, and the plaque is known to be wrong in places. Where the archive disagrees with it, this site follows the archive and records the change on the corrections page. If you served, or you know that a year here is wrong, the project wants to hear from you: people who held an office can be given the keys to their own year on the contribute page.

What it rests on

Everything here comes from what is publicly available online: the digitised Herald and SGA's own papers on TopSCHOLAR, the Talisman yearbooks, wkuherald.com, the university's news pages and the Wayback Machine. That is a fraction of what happened. Student government has met most weeks for sixty years, and the greater part of it was never written down, or was written down on paper that has never been scanned. A thin year here is a thin record, not a quiet one.

Reuse

Text on this site is the work of the project. Photographs, documents and legislation are reproduced from Western Kentucky University's own open archives and from the College Heights Herald and the Talisman; each item names the collection, issue and page it came from and links to the original.
