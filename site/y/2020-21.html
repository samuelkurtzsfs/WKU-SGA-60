J. Garrett Edmonds: The COVID year. Almost certainly the most unusual term in the organisation's history and worth a dedicated oral history.

J. Garrett Edmonds — the term

Edmonds, a senior from Island, Kentucky, spent his freshman year as chair of the Campus Improvements Committee, working with Vice President of Student Affairs Brian Kuster on projects including a campus safety walk, before winning election as executive vice president in April 2018 on a ticket with Harper Anderson and Stephen Mayer. He served two years as executive vice president under President Will Harris, pushing for a budget model geared toward campus amenities and recreational life and working to diversify Senate appointments with seats for underrepresented students.

He ran for the presidency in fall 2020 on the Edmonds-Norvell ticket, after his original running mate for administrative vice president, Nathan Terrell, dropped out and was replaced by Abbey Norvell. The campaign centered on a pledge to create a Director of Outreach position to close the communication gap between campus organizations and SGA, and the Sept. 24 Herald town hall covered WKU's COVID-19 response and the summer's Black Lives Matter protests on campus. Voting ran Sept. 28-29 on TopNet; Edmonds defeated senator MJ Mayo, becoming SGA president and, since the two offices had merged by then, student regent on the WKU Board of Regents. Norvell was elected administrative vice president alongside him, with the Herald reporting no decision yet made on a separate executive vice president post.

Taking office amid the pandemic, the senate confirmed Isaac Keller to a vice-president post 29-1 in October after Edmonds said he wanted a deputy willing to challenge him; the Herald's report on that vote first called the office executive vice president, then ran a correction changing it to administrative vice president -- the same title the Herald had given Norvell in September, a conflict the record does not resolve. Matt Barr, Ashlynn Evans, Kody Okert and Paul Brosky rounded out the executive and Shelby Robertson served as non-voting speaker of the senate. Early cabinet business set up officer office hours, an ad hoc outreach committee chaired by Me'Lon Craighead and a push toward inclusive pronoun language in Senate proceedings. That fall SGA petitioned WKU to restore Pass/D/Fail grading for COVID-disrupted students; Associate Provost Rob Hale initially rebuffed the request, calling the university's extended drop deadline sufficient, but by December the petition -- more than 3,500 signatures in three days -- had succeeded, and Edmonds thanked senators for the work behind it at the semester's final meeting alongside bills funding campus safe-driving signage and several student organizations.

In the spring his senate passed the organization's largest-ever organizational aid bill and a resolution seeking a codified vetting process for campus dining vendors after a controversial social media post by a local pizzeria owner, alongside legislation backing WKU's Naming and Symbols Task Force in renaming buildings tied to racism, a community garden, and a diversity-event push by DEI chair Fatin Yaro. Near the end of his term he declined to fill the chief justice seat vacated by Erika Puhakka, saying the choice belonged to his successor: "I think that's the only thing that is right in this scenario." He also worried publicly that only 13 students were running for senate in the April election, and closed his term addressing the Class of 2021 at the delayed Fall 2020/Spring 2021 commencement, telling graduates their perseverance through "this past year" had brought them to their day of celebration.

What happened, in order

2020-09-24

Town hall debate pitted the sitting EVP against a senator

SGA held a town hall debate in the DSU auditorium between presidential candidates Garrett Edmonds, the sitting executive vice president, and senator MJ Mayo. Edmonds's original running mate for administrative vice president, Nathan Terrell, had dropped off the Edmonds-Norvell ticket and was replaced by Abbey Norvell; the debate covered WKU's COVID-19 response and the summer's Black Lives Matter protests on campus.

WKU Talisman, Voter's Guide, 28 Sep 2020 ↗
2020-09-29

Edmonds elected president over Mayo

Garrett Edmonds, the sitting executive vice president, was elected student body president over senator MJ Mayo in balloting held on TopNet Sept. 28-29, succeeding Will Harris. Edmonds had campaigned on creating a Director of Outreach position to link campus organizations more directly with SGA.

Herald, 6 Oct 2020 ↗
2020-09-30

Norvell elected administrative vice president; EVP still open

SGA held a virtual election party over Zoom to announce results. The Edmonds-Norvell ticket defeated MJ Mayo for the presidency, and Abbey Norvell was elected administrative vice president alongside Edmonds. The Herald reported no official decision had yet been made on a separate executive vice president post, with an announcement expected at the next SGA meeting.

Herald, 30 Sep 2020 ↗
2020-10-07

Senate passed the annual budget 28-5 and elected Shelby Robertson speaker

At the first official senate meeting under the new leadership, Shelby Robertson defeated Brenna Matthews to become speaker of the senate and the budget passed 28-5. Edmonds emphasized a $6,000 Projects Fund, including $1,000 for Housing and Residence Life PPE and an expansion of the Go with the Flow menstrual products program into Pearce Ford Tower. Senator MJ Mayo challenged the balance of stipends; Edmonds said all senate stipends except the top three offices had been cut to feed the Projects Fund.

Herald, 7 Oct 2020 ↗
2020-10-13

Cabinet set office hours, an outreach committee, and pushed inclusive language

At the executive cabinet's first recorded meeting, Edmonds set up officer office hours and an ad hoc outreach committee for RSO leadership. Speaker Shelby Robertson pushed to connect senators with similar legislative interests, adopt inclusive pronoun language in the chamber, and find a parliamentarian.

2020-10-21

Keller confirmed AVP 29-1; two new committee chairs elected

The senate confirmed Isaac Keller as administrative vice president 29-1 after Edmonds said he wanted a deputy willing to challenge him when they disagreed. Roderick Maul and Tess Welch were elected committee chairs, and senators voted to form a new Ad Hoc committee chaired by Me'Lon Craighead.

Herald, 21 Oct 2020 ↗
2020-11-11

Judicial nominations confirmed; senate revives a Pass/D/Fail request

The senate unanimously confirmed Edmonds's nominations of Derek Collins and Lexi Mayne as associate justices and Addison McCoun as senator at large. Senators passed a Tess Welch/Eric Barry bill to increase campus accessibility and split funding for SGA-branded masks and hand sanitizer between the senate and SGA's PR team. A closing bill by Tate McClard and Jacob Congleton, with every senator listed as a co-author, asked WKU to restore the Pass/D/Fail grading option it had offered in spring 2020 but declined to bring back that fall.

Herald, 11 Nov 2020 (corrected) ↗
2020-11-16

Edmonds launched a P/D/F petition

President Garrett Edmonds released a student petition asking WKU to offer a Pass/D/Fail grading option for the fall semester, citing inconsistent course modalities and the strain of quarantine on students without an online-learning fallback. The petition reached half its 1,000-signature goal within an hour and the target was raised to 5,000.

Herald, 18 Nov 2020 ↗
2020-11-18

Associate provost rebuffed SGA's P/D/F request; computers bill passes 28-3

Edmonds told senators that Associate Provost Rob Hale had defended the university's decision to extend the drop deadline rather than restore Pass/D/Fail grading, and said he was "deeply disturbed" by the response; senator Brenna Mathews argued the policy left students choosing between attending in person or paying for an extra semester. Edmonds urged senators to keep circulating his petition, which had passed 3,000 signatures, and the senate voted 28-3 to fund recycled computers for the SGA office, a number scaled back to meet CDC guidelines.

Herald, 18 Nov 2020 ↗
2020-12-01

WKU relented and allowed pass grades after SGA's P/D/F campaign

Provost Cheryl Stevens told faculty that students who faced COVID-related problems could opt for a pass grade instead of a B or C, reversing the university's earlier refusal. The Faculty Senate had voted 40-9 in favor and SGA voted unanimously for the option, presenting nearly 20 pages of student testimony; SGA's petition had drawn over 3,500 signatures in three days. Students had to email their professor by Jan. 13, with the final decision left to the faculty member.

Herald, 1 Dec 2020 ↗
2020-12-02

Fall semester closed with a safe-driving pledge and organization funding

At the last meeting of the fall semester, Edmonds thanked senators for the work that led to the new P/D/F option. Senators unanimously passed a Matthew Thomas bill committing SGA's social media to a "Drive Safe Hilltoppers" safety campaign, and unanimously funded a bill supporting HonorsToppers, College Republicans, Zeta Phi Beta and the Sociology and Criminology Club.

Herald, 2 Dec 2020 ↗
2021-01-20

Spring semester opened over Zoom with a late-fee pledge

At its first meeting of the spring semester, held on Zoom, SGA heard Edmonds say the provost search was in its final stage with interviews starting within weeks, and that he planned to look at changing or removing the late fees charged when a student's financial aid arrives late. Senators unanimously nominated Jamison Moorehead as director of IT and funded Dance Big Red and a Delta Sigma Pi virtual leadership conference.

Herald, 20 Jan 2021 ↗
2021-01-26

Committee chairs set spring priorities

Legislative Research Chair Dawson McCoun said the shift to remote meetings after March 2020 had made it harder to connect with students and pass legislation. Diversity and Inclusion Chair Fatin Yaro announced a Feb. 24 diversity event and legislation to set consequences for discrimination within student organizations, and Chief of Staff Matt Barr said he would continue the "Go with the Flow" program installing free menstrual-product dispensers on campus. SGA planned a return to masked, socially distanced in-person meetings that semester.

Herald, 26 Jan 2021 ↗
2021-02-02campus

Presidential task force sought input on slaveholding college namesakes

WKU's Presidential Task Force on Naming and Symbols, formed by President Timothy Caboni in fall 2020, collected input from students and the community on the namesakes of Potter College and Ogden College, both built on wealth from slave labor. Two Potter descendants said it was time to find a new name. The task force planned to audit names and send recommendations to the administration.

Herald, 2 Feb 2021 ↗
2021-02-03

New senator seated; rally with Gov. Beshear announced

SGA nominated pre-law sophomore Will Dykeman as a new senator and Edmonds announced a virtual Student Body President rally for March 26 featuring Kentucky Gov. Andy Beshear, with Edmonds working to add state Rep. Charles Booker. Senators unanimously funded a bill by AVP Isaac Keller supporting Amazing Tones of Joy, Black Women of Western, Queer Student Union and the Business Association for Development, and voted $2,500 for ISEC's Black Magic Masquerade Ball on a bill by Me'Lon Craighead.

Herald, 3 Feb 2021 ↗
2021-02-11

Outreach signage and safe-driving bills pass

Guest speaker Ethan Logan, vice president of enrollment and student experience, briefed senators on how his office could help SGA. Edmonds pointed senators toward an upcoming provost-search update and urged them to consider running in the April officer elections. Senators unanimously passed a Me'Lon Craighead bill funding 50 laminated, QR-coded outreach signs for campus and a Matthew Thomas bill adding 12 safe-driving signs to campus parking lots; a bill on WKU's Naming and Symbols Task Force was held for the following week.

Herald, 11 Feb 2021 ↗
2021-03-03

SGA backed renaming buildings tied to racism and funded a community garden

The senate passed legislation by Brenna Matthews and Tess Welch offering SGA's support to the Naming and Symbols Task Force in changing the names of WKU buildings with ties to racism or injustice. Matthews said ties to slave owning and the Confederate army did not belong on a campus committed to diversity. Senators also unanimously funded a community garden outside the Office of Sustainability and allocated $3,500 to four student organizations.

Herald, 3 Mar 2021 ↗
2021-03-18campus

Constitution amended; Caboni's contract extended

Edmonds told senators the WKU Board of Regents had extended President Timothy Caboni's contract four years. The senate elected Addison McCoun to head the Legislative Research Committee after Dawson McCoun's resignation, passed a Tess Welch/Erika Puhakka bill amending constitutional wording 19-4, and funded a Sustainability Committee spring photo contest on a bill by Matthew Waninger and Elizabeth DeLozier.

Herald, 18 Mar 2021 ↗
2021-03-31

Largest organizational aid bill passed; chief justice resigned before elections

Edmonds announced that Chief Justice Erika Puhakka had stepped down and declined to nominate a replacement so close to elections, and warned that only 13 people were running for senate. The senate passed its largest organizational aid bill ever, funding five student groups, and passed 16-0 with two abstentions a bill by Kyle Phillips asking WKU to support a codified vetting process for campus dining vendors after a controversial social media post by the owner of Lost River Pizza.

Herald, 31 Mar 2021 ↗
2021-05-01

Edmonds addressed the Class of 2021 at the delayed commencement

As SGA president, Edmonds spoke at WKU's Fall 2020/Spring 2021 commencement ceremony, telling graduates that the class's perseverance through the pandemic year had brought them to their day of celebration.
