Kevin Smiley president and student regent, 2009-10

Standing of the record
As recorded on the plaque. Not yet corroborated in the archive.

Confirmed against Herald election coverage: Smiley won the SGA presidential election on 16 April 2009. The plaque's separate "2009" plate for Kayla Shelton is not a competing 2009-10 term - Shelton was finishing Johnathon Boles's 2008-09 term after his January 2009 resignation and has been moved to that year. By this period the SGA president automatically held the student regent seat.

Kevin T. Smiley, a senior from Danville, served as SGA president and student regent for 2009-10. His executive branch included Executive Vice President Kendrick Bryan, Administrative Vice President Justin Thurman, and four appointed officials, among them his brother Eric Smiley as chief of staff.

Smiley opened his term with controversy in September 2009 when he nominated his brother, Danville sophomore Eric Smiley, for the paid chief of staff position, saying he chose him because he had no previous political agenda and was "somebody I truly trust." Former executive vice president Skylar Baker-Jordan called the move "completely unethical," though Thurman defended the president's right to pick a qualified adviser.

In October 2009 three senators resigned after Smiley nominated Greg Capillo to the Student Publications Committee, with Michel Stephens charging the pick was not based on merit. Smiley called the accusations of favoritism and corruption unfounded, described the resignations as a misunderstanding, and said he would begin contacting fall election candidates to fill the vacant seats. His term ran through the April 2010 election of his successor.

The year, in order23

September 2009Service
#

Senate bought 1,000 scantrons and 500 blue books for students to take free

In September 2009 the Student Senate approved the purchase of 1,000 scantron forms and 500 blue books. SGA then placed them free of charge in the fifteen residence halls, the Gatton Academy, the SGA office and the computer labs in Helm Library and Mass Media and Technology Hall. The service went back to a bill of April 2007.

1,000 scantron forms and 500 blue books

WKU SGA website, About page, November 2009 (Wayback)

4 September 2009
#

Smiley nominates his brother as chief of staff

SGA President Kevin Smiley announced Danville sophomore Eric Smiley, his brother, as his nominee for chief of staff, a paid voting position on the executive cabinet. Former executive vice president Skylar Baker-Jordan called the nomination "completely unethical," while Smiley said he chose his brother because he had no previous political agenda. The senate was set to vote on all nominations Sept. 8, with a two-thirds majority needed for approval.

Herald, 4 Sep 2009

15 September 2009
#

Fall election opens; SGA gives $2,000 to Residence Hall Association

At the Sept. 15 senate meeting, Administrative Vice President Justin Thurman reported that SGA gave $2,000 from its budget to the Residence Hall Association. President Kevin Smiley announced that voting in the fall SGA election had started on TopNet, with polls open until Wednesday night and results announced about midnight. Smiley also urged senators to join the Campus Safety Walk, in which participants looked for dimly lit or overgrown areas on campus.

Herald, 15 Sep 2009

29 September 2009Service
#

Provide-A-Ride was suspended and students started their own taxi

Parking and Transportation Services announced in September 2009 that Provide-A-Ride was suspended because the contract with Bowling Green Shuttle Service had ended. Three students, Zac Newton, Devon Hilderbrandt and Ryan Holland, set up Topper Taxi in the gap, charging $5 for one rider and $1 for each additional passenger. SGA administrative vice president Justin Thurman sat on the 18 September panel that reviewed bids from prospective providers, with a new operator due to be named on 1 October.

Topper Taxi charged $5 plus $1 per extra rider; Provide-A-Ride was free

Herald, 29 Sep 2009 - "Students give gas to taxi service" (Wayback)

30 September 2009Tradition
#

SGA joined National Make A Difference Week

SGA announced on 30 September 2009 that it was taking part in National Make A Difference Week at Western, a week of causes and fundraisers. It named Habitat for Humanity, Relay for Life, Up 'til Dawn, Volunteering in Progress and United Way Worldwide as partners in the effort.

WKU SGA website, blog, 30 September 2009 (Wayback)

30 September 2009Service
#

Provide-A-Ride came back into service

SGA posted on 30 September 2009 that the Provide-A-Ride service was active again. The free late-night ride home, available from 10 p.m. to 2 a.m. Tuesday to Saturday on production of a current WKU ID, had been suspended earlier that month when the contract with the previous operator ended.

WKU SGA website, blog, 30 September 2009 (Wayback)

14 October 2009
#

SGA sponsors forum on new tailgating policy

About 30 people attended an SGA-sponsored forum where students and administrators discussed Western's amended tailgating policy, which banned vehicles from campus lawns on game days. Howard Bailey, vice president for student affairs, told students the policy would stay the same, and Speaker of the Senate Wade Pierce suggested tent giveaways to ease the transition. President Kevin Smiley said he was pleased with the attendance and that SGA would keep questioning the policy.

Herald, 14 Oct 2009

20 October 2009
#

Three senators resign over publications committee appointment

Senators Michel Stephens, Emmett Stephens and Daniel Shaw resigned at the Oct. 20 meeting. Michel Stephens cited President Kevin Smiley's nomination of Greg Capillo to the Student Publications Committee, which helps choose the Herald's editor-in-chief, saying the pick was not based on merit or experience. Smiley called the accusations of favoritism and corruption unfounded and said the seats would stay vacant until he appointed replacements for senate approval.

Herald, 20 Oct 2009

November 2009Programme
#

Organizational aid ran to more than $40,000 a year

SGA described organizational aid as providing over $40,000 a year to campus organisations, with each organisation able to apply for up to $500. Administrative Vice President Justin Thurman was the contact and the application was posted on the SGA site.

over $40,000 a year, capped at $500 per organisation

WKU SGA website, About page, November 2009 (Wayback)

10 November 2009
#

Judicial Council removes senator for unexcused absences

Chief Justice Stuart Kenderes reported that the Judicial Council removed Senator Theodore Harden, a graduate student from Otisville, N.Y., from SGA for excessive unexcused absences; Harden did not attend the council meeting to defend himself. The council also reviewed Senator Vashae Swope for absences but voted to let her remain. Attendance guidelines were posted in the SGA office window, and Rachel Wilcox was approved as a new senator at the same meeting.

Herald, 10 Nov 2009

12 November 2009Tradition
#

Hall of Distinguished Seniors opened for autumn 2009

SGA extended the deadline for its autumn 2009 Hall of Distinguished Seniors applications on 12 November, to 2 p.m. the following Monday. SGA's website described the award as the highest recognition given to graduates by the student body.

WKU SGA website, blog, November 2009 (Wayback)

16 November 2009Programme
#

Scholar Development grants opened with $12,000 behind them

SGA's Academic Affairs committee and the Director of Academic and Student Affairs were allotted $12,000 to spend on scholarships for the student body, covering textbooks, study abroad, field trips and research conferences. The Scholar Development Grant application, posted on 16 November 2009, let a student apply for $250 at a time up to $500 in an academic year, for professional conferences, research equipment and research expenses. Dave Vickery chaired the committee.

$12,000 allotted; $250 per application, $500 a year

WKU SGA website, Scholar Development Grant post, 16 November 2009 (Wayback)

18 November 2009Programme
#

SGA study abroad scholarship applications opened

SGA announced on 18 November 2009 that its study abroad scholarship was available, describing it as competitive and administered on a rolling basis. The scholarship had been created by a bill in March 2007 and proposed that April to be named for Caitlin Hammaren.

WKU SGA website, Study Abroad Scholarship post, 18 November 2009 (Wayback)

30 November 2009Programme
#

Issues Awareness Week ran on the Hill

SGA announced Issues Awareness Week on the Hill for 30 November to 4 December 2009. The announcement was posted on 28 November; the archive holds no later account of the week itself.

WKU SGA website, blog, 28 November 2009 (Wayback)

8 December 2009Programme
#

SGA sets a dinner with lawmakers

SGA set its Dine with the Decision Makers for the evening of 8 December 2009, from 4:30 to 6 p.m. in the Cupola Room of Downing University Center, so students could talk about higher education with elected officials over dinner. President Kevin Smiley said 25 students had signed up through an online RSVP, a number capped by the cost of the dinner, and that five legislators and Bowling Green mayor Elaine Walker would attend. The event came out of a decision by the Board of Student Body Presidents that autumn that Kentucky's public universities should each mount student lobbying events.

25 students signed up; numbers capped by the cost of the dinner

College Heights Herald 85:24, 8 Dec 2009 - "SGA to dine with lawmakers"

11 December 2009
#

WKU begins weighing a smoke-free campus

After the University of Kentucky and the University of Louisville went smoke-free, the Faculty Welfare Committee of Western's University Senate prepared a smoking survey for students, faculty and staff to take in February. SGA President Kevin Smiley said he would encourage the whole campus to take the survey, and President Gary Ransdell said he would look to SGA, the University Senate and Staff Council for direction on any policy.

Herald, 11 Dec 2009

27 January 2010
#

Two more senators resign; Frankfort rally buses announced

At the Jan. 27 meeting, Aaron Pawley and Eileen Forsythe resigned as senators, Pawley because of a difficult course load and Forsythe because she was studying abroad in England. President Kevin Smiley announced free buses to carry 75 students to a Feb. 9 rally in Frankfort where advocates from Kentucky's public universities would lobby legislators on higher education funding. Western student Colton Jessie was slated to speak at the rally.

Herald, 27 Jan 2010

2 February 2010
#

SGA organizes trip to Rally for Higher Education

SGA and the Provost's office offered free bus transportation for 75 students to the Rally for Higher Education on Feb. 9 at the Kentucky State Capitol, a year after winter weather derailed the previous attempt. Students who registered through the SGA website received an excused absence, and the rally included a chance to meet lawmakers and hear an address from Gov. Steve Beshear.

Herald, 2 Feb 2010

22 February 2010
#

Senate passes funding bills for baseball, a sorority event and study abroad

At its Feb. 22 meeting the senate unanimously passed a funding bill and, with single dissents apiece, a bill funding the baseball program and a $1,300 allocation for an Alpha Kappa Alpha event. It also unanimously approved several study-abroad reimbursement bills and confirmed Daniel Shaw as parliamentarian, along with a new Academic Affairs committee chair nominee and a new Student Affairs chair.

SGA senate minutes, 22 Feb 2010

17 March 2010Service
#

Western Wednesdays announced with twenty-three Bowling Green businesses

SGA announced that its Western Wednesdays discount programme would begin on 17 March 2010, rewarding students who showed a WKU ID while wearing Western apparel. The published list ran to twenty-two businesses, from half-price sandwiches at Jimmy John's and 25 percent off at Hog Wild BBQ to a $3.99 daily buffet at Cici's Pizza for a year. This is SGA's own announcement before the start date, not a report of how the scheme ran.

discounts of 10 to 25 percent at 23 businesses

WKU SGA website, Student Discounts page, March 2010 (Wayback)

23 March 2010
#

SGA passes election codes and two funding bills

The senate passed the spring 2010 election codes, a bill giving $500 to the Agriculture Students Striving for Effective Tomorrows conference for high school students, and a bill designating $1,200 for lodging for FIJIs Across America, in which eight Phi Gamma Delta members planned to cycle across the country for Alzheimer's research. Administrative Vice President Justin Thurman reported that all but three approved organizations had received their organizational aid checks.

Herald, 23 Mar 2010

April 2010
#

Colton Jessie elected SGA president for 2010-11

Bowling Green junior Colton Jessie won the SGA presidency with 63 percent of the vote, taking 726 votes to 424 for Administrative Vice President Justin Thurman, on a platform of sustainability, handicap accessibility and changing the tailgating policy. Kendrick Bryan was re-elected executive vice president with 731 votes to Currie Martin's 415, and Wade Pierce ran unopposed for administrative vice president with 1,016 votes. Results were announced at midnight Wednesday.

Herald, 1 Apr 2010

4 May 2010Programme
#

Western T-shirt swap traded rival logos for WKU red

SGA sponsored a T-shirt swap during finals week of spring 2010, letting students trade in shirts carrying other universities' logos for a Western shirt at the SGA office in DUC 130. Student affairs committee head Emmy Woosley introduced the idea; executive vice president Kendrick Bryan said the collected shirts would go to a philanthropy, though which one had not been decided. SGA also planned an event near Java City in Helm Library during the extended library hours it paid for that week.

College Heights Herald 85:51, 4 May 2010 - "SGA aims to redefine campus style with Western T-shirt swap" (TopSCHOLAR)

What this is

A year-by-year record of student government at Western Kentucky University, from the ratification of the Associated Students constitution in April 1966 to the present. Every entry names the source it came from.

Corrections

The names here start with the plaque in the SGA Chambers, and the plaque is known to be wrong in places. Where the archive disagrees with it, this site follows the archive and records the change on the corrections page. If you served, or you know that a year here is wrong, the project wants to hear from you: people who held an office can be given the keys to their own year on the contribute page.

What it rests on

Everything here comes from what is publicly available online: the digitised Herald and SGA's own papers on TopSCHOLAR, the Talisman yearbooks, wkuherald.com, the university's news pages and the Wayback Machine. That is a fraction of what happened. Student government has met most weeks for sixty years, and the greater part of it was never written down, or was written down on paper that has never been scanned. A thin year here is a thin record, not a quiet one.

Reuse

Text on this site is the work of the project. Photographs, documents and legislation are reproduced from Western Kentucky University's own open archives and from the College Heights Herald and the Talisman; each item names the collection, issue and page it came from and links to the original.
