Jamie Sears — the term

Sears, a junior public relations major from Melber, won the presidency in a landslide, defeating senior Sam Stinson 1,284 votes to 594 in the April 2002 general election that also seated her incoming executive team: John Bradley as executive vice president, Ross Pruitt as vice president of finance, Anna Coats as vice president of public relations, and Brandon Copeland as vice president of administration.

Sears opened her 2002-03 term as SGA president under immediate scrutiny. Seventeen months after congress passed a bill putting about $5,000 of leftover budget money toward an on-campus gazebo, members discovered the gazebo was never built and the money was gone. When three members petitioned congress in September over $28,000 they said was unaccounted for, Sears told the Herald, "I have absolutely no idea where that money went." The petition was withdrawn only after her executive council created an ad hoc financial review committee.

By October the money had been traced: it had slipped back into SGA's general fund and been spent on other things, including computers for the SGA office. Sears acknowledged that she and former student activities director Scott Taylor had decided during the summer where the leftover money, which included the gazebo funds, would be allocated, saying other executive council members were consulted through email. She said the financial review committee would explain the budget findings to congress, and no move was made to set aside new gazebo money.

Sears finished the year on stronger footing. She traveled with about 20 Western students to Frankfort to oppose state budget cuts to higher education, calling the trip one of SGA's greatest accomplishments: "That was a great feeling seeing students from everywhere rallying for the same cause." Under her leadership congress debated more than 55 pieces of legislation and passed the first student referendum in SGA history, a $3 fee for radio station Revolution 91.7 that students approved in the spring election. Her final meeting as president came on April 22, 2003.

What happened, in order

2002-08-22campus

Record enrollment of 17,500 strains campus

Western opened the fall with enrollment ballooning to 17,500, nearly 1,000 more students than the year before and a 20 percent increase since 1997. President Gary Ransdell warned that growth would be "painful" amid decreased state funding, and the Board of Regents approved a 10.4 percent tuition increase for 2003-04.

Herald, 22 Aug 2002 ↗
2002-09-19

Members demand answers on missing gazebo money

SGA members Brooke Smith, Dana Lockhart and Sam Stinson petitioned congress over $28,000 they said was unaccounted for, including about $5,000 that a April 2001 bill had set aside to build an on-campus gazebo that never appeared. President Jamie Sears said of the gazebo fund, "I have absolutely no idea where that money went." The petition was withdrawn after the executive council formed an ad hoc financial review committee.

Herald, 19 Sep 2002 ↗
2002-09-24

Herald editorial blasts SGA over 'Gazebo-Gate'

The Herald's 11-member editorial board attacked SGA leaders over what it called "Gazebo-Gate," saying the money had gone to computers for the SGA office instead of the promised gazebo. It called leaders' ignorance about the missing money "shameful" and challenged Sears by name to "do her job" and provide real answers, writing that "I don't know" did not cut it.

Herald, 24 Sep 2002 ↗
2002-10-01

Gazebo funds traced to SGA general fund

Vice president of finance Ross Pruitt reported that the $13,000 SGA had put toward the gazebo mistakenly went back into the organization's general fund and was spent on other things, including computers for SGA. Sears said she and former student activities director Scott Taylor decided over the summer where the leftover budget money would go, with other executive council members consulted by email. Pruitt said leaders from the past two administrations shared the blame, saying "I didn't do my homework."

Herald, 1 Oct 2002 ↗
2002-10-24

Congress votes down enrollment cap resolution

SGA congress rejected by a large margin a resolution asking Western to implement an enrollment cap, with Sears among those calling the measure too drastic. The failed proposal argued the university and state budgets had not kept pace with enrollment growth from 14,543 students in 1997 to about 17,770. Provost Barbara Burch and President Ransdell both said a cap was not the best answer to the growth problem.

Herald, 24 Oct 2002 ↗
2002-12-05

Three resolutions pass on faculty pay, enrollment, parking

SGA passed two resolutions responding to a shortage of 75 faculty members and the lowest-paid faculty in the state, asking that unbudgeted tuition surplus money, about $800,000 that year, be used to hire faculty, and encouraging President Ransdell to create a task force on enrollment. A third resolution requested a change to the policy that gave Hilltopper Athletic Foundation donors student parking spaces, including the Grise Hall lot, at basketball games.

Herald, 5 Dec 2002 ↗
2003-02-06

Freshman committee appointments tabled

SGA tabled Sears's proposal to appoint three freshmen, Tim Howard, Katie Dawson and Robert Watkins, as the student members of a joint University Senate and SGA enrollment growth committee. Sears said she picked freshmen so they could serve the committee's expected two to three year life, but critics including associate justice Dana Lockhart said the appointments would make it hard for SGA to be taken seriously.

Herald, 6 Feb 2003 ↗
2003-03-04

SGA joins Frankfort rally against budget cuts

About 200 students from six public universities rallied at the state Capitol against cuts to higher education, after the Kentucky House passed a budget cutting Western's funding by more than $1 million. Western sent a delegation of roughly 20 people including SGA members, organized through the board of Kentucky's student body presidents. Sears said 17 people "took the initiative to get on the bus."

Herald, 4 Mar 2003 ↗
2003-03-04

Congress puts $3 radio fee referendum on ballot

SGA unanimously approved a bill placing a referendum on the April 2-3 general election ballot letting students vote on a $3 student fee for campus radio station Revolution 91.7-WWHR, which was operating on a $7,000 budget unchanged since 1998. Executive vice president John Bradley, who championed the measure, said it would empower students and boost voter turnout. A yes vote would send the fee recommendation to the Board of Regents for a final decision.

Herald, 4 Mar 2003 ↗
2003-04-08

Bradley elected president; 2,014 vote

A total of 2,014 students voted in the spring SGA election, with results announced shortly after 1 a.m. Friday by chief justice Holly Skidmore. Paducah junior John Bradley won the presidency unopposed with 1,662 votes, Patti Johnson beat Dana Lockhart 1,185 to 685 for executive vice president, and Jessica Martin beat Shawn Peavie 1,056 to 686 for vice president of administration. The $3 WWHR radio fee referendum passed 1,066 to 765 and moved to the Board of Regents.

Herald, 8 Apr 2003 ↗
2003-04-29

SGA closes year after 55 pieces of legislation

SGA held its last meeting of the year on April 22, having heard and debated more than 55 pieces of legislation, including a resolution that earmarked funds for new benches across campus. Sears counted the Frankfort rally among the year's greatest accomplishments, and the Revolution 91.7 fee measure stood as the first student referendum in SGA history, awaiting a final Board of Regents vote on May 9. President-elect Bradley said the high election turnout was the semester's best moment and pledged to fight student apathy the next year.
