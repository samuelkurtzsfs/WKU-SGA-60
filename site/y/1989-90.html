Amos Gott president and student regent, 1989-90

Office
President and student regent
Term recorded here
1989-90
Place in the line
the 23rd president and the 21st student regent
Standing of the record
As recorded on the plaque. Not yet corroborated in the archive.

The best-documented pre-1992 presidency in the archive. Signed correspondence survives on extended library hours, the DUC flagpole, the stadium press box and a local pizza vendor dispute.

Gott was a visible member before he was president, signing Herald letters on ASG's behalf, including a January 1989 piece promoting Campus Pride '89 and an earlier letter urging readers to understand the organization.

He won the presidency in April 1989, edging Lynn Groemling in a race that had drawn candidate complaints about the brief primary.

His presidency ran on correspondence: resolutions went out over his signature as memoranda to named administrators and answers came back. In spring 1990 he wrote to six administrators under Resolution 90-6-S seeking changes to the final exam schedule, part of a surviving file that also covers extended library hours, the Downing University Center flagpole, mace sales at the bookstore, AIDS education and larger diplomas.

Gott was already writing on Associated Student Government's behalf before he led it, signing a January 1989 Herald piece promoting Campus Pride '89. That spring he ran for president, survived a primary that several candidates complained had been too brief, and then edged Lynn Groemling in the runoff to take the office for 1989-90.

He started early. The Herald's second issue of the fall reported that Gott had gotten a jump on the ASG semester, running alongside a piece urging students to participate. The fall that followed was busy and money was tight: the paper reported ASG's budget was tiny compared with other schools, the congress approved a new statewide board before admitting it could not afford the dues, and it spent the semester picking up and putting down a proposed Public Interest Research Group.

Gott opened the spring with a Herald letter asking students to become involved, and by February he was defending an Anti-Apathy Week in print after it drew criticism. The rest of his spring ran through the congress: opening Pearce-Ford Tower's windows, more parking on the lower end of campus, a revised WKU Glasgow constitution, and a vote to expand the congress so classes and student groups had more voice. He handed off to Michael Colvin in April 1990.

The year, in order35

1989
#

Resolution 89-3-F asks for AIDS education

Under Resolution 89-3-F, ASG corresponded with administrators requesting education on AIDS, HIV and sexually transmitted diseases. The letters survive in the SGA records alongside the year's other resolution correspondence on mace sales, larger diplomas and a food services committee.

TopSCHOLAR SGA Reports 52

24 August 1989
#

Gott gets a jump on the semester

The Herald reported that Amos Gott had gotten a jump on the ASG semester, opening his presidency before classes settled in. A companion piece urged students to participate in the organization.

Herald 65:2, 24 Aug 1989

29 August 1989
#

The year opened with Glasgow seats and a Judicial Board

The first minutes of the term, dated 29 August 1989, record Judicial Board candidates, an explanation of procedures, the inclusion of Glasgow representatives, committee organisational reports and the freshman elections. Three bylaw amendments followed on 5 September, on the president's board, on how legislation was to be written and submitted, and on the order of business.

ASG meeting minutes, 29 August 1989

12 September 1989
#

A budget tiny compared to other schools

The Herald reported that ASG's budget was tiny compared to student governments at other schools. Two days later the congress approved joining a new board but admitted it could not afford the membership.

Herald 65:6, 12 Sep 1989

21 September 1989
#

A student government organised itself on the Glasgow campus

On 21 September 1989 the students of Western's Glasgow campus held an organisational meeting to settle a name, officers and elections for a student body of their own. A week later, on 28 September, the first executive council of the WKU-Glasgow Associated Student Government met, took homecoming and Christmas activities, drew up by-laws, and made a list of businesses to approach about homecoming. The Bowling Green congress had voted in August to include Glasgow representatives, and its minutes of 19 September record a Glasgow campus constitution.

Associated Student Body-Glasgow organisational meeting minutes, 21 September 1989

28 September 1989
#

ASG joins the Board of Student Body Presidents

Associated Student Government joined the Board of Student Body Presidents, two weeks after approving the new board while saying it could not afford to join. The same issue reported that the congress had punted a decision on a Public Interest Research Group.

Herald 65:11, 28 Sep 1989

3 October 1989Programme
#

Alcohol Awareness Week

The minutes of 3 October 1989 record an Alcohol Awareness Week alongside the master plan committee, training sessions, the shuttle shelter, condoms and International Day. Alcohol came back before the congress on 7 November, when the minutes record petitions on alcohol and on twenty-four hour visitation.

ASG meeting minutes, 3 October 1989

6 October 1989Festival
#

Glasgow's homecoming, Red and White Day and Women in Transition

The Glasgow student government's minutes of 6 October 1989 record homecoming events, elections, a Red and White Day, Women in Transition and Christmas activities. Its minutes of 13 October return to homecoming and fundraising, and those of 27 October add daycare and a book exchange. This is the founding programme of a student government that had existed for a fortnight.

Associated Student Body-Glasgow meeting minutes, 6 October 1989

10 October 1989Programme
#

Kentucky Higher Education Week and a professor of the year

The minutes of 10 October 1989 record Kentucky Higher Education Week, homecoming, and Luther Hughes as professor of the year. A leadership reception follows in the minutes of 14 November.

ASG meeting minutes, 10 October 1989

12 October 1989
#

A push for weekend Unicorn pizza

The Herald reported that Associated Student Government wanted weekend service from the Unicorn pizza vendor. The dispute stayed in the paper: on 24 October the Herald ran an editorial cartoon showing ASG and the administration riding a unicorn.

Herald 65:15, 12 Oct 1989

17 October 1989
#

Student government goes to Glasgow

Student government went to the WKU Glasgow campus, and the congress agreed to look again at a Public Interest Research Group. Both stories ran in the same mid-October issue.

Herald 65:16, 17 Oct 1989

27 October 1989
#

Unicorn Pizza's weekend hours were granted on trial

Correspondence dated 27 October 1989 replied to Resolution 89-2-F by expanding Unicorn Pizza's weekend hours on a trial basis. The congress had asked for the extension on 10 October, after the Herald reported the demand on 12 October and then drew ASG and the administration riding a unicorn. It is one of the year's few requests with a recorded answer.

SGA correspondence on Unicorn Pizza, 27 October 1989

31 October 1989
#

A student food services committee, and a forum with it

Resolution 89-6-F, dated 31 October 1989, asked for a student advisory food services committee. The minutes of 24 October record a food service forum, and correspondence answering the resolution survives dated 9 November 1989. The same 31 October meeting produced Resolution 89-4-F on weekend library hours.

Resolution 89-6-F, Food Services Committee, 31 October 1989

Extended library hours

Correspondence between President Amos Gott, Howard Bailey, Sally Ann Strickler and Michael Binder. Resolutions went out as letters to named administrators and answers came back.

Glasgow planned Christmas activities and a parade

Big Red Day for the mascot's tenth birthday

Resolution 89-9-F, dated 28 November 1989, made 2 December 1989 Big Red Day in honour of the mascot's tenth anniversary. ASG voted down the proposed Public Interest Research Group, saying its fee system was the culprit. The decision ended a semester of on-and-off consideration of the group.

Herald 65:27, 30 Nov 1989

A recorded information line asked for again

Resolution 90-2-S, dated 16 January 1990, asked for a recorded announcement telephone line. The congress had tabled the same idea in February 1987, when the equipment was costed at $500 with $60 for installation and $17 a month, and had set up a Wednesday afternoon hotline instead in October 1988.

The Downing University Center flagpole

One item in a working inventory of the job that year: the flagpole, the stadium press box, a dispute involving a local pizza vendor and a proposed budget for the Board of Student Body Presidents.

A Valentine's dance and the organisational presidents roundtable

The minutes of 13 February 1990 record a Valentine's dance and the organisational presidents roundtable, with parking, awards and vacancies. The roundtable was put on a formal footing that autumn by Bill 90-2-F.

ASG meeting minutes, 13 February 1990

15 February 1990
#

Anti-Apathy Week on tap

The Herald reported that Associated Student Government had an Anti-Apathy Week coming up. The idea drew enough criticism that President Amos Gott wrote a defense of it in the paper a week later.

Herald 65:40, 15 Feb 1990

20 February 1990Programme
#

Earth Day, Earth Week and a tree planting

Earth Day is in the minutes of 20 February 1990 with the telephone surveys and the elections, Earth Week in those of 27 February and 10 April, and a tree planting in those of 3 April. The 1990 Earth Day was its twentieth anniversary and the Herald reported on 24 April that its organisers hoped public interest would hold.

ASG meeting minutes, 20 February 1990

22 February 1990
#

Open the windows in Pearce-Ford Tower

Associated Student Government said it wanted the windows in Pearce-Ford Tower opened. The Herald had reported two days earlier that the congress was set to vote on the resolution. The same issue carried Gott's defense of Anti-Apathy Week.

Herald 65:42, 22 Feb 1990

13 March 1990Lecture
#

A Mitch McConnell lecture put on the congress's agenda

The minutes of 13 March 1990 record a Mitch McConnell lecture alongside a Drug Awareness and Health Enrichment Week and the coming elections. McConnell was then Kentucky's junior United States senator. The minutes record what was arranged; they do not report the lecture.

ASG meeting minutes, 13 March 1990

20 March 1990Lecture
#

Gatewood Galbraith spoke on legalising drugs

The Herald of 22 March 1990 reported Gatewood Galbraith arguing for the legalisation of drugs, in a piece by Angie Jones. The minutes of 20 March 1990 record a Gatewood Galbraith lecture on the legalisation of drugs alongside elections and open positions.

Herald 65:47, 22 Mar 1990

20 March 1990
#

Two twenty-four hour visitation halls asked for

Resolution 90-13-S, dated 20 March 1990, asked for two residence halls with twenty-four hour coed visitation. The minutes of 24 October 1989 record twenty-four hour visitation among the congress's business, and those of 7 November record petitions on it and on alcohol.

Resolution 90-13-S, 24 Hour Visitation, 20 March 1990

29 March 1990
#

Congress votes to expand

ASG voted to expand its congress, a week after reporting it might add representatives. A follow-up story said classes and student groups would get more voice in the organization.

Herald 65:49, 29 Mar 1990

10 April 1990
#

A resolution on getting into the buildings

Resolution 90-17-S, dated 10 April 1990, asked for improvements to the accessibility of campus buildings. It was one of the last pieces of legislation of Amos Gott's term.

Resolution 90-17-S, Handicapped Accessibility, 10 April 1990

12 April 1990
#

Withdrawals save an invalid primary

Candidates withdrew from the ASG races, saving a primary that would otherwise have been invalid; only two candidates were running for president. The Herald had already reported that interest in the organization might be lower, illustrated by a cartoon of student government in an empty room.

Herald 65:53, 12 Apr 1990

17 April 1990
#

ASG endorsed the fee rise that built the Preston Center

Resolution 90-18-S, dated 17 April 1990, approved the Board of Regents' increase in the student fee used to pay construction costs for the Preston Health and Activities Center. The regents set the 1990-91 budget and tuition six weeks later.

student construction fee increase approved by the regents

Resolution 90-18-S, Preston Health & Activities Center Construction Fees, 17 April 1990

19 April 1990
#

Michael Colvin wins, and a call to raise the activity fee

Michael Colvin aced the Associated Student Government election to succeed Amos Gott. The same issue reported that ASG wanted the student activity fee raised, a direct answer to the budget complaints that had run through the year.

Herald 65:55, 19 Apr 1990

30 May 1990
#

Regents set the 1990-91 tuition and operating budget

WKU's Board of Regents met in special session and approved the university's 1990-91 operating budget, tuition rates, and personnel changes ahead of Colvin's term.

WKU Board of Regents, special meeting, 30 May 1990

What this is

A year-by-year record of student government at Western Kentucky University, from the ratification of the Associated Students constitution in April 1966 to the present. Every entry names the source it came from.

Corrections

The names here start with the plaque in the SGA Chambers, and the plaque is known to be wrong in places. Where the archive disagrees with it, this site follows the archive and records the change on the corrections page. If you served, or you know that a year here is wrong, the project wants to hear from you: people who held an office can be given the keys to their own year on the contribute page.

What it rests on

Everything here comes from what is publicly available online: the digitised Herald and SGA's own papers on TopSCHOLAR, the Talisman yearbooks, wkuherald.com, the university's news pages and the Wayback Machine. That is a fraction of what happened. Student government has met most weeks for sixty years, and the greater part of it was never written down, or was written down on paper that has never been scanned. A thin year here is a thin record, not a quiet one.

Reuse

Text on this site is the work of the project. Photographs, documents and legislation are reproduced from Western Kentucky University's own open archives and from the College Heights Herald and the Talisman; each item names the collection, issue and page it came from and links to the original.
