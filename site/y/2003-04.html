John Bradley president and student regent, 2003-04

Office
President and student regent
Term recorded here
2003-04
Place in the line
the 37th president and the 35th student regent
Standing of the record
As recorded on the plaque. Not yet corroborated in the archive.

Presided over the 2004 Constitutional Convention that created the three-branch structure. Quoted in Herald coverage of the investigation into his successor-elect.

Bradley, a Paducah junior and sitting executive vice president, ran unopposed for SGA president in spring 2003, campaigning alongside Jessica Martin and Dana Lockhart on parking, keeping the cost of education low and making campus friendlier to students with disabilities. He counted the WWHR radio-fee student referendum among his accomplishments as executive vice president, and said he had worked to "depolarize the organization." As president he would also serve as the student member of the Board of Regents.

He opened the fall 2003 semester by naming low education costs his top priority, opposing plans to pass Greek Village land costs on to all students, and steering SGA toward campus safety after serving on the summer's Campus Safety Task Force. He launched an SGA Ambassador Council to visit student organizations weekly and scheduled the semester's first forum on campus safety.

The year, in order39

19 August 2003
#

Bradley opens the year with cost, safety and involvement goals

New SGA president John Bradley opened the fall by naming low education costs his first goal and warning that fees for the proposed Greek Village should not be passed to the whole student body. SGA created an Ambassador Council to visit student organizations weekly, and planned its first forum of the semester on campus safety, an issue Bradley had worked on as a member of the Campus Safety Task Force formed after the death of freshman Katie Autry. The first congress meeting of the semester was set for August 26 in Downing University Center.

Herald, 19 Aug 2003

28 August 2003
#

Greek Village debate opens at SGA meeting

Students began voicing opinions about the proposed Greek Village, and how it might be paid for, at a Student Government Association meeting. The Herald reported some saw the project as a way to bring Greeks together while others called it a waste of money.

Herald, 28 Aug 2003

11 September 2003
#

SGA proposes campus safety commission

Campus improvements committee chair Shawn Peavie, a Monticello junior, announced that SGA was looking into creating a campus safety commission. The commission would test safety measures ranging from checking locks to recording emergency response times.

Herald, 11 Sep 2003

11 September 2003Programme
#

Campus safety forum in DUC 305

President John Bradley opened SGA's campus safety forum on 11 September 2003 in Downing University Center room 305, taking questions from the floor and from students who had emailed. Chief Deane, Captain Wallace, Dean Howard Bailey and Dr Tice answered on the police escort service, crosswalks after a pedestrian was struck on University Boulevard, and unlit paths behind Rhodes-Harlin and McCormack.

SGA, Notes: SGA Campus Safety Forum, 11 Sep 2003 (Wayback capture)

18 September 2003
#

IT vice president asks SGA to cut directory data

Richard Kirchmeyer, vice president for Information Technology, told SGA at its Tuesday meeting that some personal student information should be removed from the print and online campus directory to protect against identity theft. SGA weighed whether to adopt the recommendations.

Herald, 18 Sep 2003

25 September 2003
#

SGA lines up against plus/minus grading

SGA members voiced concern at their Tuesday meeting over a plus/minus grading system proposed in the University Senate by economics professor Brian Strow. Bradley said he planned to oppose it, reporting that the state's other student body presidents had urged him to fight it, and the Academic Affairs committee prepared a student questionnaire on the change. Chief Justice Troy Ransdell argued the system would penalize students who scored best.

Herald, 25 Sep 2003

30 September 2003
#

SGA takes up $8,000 shuttle shelter legislation

Legislation requesting $8,000 for a shuttle bus shelter at the Jones-Jaggers stop was set for SGA's next meeting. Executive Vice President Patti Johnson said the Campus Improvements Committee brought it forward. Approval of student representatives to the University Senate was also on the agenda.

Herald, 30 Sep 2003

30 September 2003Programme
#

$2,012.50 of giveaways to make SGA visible

Bill 03-02-F allocated up to $2,012.50 for more than 4,750 promotional items, pens, note pads, sweets, lanyards and flashlight keychains, all carrying the SGA name. They were given away at SGA Day, at JUMP tables and at other events, and a follow-up bill added $65 in November when the supplier had underquoted.

$2,012.50, plus $65 added in November

14 October 2003Programme
#

Organizational Aid reaches $19,000

Bill 03-04-F disbursed $19,000 in Organizational Aid, spread across dozens of registered organisations and the largest Organizational Aid payout this archive holds for the decade. Awards ran from $50 to Beta Gamma Sigma up to $425 for Amazing Tones of Joy and $400 for the Black Student Alliance, and SGA took sponsorship credit under its bylaws for every event the grants paid for.

$19,000 total

SGA unanimously passes legislation against plus/minus grading

SGA unanimously passed legislation at its Tuesday meeting objecting to a proposed plus/minus grading system. Economics professor Brian Strow was to formally propose the system to the University Senate that day.

Herald, 16 Oct 2003

SGA escalates plus/minus fight to rally and donors

SGA President John Bradley said the association would use all its possible tools to stop the plus/minus grading proposal, including a rally and meetings with university donors. The escalation followed SGA's passage of legislation opposing the system.

Herald, 21 Oct 2003

SGA demands two weeks notice on dining service changes

SGA unanimously passed legislation calling on Dining Services to give students more notification of changes to service hours or facilities. The resolution recommended that changes be posted and advertised at least two weeks before taking effect.

30 October 2003
#

Congress urges students to vote as grading petition circulates

SGA unanimously passed a resolution recommending that Western students vote in the November 4 state election and research the candidates' stands on higher education, suspending the bylaws so it could pass before the vote. Members also reported that the petition against plus/minus grading had gathered 350 signatures with more petitions in circulation, and SGA planned a Homecoming tent on the Downing University Center south lawn.

Herald, 30 Oct 2003

#

SGA suspends by-laws to urge students to vote Nov. 4

SGA unanimously passed a resolution at its Tuesday meeting recommending that Western's student body vote in the Nov. 4 election. Bowling Green sophomore Robert Watkins, an SGA member and the author of the legislation, moved to suspend the by-laws so the resolution could be passed.

Herald, 30 Oct 2003

Correction clarifies Bradley stance on student regent tuition votes

The Herald corrected a front-page misquotation of SGA President John Bradley. There was a consensus among Kentucky student government presidents that student regents should not vote for tuition increases, but Bradley said he had never personally committed to withholding such a vote.

The last Congress meeting of the autumn, with the banquet to follow

SGA asks that student addresses in directories be optional

SGA passed a resolution on Tuesday asking the university to make access to student addresses in the printed and online directories optional. The legislation stated that students' permission should be obtained before their personal information is published.

The Constitutional Convention rebuilds the body

Three branches - Executive Cabinet, Senate and Judicial Council - drawn from the student body at large. The most consequential clause is the quietest: every enrolled student became a member of SGA with the right to vote in its elections.

WKU SGA history

29 January 2004
#

SGA organizes for a higher education rally in Frankfort

SGA prepared to join student governments from across Kentucky at a February 5 rally at the state Capitol against cuts to higher education funding, with slogans such as "Don't Balance the Budget on Our Backs" and students asked to wear backpacks. Congress approved $650 for transportation and reserved 47 spots for the trip. Bradley conceded change that year might be "unreasonable" to expect but said rallying could affect the future.

Herald, 29 Jan 2004

5 February 2004
#

SGA budget frozen as Western absorbs $5.6 million in cuts

SGA announced at its Tuesday meeting that its budget, along with the Campus Activities Board's, had been informally frozen while Western worked out how to cut $5.6 million ordered by Gov. Ernie Fletcher's administration. SGA held $65,309.52 in its 2003-04 budget at the time, plus about $7,400 in an Aramark contract and foundation accounts that were not affected. Bradley said congress could still pass legislation requiring money, with the director of student activities deciding whether funds were available.

Herald, 5 Feb 2004

5 February 2004

SGA and CAB freeze spending ahead of budget cuts

SGA and the Campus Activities Board informally froze their budgets in anticipation of budget cuts to university departments. The groups would spend no money until President Gary Ransdell presented his budget cut plan to the Board of Regents.

Herald, 5 Feb 2004

12 February 2004

Congress approved the new constitution on the fifth attempt

The Herald reported, in its issue of 12 February 2004, that the Student Government Association had approved a new constitution on the fifth attempt, in an article bylined Ashlee Clark.

College Heights Herald 78:37 [Vol. 79], 12 Feb 2004, index (TopSCHOLAR)

26 February 2004
#

SGA budget to fall by up to $17,000

SGA expected a $15,000 to $17,000 decrease in its 2004-2005 budget as part of university-wide cuts. SGA vice president Nick Todd announced the reduction at Tuesday's meeting, and the Herald reported it could affect the campus improvements committee and organizational aid.

Herald, 26 Feb 2004

16 March 2004
#

Editorial faults students after 132 of 18,000 vote

A Herald editorial criticized student participation in SGA after only 132 of about 18,000 students voted on the organization's new constitution. The editorial contrasted that figure with the 86 percent of citizens who voted in South Africa's 1999 general elections.

Herald, 16 Mar 2004

18 March 2004
#

Nick Todd elected SGA president as 1,232 students vote

Vice president of finance Nick Todd won the SGA presidency with 772 votes to Jessica Martin's 424, in a two-day online election in which 1,232 students voted. Katie Dawson won executive vice president with 778 votes and Evelina Petkova won administrative vice president with 692, with the first elected senate under the new constitution chosen at the same time. Bradley said he was disappointed by the turnout, and the winners were set to be sworn in at the SGA banquet on April 27.

Herald, 18 Mar 2004

18 March 2004
#

Congress buys stepping stones for muddy campus paths

SGA unanimously approved legislation allocating $200 from its campus improvements account for stepping stones near Mass Media and Technology Hall, Tate Page Hall and Southwest Hall, where student foot traffic had worn dirt and mud paths. The bill's authors, Shawn Peavie and Sarah Cecil, said sidewalks had been considered but were too expensive. In the same meeting a straw poll on a proposed dorm Internet service fee drew 3 votes in support, 11 against and 11 abstentions.

Herald, 18 Mar 2004

20 March 2004Service
#

Buy-A-Book gets a publicity campaign

Bill 04-07-S committed SGA to promote the Buy-A-Book programme through Channel 12, Revolution 91.7, the SGA website, campus kiosks, residence halls and organisation mailboxes. The authors wrote it because students had complained they never heard about the scholarship.

TopSCHOLAR - Bill 04-07-S, Buy-A-Book Program Promotion

8 April 2004
#

SGA races to spend its remaining $45,000

With about $45,000 left of its original $105,000 budget for 2003-04, SGA moved to spend the remainder before the semester ended, since leftover funds would go to offset budget cuts across the university rather than carry over. Officers attributed the surplus partly to frugality during the spring budget freeze, and Todd suggested putting the money toward campus improvements such as benches or shuttle shelters. SGA also sponsored a dining services forum that week with Auxiliary Services and Dining Services officials.

Herald, 8 Apr 2004

13 April 2004
#

Robert Watkins elected first speaker of the senate on a disputed 9-8 vote

Congress elected Bowling Green sophomore Robert Watkins, who wrote the new constitution, as the first speaker of the senate by 9 votes to 8 over Brittany Fausey, with 17 of 20 members present voting. The result was immediately questioned because the new constitution was unclear on whether two-thirds of eligible voters or of total membership was required, and three members present were barred from voting under a judicial council ruling. Martin warned of a "semester of frustration" if the dispute was not settled at the year's final meeting. Fausey declined to challenge the result. SGA's own minutes date the vote to the 13 April meeting; the Herald's report ran two days later.

The president-elect is investigated before he takes office

The Herald reported an investigation into missing funds from an SGA dining account. Articles of impeachment were drafted against the president-elect's existing finance post but the judicial council found the two-week process could not finish before the term ended. Because state law governs the student regent seat, the new constitution required a separate special election to fill it.

SGA president-elect investigated over Dining Dollars account

Nick Todd, SGA's president-elect and vice president of finance, was suspected of misusing more than $600 through food purchases from an SGA Dining Dollars account. The Herald reported the allegation could jeopardize his status as president-elect and that Todd could not be reached for comment despite repeated attempts.

Herald, 20 Apr 2004

What this is

A year-by-year record of student government at Western Kentucky University, from the ratification of the Associated Students constitution in April 1966 to the present. Every entry names the source it came from.

Corrections

The names here start with the plaque in the SGA Chambers, and the plaque is known to be wrong in places. Where the archive disagrees with it, this site follows the archive and records the change on the corrections page. If you served, or you know that a year here is wrong, the project wants to hear from you: people who held an office can be given the keys to their own year on the contribute page.

What it rests on

Everything here comes from what is publicly available online: the digitised Herald and SGA's own papers on TopSCHOLAR, the Talisman yearbooks, wkuherald.com, the university's news pages and the Wayback Machine. That is a fraction of what happened. Student government has met most weeks for sixty years, and the greater part of it was never written down, or was written down on paper that has never been scanned. A thin year here is a thin record, not a quiet one.
