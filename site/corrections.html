What the plaque gets wrong

Corrections and open questions

The names in this archive begin as a claim made by the plaque in the SGA Chambers. People who served have reported that their year on it is wrong, so every plate is treated as something to check. This page is the running account of what has been moved, what is still unsettled, and what has simply not been confirmed yet.

A correction is not an embarrassment. It is the part of the record that shows the work.

Corrections made9

The archive placed these terms somewhere other than the plaque does. The site follows the archive.

Larry Zielke 1969-70

What the plaque reads
1970-71
Where this site places the term
1969-70
Office
President

Plaque reads 1970-71, paired with John Lyne. Herald coverage places Zielke's actual term here, in 1969-70: he and Frank Genzianelli 'clash for the presidency' in the 25 March 1969 special election edition, and by 22 May 1969 the Herald reports Zielke and running mate David Porter outlining their incoming administration - the term that would open that fall. Lyne, who lost the companion 'second spot' race to Porter in the same 1969 election, went on to win the presidency unopposed in March 1970 for 1970-71 (see that year). The plaque's 1970-71 pairing of Lyne and Zielke conflates two separate one-year terms.

Christy Vogt 1976-77

What the plaque reads
1976-77
Where this site places the term
1976-77
Office
President

The plaque reads "Christy Mollozzi". Every contemporaneous source calls her Christy Vogt: she bylined Herald pieces as Vogt through 1976-77, the paper covered her as ASG president under that name, and SGA's own roster of former presidents, archived in September 2001, lists "Christy Vogt, Louisville, 1976-77" and stars her as a student regent. This site follows the sources. Vogt was administrative vice president in 1975-76 before winning the presidency. As with Coates and Lich, and Seay and Taylor, the plaque's later surname is most likely a married name, but no source found states the connection. The Herald's 9 April 1976 headline "Christy Vogt Wins Election" and her own "Winner Says Thanks" the same issue confirm the 1976-77 ASG presidency was won under the name Christy Vogt; her byline runs through the term (campaign endorsement 26 Mar 1976, "Urges Involvement" 12 Apr 1977, the closing letter to Congress 26 Apr 1977). The plaque records her as Christy Mollozzi. WKU's own archive ties the two names together in multiple places written by the university itself, not by this project: a WKU Archives research notecard indexed as "Mollozzi, Christy Vogt," and Christy Mollozzi appearing in the President's Office finding aids for the Meredith and Ransdell administrations in connection with Marketing & Promotions, meaning she had a further career on the WKU staff. "Christy Vogt Mollozzi" follows the ordinary American convention of a married name with the maiden name kept as a middle name. No single source states outright "formerly Christy Vogt," so this is an inference from a consistent name pattern repeated across independent WKU records over three decades, not a documented fact - flagged here rather than asserted as certain.

The plaque reads "Marcell Bush" with two Ls. Herald bylines spell it Marcel throughout, and SGA's own roster of former presidents, archived in September 2001, lists "Marcel Bush, Lexington, 1981-82" with a footnote recording that he resigned in January 1982 and that administrative vice president David Payne filled the position. This site follows the sources. The roster stars Bush as a student regent and does not star Payne, which is why the Board seat went to a special election in February 1982 rather than passing with the presidency. Resigned the ASG presidency 14 January 1982; David Payne succeeded him for the balance of the term.

Amanda Coates 1999-00

What the plaque reads
1999-2000
Where this site places the term
1999-00
Office
President

The plaque reads "Amanda Lich". No source from her SGA year uses that surname; the Herald has her winning the presidency in a recount on 15 April 1999, covers her as president through the autumn, and ran her year-end reflection on 25 April 2000, all as Amanda Coates. The two names are now confirmed as one person, from two sittings of the Kentucky Council on Postsecondary Education: its minutes of 13 November 2000 introduce the incoming holder of the council's statewide student seat as Amanda Coates, "a graduate of Western Kentucky University", and its minutes of 30 July 2001, recording Governor Patton's appointment of her successor, name the outgoing member "Amanda Coates Lich." The first carries the Coates of Western into the seat and the second carries the surname Lich out of it. This site does not otherwise report what became of her after her SGA service, in keeping with its rule against publishing a living person's post-office life absent a source tying it directly to that service or a notable public career.

Kayla Shelton 2008-09

What the plaque reads
2009
Where this site places the term
2008-09
Office
President

Plaque plate reads "2009" and had been filed under 2009-10, overlapping Kevin Smiley there; moved to 2008-09. Shelton was executive vice president, elected in spring 2008 alongside Johnathon Boles, and became president - but not student regent - when Boles resigned on 30 Jan 2009, finishing out his 2008-09 term. Kevin Smiley won the next regular election on 16 April 2009 for the 2009-10 term, so Shelton's presidency did not extend into 2009-10. Confirmed: the Herald of April 2009 describes her as the sitting Student Government Association president, having taken office after Johnathon Boles resigned, and previously senator, student affairs chair, speaker of the senate and executive vice president. She did not take the seat on the Board of Regents with the presidency: that seat had been vacated by Boles's resignation and was filled separately by Reagan Gilley in a special election in February 2009.

The chambers plaque carries two consecutive plates for this year: "Janet 'Nicki' Seay" and "Nicki Taylor." Herald coverage shows one continuous officeholder, not a succession: she opened the year as "Nicki Seay" (Herald, 28 Aug and 25 Sep 2014), was already being called "Nicki Taylor" by the very next issue on 15 Oct 2014, and was still SGA president under that name through the 2 Apr 2015 election that chose her successor - no election, resignation or hand-off for a second officeholder appears anywhere in that span. Treated here as a single president who changed her surname partway through the term.

Reed Morgan 1968-69

What the plaque reads
1968
Where this site places the term
1968-69
Office
Role unresolved

Single-year plate reading 1968, sitting between William Menser and Bill Straeffer. Four independent checks now say it does not record a term as president, and the line of presidents is numbered as though it does not. First, a complete harvest of the digitised collection's article index, 11,850 items and 17,601 lines with no keyword filter, carries this name once: in the spring of 1966, as a graduating senior chairing the committee that drafted the Associated Students constitution and receiving President Kelly Thompson's approval letter on 1 April 1966. He appears nowhere in 1968. Second and third, the rosters the President's Office kept of the first Congress elected in May 1966 and of the Congress of 1967-68 both survive and name every officer and representative; he is on neither. Fourth, SGA's own roster of former presidents, published on its website and archived in September 2001, numbers the office continuously through this period, Haynes first, Menser second, Straeffer third, Zielke fourth, with no gap for anyone else and no mention of this name. The regent seat does not explain it either. The seat was created in April 1968 and Menser took it; the Herald's coverage of the Board of Regents from 1967 to 1969 never names Morgan. It has been suggested he might have served in 1968 as a graduate student, which is chronologically possible for a 1966 graduate, but no source found places him in any office that year. The likeliest reading remains that the plate honours the chair of the committee that wrote the constitution, rather than recording a term in office. The archive has not found a source that says so, and it will not move a name on an inference, so he is left here, marked unresolved, and is not counted in the line of presidents.

Spellings the project will not quietly fix

One name on the plaque is still recorded here exactly as it appears on it, because no source has been found that settles it: Keyanna, which appears elsewhere as Keyana. Guessing at a spelling would put a person in the record under a name they never used. Hargroave and Marcell are settled and no longer guesses: the Herald and SGA's own roster of former presidents, archived in September 2001, both give Hargrove and Marcel, so the site follows them and prints the plaque's reading beside each.

Telling us

If you served, or you know that a year here is wrong, the project wants to hear it. Corrections come through the Student Government Association office at Western Kentucky University, and they are recorded on this page with the evidence that settled them.

What this is

A year-by-year record of student government at Western Kentucky University, from the ratification of the Associated Students constitution in April 1966 to the present. Every entry names the source it came from.

Corrections

The names here start with the plaque in the SGA Chambers, and the plaque is known to be wrong in places. Where the archive disagrees with it, this site follows the archive and records the change on the corrections page. If you served, or you know that a year here is wrong, the project wants to hear from you: people who held an office can be given the keys to their own year on the contribute page.

What it rests on

Everything here comes from what is publicly available online: the digitised Herald and SGA's own papers on TopSCHOLAR, the Talisman yearbooks, wkuherald.com, the university's news pages and the Wayback Machine. That is a fraction of what happened. Student government has met most weeks for sixty years, and the greater part of it was never written down, or was written down on paper that has never been scanned. A thin year here is a thin record, not a quiet one.

Text on this site is the work of the project. Photographs, documents and legislation are reproduced from Western Kentucky University's own open archives and from the College Heights Herald and the Talisman; each item names the collection, issue and page it came from and links to the original.
