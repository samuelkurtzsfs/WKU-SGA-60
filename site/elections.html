Sixty years of choosing officers

The elections

Every election student government has held that the archive can see, year by year: who stood, who won, by how many where the count survives, and what went wrong along the way. The disputes are here with the results, because a fair number of these were decided twice, and one presidency was taken away by the Judicial Council and given back.

30 results across 61 years carry a head-to-head, and 459 dated entries carry the rest. Where a year has no result block it is because the archive has not settled one, not because the election was quiet: the count is often the part of an election that nobody wrote down.

  2. Caden Lucas wins SGA presidential election 15 April 2026

  9. Council censured a presidential ticket during the election 13 April 2026
    Acting on its own initiative rather than a complaint, the council found the Marshall, Blankenship and VanRude ticket guilty 4-0 of breaching section 3.11 of the election codes, which barred distribution of perishable food within 48 hours of an election, after Red Bull cans were handed out at their table and a photograph was reposted to their campaign story. Chief Justice Sophie Stirling and Associate Chief Justice Hannah Hash held that the legal test for perishability was not open to debate and that ticket members were responsible for educating their campaign team. The council issued a formal censure but declined to remove the ticket from the ballot.Judicial Council hearing minutes, 13 Apr 2026
  10. Council disbanded a campaign team and narrowed its own precedent 15 April 2026
    On an anonymous complaint of campaigning in the Commons while voting was open, backed by video the chief justice and associate chief justice obtained themselves, the council found the same ticket guilty 5-0 of breaching election codes 3.13 and 3.13.1 on polling places and not guilty on 3.6.8 on posters. It held that its precedent in Lee, Rosenbaum, Willmes v. Cook of fall 2025, which made a candidate wholly responsible for campaign materials, was too broad for acts by a team member, and narrowed it. It censured and disbanded the MBV campaign team rather than issuing the ticket a second censure, ruled that future breaches by designated team members would be attributable to the ticket, and required all tickets to file campaign team rosters before elections.Judicial Council hearing minutes, 15 Apr 2026
  11. Turnout jumps 66% in a contested race 15 April 2026

    Robinson and Mental Health and Wellbeing Committee chair Veronica Butler spent the year pressing WKU on student mental health, citing data from social work professor Jay Gabbard that 44 percent of students reported depression symptoms and 10 percent had considered suicide in the past year. SGA co-ran Mental Health Week with the WKU Counseling Center and campus NAMI chapter, launched a Toppers Talk About It campaign, and forwarded student recommendations - including moving counseling services out of Potter Hall - through the Counseling Center Review Committee. The year closed with an Out of Darkness suicide-remembrance walk on April 18; incoming president Caden Lucas said the focus would continue.Herald, 29 Apr 2026

  6. Fourteen constitutional amendments were read together 12 March 2024
    On 12 March 2024 the Senate gave a first reading to a fourteen-bill package, Bills 19-24-S through 32-24-S, drafted by a Constitutional Referendum Committee and sponsored by the Legislative Operations Committee with the Judicial Council, which sponsored all but 22-24-S. All fourteen carry a second reading of 28 March 2024. None of them records an outcome: the Pass, Fail and Other lines are blank on every bill of the session, so what the Senate and the Executive Cabinet did with them on the day is not on the documents. What the package proposed is what the constitution now in force says. The directorships of information technology and of enrollment and student experience are gone; the Executive Vice President and Administrative Vice President are now the Vice President and the Chief Financial Officer; the grade point average floors stand at 2.75 for the executive branch and 2.5 for the legislative and judicial branches; a member censured twice in an academic year is removed; executive spending must be documented; and committee heads are appointed by the Speaker. Each of those is traceable in the constitution as it stands — the 2.75 floor at section 2.2.1, the 2.5 floors at 3.5.1 and 4.2.3, removal after a second censure at 4.6.12, documented executive spending at 8.2, and the Speaker's appointment of committee heads at 3.8.1.2. Bill 30-24-S, which the archive previously described as tabled, is there too: the clause it drafted binding the Legislative Operations Committee to ensure bills are nonpartisan stands at 3.8.2.5 in the bill's own words.SGA Bills 19-24-S to 32-24-S and WKU SGA Legislation Archive
  7. SGA joined Hope Harbor for Denim Day 12 March 2024
    Bill 16-24-S allocated $280 from the legislative discretionary fund to Hope Harbor X Denim Day: Inspiring the Next Generation of Volunteers. Hope Harbor is a Bowling Green non-profit supporting people affected by sexual trauma, and Denim Day is an international rape prevention education campaign that asks students, businesses and elected officials to wear jeans as a visible protest against misconceptions about sexual violence. The bill was read a second time on 12 March 2024.SGA Bill 16-24-S, Funding for the Hope Harbor X Denim Day Event
  8. Senate lowers the bar for changing its own constitution 28 March 2024
    Bill 22-24-S, one of the package given a second reading on 28 March 2024, would amend clause 10.1 so that the constitution may be amended by a two-thirds vote of the present senators rather than of the full membership of the Senate. The bill stated that the old rule limited amendment votes to meetings at which at least two-thirds of the full Senate were in attendance. Bill 23-24-S, read the same night, would move constitutional amendment elections from the end of the fall and spring semesters to the fall and spring senator elections, citing higher turnout in representative elections. Neither bill sheet records a vote, but both changes are in the constitution now in force, in the words the bills drafted: 10.1 turns on the present senators, and 10.2.1 sets amendment elections at the fall and spring senator elections.SGA Bills 22-24-S and 23-24-S

What this is

A year-by-year record of student government at Western Kentucky University, from the ratification of the Associated Students constitution in April 1966 to the present. Every entry names the source it came from.

Corrections

The names here start with the plaque in the SGA Chambers, and the plaque is known to be wrong in places. Where the archive disagrees with it, this site follows the archive and records the change on the corrections page. If you served, or you know that a year here is wrong, the project wants to hear from you: people who held an office can be given the keys to their own year on the contribute page.

What it rests on

Everything here comes from what is publicly available online: the digitised Herald and SGA's own papers on TopSCHOLAR, the Talisman yearbooks, wkuherald.com, the university's news pages and the Wayback Machine. That is a fraction of what happened. Student government has met most weeks for sixty years, and the greater part of it was never written down, or was written down on paper that has never been scanned. A thin year here is a thin record, not a quiet one.
